Chapter 317

Not that she could make sense of any of it anyway.

Hearing the door, Marcus stopped working immediately, pushed up his glasses, shut the laptop, and stood, his expression warm.

"You're back."

This was only the second time they'd really been alone together.

The first time, she'd pulled him out of that basement.

He'd been drawn to her from the very first moment he saw her.

Paisley didn't know him well, barely any real overlap between them. She gave a polite nod, her tone distant but courteous.

"Keep working. I'm going to make dinner."

She knew exactly why she'd brought him along in the first place — his research value. Somewhere down the line, in three years' time, he was the key to cracking the undead virus and developing a real cure.

So far, Marcus had been steady and quiet, never causing trouble, never competing for resources, never adding friction to the group. He was, honestly, easy to have around.

Marcus watched her walk toward the kitchen and spoke up gently.

"Need a hand?"

He wanted an excuse to be near her, to talk to her a little longer.

The villa's pantry was well-stocked — fresh vegetables, eggs, meat in the fridge, rice and grain in the cabinets. Plenty to put together a real meal.

Without slowing down, Paisley reached into her storage and pulled out a lobster, easily five and a half pounds, its shell a bright, glossy red, still very much alive.

She set it on the counter and glanced over at Marcus, her tone matter-of-fact.

"Rinse the rice and start the cooker."

"Sure."

He got right to it, a little clumsy, measuring out rice, rinsing it, pressing the start button.

The kitchen filled with warm light, the atmosphere settling into something quiet and comfortable.

Paisley rolled up her sleeves, baring slim, pale wrists, and set to work on the lobster, her movements practiced and efficient.

A clean cut split the firm red shell, revealing plump white meat, tight-grained and glistening, no hint of any off smell.

Her hands moved fast and clean — devein, portion, cut into pieces. She worked through the rest of the haul next, pulling the crab, scallops, and shrimp from storage, cleaning each one with care.

Marcus stood quietly to the side, not getting in the way, tidying the counter and wiping down the sink, his eyes drifting occasionally to her focused profile.

The warm light softened her usual sharpness, lending her an unfamiliar, gentle warmth.

Someone usually so decisive and formidable, standing here cooking, calm and unguarded — the contrast struck him.

He stood there quietly, thinking to himself.

He hoped this moment could last.

Before long, the seafood was fully prepped.

Paisley laid it out — some lobster and crab steamed plain to preserve the natural sweetness, some stir-fried with a rich, savory sauce, scallops topped with garlic and glass noodles, steamed hard and fast, the smell flooding the kitchen instantly.

The rich scent of seafood spread through the entire house, chasing away the cold, grim air that always seemed to linger these days, replacing it with something warm and alive.

Marcus stood off to the side, taking in the colorful spread laid out across the counter, something like quiet surprise crossing his face.

He'd never imagined, in a world this short on resources, where everyone was just trying to eat enough to survive, that a meal like this was even possible.

She really could do anything.

The smell of rice mixed with the sweetness of fresh seafood, warm and comforting.

Paisley covered the steamer, adjusting the heat, then glanced over at Marcus, her tone easy.

"If you're bored, go wash those vegetables."

"Sure."

He nodded and moved to the sink, washing the greens carefully.

The kitchen stayed quiet, just the sound of running water and the soft hiss of the steamer, warm and alive.

Twenty minutes later, dish after dish came off the stove.

Garlic noodle scallops sizzled with residual heat, garlic and seafood mingling in the air; the steamed lobster gleamed, meat firm and white; the stir-fried crab glistened red, the sauce rich and fragrant.

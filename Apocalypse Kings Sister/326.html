Chapter 326

A deep rumble tore through the air.

Freed of its locks, the heavy alloy door swung inward.

A rush of cold, foul air burst out — blood, rot, and sharp chemical residue all at once.

The dim interior came into view. Dark, stained patches of dried blood covered the walls. Discarded equipment lay scattered everywhere, broken glassware and loose parts littering the floor — a scene of total ruin.

Deep inside the main chamber, a dozen or more twisted, misshapen figures stood motionless.

These were the facility's failed experiments — humanoid test subjects.

Each one looked different. Some had arms swollen to several times their normal size, flesh warped and raw. Others had bone spurs jutting from their spines, clothing hanging in tattered shreds.

Every one of them shared the same sickly gray-white skin, clouded eyes, no trace of coherent thought left — nothing but the rawest, most primal bloodlust buried in their pupils.

The moment the door swung open, every subject turned at once, their hollow, violent eyes locking onto the six figures in the doorway, a chorus of low, rasping growls rising from their throats, harsh and wrong.

"Stay sharp, get ready."

Gabriel gave the order, his Metal Affinity surging fully to life, a brilliant gold light flaring around him as a blade of condensed metal formed in his palm, and he moved to engage.

The test subjects broke loose.

Feeling nothing, they charged on all fours, moving fast enough to blur, their heavy footsteps hammering the floor, cold air rushing through the room ahead of their ruined bodies as they bore down on the group.

The three leading the charge moved with startling power, their claws tearing the air with enough force to split it, closing in fast.

Gabriel moved first, a blur of motion, his form cutting a clean arc through the dim light.

His condensed metal blade flashed, sidestepping the kill zones entirely, targeting joints and limbs instead. Three precise strikes landed in quick succession, severing the forelimbs of all three subjects in one fluid motion.

A spray of dark, corrupted blood splattered across the floor, severed limbs dropping, the scene brutal and clean.

But what happened next unsettled everyone.

The wounds began knitting themselves shut, visibly, in real time, torn flesh regenerating, reconnecting, scabbing over in seconds.

Within two seconds, the gruesome wounds had fully healed, the severed limbs regrown entirely. Whatever bloodlust had driven them before only intensified, and they surged forward again, more vicious than before.

"Their regeneration's too strong. Normal methods aren't going to kill these things."

Lightning crackled across Elijah's palm, blue-white light coiling around his hand, cutting through the gloom. He swung, and a thick bolt of lightning tore through the air, locking onto one of the subjects, electricity surging through its body, flesh sizzling under the charge.

Orion stepped forward immediately, Fire Affinity surging through him, flames roaring to life in his palm, heat radiating off him in waves.

He swept his arm out, a wall of fire rolling forward, engulfing the front row of subjects. The flames clung to them, burning steadily, slowing their advance just enough.

The squad's lone Earth Affinity team leader planted both hands against the floor, a heavy wave of earth energy rolling outward.

The floor buckled upward, several thick stone walls rising in sequence, blocking the swarm pouring in from behind.

The walls shuddered under the impact, cracks spreading fast as the subjects slammed into them again and again — they wouldn't hold much longer.

The group's defense was already under heavy strain, the fight tipping toward something dangerous.

The subjects scorched black by fire were already regenerating, visibly, burnt flesh writhing back together, bones knitting, skin reforming — fully healed within moments, charging forward again without missing a beat.

Their healing speed was absurd. These things weren't test subjects anymore. They were unkillable killing machines.

Paisley, shielded in the center of the group, watched it all unfold through the chaos of bodies and motion.

Something clicked, cold and certain — this matched exactly what they'd seen at the aquarium facility.

Extreme regeneration. No conventional method could put them down.

"Their crystal cores are in their foreheads! Dig those out and it's over!"

Paisley raised her hand, Affinity surging out, countless green vines tearing up from the floor, thick and pliant, weaving together like a massive net, wrapping around the limbs and bodies of every charging subject in an instant.

Chapter 349

Her father looked out the window and let out a heavy sigh, his tone grave.

"Orion. You're the eldest. From now on, this family's weight falls on your shoulders."

"You need to understand exactly where things stand in this base right now. No room for carelessness.

The whole base is a tangle of competing interests, split three ways, each side keeping the others in check."

"The Blackwoods, the Quinns, and the Lennox main family.

We're aligned with the Blackwoods."

"When the apocalypse hit and everything fell apart, if the Blackwoods hadn't backed us completely, this house, what's left of our holdings, everything we've built over the years — all of it would've been gone."

"They're our biggest shield, our whole foundation. If the Blackwoods ever fall, everything we have right now — the safety, the resources, the standing — goes with them, and there's no coming back from that."

Her father kept his eyes on the window, worry etched deep into his face, his tone growing heavier.

"There's something else you need to know. Your uncle's family already switched sides to the Quinns."

One sentence, and it summed up the whole cold truth of a world where even blood ties broke along faction lines.

"Now that you and your sisters and brothers are all safely back, this family's whole future rests on you."

He lowered his eyes, a note of weariness in his voice.

"I'm getting old. I never awakened an Affinity after the outbreak either. Just an ordinary man who can't carry this family through a storm like this."

He looked up at Orion, a note of hope in his question.

"What Affinity have you awakened? How strong are you now?"

Orion nodded firmly, his tone grave.

"I understand, Dad. I'll hold this family together. I'll protect everyone."

He went on, plain and direct.

"I'm Level Six Metal Affinity. Spencer's Level Six Plant, Thomas is Level Six Fire, and Ashley's Level Six Earth."

He paused slightly at the end, his expression turning careful, his voice dropping lower.

"Pais is different. She's got at least two Affinities — Storage and Plant. Dad, this has to stay completely secret. No one outside this family can ever know."

"What's on record is just Level Six Plant."

"All of us, every one, are Level Six."

Her father's eyes went wide with disbelief, days of worry wiped away in an instant, his voice trembling with excitement.

"Good. Good! That's wonderful!"

"Level Six users are rare across the whole base, and here you all are, every single one of you! This is the best news I've heard in years!"

He steadied himself and pressed further.

"Your talent's always been good, but this kind of speed is unheard of. How did you all climb to Level Six so fast?"

"All thanks to Pais."

At the mention of her name, pride and warmth flooded Orion's face, his tone genuinely proud.

"She found a sacred fungus and made soup for all of us. It helped us break through our bottlenecks so fast it blew past anything normal."

"Spencer and Thomas owe their Affinities to her too, honestly.

We only made it back alive because of her."

They'd assumed they were just bringing home a long-lost sister who needed their protection and care.

But looking back, it wasn't them protecting her at all. She'd been the one holding up the whole family's future single-handedly, bringing them all home safe.

Orion steadied himself and laid it all out in detail — the zombie sieges, the ambushes, the shortages, and Paisley stepping in every single time, carrying the team through every disaster on her own.

The study fell so quiet you could hear a pin drop.

Her father listened without a word, his face moving from shock to awe to something quietly moved and aching.

When Orion finished, he sat frozen for a long moment, a tangle of emotion in his chest.

His throat worked once, his voice rough as he murmured it over and over.

"Good... good... she really is my daughter..."

That kind of character, that kind of strength, that kind of heart — she outstripped everyone her age by miles.

After a moment, Orion gathered himself and asked the question that had been on his mind.

"Dad, why did Uncle's family side with the Quinns, flat against us?"

Chapter 59

Elijah darted forward, quick and precise on his feet. He dodged the zombies' lunges as his blade swept through the air, slicing clean through several of their necks.

With a few wet, sickening crunches, black blood sprayed, and heads hit the ground. Every strike was clean and fast, without a second of hesitation.

The rest of the zombies on both sides split up right away to flank them. They were trying to sneak in from the sides, surround the three guys, and break up their formation.

Orion narrowed his eyes. His Metal Affinity summoned a swarm of metal blades that stabbed through the feet of the charging zombies, pinning dozens of them in place.

Right after that, several blades spun through the air in wild, unpredictable arcs and lopped off the heads of the pinned zombies.

One head after another dropped to the ground with a wet thud.

Mason swung his blade with brutal, heavy force!

Thud after thud rang out as the zombies rushing him went flying, and bones cracked one after another.

Mason's fighting style was all about fast blocks, heavy hits, wide sweeps, and finishing blows, all in one smooth flow. Everywhere he went, zombies dropped by the dozen.

The three of them worked together like a well-oiled machine.

Snarls, the whoosh of blades, and the crunch of breaking bones all blended together. It was one hell of a fight...

Meanwhile, Paisley easily shot the lock off the silo door and walked inside.

The second the door swung open, the fresh smell of grain hit her. Everything was dry and in perfect condition, thanks to the sealed, climate-controlled storage.

The three silos held an insane amount of food. Mountains of sacks were stacked as far as the eye could see, filling every inch of the place.

The first silo held nothing but vacuum-sealed bags of premium oats, stacked neatly all the way up to the domed ceiling. Every bag was dry and perfectly preserved.

The second silo was filled with bags of fine, pure white wheat flour.

The third silo was packed with bundles and boxes of dried pasta, from spaghetti and macaroni to whole-wheat penne.

Paisley's face lit up. With a single thought, the endless stacks of oats, flour, and pasta vanished like a wave pulling back from the shore.

Bag after bag of oats and flour and box after box of pasta disappeared, whole stacks and whole sections at a time. She cleaned out everything from the ceiling to the floor and from the main silos to the side storerooms.

Not even the loose grain on the floor, the spare bags, the storage tools, or the moisture-proof liners escaped her. Paisley picked the place clean.

In just a few minutes, all three massive government silos were completely empty.

When Paisley strolled out of the silo, the guys had just finished wiping out the zombies in the yard.

Zombie corpses were scattered all over the ground.

All three of them were breathing hard, their foreheads damp with sweat, but they were still riding the high of the fight. Their strength, their combat experience, and their teamwork had all gotten a lot better.

"All clear!"

Orion was still pumped from the fight, but he looked at Paisley with concern. "You get everything?"

Paisley nodded. "Yep, all done."

Orion could have kept going, but his ability was almost tapped out. "Then let's go."

Paisley glanced up at the sky. "It's about to pour. We need to head back."

The four of them piled into the car.

Just as they were about to take off, a filthy, ragged figure stumbled out of the weeds and rubble by the road. She threw herself in front of the car, blocking their way.

It was Rachel!

She looked nothing like the glamorous rich girl she used to be. She was such a wreck that she was almost unrecognizable.

To cover her tracks and keep the zombies and mutant beasts from hunting her down, she'd rolled through mud pits, rotting dirt, and filthy sludge the whole way. Now, she was caked in thick black mud.

Her once-silky hair was matted with mud and dead grass and plastered to her cheeks and neck. Her face was buried under so much grime that it was impossible to tell what she looked like.

What was left of her pretty dress was ripped to shreds and covered in mud and bits of dead grass. Even her bare wrists, neck, and ankles were smeared with cold, wet mud.

There wasn't a clean inch on her from head to toe. She reeked of dirt and rot, like a beggar who'd clawed her way out of a mud pit.

After the mutant carried her off, it had forced her to have sex with it. While it was asleep, she'd slipped the poison she always carried into its water, and it drank every drop.

That was how she'd finally gotten her chance to run, and she'd barely made it out alive.

I can't believe my luck. Real people, all the way out here?

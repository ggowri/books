Chapter 155

******

Paisley looked over the group, serious.

"Stay sharp. There are plenty of zombies in here. Pair up, and don't take unnecessary risks."

As she spoke, her fingers moved, and a handful of small, cool, silver metal tokens appeared in her palm — simple, round, unassuming. Nothing about them hinted at the space folded inside.

She handed one to each person.

"Storage tokens. A hundred square feet of private space inside, each one. Use them for crystal cores, anything you pick up today."

Everyone stared down at the little tokens in their palms, stunned.

Ashley ran a finger over the cool metal, eyes wide.

"These are real? I thought storage tokens were just something out of sci-fi novels — I never thought they actually existed!"

Spencer turned his over, marveling.

"A hundred square feet, folded into something the size of a button. That's insane."

The group kept passing the tokens around, awestruck.

Paisley cleared her throat, ears warming slightly, privately relieved.

If it weren't for last night's bulk trade with the interstellar S-tier matriarch, she wouldn't have a whole box of these to spare — and she'd never have handed out something this rare so freely.

More importantly, these tokens gave her a perfect cover. Storing and pulling out supplies from now on had a built-in excuse. No one would ever question it.

She tucked the thought away and explained the mechanics.

"Two ways to use it."

"First — just press the little button on the side. Simple, fast. Downside, no living things, and nothing inside stays fresh. Also easy for someone else to take from you."

"Second — a blood bond.

A drop of blood on the token locks it to you. After that, it's yours alone. No one else can open it.

Bonding unlocks a preservation function too, but living things still don't work."

By the time she finished, the group had gone silent with disbelief, faces lit with something close to awe.

Everyone understood exactly what this meant. In a world where carrying supplies was a constant struggle, a stable, private storage space was priceless.

No more hauling everything by hand, no more losing half of it to spoilage on the road. A hundred square feet covered everything a person could need to live.

Ashley clutched hers, visibly moved.

"Paisley, this is insanely generous. Giving one of these to each of us?"

Marcus looked down at the token in his palm, something settling firmer in his chest. Whatever Paisley had access to, it went far beyond what any of them had guessed. It only made him want to stay closer, to protect her, all the more.

Thomas and Spencer felt the weight of it too — the trust behind the gesture meant more than the token itself.

Madison and Mason cradled theirs, awe and gratitude swirling together, both quietly resolving the same thing.

If Paisley was willing to hand out something this rare, she'd already decided they were hers. They owed her. One day, they'd pay it back properly.

Madison spoke first, a little guilty.

"This is too valuable. We can't just take it — how do we pay you back?"

After all, the rules of the world had changed completely.

Mason nodded along, earnest.

"She's right, Miss Lennox. We can't keep taking from you with nothing in return."

Ashley, Spencer, and Thomas sobered too, looking at Paisley with open discomfort.

"Paisley, this is too rare. We don't have anything worth trading for it."

They all remembered, too — they still owed her two hundred Level Two crystal cores for last night's soup.

Watching them all fumble with guilt, clutching the tokens like they didn't quite believe them, Paisley finally spoke, settling the matter.

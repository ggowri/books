Chapter 311

The youngest hadn't awakened an Affinity either, grinding through the dirtiest, hardest labor jobs just to scrape by.

All three of them were ordinary people now, stuck at the bottom with nowhere to turn. Stepping outside the base walls meant certain death without an Affinity to protect them.

Their only hope was scraping together enough points to save Owen — the one chance they had of ever clawing their way out of the bottom.

Reid had cleaned himself up and come out today hoping to get lucky — find some wealthy woman or a high-tier Affinity user to latch onto, something to pull him out of this grind, maybe even land a decent meal with actual fat in it for once.

Instead, his plan had blown up in his face, landing him in public humiliation.

Growing up, he'd been the golden child of the Lennox household. He'd never been treated like this in his life.

He couldn't bring himself to actually slap himself fifty times.

And the two hundred points he needed, he didn't have, not even close.

Cornered and desperate, his eyes swept the crowd and landed on a familiar, composed figure.

Paisley.

She stood near the back of the crowd, tall and poised, standing out from everyone else without even trying. That effortless, untouchable calm she carried now — it was something he'd never be able to reach again.

Right then, the shopkeeper hurried over to Paisley, beaming.

"Ma'am, congratulations! You've spent over twenty thousand points today, which qualifies you for a free premium lucky draw! Could get you a high-tier crystal core, maybe even a defensive relic. Right this way, please."

The number — twenty thousand points — landed in Reid's ears with crystal clarity.

It hit him like a physical blow.

Twenty thousand points.

He could work himself to death for years and never save up half that much.

And Paisley had just dropped it on a handful of gifts without a second thought.

The gap between them hit him like a wave, jealousy, resentment, and humiliation tangling together until he could barely breathe.

He stared at her, something desperate flaring in his eyes, like he'd just spotted his last lifeline. He shoved through the crowd and stormed toward her.

People scattered out of his way, murmurs rippling through the crowd, every eye now locked on the two of them.

He skidded to a stop in front of her, chest heaving, the red handprint still fresh on his cheek, sweat and grime streaking his face.

He looked at her — composed, untouchable — and what rose up in him wasn't guilt. It was entitlement.

In his mind, Paisley had always owed the Lennoxes. She'd been raised in that house, fed and sheltered by them. Now that he was in trouble, her helping him out was just how things were supposed to work.

"Paisley, you have to help me."

His words came out fast, that old condescension still bleeding through, like he was giving an order instead of asking for help.

"I just accidentally got some water on her clothes. She wants two hundred points for it."

"You just dropped twenty thousand without blinking. Two hundred is nothing to you. Just pay her for me."

He even tilted his chin up slightly as he said it, some leftover arrogance surfacing, like he genuinely believed she couldn't say no.

She'd grown up eating Lennox food. In front of this many people, surely she wouldn't just let him stand here and take the hit.

The crowd murmured in disbelief. Nobody had expected this broke janitor to demand anything from a woman who'd just dropped twenty thousand points like it was pocket change — let alone with that kind of entitlement.

The woman crossed her arms, watching with a raised eyebrow, settling in for the show.

Paisley stood still, the fox nuzzling against her neck like it sensed something.

Her eyes stayed flat, unmoved, as she looked at Reid's desperate, grasping face. Two words, nothing more.

"No."

"Who are you to me?"

"Why would I?"

Clean, immediate, no hesitation at all.

Reid froze, his arrogance cracking into disbelief, then into something close to panic.

Chapter 76

Thank God I never got on Her Royal Highness's bad side.

Elijah, on the other hand, wasn't surprised at all.

He'd already seen what that little white cat could do.

It was a Level Four Ice Affinity mutant cat, after all.

Owen had tried to hurt Paisley, so ending up like this was his own damn fault. He'd gotten exactly what he deserved.

Paisley looked at Owen writhing on the ground in agony, then at Rachel, drained and pale. Her face was blank. She felt nothing but bone-deep indifference.

Just then, Reid and Ryan came out of the villa.

They were regular people with no abilities, and they didn't dare mess with Paisley anymore.

They kept their heads down and hurried over to take care of Owen.

Around the same time, Orion and the others finished packing and came outside too.

Orion's eyes went straight to the shattered pendant on the ground, its pieces scattered everywhere. He opened his mouth to ask what had happened.

But Paisley had gotten her revenge, and she was in a great mood.

She picked up the broken pendant and tugged Orion toward the car. "It's nothing, Orion. Let's go!"

Elijah had seen right through Paisley's little act from the start.

He got in the car first. "Let's move out."

Everyone else piled in after him.

Jasper couldn't care less whether Owen lived or died. "Let's go," he ordered his men. "Follow them."

He'd seen what they could do. On the way back, sticking close to them was the only way he'd make it to Aetheris in one piece.

Luckily, he'd picked up quite a few new ability users over the past two days.

Rachel knew it too. She had to stick with Jasper's group. Staying behind wasn't an option.

Owen's injuries didn't matter to her either. She just had Reid and Ryan haul him into the car.

And just like that, the whole group rolled out in one big convoy.

Paisley popped a lollipop into her mouth and stuck one in the kitten's mouth too.

When she caught sight of Rachel's group in the rearview mirror, she burst out laughing.

"Karma's a bitch. Serves them right.

"Owen's hurt that bad, and they won't even let him rest. They're just dragging him along like that."

Orion glanced back at the end of the convoy.

"Pais, if you don't want them following us, want me to go take care of them?

"And what happened to your pendant? Did they do that?"

Paisley shook her head. "Don't worry about them, Orion. Sometimes, letting them live in misery is worse than killing them."

She took out the pendant and sighed. "It's just a shame it broke."

Even though it was a fake, it was still worth a fortune.

It really was a waste that Rachel had smashed it like that.

Clearly, Rachel had been up to no good, just like Paisley had thought.

But at least now, she'd never go after the Storage Dimension pendant again.

As Orion drove, he tried to cheer her up.

"Once I find some gold, I'll fix it for you. It'll look even better than before, I promise. Don't be sad."

Paisley smiled and pulled a heavy gold bar straight out of her Storage Dimension, holding it out to Orion. "Here. Give it a shot."

Orion caught sight of the gleaming, high-quality gold bar out of the corner of his eye. His hands froze on the wheel for a second, and he looked stunned. Then, a fond, helpless smile spread across his face.

Paisley had just casually pulled out a solid gold bar weighing almost two ounces.

Talk about blowing money like it's nothing.

"Oh, you," Orion said with a low laugh, sounding fond and exasperated. He turned the wheel and pulled over on the side of the road outside the city.

Paisley was his little sister. Of course he was going to spoil her.

Orion took the broken pieces and the gold from Paisley and called on his Metal Affinity.

Invisible energy flowed out of him. It wrapped around the heavy gold bar, then settled around the broken pieces of the pendant.

He didn't need to cut or hammer anything. Under the control of his Metal Affinity, the gold bar softened into smooth, liquid gold. It flowed into every crack in the pendant and pulled all the pieces perfectly back together.

The liquid gold hardened as it followed the pendant's original pattern, slowly shaping itself into a spray of delicate, raised lilies. Every petal was crisp and distinct, and the slender stems covered every last crack perfectly.

In just a few minutes, the shattered pendant looked brand new, with elegant gold lilies winding across its surface.

The smooth, pale stone glowed against the bright gold. It looked classy and luxurious, even more stunning and unique than the plain design it had before.

Chapter 195

In her last life, her point balance had never once cracked five digits, not even at the end.

In a world that ate people alive, getting strong yourself was really the only way through.

Soon the door opened, and cafeteria staff wheeled in a cart.

The simmering pot went down first, the red broth bubbling away, rich steam rising thick and fragrant, filling the room with mist. The warm, spicy smell cut straight through the cold, dead quiet that had settled over this world for years.

Plate after plate came out, neat and full—tender meat, mushrooms, greens, meatballs, tofu, all of it bright and whole. In a world like this, it counted as the height of luxury.

The steam curled around every face in the room, warm and soft, chasing off days of mission fatigue, and the whole room came alive.

Ashley, liveliest of them all, grabbed her fork before anyone else, eyes shining.

“Let's eat, let's eat! This smells amazing. Thanks, Paisley—we'd never get a spread like this if it weren't for you!”

The mood caught fire. Everyone dug in, laughter weaving through the warmth of the room.

They ate like it was nothing, but every so often their eyes drifted toward Paisley, each of them quietly trying to outdo the others.

Orion sat without saying much, quietly dropping the tenderest cuts of meat into the pot and, once cooked, fishing them straight into her bowl, the whole motion effortless, gentle in every detail.

Elijah served everyone else with practiced ease, all the while cooking the vegetables she liked best, the fondness in his eyes impossible to hide.

“Slow down, don't burn yourself.”

Spencer and Thomas weren't about to be left out either, taking turns feeding her in kind—one with meat, the other with vegetables, practically racing to do it the moment her bowl ran low, childish as it was, every bit of it sincere.

Marcus said the least, sitting apart with his usual reserve, but he kept track of her all the same, dropping in her favorite ingredients whenever the pot came to a boil.

The whole room steamed with warmth, laughter never stopping, full of the kind of life this world so rarely offered.

Fed from every direction until her bowl overflowed, Paisley's cheeks had gone a soft pink from the heat, warmth settling deep in her chest.

In a world gone cold and bare, this small room held more warmth than she'd known in a long time.

******

Early the next morning, today was the day for the aquarium mission. Everyone geared up, ready to push into the contaminated zone and retrieve the sealed research data and equipment inside.

As usual before deployment, every team member gathered in the top-floor command office to go over assignments, routes, and contingency plans.

By the time Paisley, Elijah, and Orion arrived, the room was already full of the base's best Affinity users, the mood tight and serious.

At the front, near the head seat, someone lounged with deliberate flair, arrogance radiating off him, every line of his posture shouting entitlement.

This was Chase Sullivan, the early Level Five Psychic Affinity user the Director had specifically brought in to assist.

His psychic power was a rare kind—able to sense danger over a wide area, detect movement, even cow mutant beasts into stillness. On high-risk missions, there was no replacing that.

That singular ability had earned him an untouchable position at Rivergate, privileges no one else got.

He never bothered with the base's day-to-day missions, large or small.

And yet he got the best cultivation resources, the best treatment the base had, free to act however he pleased without anyone daring to say a word.

Over time he'd only grown more arrogant, more insufferable, and most people at the base had learned to just stay out of his way and bear it.

If this mission hadn't been dangerous enough to genuinely need him, the higher-ups wouldn't have bothered asking.

Getting him to lift a finger never came cheap.

Chase glanced over the room as people filed in, lazy and arrogant, and when he opened his mouth, there was no room for argument in it.

“I'm leading this one. Non-negotiable.”

“What I say goes, no debate. Or I walk right now, and you can all go die in there without me.”

Every word of it domineering, with no trace of teamwork—just a man trading on his own rarity and the base's indulgence.

The veteran team members and high-tier Affinity users in the room all stayed quiet, no one willing to push back, just swallowing it the way they always did.

That was when Paisley's group walked in.

The moment Chase spotted her, slight and striking among the others, contempt and a flicker of amusement crossed his face, his arrogance climbing another notch.

He let out a short laugh and leaned in, his tone light and mocking, pitched just loud enough for everyone to hear.

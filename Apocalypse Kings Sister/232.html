Chapter 232

Jealousy coiled tight and ugly in Rachel's chest. Her hands clenched at her sides, resentment and bitterness stacking layer on layer.

The pendant. She had to get her hands on it.

It had belonged to her in her last life. It belonged to her in this one too.

While Rachel seethed, the mood in the front rows of the hall ran hot in the opposite direction.

Elijah's gaze stayed fixed on the girl standing calm and composed on the stage, something warm and unmistakable glowing behind his eyes.

With the smoke cleared and the whole hall's attention on her, Paisley looked impossible to look away from.

Orion stood tall, pride written plain across his face, leaning over to murmur to the rest of the Thompsons.

"See that? That's Paisley up there. Beautiful, isn't she? And even more badass."

Ashley, the youngest, stared up at Paisley with bright, shining eyes, pure excitement in her voice as she cheered quietly.

"Paisley, you're incredible! I'm so proud of you!

I'm going to train hard too, and someday I'll be just as strong, just as brave as her."

Thomas's eyes shone with pride.

"That's Paisley. Unbelievable. She turned this whole fight around single-handedly and saved everyone at this base."

Spencer looked equally moved.

"She's incredible. If Mom and Dad knew what she'd pulled off, they'd be so proud of her."

Off in a quiet corner of the hall, Owen Lennox, Reid Lennox, and Ryan Lennox watched Paisley hold her medal on stage, a tangle of regret and emptiness in their chests.

The whole brutal fight today, the line nearly breaking more than once, countless high-tier mutants crashing against the defense, and the three of them had barely managed to take down a few low-tier stragglers between them, even combining their efforts.

The meager points they'd earned from the fight barely covered a few pieces of hardtack and some water, not even enough to cover basic survival.

Meanwhile, Paisley had walked away with a single-handed legend, ten thousand points, a First-Class Commendation, heights the three of them might never reach in a lifetime of fighting.

The gap hit them hard, regret washing over them like a tide.

If they hadn't let themselves get blinded, if they hadn't sided with Rachel over and over, hadn't punished and neglected and hurt Paisley again and again,

if they hadn't cut ties with her so decisively, none of them would be standing here now, this broken, this low.

They were finally starting to understand. Maybe all those years of conflict, all those fights over who was right, hadn't been Paisley's fault at all.

Rachel had always known how to play the victim, how to twist the truth and win sympathy, which had led them, time after time, to take her side and push away the one who'd actually meant it.

It was almost funny, in the darkest way. They'd clung to blood ties and thought protecting fragile Rachel was the right thing to do.

The person actually worth keeping, they'd pushed away with their own hands.

The hollow, poisonous fake, they'd treated like something precious.

Only now did they truly understand. They'd pushed away the sister who'd always put them first, the sweet, thoughtful girl who'd never once asked for anything back.

Endless regret wound tight around their chests, the three of them stiff and silent, the bitterness too much to put into words.

If they hadn't severed ties, this honor, this glory everyone else envied, would have belonged partly to them too.

At the very least, Paisley wouldn't have let them go hungry.

The ceremony wound down. Paisley took her medal and walked down off the stage.

Tall, composed, her expression cool and distant, the medal gleaming against her chest, only making her presence shine brighter.

Owen, Reid, and Ryan's hearts seized at once. They stepped forward instinctively, a faint, desperate hope rising in them. She'd always been soft-hearted, always cared so much about them. Maybe, just maybe, she'd forgive them.

But the next moment, they watched Paisley walk straight past them, heading in another direction entirely, toward the Thompsons.

The Thompsons closed around her immediately, open affection and pride in every face, warm laughter and welcome wrapping her in at the center of it all.

Meanwhile, the three of them sat off in an unnoticed corner, out of place, humiliated.

The Paisley they'd once thrown away without a second thought now stood somewhere they could never reach, surrounded by a family who genuinely cherished her.

They wanted to close the distance, but there was no room left for them anywhere near her now, every space already filled by people who truly cared.

Their stomachs growled. That was their reality now.

The ceremony ended, the crowd drifting apart, the noise settling into quiet.

Paisley returned alone to her assigned room.

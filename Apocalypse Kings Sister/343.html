Chapter 343

Paisley answered easily.

[I trust you. We're going to be working together for a long time — better for both of us that way.]

[Besides, you won't know if the trade's worth it until you try it yourself.]

Warmth flickered across the woman's face, and she nodded seriously.

[I give you my word. I won't go back on this.]

The link cut out.

Trovie bounced around the three warehouses, giddy.

[Host, you're unbelievable. Look at all this. We're rich!]

Paisley yawned.

[Where's my reward?]

Trovie smacked its own head.

[Bulk trade bonus: 10,000 points!]

[Host's current total: 1,415,360 points!]

[Host! I'm almost close enough to the threshold to take physical form! Keep closing trades, keep stacking points, and soon I'll really be able to stand beside you!]

A small smile crossed Paisley's face.

[Good. I'll work harder, then.]

With that settled, she pulled her mind back and left the Storage Dimension, returning to her real room. After washing up, she lay down to rest.

Meanwhile,

on the second floor of the base hospital, in the ICU.

Night pressed in thick and dark outside, and inside the room, only the beep of machines broke the oppressive silence.

Reid sat rigid by the bed, his eyes bloodshot, staring at Rachel.

In just a few hours, her face had turned even paler and gaunt, her breath shallower, her whole body withering — the life visibly draining out of her.

Reid's chest churned with panic.

She was still the sister he cared about most.

Even if she was selfish, even if she treated people badly sometimes.

He didn't want her to just die like this.

She was still more useful to him alive, after all.

Seeing her wither like this only made him think of Paisley, and the hatred set his teeth on edge.

Even someone as powerful as Mr. Quinn hadn't managed to force Paisley to come heal her. Was his sister really going to sleep forever, never waking up?

Just then, the door swung open.

Damon walked in, cold and composed as always, an aide trailing behind him carrying a bowl of medicine, steam curling off it, the rich scent of fungus heavy in the air.

Reid shot to his feet, confused.

"Mr. Quinn, what's this?"

Damon's expression stayed flat, and he didn't bother answering.

Reid simply wasn't worth the effort.

The aide stepped in instead, polite enough.

"This is medicine brewed from the sacred fungus our master bought tonight, at a very steep price. Drink it and they're sure to be cured and woken up."

That landed on Reid like a thunderclap, his pupils blown wide, his mind reeling.

He'd overheard the nurses outside, gossiping about the auction's grand prize that afternoon — how the sacred fungus was a miracle, able to awaken Affinities in ordinary people and push existing users to new heights.

He'd never imagined something that precious would end up in Mr. Quinn's hands, let alone be brewed and brought right here.

His gaze locked onto the bowl, greedy and burning with want.

An Affinity!

He'd dreamed of having one his whole life!

He didn't want to keep being some powerless nobody everyone could push around!

He didn't want to be beaten and humiliated in public, unable to even fight back!

He forced the wild hunger down and put on his most earnest face, pleading.

"Mr. Quinn, please — could my sister have some of that broth too? She's Chase's girlfriend. If he wakes up, he'd want her to as well."

Damon's gaze flicked to the bed, his tone flat.

"Give him a bowl."

The aide obeyed at once.

Reid's whole body trembled with excitement, his heart hammering.

The moment Damon and the aide turned to check on Chase, a flash of selfish cruelty crossed Reid's eyes. He snatched up the bowl and gulped down most of it in secret.

He grudgingly fed the last half-bowl to the unconscious Rachel, with no care at all.

Look out for yourself, or no one else will.

He felt not a shred of guilt, only cold, absolute self-interest.

Rachel wasn't the sweet, obedient little sister she used to be anymore anyway — just selfish, spiteful, consumed with scheming and spite.

Why should he sacrifice his own shot at this for a sister like that?

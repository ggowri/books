Chapter 37

The whole area was dead quiet. The air reeked of blood and rot, so thick it was hard to breathe.

Zombies wandered all over the wrecked hospital grounds, inside and out.

Their bodies were rotting and torn apart, and they moved stiffly, dragging their feet. Their cloudy gray eyes locked onto anything alive, and low, raspy growls rumbled from their throats. They were everywhere, from the plaza to the hallways to the flower beds, and they had the whole hospital surrounded.

Everyone climbed out of the cars and quickly fell into a tight formation.

Each of them held a service pistol, finger on the trigger, ready to fight.

Elijah flicked the safety off the gun at his hip. Blue sparks crackled at his fingertips, and the raw power of his high-level Lightning Affinity rolled outward, putting the restless zombies nearby on edge.

He turned his cold gaze on Jasper and gave an order that wasn't up for debate. "We'll split up. You take your people and check the west building, and we'll go in through the east building.

"Once we find what we're looking for, we meet back here."

Jasper didn't have much choice. He didn't like it, but he kept his lazy smile on and nodded. "Fine."

Then, he waved his men along and headed for the stairwell of the run-down west building.

As she walked past, Rachel slowed down for a second. She gave Paisley a long, lingering look, full of greed and malice.

Then, she ducked her head and hurried after Jasper into the dark, creepy stairwell.

Paisley saw it all, but her face stayed perfectly calm.

Her senses already stretched across the whole hospital.

The west building looked like it only had a few zombies, but two vicious Level Two mutants were hiding in there. They were great at hiding and really deadly, more than enough to give Jasper's group a hard time.

And the research lab with the virus analysis equipment was on the third floor of this building.

She pointed toward the east stairwell. "The equipment should be up there, on the third floor," she said calmly, without explaining any further.

Elijah shot her a surprised look, but he didn't question it for a second. "Then we'll do it your way, Paisley," he said gently.

He sounded so fond of her, and he trusted her completely.

Then, he took the lead with his gun raised and stepped into the dark stairwell of the east building. Blue lightning coiled around him as he started clearing out the zombies in their way.

Orion and Mason followed close behind with their machetes, one on each side of Paisley, keeping her right in the middle. They watched every dark corner like hawks.

Paisley gripped her dagger tight. Whenever a zombie popped up, she finished it off fast and clean.

Leveling up had made her body even stronger. She was several times faster and stronger than before, and killing zombies was way easier now.

Ordinary zombies kept coming out of the stairwell one after another, lunging at them with raspy growls.

Around Elijah, blue lightning crackled and snapped. Every time he raised his hand, a bolt slammed into the zombies coming at him, stunning them and blowing them apart. In one sweep, he could clear a whole stretch of the stairs.

Orion and Mason fought up close with their machetes, hacking into zombie skulls with steady, brutal swings.

Paisley finished off anything that got past them.

The four of them worked together perfectly, pushing up the dark stairwell, and it didn't take long to fight their way to the third floor.

The whole floor was dead silent. Compared to the chaos downstairs, it was creepily quiet, and the air was thick with the smell of blood mixed with old disinfectant.

At the end of the hallway, there was a sealed metal door with a faded warning label on it. This was the hospital's main research lab.

Elijah stopped and turned to give Paisley a long, searching look.

His intel had only said the equipment was somewhere in the main building. He'd had no way of pinning down exactly where, but Paisley had known.

There was no way that was a coincidence.

Just how many more secrets did she have?

The more mysterious she was, the more he wanted to know.

"This is it," he said.

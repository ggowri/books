Chapter 170

Trovie ran a quick database search and came back with an answer.

[It's afraid of spicy food! Extremely sensitive to intense heat and spice!]

Right then, the massive flower stirred.

Whoosh —

The petals shuddered and snapped open, and a storm of purple seeds burst out, carrying thick corrosive poison, raining down on the group like a volley of arrows.

Every seed carried lethal poison and piercing force — on impact, they'd sprout poison vines or burst into toxic gas, sealing off every avenue of escape, no room left to dodge.

"Defend!"

Spencer shouted.

No one hesitated. Everyone threw everything they had into defense and counterattack at once.

Spencer's fire roared out, trying to burn the incoming seeds from the sky. Thomas's vines wove into a dense net overhead. Ashley raised thick stone walls. Mason's wind blades spun, trying to scatter the poison and cut the seeds apart. Marcus fired rock spikes at range. Madison raised a thick water barrier to block the toxin.

Fire, vines, stone, wind, water — all six affinities poured out at once, a spectacle of light and force.

But something strange happened.

Every attack that hit the seeds, petals, or roots did nothing — barely a wisp of smoke, no visible damage, let alone a wound.

Fire couldn't burn through the poison coating. Vines couldn't hold the thick roots. Stone couldn't block the venom. Wind couldn't cut the petals. Water couldn't purify any of it.

Everyone had gone all-in, and not one attack had landed.

The storm of poison seeds tore through every defense, closing in fast, and the group's faces went pale with dread, cold fear creeping through their limbs.

Spencer's fists clenched, cold sweat beading on his palms, the fire around him dimming on its own, strength draining from his body.

Steady, resilient, rarely rattled — he'd never felt this helpless before. His voice came out hoarse, bitter.

"What do we do? Nothing's working. We threw everything at it and didn't even dent its defense. This thing is way stronger than we thought. We might actually die here today."

Thomas stared at his shattered vine barrier, watching the unstoppable rain of seeds, his confidence collapsing completely.

Usually meticulous, always with a plan — right now every tactic, every backup option had turned into a joke. His voice came out flat, hopeless.

"No weakness. Immune to physical and affinity attacks both. The poison gets through everything... we can't even get close. There's nothing to fight."

Ashley huddled in the middle of the group, face bloodless, eyes red and wet, fingers locked onto her own sleeves, shaking hard.

Her stone wall had just dissolved under the poison mist, taking the last of her confidence with it. She choked out through tears.

"It's too strong... it's really too strong... we can't block it, we can't block any of it..."

Mason, usually fearless, built for taking hits head-on, stared at the storm of seeds blotting out the sky, a deep helplessness creeping into his voice, rough and shaking.

"Can't hit it, can't block it, can't dodge it. First time I've run into something this far above us. It's not even a fight — it's a slaughter. We've got nothing."

Marcus watched the rock spikes he'd summoned dissolve uselessly, a shadow settling over his eyes.

Normally precise, always finding the gap in a defense — but there was no gap here, nothing to aim at. His voice came out low, defeated.

"Every attack's useless. No opening anywhere. Are we actually going to die here?"

Madison watched her carefully built water shield dissolve under the poison mist, and something in her went cold.

Water was supposed to defend and purify both — but the toxin was eating through her barrier faster than she could rebuild it. Her voice came out in a quiet, trembling sigh.

"The poison's too corrosive. Purification can't keep up with it. We... we can't hold out much longer."

All six of them felt their resolve crumble at once, despair washing over them like floodwater, the cold shadow of death settling over every heart.

Panicked, they all turned at once, eyes landing on the one person still standing calm — Paisley.

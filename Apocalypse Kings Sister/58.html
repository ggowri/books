Chapter 58

The other survivors chimed in, every one of them sincere.

They'd seen how strong these four were with their own eyes. There was no doubt they were the real deal.

If the town could get on their good side and maybe even earn their protection, it would have a much better shot at surviving the apocalypse. Even just getting to host them for one good meal would be an honor.

Elijah gave a slight shake of his head and politely turned them down.

"Thanks for the offer, but we've got urgent business to take care of. We won't impose.

"It's getting dark, and the storm's almost here. You should all head home fast!"

Orion and Mason turned them down, too.

They were all on the same page. A strange town came with way too many unknowns and could easily mean trouble. Instead of wasting time on small talk, they'd rather spend every second stocking up.

Their minds were clearly made up, so the survivors didn't push. They just thanked them again, clearly disappointed.

"Since you have important things to do, we won't keep you! We'll never forget that you saved our lives today. Stay safe out there, and be careful!"

Paisley stood behind the guys the whole time, petting her cat and not saying a word.

Once the survivors were gone, Paisley got to work.

The plant's warehouse was packed with crates of bottled water, big jugs of purified water, and every kind of drink imaginable. There was a huge amount of it.

With a single thought, Paisley made it all disappear. Crate after crate of bottled water, jug after jug of purified water, and stacks of sports drinks vanished into her Storage Dimension, along with the plant's purification equipment, filters, and giant storage tanks.

In just a few moments, the massive warehouse was completely empty.

With the water taken care of, the four of them didn't waste any time. They drove off and headed for their next stop, the emergency food reserve.

Fifteen minutes later, they pulled up at the reserve.

It was an impressive sight. This was the biggest emergency food storage site in all of Southspire.

The whole compound was made up of three giant sealed grain silos. Their walls were built from thick, blast-proof concrete, tall and sturdy and sealed tight against moisture.

Domed roofs kept out the heat and the rain, and a ten-foot iron fence ran around the whole place, with a heavy, solid front gate. It was a government-run strategic reserve.

When the apocalypse hit, the staff had cleared out in a hurry. Every bit of food in the compound was still sealed up and untouched.

The place held enough emergency food to feed all of Southspire for three years.

The second the car stopped, they heard shuffling footsteps and raspy, guttural snarls coming from inside the compound. From the noise, there were a lot of zombies in there.

Elijah pushed his door open and got out. His sharp eyes swept across the compound, and he spoke in a low voice.

"There are a few hundred low-level zombies in there. Perfect practice for the three of us."

He turned to Paisley. "Leave this to us."

Paisley nodded, the little white cat snuggled in her arms.

"Okay. I'll go collect the supplies, and you guys be careful.

"There's nothing high-level in there, so you can go all out."

The second she finished speaking, she slipped away, circled around to the silos' sealed side door, and went inside.

Elijah switched into fight mode. He drew his long blade, which flashed cold in the light, and his presence turned steady and commanding.

"I'll charge in and keep the main pack busy. Orion, use your ability to box them in. Mason, cover the sides, and let's clear them out!"

"Got it!"

Orion fired up his Metal Affinity at full power. Several sharp metal blades formed in his hands, and a dense, solid metal shield rose in front of him, ready for battle.

With one kick, the heavy iron door flew open, and over a hundred Level One zombies came pouring out, snarling!

Mason was right behind Orion, a light whirlwind spinning around his body!

He had Wind Affinity and was built for speed, which was his biggest advantage.

The moment his ability kicked in, the air around him churned wildly, and his clothes snapped in the wind. He suddenly felt several times lighter on his feet, and every time he pushed off, he left a blur behind him.

Everyone else moved like they were wading through mud. Meanwhile, Mason rode the wind and shot ten feet in a single burst. He was so fast and slippery that the eye could barely follow him.

The air swirling around him didn't just boost his speed. It also softened the zombies' lunges and helped him read their movements. Even a Level Two zombie's lightning-fast attack would be a half-second too slow for him.

The zombies in the front rows lunged first, bringing a wave of rotten stench with them. Their sharp nails went straight for everyone's faces, and every strike was vicious enough to kill.

Chapter 316

Her balance before all this had been 125,690. Added together, her total now sat at 181,690. She felt, for a moment, genuinely rich.

Having gone through poverty in her last life, this kind of security felt deeply satisfying.

Paisley looked up and spotted Stillwater's largest public market across from the hospital, busy and alive with people.

Fresh produce and meat were laid out in neat rows — one of the few places in the base with a real variety of goods.

Her eyes landed immediately on the seafood stalls at the back of the market.

Resources were scarce enough already — fresh meat and produce were hard enough to come by, let alone seafood.

A quiet craving stirred in her.

She'd always loved seafood, but back in her old life under the Lennox roof, anything fresh or expensive — seafood, snacks, anything good — had always gone to Rachel and the three brothers first.

Even when they didn't want it, even when it went to waste, none of it had ever been hers.

Her preferences, her wants, had never mattered to any of them.

And with no money of her own back then, that small craving had gone unmet for years, lingering like an itch she could never scratch.

Now she had more points than she knew what to do with. No more waiting for scraps, no more holding back. Whatever she'd wanted and never had, she could finally just take.

She turned and walked straight toward the seafood stalls.

The vendor spotted her approaching — composed, well-dressed, clearly someone with real standing — and hurried over with an eager smile.

"Looking for seafood, ma'am? Fresh shipment today, all cold-chain, top quality."

The stall was packed, overflowing with a rich selection.

Whole lobsters, shells a deep, vivid red, claws snapping, still full of life.

Massive dark crabs with hard, glossy shells, claws thick and heavy, clearly loaded with meat.

Beyond that, plump oysters, fresh scallops, firm shrimp, an assortment of fish — every piece gleaming fresh, the kind of luxury that barely existed anymore.

Looking at the stall piled high with fresh seafood, something warm stirred behind Paisley's eyes, a years-old ache finally loosening its grip.

She looked up at the vendor, her tone calm and direct.

"I'll take everything on this stall."

The vendor blinked, stunned.

"Ma'am? You— you want all of it?"

He'd run this stall for a long time and never once had anyone buy out the whole thing at once. Seafood ran expensive — even most high-tier Affinity users only bought small amounts.

"Yes. All of it."

Paisley nodded, certain.

"Don't bother packaging it or cleaning it. Just give it to me as is."

Delighted beyond words, the vendor quickly tallied the total: three thousand points.

Not a small sum, but for someone sitting on nearly two hundred thousand points, it barely registered.

She paid instantly, clean and efficient.

Current balance: 1,224,060.

The moment the transaction cleared, Paisley activated her storage.

An invisible pull of space settled over the stall, and in the next instant, the entire haul of fresh seafood vanished, absorbed into her storage.

Her Storage Affinity had been refined by Trovie to the point that it could now regulate water, temperature, and environment on its own.

Once the lobsters, crabs, fish, and shellfish landed inside, Trovie set them up in a dedicated holding pool, filled with spirit water, which would only make the meat taste better over time.

The vendor stared at the empty stall, still processing what had just happened, awed to realize she must be a rare Storage Affinity user — that explained the spending.

It was his first time seeing a storage space that could keep live animals.

Paisley looked at the lively haul now swimming in her storage, deeply satisfied.

She couldn't wait to head back to the villa and cook herself a proper feast.

Ten minutes later, five in the evening.

She returned to the villa.

The house was quiet, only Marcus seated on the couch, typing away at his laptop, the screen filled with dense data and formulas. He was probably working through research on the undead toxin.

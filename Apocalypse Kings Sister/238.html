Chapter 238

Both of them clung to a faint, hopeful thread.

But Rachel shot a cold glance toward Paisley in the distance, darkness and resentment thick in her eyes, cutting their hope short with a sharp remark.

"You still don't get it, do you?"

"Paisley watched us fall just now and didn't lift a finger. She'd rather save total strangers than help us.

She's already decided we're her enemies. She won't even look at us twice, let alone hand us any food."

She bit down hard, letting out a bitter laugh.

"At least fate's being fair about it.

We're stranded here, but so are they, same middle of nowhere, no transportation. They'll have to walk out of here on their own two feet just like us. They're probably not much better off than we are."

Owen swallowed hard against his dry throat, pushing down the hunger and regret churning in his gut, and nodded grimly.

"Rachel's right.

Paisley stopped thinking of us as family a long time ago. She's not handing out charity.

We're on our own now."

He looked out at the shimmering lake, forcing himself to focus and take charge.

"There should be fish in there.

Reid, Ryan, go find some dry firewood. I'll handle the fishing, and we'll cook over a fire."

Reid and Ryan gritted their teeth and agreed, dragging their soaked, freezing bodies through the overgrown brush in search of dry branches.

The late-autumn woods were damp through and through. Both of them worked with fingers gone numb and purple, hands and feet stiff with cold, barely managing to scrape together a small pile of half-wet wood, and it took forever of repeated attempts before they finally coaxed a weak, flickering flame to life.

Owen crouched by the lake for half an hour before finally catching a few small carp, scraping off the scales and gutting them quickly before setting them over the fire to roast slowly.

Rachel sat off to the side the entire time, not lifting a finger to help, just watching coldly, waiting to eat.

Smoke curled up as the fish slowly turned golden and started dripping fat, and even without any seasoning, the faint aroma was enough to make all three starving stomachs twist with hunger.

But before Reid or Ryan could reach for it, Rachel moved first, grabbing the largest, fattest, most fully cooked fish off the fire without a trace of hesitation, as if it had always been hers by default.

She tore into the meat unhurriedly, eating with total ease, not a word of thanks, completely ignoring the other three.

After all their effort, Reid and Ryan ended up splitting two scrawny, charred little fish between them, barely enough to take the edge off, nowhere close to filling.

In that moment, Rachel's selfishness, her coldness, her sheer cruelty, lay completely exposed.

Owen watched the whole scene, gnawing at the dry, bitter remains of his own tiny fish, the bitterness and regret finally too heavy to hold back.

They'd been blind back then.

They'd cast aside the sister who'd genuinely cared for them, and poured all their warmth and patience into this selfish, cold-hearted girl in front of him.

Ten years of favoritism, of refusing to see right from wrong, and now here they were, starving, freezing, with absolutely nothing to show for it. They deserved exactly this.

Endless regret gnawed at them, but it was far too late to do anything about it.

The damage was done, and whatever warmth had existed between them had long since gone cold. No amount of regret could bring any of it back.

Meanwhile, the others had eaten their fill and turned their attention to the road ahead.

Paisley unrolled a clear route map, tapping the paper with one finger, her voice calm and clear as she explained to the group.

"We're currently in Stillwater territory, about five cities away from Aetheris.

Driving, that's about a city a day. Five days, and we'll be back in Aetheris safely."

Elijah followed her finger to a red dot marked on the map, something thoughtful crossing his face as he spoke, voice low.

"There's a large abandoned munitions depot marked near here."

A thought flickered through his mind, memories of everything that had happened at the lab beneath that aquarium resurfacing.

After the zombie horde, the sea-mutant assault, the mutant crows, it was obvious the people behind all this wanted them dead.

He understood it clearly now. The plane crash today hadn't been an accident either.

In a world like this, strength alone wasn't enough. Only real firepower could actually protect the people he cared about.

Some things you could only count on if you held them in your own hands, if you had the means to protect what mattered without being caught flat-footed, without being at someone else's mercy ever again.

He couldn't afford to put his trust in anyone else's hands anymore.

Orion raised an eyebrow at him, a note of surprise in his voice.

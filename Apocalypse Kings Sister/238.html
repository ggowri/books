Chapter 238

Reid and Ryan were full of hope, a faint glimmer of it in their eyes.

But Rachel shot a cold glance at Paisley in the distance, full of gloom and resentment, and cut their fantasy short. "Don't you get it yet?

"Just now, up in the air, Paisley watched us fall and chose to save strangers instead of helping us.

"She's changed. She sees us as her enemies now, so she won't even look at us twice, let alone give us food."

She clenched her teeth and scoffed. "But at least fate's being fair.

"We're stuck here, but so are they, out in the middle of nowhere with no transportation. They'll have to walk out of here one step at a time too, so they won't be much better off than us."

Owen swallowed hard, his throat dry, pushed down his churning hunger and regret, and nodded heavily. "Rachel's right.

"Paisley stopped seeing us as family a long time ago, and she's not going to hand us food. We're on our own now."

He looked out at the sparkling lake and forced himself to take charge. "There should be fish in the lake.

"Reid, Ryan, go find some dry firewood right now. I'll catch some fish, and we'll roast them."

Reid and Ryan gritted their teeth and agreed. They dragged their soaked, freezing bodies through the overgrown weeds, looking for dry branches.

The late-autumn countryside was damp everywhere. The two of them were so cold their fingertips turned purple and their hands and feet went stiff. They barely managed to gather a small pile of damp wood that might burn, and after trying to light it over and over for a long time, they finally got a weak little flame going.

Meanwhile, Owen crouched by the lake for half an hour before he finally caught a few small carp. He quickly scraped off the scales, cleaned them, and set them over the fire to roast slowly.

Rachel sat lazily off to the side by herself. She wouldn't lift a finger to help and just watched coldly, waiting to eat.

Smoke curled up, and the fish slowly turned golden and started dripping fat. Even without seasoning, they gave off a faint, tasty smell that made the three starving brothers swallow over and over.

But before Reid and Ryan could reach for one, Rachel stepped in first. Without any hesitation, she took the biggest, fattest, most perfectly roasted fish off the fire, like it was obviously hers and always had been.

She tore into the fish slowly, eating with a clear conscience. She didn't say a word of thanks and ignored the way the three of them were looking at her.

After all their hard work, Reid and Ryan only got two tiny, shriveled, burned little fish. It was barely a mouthful, and they were still starving.

In that moment, Rachel's selfishness, coldness, and cruelty were laid bare.

Owen watched the painful scene as he chewed on his dry, bitter little fish, and he couldn't hold back the waves of bitterness and regret anymore.

They really had been blind back then.

They hadn't cherished Paisley, who'd truly cared about them. Instead, they'd always taken Rachel's side and given all their warmth and patience to this selfish, cold-hearted girl right in front of him.

Ten years of favoritism and refusing to see right from wrong, and now they had nothing. They were starving, freezing, and miserable, and they deserved every bit of it.

Endless regret gnawed at them, but it was all too late.

The damage was done, and Paisley's heart had long since gone cold. No matter how much they regretted it, they could never go back.

On the other side, everyone had eaten their fill, and now they had to figure out which way to go next.

Paisley looked down and unfolded a clear route map. She tapped the paper with her fingertip and explained everything to the group in a clear, calm voice.

"We're in the Stillwater area right now, five cities away from Aetheris.

"If we drive, we can get through one city a day, so we'll be back in Aetheris in five days."

Elijah followed her finger and looked at a red dot marked on the map, his expression darkening. "There's an abandoned munitions depot near here, the biggest one in the whole region."

A thoughtful look crossed his face as he remembered what had happened at the aquarium lab in Rivergate.

After the controlled zombie horde, the beast horde, and the mutant crows, it was clear the people behind all this wanted them dead.

He saw it more clearly now. Today's plane crash hadn't been an accident either.

In a world this chaotic, strength alone wasn't nearly enough. Only enough weapons could really protect the people close to him.

Some things only gave him the confidence to protect the people he cared about if he kept them firmly in his own hands. That way, he'd never again be stuck taking hits or be at anyone's mercy.

He couldn't put his hopes in anyone else's hands anymore.

Orion raised an eyebrow at him, sounding a little surprised.

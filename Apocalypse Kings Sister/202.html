Chapter 202

The two of them, each with their own agenda, fell into easy agreement, settling a plan against Paisley's group between them, just waiting for the right moment once they arrived.

The convoy raced on, jolting through the ruined, abandoned outskirts of the city.

Half an hour later, all three vehicles rolled to a stop at the dead, silent gates of the aquarium.

The once-bustling aquarium had collapsed into ruin, its outer walls crumbling, glass shattered across the ground, dark patches of contaminated moss spreading everywhere. The sea wind carried a thick, rotten stench, heavy and unsettling.

The moment the convoy stopped, it stirred whatever had been lying in wait nearby.

Swarms of low-tier zombies poured out from the ruins, the overgrown brush, every dark alley around them, lurching and shrieking toward the vehicles, a wall of black closing in.

The team pushed the doors open and dropped into formation at once.

Paisley's eyes sharpened. On instinct, she opened her mind fully, sending out her high-tier psychic power to read the area and flag any hidden danger.

But the next moment, surprise flickered across her face.

What should have covered the whole aquarium came back blank, blocked entirely.

Strange.

With the power she had now, nothing should be able to shut her out like this.

There was only one explanation: some kind of sealing field, a strange electromagnetic disturbance, or a psychic mutant even stronger than her, blocking every kind of mental probe.

Places that blocked detection like this usually hid the worst danger.

Her guard went up at once.

Elijah's earlier question had gotten an answer by then too—two team members wanted to buy energy guns, but could only put down five thousand points between them for now.

The rest would have to come later.

With their five thousand points transferred over, she now had 71,690 in total.

With a thought, two more energy guns appeared in her hands, and she passed them over.

“Pay the rest whenever you have it.”

They took the guns, both struck somewhere between shock and excitement. No wonder these cost ten thousand points—this was the real deal.

Time to see what they could do.

Blue light tore through the air again and again, cutting down the low-tier zombies in waves, each shot miles beyond anything ordinary firearms could manage.

In a few minutes, every zombie closing in was dead, the ground littered with bodies and scattered low-tier cores.

They scooped up the cores, buzzing with excitement.

These guns were incredible.

Worth every point.

The others, who'd balked at the cost and worried about overdrawing their own Affinity, were stuck hacking away at zombies with regular pistols and blades, putting everything they had into it.

The difference told the whole story on its own.

Watching the guns at work, the rest of the group started looking interested too.

Soon every zombie at the gate was down.

Only then did Chase amble out of his car, Rachel's waist tucked lazily under his arm.

The two of them carried on close and careless, nothing in their manner suggesting they were walking into a high-risk mission—more like they'd come for an afternoon stroll.

Everyone watching read the same contempt and irritation off it.

Elijah stood at the front of the group, posture sharp and rigid, every trace of warmth gone from his face, frost running clean through it.

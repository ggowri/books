Chapter 7

Ashley had been raised by the Thompson family for over a decade. She had grown up by their side, and their bond with her ran deep.

And Paisley was just the biological sister who had shown up out of nowhere.

They shared years of memories together. Would they really accept someone who just dropped into their lives?

The warmth she'd felt moments ago—surrounded by family and basking in their attention—was instantly doused, like ice water had been poured over it. That flicker of hope in her chest cooled, replaced once more by a familiar layer of distance and wariness.

Right. I shouldn't let my guard down so easily. I shouldn't trust warmth so quickly.

In the end, I am the outsider here. I am still on my own.

Ashley's hand hovered in the air for a second. A hint of disappointment flashed in her eyes, but it was gone almost instantly, smoothed over by a gentle, persistent smile.

"Hi, I'm Ashley," she said softly. "It's so good to finally meet you."

She carefully pulled out a small velvet box and held it out to me. Her voice was earnest, almost eager to please.

"I saved up my allowance for months to get this for you—a welcome gift. I really hope you like it."

Paisley was taken aback. Pushing down the unease stirring inside, she accepted the box and opened it slowly.

Inside, nestled against white satin, was a natural ruby the size of a small egg. Its color was a deep, fiery red, clear and vivid, without a single flaw.

Even having lived two lives, Paisley'd never seen a gem of such quality and size up close. It must have been worth a fortune.

Paisley stared, genuinely stunned. She hadn't expected a gift this valuable.

Without realizing it, some of the caution in her heart loosened its grip. "Thank you," she said quietly. "It's beautiful."

Just those few words made Ashley's face light up. Her eyes softened, and she looked genuinely overjoyed, as if Paisley's simple approval meant the world to her.

Seeing the tension ease, Orion continued the introductions.

"And this is Madison Hughes."

Madison stepped forward—polished, elegant, with a capable air about her. She took Paisley's hand warmly.

"Welcome to the family, sweetie. Dinner's all ready, by the way. We should all sit and eat."

Finally, Orion glanced at the girl beside him.

"This is Kelly Walker. We all grew up together."

Kelly shook Paisley's hand with practiced warmth, though her smile didn't quite reach her eyes.

"Glad you're finally here," she said. "Now we can all head back to Aetheris."

Paisley subtly pulled her hand back, her lips pressing into a thin line. She didn't reply.

I don't like her.

Just then, Mason and another guard—Daniel Clarke—came in from their post outside. After another quick round of hellos, the table was set.

No one made much small talk after that. They just took their seats.

The spread was surprisingly ample for the apocalypse: six dishes and a soup, still steaming. Meat and vegetables, a real feast by today's standards.

Paisley's seat had deliberately been placed in the safest spot, right in the middle. Orion sat to her right, Ashley to her left.

Ashley sat close, her movements careful but warm. She picked up a clean set of tongs, served a piece of glazed pork belly, and placed it gently on Paisley's plate.

"I made this myself," she said softly. "Hope you like it."

Paisley hesitated for a second, then pushed down her reservations. She tried it.

The meat was tender, rich with flavor—sweet but not greasy. For three years in the wasteland, she'd survived on stale protein bars and cold scraps. She'd almost forgotten what real, warm food tasted like.

This was the first proper meal she'd had since coming back.

Some of the tension in her chest eased. "It's really good," Paisley said. "Thanks."

Ashley beamed, her eyes lighting up. She quickly served Paisley another piece. "Have more, then!"

Orion, watching from Paisley's other side, seemed almost awkward. Usually so stoic, he quietly picked up a tender rib and placed it on her plate. His tone was even, but the gesture felt deliberate.

"Don't stand on ceremony," he said. "Eat up."

Across from Paisley, Thomas watched as Ashley and Orion took turns serving her. He looked almost jealous. Leaning over, he neatly added a braised rib to Paisley's already growing pile.

"Save a seat for me next to you next time," he said with a warm grin. "I wanna take care of you, too."

Not to be outdone, Spencer quickly piled a juicy chicken drumstick onto her plate. "You're way too thin," he said, his voice laced with concern. "After all those years out there alone... you must've been through hell."

In no time, Paisley's plate was heaped with all sorts of food. The warmth of the meal spread through her, slowly thawing a heart that had long gone cold.

She glanced up at the faces around the table—people treating her with a sincerity she hadn't known in years—and gently tried to wave them off.

"Really, that's enough. I won't be able to finish all this. You should eat too."

It had been so long since anyone had cared like this.

Back with the Lennox family, she was always invisible—doing the dirtiest work, eating the smallest share. Compared to that, this... this felt like a different life. And she was grateful for it.

After dinner, they tidied up quickly and gathered in the living room for a brief meeting.

Elijah, as their leader, spoke first.

"Now that we've found her, we head out tomorrow to restock supplies. Once that's done, we make straight for Aetheris."

Orion turned to Paisley, his expression anxious but hopeful.

"You'll come back with us, won't you? Mom and Dad are there. They've never stopped looking for you—this would mean everything to them."

Paisley held his gaze, then nodded.

"Okay."

She'd always been on her own.

Even after getting this second chance—this reboot—all she'd wanted was payback. Finding a family she never knew she had? That was something she hadn't dared to hope for.

But if they kept treating her like this... yeah, she could stay.

No one objected.

Elijah moved on, clear and practical.

"The villa's fully cleared—no zombies left. It's secure.

"Tonight, everyone takes a clean room. And for safety, I'd suggest the girls pair up. Just in case."

Before anyone else could respond, Paisley spoke evenly, "I sleep alone."

Beside her, Ashley's bright smile faltered. Her eyes dimmed, voice dropping to a hesitant murmur.

"But... I was really hoping we could share a room..."

Paisley's gaze shifted slightly. She didn't reply.

Letting her guard down wasn't an option—not anymore. Ashley seemed sweet, too sweet maybe, but Rachel's act had taught her a brutal lesson. That kind of betrayal stayed with you, etched right into the bone.

She couldn't risk it again. What if history repeated itself?

Some gambles were just too costly.

Orion, always protective of his little sister, stepped in.

"If Paisley prefers her own space, that's fine.

"Take the room next to mine. Anything happens at night, just shout—I'll be right there."

Thomas chimed in right after, "I'll take the room on her other side. Keep an eye out."

Spencer grinned, jumping in.

"Guess that leaves me with Thomas. We'll make sure you're covered, Paisley."

Ashley stayed quiet, her disappointment hanging in the air as she stared down at her hands.

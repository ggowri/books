Chapter 38

"Orion, get the lock," Elijah said.

Orion stepped up to the metal door right away and felt along the lock. It was a keypad lock with a simple mechanical latch.

He hooked his fingers into the lock and gave it a sharp twist. With a soft click, the door popped open.

The heavy door swung inward, revealing a clean, tidy lab. It was a world away from the bloody wreckage outside.

The zombies had never gotten in here, and the lab was in perfect shape.

The whole room was sterile, with spotless white walls and an anti-slip, anti-static floor. Every surface was immaculate, and every piece of equipment sat in neat rows without a scratch on it.

A large, silver-gray machine stood in the middle of the room.

It was covered in data ports, sensor probes, and an HD screen, and its side was stamped with labels for virus analysis and gene sequencing. This was it, the zombie virus analyzer they'd come all this way for.

The machine was completely intact, with neat wiring and not a single dent. It would probably still turn on and run just fine.

Next to it was a row of support equipment, including a high-speed centrifuge, an incubator, a PCR machine, and a microscope scanner. Each one was top-of-the-line medical equipment and worth a fortune. In the apocalypse, no amount of money could buy stuff like this.

A whole row of sealed cabinets and file shelves along the wall was untouched too.

The shelves were stacked with thick research notes, lab logs, virus analysis reports, and dozens of backup hard drives and USB drives.

With all of this, researchers would have a huge head start on figuring out the zombie virus.

Elijah turned to Paisley. "Could you grab all of this for us?

"With this equipment and these records, I bet it won't be long before we find a cure for the zombie virus."

Orion put his machete away and looked around the room full of equipment and records, beaming with relief. "Nice work, Paisley."

Mason stood guard at the lab door, watching the hallway in case of a zombie ambush.

When he heard them, he glanced back at the equipment and frowned. Something didn't add up.

Ever since they'd set out, Elijah and Orion had treated Paisley like she was made of glass. They trusted every call she made without question, and she'd led them straight to the lab without a single wrong turn.

Could sweet, fragile Paisley be hiding some kind of secret?

Paisley gave a small nod, and with a single thought, everything in the lab vanished into thin air.

The priceless virus analyzer and all the support equipment disappeared. So did the stacks of records, the hard drives, and even the sealed zombie virus samples in the cold-storage fridge in the corner.

In barely two seconds, the packed lab was empty.

Elijah finally relaxed, then turned to Mason, who was frozen in shock. "Paisley's Storage Dimension stays a secret, got it? Not one word to anyone.

"If Jasper, Rachel, or anyone else asks, we all stick to the same story. We didn't find anything in the east building, no equipment and no research."

Mason was dumbstruck. He stood frozen in place, and his mind went blank.

A Storage Dimension? That's the rarest, most insane ability there is!

He'd always figured Paisley was just observant and had good instincts, and that was how she kept helping the team avoid trouble. He'd never dreamed she'd awakened a Storage Dimension.

In a world like this, nothing was more useful.

He was so shocked it took him a long moment to snap out of it.

Then, it hit him. Letting him in on this meant Elijah trusted him completely.

This secret could decide whether the team survived. It might even decide whether humanity ever beat the virus, and countless lives were riding on it.

Mason had never looked so serious. He raised his hand and made a solemn vow.

"I swear on my life and my honor as a soldier. I'll never tell a soul as long as I live! I'll take this secret to my grave, and I won't let you or anyone else down, Elijah!"

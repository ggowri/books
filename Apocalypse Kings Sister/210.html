Chapter 210

"This whole underground sea feeds its roots, and the water down here is all connected and never ends," Elijah said. "If we keep burning it, we'll just keep draining our powers, and the longer this drags on, the worse it gets for us."

The other three team members watched the creepy seaweed that couldn't be killed or burned out, and the fear they'd only just pushed down came flooding back.

The magnetic field underground was a mess, they couldn't see far, and currents churned under their feet. Nobody knew how many unknown dangers were hiding deep in this endless seaweed.

Everyone's sense of danger kept growing. Basement Level 15 really was trickier and far more dangerous than they'd expected.

While everyone was trying to figure out a way through, Paisley's cool, calm voice rang out. "Don't waste your energy burning all of it. Just find the main root, and we can wipe it out for good."

She lifted her hand and looked up, her eyes locking right onto a flooded hollow to the left of the elevator exit. The seaweed grew thickest there, the color was darkest, and the slime was piled up heaviest. It was the living heart of the whole wall.

"Every strand of this stuff feeds off that one root," she said. "Cut it, and the whole patch dies right away."

When the seaweed there burned away and the main root was exposed, they saw it was wrapped around a glossy, bright green Plant crystal core that glowed with a soft light of life.

Paisley reached out with a thread of her power, pulled the core free with precision, and put the Level Five Plant crystal core away.

Without the core and the main root to feed it, the whole wild patch of Level Five mutant seaweed died on the spot. Every swaying leaf drooped, charred, and withered, and the thick layers of seaweed crumbled into scorched bits all over the floor.

Just like that, the blocked path was clear.

A murky, damp underground breeze blew slowly past and carried away the leftover smell of burned plants and the corrosive stench. The long, pitch-black tunnel ahead finally lay wide open in front of them.

Old, dim fluorescent lights ran along both sides of the tunnel. The light kept flickering on and off, stretching the puddles on the floor and the shadows on the walls into weird, twisted shapes. The air was full of a strange, damp, rotting smell, and the chaotic magnetic field made their hearts beat a little faster.

"The path's clear. Let's keep moving," Elijah said in a low voice, his eyes still sweeping the area.

Orion stayed right by Paisley's side, the warm glow of the flame in his palm still pushing back the cold. He kept guarding her left side.

Everyone held their breath and followed carefully.

The tunnel went on and on, and the floor was covered in puddles of different depths. Bits of seaweed roots and some unknown slime floated just under the water. Every step made a soft splash that sounded painfully loud in the dead silence underground.

After they'd walked a few dozen yards, a faint, sticky rustling suddenly came from the darkness at the far end of the tunnel. It sounded like countless soft things were slowly creeping and sliding across the floor and walls.

The rustling was constant and creepy, and it made their skin crawl.

"Stop," Elijah ordered quietly, his face grim.

Everyone stopped dead and went on full alert, their powers ready, their eyes locked on the darkness ahead.

A second later, a strange figure slowly appeared in the dark, where dim light and shadows crossed.

It was a horribly deformed, terrifying mutant.

It had a full human upper body, thin and gaunt with skinny arms, and its neck and head were human-shaped. Its skin was so pale it was almost see-through.

Its face was blurry and twisted, with no clear eyes or eyebrows, just a smooth, flat surface. Only its mouth stood out, split all the way to its ears and covered in fine, creepy lines.

It was even still wearing a white lab coat.

But from the chest down, where its torso, stomach, and legs should have been, there wasn't a single human bone. Instead, there was a dense mass of soft, slimy tentacles with no roots.

Thousands of dark blue-green tentacles of different lengths spread out everywhere, coiling, dragging, and slapping against the floor and walls.

Sticky fluid kept dripping off the tentacles into the standing water, spreading ripple after murky ripple, and it carried a faint, corrosive poison.

It used the countless tentacles to hold itself up and glide around, its whole body floating about half a foot off the ground. It hovered quietly in the middle of the tunnel, blocking the way.

A cold, clammy, high-level aura slowly settled over everyone, several times more vicious than the mutant seaweed.

The team members went white, held their breath without thinking, and felt a chill spreading inside them.

It was a humanoid mutant!

Judging from what was left of its upper body and the ragged old lab coat it wore, it wasn't hard to tell that it had probably worked at this undersea research lab before the apocalypse.

Chapter 210

“This whole underground sea is probably feeding its root system. The water goes on forever down here. Burning it out by brute force just drains our Affinity and buys the enemy time, not us.”

The three other team members, watching this weed that wouldn't die, wouldn't burn out, felt the fear they'd just barely swallowed come rushing back.

The field down here was scrambled, visibility was poor, and currents churned underfoot. No telling what else was hiding in the depths of all this weed.

Everyone's sense of danger kept climbing. Basement Fifteen was turning out to be far worse, far trickier, than anyone had planned for.

As everyone racked their brains for a way through, Paisley's cool, level voice cut in.

“No need to waste effort burning the whole thing. Find the main root, and you clear it out for good.”

She lifted a hand, her eyes settling on a flooded hollow to the left of the elevator exit, where the growth was thickest, the color darkest, the slime pooled heaviest—the living core of the whole wall.

“Every branch of this thing feeds off that one root. Cut the root, and the whole mass dies within seconds.”

The moment the root was exposed, it turned out to be wrapped around a bright green Plant Affinity core, a faint glow of life pulsing through it.

Paisley reached out with a thread of her own power, pulled it free in one clean motion, and pocketed the Level Five Plant Affinity core.

Cut off from both the core and the root, the whole sprawling mass of mutant weed died in an instant, every swaying leaf drooping, charring, crumbling into ash-dry wreckage on the floor.

The blocked path cleared in a heartbeat.

A damp underground breeze drifted through, carrying off the last of the charred smell, and the long, black tunnel ahead finally lay open before them.

Old yellow lights flickered along both walls, the light stuttering, stretching the puddles and shadows into strange, warped shapes. The air hung thick with a damp, rotten smell, the field unsteady enough to set nerves on edge.

“Path's clear. Keep moving.”

Elijah said it low, his eyes never stopping their sweep of the tunnel.

Orion stayed close at Paisley's side, a small flame still burning in his palm, pushing back the cold, guarding her left flank without fail.

The team held its breath and moved forward, careful with every step.

The tunnel ran deep, the floor pooled unevenly with water, bits of root and unknown slime drifting just beneath the surface. Every step made a soft splash, loud as a gunshot in the dead quiet underground.

Several dozen yards in, a faint, wet, scraping sound drifted up from the dark ahead—like countless soft bodies dragging slowly across floor and wall.

The sound was thick and constant, crawling under the skin.

“Stop…”

Elijah's order came grim and low.

Everyone froze at once, Affinity ready, eyes locked on the dark ahead.

A moment later, something humanoid and wrong began to take shape in the shifting dark.

******

It was a mutant, grotesque beyond anything they'd seen.

From the waist up, it kept a human shape—thin, gaunt, its neck and skull still human in outline, skin pale to the point of transparency.

Its features had blurred and warped—no clear eyes, no clear brow, just a flat, smooth face, except for a mouth split wide all the way to its ears, covered in strange, hairline markings.

It still wore a white lab coat.

But below the chest, where a torso and legs should have been, there was no skeleton at all—just a mass of countless soft, slick, rootless tendrils.

Thousands of dark blue-green tendrils spread out in every direction, uneven in length, coiling and dragging and slapping against floor and wall at random.

A thick fluid dripped steadily off them, each drop landing in the standing water with a ripple, a faint corrosive edge to the smell of it.

It moved on the tendrils alone, gliding, floating half a foot off the ground, hovering dead center in the passage, blocking the way forward.

A cold, clammy, high-tier presence settled over the whole tunnel, worse by far than the seaweed had been.

Every face in the group went bone-white, breath catching on instinct, a deep chill spreading fast.

A humanoid mutant.

Judging by what was left of its human half and the ruined old uniform, it had likely once worked at this undersea research facility, before the world ended.

Chapter 247

Dusk fell early, gray clouds pressing low over the wasteland, draping everything in shadow.

Traveling at night was a bad idea. Pushing on now would only mean running into more unknown danger.

Just then, a cluster of buildings came into view at the edge of the wasteland.

A village.

An old, weathered stone marker stood at the entrance, its carved letters worn nearly smooth by wind and rain. Barely legible: Wexford.

The whole village sat dead quiet, every window shuttered, every street empty. No voices, no animals, not even wind through the grass. The silence felt wrong.

Empty hills, an empty village, a silence heavy enough to set anyone's nerves on edge.

Elijah studied the village through the window, his expression grave, and spoke low to the group.

"We'll find an empty house on the edge of the village and hole up there for the night."

"Keep quiet. Don't stir anything up."

He paused, his tone turning grimmer.

"In this world, people are worse than disasters. Everyone stay sharp."

After everything they'd been through, no one needed convincing.

******

Paisley's brow drew in slightly as she quietly spread her Psychic Affinity out across the area.

It stretched five hundred yards in every direction. The village looked calm on the surface, but something sour and wrong hung beneath the stillness. Something was off, badly.

Wexford was hiding something.

But full dark was closing in, and the unknown dangers of the open wasteland were worse than whatever was wrong with this place. Going in was still the safer bet.

The cars rolled in, and soon they spotted a large standalone villa at the edge of the village, surrounded by empty ground, long abandoned by the look of it.

Elijah and Orion got out first, sweeping the house floor by floor, checking every corner for zombies. Once it was clear, they waved the others in.

"Clear. This'll do."

Gathered in the living room, Elijah laid it out.

"Pick your own rooms. We'll stay the night and move at dawn."

Paisley, having tucked the cars back into her dimension, walked in and spoke up.

"I'll take a room on the second floor. By myself."

Ashley pouted and looped her arm through Madison's instead.

"Madison, I guess I'm bunking with you tonight."

Madison smiled gently and nodded.

"Sure thing."

Kelly, quiet the whole ride, kept her tone humble.

"Any room's fine for me."

Then, trying to earn some goodwill, she offered.

"I'll cook dinner. Can't keep freeloading forever."

Thomas gave a soft scoff, faintly mocking.

"Good to know you've got some sense."

Elijah glanced around the room.

"We'll split the night watch. I'll take the first half."

Orion spoke up right away.

"I'll take the second."

Ashley looked at them both, eyes soft with gratitude.

"Thanks, Orion. Elijah. I can actually sleep tonight because of you two."

While no one was looking, Paisley slipped off to the kitchen alone.

A faint shimmer, and the counter was suddenly piled with fresh ingredients.

Crisp seasonal vegetables, cleaned cuts of meat, plump grains of rice, all laid out fresh and plentiful.

She kept her guard up, quietly.

With Kelly in the group now, people's motives were hard to read. Best not to show off resources or abilities more than she had to.

Keep an eye on her.

With that done, Paisley turned and headed upstairs.

"I'm going to rest. Call me when dinner's ready."

She picked the cleanest room on the second floor and shut the door quietly, sealing out every sound from downstairs.

Inside her dimension, she sat cross-legged on the couch, palm open, a scatter of clear, high-tier crystal cores floating up from nowhere.

She'd gathered them over the past two days of fighting. The richest of them all was the core from that afternoon's Level Six bear, dense with power.

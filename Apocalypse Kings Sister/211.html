Chapter 211

No one knew why the research lab had been built beneath the aquarium.

Maybe it was the toxins, the warped magnetic currents down there, soaking into flesh for years until something twisted and half-animal crawled out the other side.

As if sensing living prey, the mutant lifted its head. Its eyes were black and clouded, locked onto them with a stare that held no warmth at all.

Matted black hair clung wet to a bloated, bone-white face. Its lips had split and curled back, baring rows of thin, needle-sharp teeth, white and gleaming cold.

Its fingers had twisted into long blades, tipped with a sickly blue poison sheen, sharp enough to open skin like paper.

Everyone's scalp crawled under that stare. Cold sweat broke out, and every hair on their arms stood up.

Dread swept the tunnel. One operator, his nerves shredded, couldn't hold back any longer. He threw up a hand and fired off his Affinity, desperate to strike first.

The instant his power left his fingertips, a mass of dark-teal tendrils exploded up from the tunnel floor, moving so fast they blurred into streaks.

Supple and dripping with venom, the tendrils whipped around his waist and hauled him clean off the ground.

The moment they touched his skin, a bruise-black stain crawled up his neck and arms. Numbness tore through his limbs, and poison flooded his whole body in seconds.

His body locked rigid, then went slack. Pain and paralysis crushed him at once. He thrashed uselessly, voice cracking as he screamed:

"Help me! Someone help me, please!"

Seeing him caught, the others opened fire without hesitation, fire and lightning and wind-blades stacking in layers, all aimed at the mutant.

That was when the humanoid thing threw its head back, chest heaving, and let out a shriek that split the air.

BOOM—

An invisible shockwave tore through the tunnel like a blast of pressure, slamming into every square foot of the space.

Pain exploded behind everyone's eyes. Temples pounded, skulls felt ready to crack, and their minds scattered into static.

Every Affinity they'd thrown collapsed midair under the sound wave. None of it so much as grazed the creature.

Pure terror gripped the squad. One man, pale as paper, clenched his jaw against the pain splitting his skull, sweat beading cold on his forehead.

Someone else clutched his head and groaned, swaying on his feet, eyes already glassy with the look of a man who expected to die here.

The tunnel was narrow, with nowhere to retreat. The creature ahead of them was strange and savage, and it clearly had no intention of letting anyone walk away. Despair settled over the group like a weight.

Even with their own minds screaming from the shockwave, Elijah Blackwood and Orion Thompson's first instinct was still to shield Paisley Lennox.

Elijah forced down the pain splitting his skull, forced back the dizziness clawing at him, and stepped in front of her on instinct, using his own body to block the worst of the blast.

His eyes stayed locked, hard and grim, on the shrieking thing ahead. His voice came out clipped:

"Does anyone have a way to deal with this? What even is it? What level is its Affinity?"

Something dark crossed his gaze. His tone turned heavy.

"Its strength might be beyond all of us combined. If this keeps up, we're all going to die down here."

Orion ignored the pain stabbing through his own mind and clamped both hands over Paisley's ears, trying to shield her from the worst of the sound. His voice shook, just barely, as he asked:

"Paisley, do you have anything?"

His gaze flicked toward the poisoned teammate, blackened and convulsing in the tendrils' grip, and his chest tightened.

"Whatever's in that poison is brutal. He won't last past five minutes."

In the middle of all that despair, Paisley stayed cold and still, untouched by the panic around her.

A faint glint sparked at her fingertips, and a sleek, blue-lit energy gun appeared in her palm.

Her eyes narrowed slightly. She said, almost offhand:

"Might be worth a shot."

Elijah's pupils flickered with surprise.

He stared at the brand-new gun in her hand, a question rising in his throat — she'd had this the whole time, and never once pulled it out?

The words piled up and went nowhere. He swallowed them.

He didn't want to look like a fool in front of her.

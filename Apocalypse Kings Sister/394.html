Chapter 394

He'd always favored the senior branch, indulging and covering for every one of them without fail, especially when it came to choosing sides between Brenda and the junior branch's daughter-in-law, he'd sided with the senior branch, without exception, every single time.

He'd leaned on his status as an elder to pressure the junior branch into silence, confident he had the situation under control.

But now, with ironclad evidence laid bare and the whole crowd watching and judging, there was no room left for him to defend or protect anyone.

Every eye in the crowd felt like a blade pointed straight at him.

Listening to them condemn him, calling him blind to right and wrong, an enabler of cruelty, he realized he'd never felt this humiliated in his entire life.

Grandpa Thompson's gaze landed on Paisley. This was the child they'd just found. He hadn't realized she'd grown up so much already.

He'd heard the stories about her, too.

She was a good kid.

Had he really been wrong this whole time?

Regret crept through him, deep and unwelcome.

With public opinion against her and raw strength crushing whatever pride she had left, Brenda's shoulders trembled faintly, unwilling, but forced to bow her head.

Under everyone's watchful eyes, she turned and reluctantly stalked back into the house, emerging moments later with armfuls of rice, flour, vegetables, and rare side goods she'd stolen and hoarded over the past while, dropping it all, heavy and unceremonious, onto the ground in front of Diane.

The pile of supplies sat there, glaring evidence of her long-standing greed and misconduct.

With that done, she gritted her teeth and, lowering her voice, forced out an apology, utterly insincere.

"Sorry. I was wrong. I shouldn't have stolen your supplies and bullied you, over and over."

Her voice came out mumbled and weak, dripping with resentment, without a shred of sincerity.

Diane's gaze stayed cool, her presence steady, nowhere near the easily-moved woman she used to be. She said flatly.

"Too quiet. No sincerity at all."

"Say it again."

Three simple words, carrying the faint, unmistakable pressure of Level Four Metal Affinity, made Brenda's whole body stiffen.

Backed into a corner with nowhere left to go, facing everyone's judging, disdainful stares, she had no choice but to raise her voice, forcing out the words through gritted humiliation.

"I was wrong! I shouldn't have used my Affinity to bully ordinary people, shouldn't have kept stealing from my sister-in-law and humiliating her in public! I'll never do it again!"

The words came out, her cheeks burning, her chest aching with suppressed resentment, the hatred inside her only growing more intense.

Paisley watched her miserable expression coldly, then spoke, cutting straight through any lingering thoughts of revenge.

"Don't even think about getting back at us."

"From this day forward, if you so much as touch my mother again, if you dare throw your weight around and cause trouble again, losing face today will be the least of what happens to you."

"Trust me, you don't want to find out what I'm capable of."

Her voice carried no obvious threat at all, yet every word rang with absolute certainty, leaving no doubt that she meant it.

A chill ran through Brenda, and whatever lingering thought of revenge she'd been nursing was snuffed out instantly.

She stole a glance at Paisley's calm, unreadable eyes, eyes that looked peaceful on the surface but held a deep, cold ruthlessness underneath, terrifying enough to make her tremble.

She'd never encountered anyone this young with such a commanding, bone-chilling presence.

In that moment, she had no doubt at all, if she ever dared cross Paisley again, Paisley would act without a second's hesitation, and make her disappear completely.

Fear crushed whatever resentment and malice remained. Brenda kept her head down, not daring to breathe too loud.

Diane stood quietly, looking at the small mountain of returned supplies in front of her, at Brenda's pathetic, humbled state, and then glanced over at Grandpa Thompson, his face ashen with humiliation.

Every bit of grievance, suppression, and endurance she'd carried for so long dissolved completely in that moment.

She'd spent half her life yielding, prioritizing harmony, accommodating everyone around her, and what had it earned her? Not peace, but worse treatment, time after time.

Today, she'd finally won justice for herself.

Warm sunlight fell over her, the faint golden glow of her Affinity lingering softly around her, gentle, yet undeniably sharp.

The timidity that had been carved into her for so long had vanished completely, replaced by a newfound edge.

Diane turned her head, a genuine, warm smile blooming in her eyes as she looked at Paisley beside her, gratitude and tenderness welling up inside her.

Good. Her daughter was home.

It was Pais who'd given her the strength to reclaim the confidence and dignity she'd lost long ago.

Diane looked at the mountain of supplies piled up before her, quietly worrying about how the two of them would possibly carry it all home.

Paisley raised a hand. A faint flash of light, and the entire mountain of supplies vanished instantly, swallowed into her storage ring.

"Mom, let's go home!"

Diane blinked, startled.

"..."

Chapter 182

“This is the real thing! Zero contamination, high-grade energy fruit!”

He'd worked this counter since the world fell, handling more trades than he could count, and almost every fruit, vegetable, or energy crop that came through still carried some trace of radiation or toxin. All of it needed layers of purification before anyone could eat it.

Fresh fruit this pure—a hundred percent clean, zero contamination, carrying its own energy—was something you couldn't buy at any price in this world.

The onlookers who'd been quietly watching lost it the second they heard the results, every face slack with shock.

“Zero contamination?! Where in this world are you even finding radiation-free fruit anymore?”

“And thirty-five percent energy content on top of that!”

“That's a legend for you.”

“Now I know what it means to have legend-tier luck. We're over here fighting over ration bars and low-grade scraps, and she just casually pulls out something this precious.”

The young Affinity users around her stared at the glittering cluster of grapes on the counter, pure envy in their eyes, unable to look away.

They'd already admired her for her monstrous combat strength. Now they envied her luck too, and the rare resources that seemed to just find their way to her.

The clerk swallowed down his shock and looked up, his tone respectful.

“Ms. Lennox, this grade of energy grape doesn't circulate on the open market at all. Going by the base's top exchange rate, I can offer five Level Three crystal cores for this cluster. Would you like to confirm the trade?”

The price alone set off another wave of murmurs.

An ordinary cluster of wild mutant fruit was worth a couple of Level Two cores at best. Paisley's flawlessly purified energy grapes blew every other item on the market out of the water—worth about as much as high-tier mutant beast materials.

Paisley kept her expression even, though she was a little surprised underneath it. She hadn't expected the grapes to be worth quite this much.

She nodded, her answer brisk.

“Trade it.”

The words had barely left her mouth when, with another flicker of intent, she set five more identical clusters down on the counter, plump and gleaming, spirit energy curling off them—every bit as fresh and flawless as the first.

“These too, if you don't mind.”

The shock the clerk had only just swallowed came roaring back. His pupils shrank, and he went completely still.

He grabbed the scanner and ran it over each cluster in turn. Every single reading came back identical:

[Item scanned: fresh energy grapes] [Contamination level: 0 (fully purified, no apocalypse-era toxin residue)] [Energy content: 35%]

Leave it to a legend. Six identical clusters, all top-grade and spotless. That kind of luck…

The crowd lost whatever composure it had left. Gasps and exclamations came one after another, and everyone present was stunned speechless.

“Oh my god—five more, all the same quality?!”

“Six clusters of top-grade energy grapes, and that's forty-eight Level Three cores! That's insane money!”

“That's a legend for you. She doesn't do anything small.”

“I work myself ragged all day and can't scrape together one Level Two core. Comparing yourself to people like that will kill you.”

“I wonder what grapes like that even taste like.”

“What I really want to know is how she's even growing fruit like this, in a world like this.”

“Who'd have guessed someone could mass-produce contamination-free, high-grade energy fruit after the apocalypse. Whatever's backing her is terrifying.”

...

People stared at the grapes and swallowed. These days they lived on hardtack and cold water; they couldn't remember the last time they'd eaten actual fruit.

Let alone fruit of this quality.

If only I could grab onto her coattails. Wonder if she's taking applications.

The clerk's hands trembled slightly. He forced down his shock and excitement, steadied himself, and spoke, his tone respectful but uneasy.

“M-Ms. Lennox, all six clusters check out. Top grade, every one. That comes to forty-eight Level Three crystal cores.”

He counted out the cores at once, sealed them in a specially made pouch, and handed them over with both hands.

“Please come again, Ms. Lennox!”

Paisley took the pouch and tucked it away without ceremony, her expression as composed as ever.

These cores would last her a good while.

She wouldn't be coming back to trade again anytime soon.

Strange, how everyone seemed to know her face already. Best to keep a lower profile from now on.

Paisley gave a small nod, turned, and walked off, her steps light, utterly indifferent to all the eyes following her.

Chapter 355

He paused, his gaze darkening slightly.

"His foundation's intact, at least. With proper rest and recovery, he should be back at his peak before long."

With that, he swept a hand across the room, delivering his final orders.

"That's enough for today. Everyone focus on these two priorities. No mistakes."

"First, push the lab to accelerate the cure's development. We need that leverage locked down as soon as possible."

"Second, keep working the wedge between the Lennoxes and the Blackwoods. Push them toward open conflict. We just sit back and profit."

Every official bowed in unison, solemn, and filed out in order to begin their own share of the scheme, an invisible conspiracy settling quietly over the whole base.

Time slid along to six in the evening.

Night settled gently over the core villa district, lights flickering on one by one, warm glow spilling across the courtyards, the day's tension giving way to something softer, more domestic.

The Thompson villa was already lit up, everything ready.

The courtyard was spotless, rare greenery arranged just so, warm lighting laid out in careful, elegant layers.

Inside, the banquet hall stood spacious and grand, tables set, dishes already plated and fragrant, lavish and tasteful all at once.

Nathaniel Blackwood, Mrs. Blackwood, and Elijah stepped into the courtyard together.

All three carried themselves with the composed, unmistakable dignity of old family.

Elijah stood tall, his dark shirt setting off his sharp, composed features, but the moment his gaze found the slender figure at the center of the hall, every bit of that usual coolness melted into something soft.

Her parents hurried forward with the Thompson siblings to greet their guests, the two sets of elders exchanging warm, easy pleasantries, the laughter softening the usual tension of their new world.

"Blackwood, Mrs. Blackwood, please, sit, rest a while."

"Thank you for going to all this trouble. Really, it's too much."

Nathaniel answered with an easy smile, every courtesy observed.

After the usual pleasantries, Mrs. Blackwood's gaze settled naturally on Paisley among the crowd.

The girl stood tall and poised, her features clean and striking, composed and steady at her mother's side.

Putting it together with everything her son had said, Mrs. Blackwood understood at once — this was the girl he couldn't stop thinking about. A genuine smile broke across her face as she hurried over, her voice warm.

"You must be Paisley — the Thompsons' daughter they just found again?"

Caught off guard by the attention, Paisley reined in her usual ease, inclining her head and stepping forward with perfect courtesy.

"Hello, ma'am. I'm Paisley."

"What a lovely girl — clean features, steady eyes. Anyone can see you're a good kid."

The more she looked, the more pleased Mrs. Blackwood seemed, delight plain on her face — no wonder her stone-faced son had finally fallen for someone. She pulled out a smooth rosewood box and held it out without ceremony, her smile gentle.

"You're so pretty — I liked you the second I saw you. This is a little welcome gift. You have to take it."

Paisley's hand paused. Instead of taking it right away, she glanced toward her mother, asking without words.

Only then did Diane get a clear look at the box in Mrs. Blackwood's hands. Her eyes went wide with shock, and she reached out to block it at once, her voice urgent.

"Mrs. Blackwood, I absolutely can't let you do this!

I recognize this. It's the Blackwood family heirloom pendant, passed down generation after generation, only ever given to the family's daughters-in-law. Why would you hand this to my daughter?"

She pushed the box firmly back, her tone brooking no argument.

"No. Take it back, please."

Inwardly, her mother sighed.

Her Pais had been lost for over a decade, had suffered through half a lifetime of hardship, and had only just come home. She wanted to shower her with every bit of love the world had to give, keep her safe and carefree.

But the Blackwoods handing over their most sacred heirloom this fast made their intentions far too obvious — the scheming practically slapped her in the face.

She knew, of course, that the two families had made a verbal betrothal years ago, but so much time had passed, the world had changed so completely — an old-world engagement like that didn't carry weight anymore.

Pais had only just come home, and she was still so young. All her mother wanted was to keep her close, let her live however she pleased, without being tied down by old contracts or family obligations, let alone turned into some bargaining chip in an alliance.

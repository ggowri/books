Chapter 6

A sudden, enveloping warmth wrapped around Paisley, layer by layer.

In her past life—or what felt like a lifetime ago—no one had ever shown her such favor or closeness. She had endured years of coldness and blame, never knowing what it was like to be cherished or placed at the center of anyone's attention. For a moment, she froze, unsure how to respond, a flicker of awkwardness in her eyes.

Orion noticed his younger brothers' over-the-top excitement. Worried they might scare her off, he spoke up, his voice a low, steady warning.

"Hey, give her some space. You're crowding her."

Thomas and Spencer immediately backed off, shoving their hands into their pockets, but their eyes stayed glued to Paisley, not wanting to miss a second.

Looking up, Paisley saw all three of them watching her with unwavering focus. That warm feeling surged in her chest again.

This is my family.

After a lifetime of wandering alone, she finally had people who were truly hers.

Pushing down the swell of emotion, she softened her expression and spoke gently, her voice sweet and compliant.

"Hey, Thomas. Spencer."

That simple, willing acknowledgment seemed to smooth over years of longing and searching for the two brothers.

Thomas' face lit up with a smile so tender it could melt stone.

"Good to have you here, Paisley," he said warmly, pulling a small matte black gift box from his jacket. He held it out carefully, his eyes full of affection. "I made this for you—a welcome gift. Open it."

Paisley took the box and lifted the lid. Inside lay a sleek, elegantly designed women's watch. It was minimalist yet refined, clearly custom-made and expensive.

"It's beautiful," she said, looking up with a genuine smile. "Thank you, Thomas. I love it."

Not to be outdone, Spencer quickly produced his own gift—a slender velvet case. He handed it over almost shyly. "From me. Hope you like it."

Paisley accepted it and opened the lid. A hair clip lay inside, set with a clear, brilliant gemstone.

The stone was perfectly round, catching the light with a warm glow. The craftsmanship was exquisite, the materials unmistakably luxurious—a piece meant to last.

She could feel the weight of their care behind these gifts.

"Thank you, Spencer," she said softly, her smile gentle. "It's perfect."

Orion, watching from the side, suddenly felt awkward. His brothers had already presented their gifts, and his was still tucked away. His ears tinged slightly pink as he tried to play it cool, pulling a rigid, gunmetal-gray case from his pocket.

"Here," he said, offering it to Paisley. "From me."

His tone carried a hint of nerves, barely noticeable.

Paisley's eyes brightened as she took the case and opened it.

Resting on the black foam inside was a compact, cold-forged tactical dagger.

The blade was slim and razor-sharp, its edge gleaming with a deadly chill. The sheath was silver-plated and studded with a subtle row of micro diamonds—flashy yet functional, beautiful but brutally efficient.

This wasn't some decorative trinket. It was a high-grade close-combat blade.

Real delight flashed in Paisley's eyes. This spoke to her.

In the apocalypse, pretty jewelry meant nothing. A weapon that could save your life? That was priceless.

Thomas' watch was elegant, Spencer's clip was beautiful, but Orion's dagger was useful. Perfectly so.

She ran her fingertips lightly along the cool metal. The grip was firm, the balance excellent. Sheathed, it was discreet. Unsheathed, it would bite deep. An ideal tool for handling zombies—or anything else that needed handling.

Her smile turned genuine, bright and appreciative. "Thanks, Orion. I really love this."

Orion let out a quiet breath, tension leaving his shoulders. He caught his brothers' eyes and tipped his chin up just slightly—a silent told you so.

Thomas and Spencer just shook their heads, smiling faintly but truly happy for her.

Finally, Orion remembered the man still seated quietly on the sofa, elegant and detached. He guided Paisley gently toward him.

"Paisley, this is Elijah Blackwood—a good friend of mine. You can call him Elijah."

Hearing that, Elijah—who had remained silent the whole time with an imposing presence—slowly rose to his feet.

He stood tall and straight, his military uniform accentuating his lean, cool stature. His features were sharp, his gaze detached, yet he carried a calm, commanding air that seemed to fill the room. Even standing still, he radiated the pressure of someone used to power.

Paisley looked up at him, her heart pounding so hard she was sure everyone could hear it.

Oh my god. It's really him. Elijah.

The man who, side by side with Orion, had built Epoch Enclave from the ground up. The one who'd carved the wasteland's power structure in two. In her past life, people had killed and begged for a fraction of his attention—and still couldn't get close.

And now... not only did the two most powerful men in the apocalypse know each other, they were like brothers. In a single day of searching for her family, she'd somehow ended up grabbing hold of both of their coattails.

The rush of luck hit her like a wave, hot and dizzying. She had to force herself to keep still.

Elijah studied the sweet-faced girl before him, and something in his cool, hardened expression softened slightly.

"Welcome, Paisley," he said, his voice low and even.

He pulled a slim, dark gift box from his pocket and held it out.

"A small gift. Hope you like it."

Paisley took it with both hands, fingers trembling just a little as she lifted the lid.

Inside lay a standard-issue pistol, polished to a smooth, matte finish. Brand new, not a scratch on it—the kind of weapon that was worth a fortune in the early days of the apocalypse, when ammo was scarce and trust was scarcer.

In a world overrun by the infected, a gun wasn't just a tool. It was a lifeline.

He really went and got me a pistol. So typical.

But she couldn't deny it was exactly what she needed.

"Thank you, Elijah," she said softly, meeting his eyes. "It's perfect."

He gave a slight nod and turned to sit back down, just as the noise from the kitchen faded and three more people walked into the room.

Orion took the chance to make introductions, starting with the delicate-looking girl at the front.

"Paisley, this is Ashley Thompson. She's part of the family—the Thompsons took her in years ago. Think of her as your sister."

Ashley offered a gentle smile, sweet and kind, and reached out a hand in greeting.

But the moment Orion said "taken in", something cold tightened in Paisley's chest. Memories of Rachel flashed sharp and ugly in her mind—the replacement, the impostor, the one who'd taken everything.

Without thinking, she took a small step back, avoiding Ashley's hand.

The movement was subtle, but in the quiet of the room, it spoke louder than words.

Chapter 194

Orion's eyes, which had gone soft a moment ago, turned cool all at once, his lashes dropping to hide the irritation churning underneath.

His fingers tightened, barely enough to notice, his usual cool presence dropping lower still.

He didn't like strangers eyeing the sister he'd only just gotten back.

No one deserved her.

He hadn't even added Paisley himself yet.

And this man had a pretty-boy look to him that got more irritating the longer he stared.

Spencer's brow furrowed, grumbling to himself. Their sister was just too kind, too pure-hearted—she had no idea how ugly people could be.

Thomas, for his part, decided he'd find time later to teach Pais how to turn people down, man or woman.

In this world, you couldn't afford to be that trusting.

Ashley's brow knit too, clearly no happier with how her sister had handled it.

Marcus Whitfield was no more pleased.

His jaw tightened faintly, his cool eyes sweeping over the doctor, calm on the surface but carrying a sliver of real chill underneath.

A quiet, cold pressure spread off him, and he went even further into himself, saying nothing at all.

He said nothing, though underneath, he deeply resented the doctor's approach.

He hadn't even gotten in line himself. Who did this man think he was?

Of course someone this good would draw more and more admirers over time.

Still, it didn't sit right with him.

Something as simple as adding a contact had thrown the whole group into a quiet uproar.

Paisley, who'd caught every flicker of expression the whole time, felt half exasperated, half amused.

Did her family and friends really think she was this helpless?

She wasn't some naive little flower who didn't know the world.

She didn't bother calling out their petty, obvious little jealousies. She just looked up and spoke, breaking the stalled quiet.

“Come on, let's go get dinner.”

With that, she led the way out.

In an instant, every bit of sourness vanished, and they all fell in step behind her without a word.

The other doctors and nurses in the hall watched them go, murmuring in quiet envy.

“Who even is that girl?”

“Every single person around her is top-tier.”

“Look at how careful they are with her. It's obvious—she means everything to them.”

The doctor stayed where he was, pulling up his contact list, looking at her simple, clean profile icon with a small smile he couldn't quite help.

Meanwhile, the group made their way to the base cafeteria.

The cafeteria took either contribution points or crystal cores.

With ten thousand fresh points in her account, Paisley had no reason to be stingy. She booked a private room without a second thought.

“Stewpot's on me tonight.”

Supplies were thin in this world, produce scarce, meat a luxury. Even a hot meal was indulgence enough, let alone a full spread simmering in a communal stewpot—something almost none of them had dared to hope for.

Every face around her lit up at once.

Ashley burst out happily.

“A stewpot?! Paisley, really? That's way too much!”

“No, no, we can't let you pay for this, let us cover it!”

But underneath the protests, everyone else was already overflowing with happiness, eyes bright with anticipation.

Only Elijah stayed unhurried, his gentle eyes full of indulgence as he nodded, his voice warm.

“Then thank you, our Pais.”

He looked up and smiled, an easy, striking thing, the kind of face that belonged in a painting—there was simply no one else like him.

The sheer brilliance of that smile caught her off guard, and for a second her eyes went blank, caught on him.

Her chest gave a small, startled lurch. Color rushed to the tips of her ears, a faint pink spreading over her cheeks, her heartbeat stumbling out of rhythm.

******

She pushed the feeling down fast, turned her flushed face away, and went back to studying the menu as if nothing had happened.

Seeing that, the others stopped arguing and started calling out what they wanted, a solid mix of meat and vegetables, more than twenty plates in all.

When the bill came, the price turned out to be a surprise.

More than twenty generous plates, and it only came to twenty-four contribution points total—about one point a plate.

Paisley checked her balance, pleased.

She now had 61,714 contribution points. After the meal, she'd have 61,690 left—a solid cushion, by any measure of this world.

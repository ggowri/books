Chapter 159

[Host! This is it! I smell precious metal — so much gold!]

Trovie's voice buzzed, spinning in excited circles.

Paisley crossed to the safe and ran her fingers over the cold metal.

It was built tougher than it looked — reinforced alloy plating, a digital lock embedded dead center, screen dark, keypad unresponsive. Whoever owned it had locked it down for good. No cracking this one open.

She didn't bother. A flick of her eyes, and she gripped the Frostbloom Sword, pouring energy into the blade. Frost surged along the edge, sharp enough to cut bone.

Whoosh —

One clean strike, straight down.

The thick alloy panel and its dead lock split apart in one motion, the cut smooth and even, the door falling open.

Brutal. But effective.

Paisley allowed herself a small, satisfied thought. This sword had been worth every point.

Alloy, reinforced locks, stone — none of it held up to this blade. Just like Trovie promised. Cuts through anything.

Gold light poured out of the open safe, lighting up the dim office.

Rows of gold bars stacked neatly inside, solid and heavy, the shine unmistakable, packed floor to ceiling.

Beyond the bars: sealed gold coins, jewelry, platinum pieces, even a few chunks of raw gold, polished smooth. Priceless.

In the gray ruin of the apocalypse, a hoard like this hit hard — a jarring burst of color against all the decay.

Since the old world fell, money had become worthless. Only she, it seemed, could still get excited about a pile of gold.

Trovie:

[Host, we struck it rich. Five hundred fifty pounds of gold.]

Without hesitation, Paisley opened her Storage Dimension.

Take it!

Every gold bar, gone.

Take it!

Loose coins, jewelry, raw gold chunks — every last piece, gone.

In seconds, the safe sat empty.

Trovie's wings blurred with excitement, its voice cheering.

[Congratulations, Host! We're rich!]

Paisley had figured out one thing by now — collecting things made Trovie unreasonably happy.

A faint smile crossed her face, calm as ever.

[Still not enough. Only five hundred fifty pounds.]

She said it lightly, then gave a quiet instruction.

[Trovie, scan again. Where else is there gold?]

Trovie scanned the whole mall in a flash, blue light sweeping every floor, and came back with an answer.

[Host! Fourth floor! Tons of precious metal and jewelry up there!]

A mental map of the mall's layout surfaced in Paisley's mind.

First floor: groceries, food, daily supplies.

Second floor: clothing, bedding, home goods.

Third floor: electronics.

Fourth floor: the luxury section — gold, jewelry, the richest floor in the whole building.

Fifth floor: the movie theater and food court.

The basement — supplies, crystal cores, gold — was already cleared out.

No time to waste. Paisley headed for the second floor.

******

She turned and walked out of the office, heading up, Kitty trotting close behind.

The second floor was eerily quiet compared to the chaos below. Only a scattering of Level Three rotting zombies wandered here, likely scared off by the noise from downstairs.

The few that noticed her turned stiffly and lunged, only to drop before they even got close, cut down by a few casual slashes, barely worth the effort.

With the floor cleared, the full layout came into view.

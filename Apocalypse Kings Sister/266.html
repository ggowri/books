Chapter 266

The cold, cutting pain faded fast, strength returning to his limbs as his blood began to flow freely again.

The suffocating fog in his head lifted slowly along with the healing light.

Elijah forced his eyes open, catching a hazy glimpse of something achingly gentle.

Paisley's eyes were lowered, focused, soft green light wrapped around her fingers, warmth soaking into him and chasing away the cold.

She can heal too.

Healing and Storage, both among the rarest Affinities in this ruined world.

On top of Ice and Plant. How much more is she hiding?

Something indescribable churned in his chest, equal parts awe and gratitude.

Someone this extraordinary, and she's the one I love.

If not for her, I'd already be dead.

By the time the last of the venom was gone and the wound had closed, he finally found his voice, rough but sincere.

"Thank you..."

Seeing his eyes open and his breathing steady, Paisley finally let herself relax, her expression softening.

"Don't mention it."

She leaned in to check him over properly.

"Anywhere else hurt?"

He glanced down at the wound that had been gruesome minutes ago. Now there was only a faint pink scar, no sign the poison had ever been there.

His body felt clear, light, most of his strength already back.

Her healing is that powerful.

The wound's just gone.

It's unbelievable.

Then, the next second, he went rigid.

******

He was still slumped against her, close enough to catch the clean, faint scent of her.

His ears flushed red, heat crawling down his neck and up his cheeks.

He'd always kept his distance from anyone, always stayed composed. He'd never been this close to a woman before, and now he didn't know where to put his hands.

His heart raced, panic and longing tangled together. Fully healed now, he still didn't want to move, didn't want to give up the warmth.

Seeing him go quiet and stiff, Paisley frowned slightly, confused.

She asked, gentle.

"What's wrong? Still hurting somewhere?"

That shouldn't be. I healed everything.

My healing's reliable. He shouldn't still be reacting like this.

Without thinking, she pressed a hand to his forehead to check his temperature.

It felt warmer than it should.

She took it at face value.

"You feel a little warm. Probably a mild fever."

"Let's get you back to the car. Some fever medicine and some rest should do it."

Before he could respond, she scooped him up, one arm under his back, one under his knees, and started toward the door.

A woman just over five foot seven, carrying someone over six foot three, without any apparent effort at all.

Once the weightlessness faded, all that was left was pure, burning nerves.

He didn't dare lift his head, didn't dare move, just lay stiff against her, her clean scent covering every trace of blood around him.

He cracked his eyes open just enough to watch the set of her jaw.

Moments ago she'd been cutting down a Level Six without blinking. Now she carried him like he might break.

The walk to the car felt endless, and over in a heartbeat, both at once.

His heart pounded, every second agony and something he wanted to keep.

It was over too fast because the car was close.

With one hand she opened the back door and set him down gently inside.

The cabin was quiet, sealed off from the wind and the wasteland, the small space warming with something unspoken.

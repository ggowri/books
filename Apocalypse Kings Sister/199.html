Chapter 199

Did she really not know her own limits?

Knowing how dangerous the aquarium was and still signing up anyway—you had to give her some credit for nerve.

Still, fine.

If she wanted to perform that badly, wanted the credit that badly, let her have her chance.

This wasn't the life where Rachel Lennox got whatever she wanted anymore.

Out of the corner of her eye, she caught Elijah and Orion's brows tightening, clearly about to object to Rachel joining. She raised a hand at once, signaling them without a word to let it go.

Seeing she had no objection, the two of them swallowed their misgivings and nodded along in silence.

With the roster settled, the Director called out the order.

“Everyone in position! Move out now—the aquarium mission starts!”

At the word, fourteen team members filed out in order, heading for the motor pool.

Three military SUVs had been assigned for the trip, heavy-bodied, hardened against blasts and collisions—built specifically for high-risk excursions into the ruins.

Paisley, Elijah, and Orion took the lead vehicle.

Rachel, determined to latch onto her target, got her wish and was placed in the second vehicle with Chase, along with three other team members, five in all.

The rest took the third car, and the convoy moved out together.

The vehicles rolled steadily out through the base gates, racing toward the abandoned coastal district on the city's edge.

In the first car, Paisley sat in the back seat, and with a thought, spent a thousand points at the Trading System's marketplace for two Poison Immunity Pills.

Each one gave twenty-four hours of total immunity to poison, warding off any toxin, contamination, or mutant venom.

The aquarium's toxins were unpredictable, and the group had nearly died to them yesterday. She wasn't about to gamble on luck twice.

A thousand points wasn't a steep price, well within what she could spare—and worth every bit of it to protect the people who mattered to her.

Trovie's voice chimed, soft as ever.

[1,000 points deducted. Two Poison Immunity Pills acquired.] [Remaining points: 288,060.]

With a flick of her fingers, two pills appeared in her palm, pure white and faintly fragrant.

******

Once, she'd thought of them only as useful allies, people worth sticking close to.

But these past days had shown her something else—people who'd shielded her, favored her, thought of her before anything else, over and over.

They mattered to her now more than almost anyone.

Of course she'd think of them in return.

She reached out and handed a pill each to Elijah and Orion.

“The toxins in there are nasty and hard to guard against. These are Poison Immunity Pills, good for twenty-four hours. Take them and you're clear of every kind of contamination.”

They trusted her without a second thought, took the pills, and swallowed them down.

The pills dissolved on contact, a warm, sweet energy spreading from their throats out through their whole bodies, a faint protective layer settling over them, every inch of them at ease.

Before either of them had fully caught their breath, Paisley raised her hand again, and two matte black rings appeared in her palm, finely made.

“And this.”

“Storage rings—about a hundred square feet of space in each. Should be more than enough to hold whatever you pick up today.”

She held them out, one to each.

Both took theirs at the same time. The moment their fingers brushed the cool, smooth metal, something enormous broke loose behind their eyes.

They looked at her together, shock and disbelief giving way, in the end, to something closer to being moved.

A storage item. Of all things.

This wasn't anything this era was supposed to have.

In a world this starved for resources, storage items were priceless beyond market value—even a basic hundred-square-foot ring was something nobody could get their hands on, no matter what they offered.

They'd never imagined she'd be carrying something this precious, let alone handing it over without a second thought, one to each of them.

Elijah closed his fingers around the ring, something bright and warm filling his eyes, his gaze fixed on her, too moved to find words.

She looked cool and distant on the surface, but underneath, she was soft as anything—never one for warm words, but giving them everything good she had in her own quiet way.

This looked almost like a ring. Someday, he'd put a real one on her finger—one that belonged to just the two of them.

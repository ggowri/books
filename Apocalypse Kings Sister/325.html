Chapter 325

Thick, rotten venom-stench hit everyone at once, making heads spin, vision blurring at the edges.

Rachel, standing off to the side, hadn't gotten a clear view yet, but the second she did — rows of blood-red slit pupils filling the dark, that cold hissing right against her ears — raw terror seized her chest.

Her legs buckled, knees shaking uncontrollably, cold sweat soaking her palms, her grip on her rifle going stiff, barely able to keep a finger on the trigger.

In that moment, there was nothing left in her but fear, bone-deep and total.

She'd seen mutant beasts before, but never anything like this — these twisted, half-human horrors.

Half-human, half-serpent, armored in scales that shrugged off bullets, claws dripping corrosive venom, and those cold, bloodthirsty eyes, empty of anything but the drive to kill. Every detail of it was wrong.

Worse, the scraping of scales still echoing out from the tomb's depths made it clear — there were more. Countless more, waiting in the dark, ready to strike at any second.

"How— how are there so many…"

Rachel's teeth chattered, her voice shaking apart, thin and tearful, every ounce of her earlier arrogance gone.

She instinctively shrank toward the center of the group, desperate to avoid facing the serpents directly, pressing close to Chase, searching for anywhere that felt safe.

The two lead serpents leapt into the air, claws dark with venom slicing down toward them, death closing in fast.

Rachel squeezed her eyes shut, her heart hammering against her ribs, her whole body locking up, cold and useless, her breath coming in short, panicked gasps.

Despair crashed over her completely.

She regretted it with everything she had.

Why had she pushed so hard to join a mission this dangerous?

Why hadn't she just stayed safe at the base?

Those venom-proof scales, the endless threat pouring out of the dark, their escape route sealed — were they just going to die here?

The terror finally overwhelmed whatever composure she had left.

Everything went black, and Rachel's knees gave out. She crumpled to the ground, unconscious.

...

Meanwhile, on the other side of the forest, at the surface facility entrance.

A heavy, oppressive silence hung over the place.

Paisley, Elijah, Orion, Gabriel, and the others stood before the thick alloy door, the surrounding area dead quiet.

Inside the sealed facility, though, a steady, muffled pounding echoed out — thud, thud — like something, or many things, were slamming themselves against the door from the inside, over and over, the force growing with each strike.

Whatever was in there felt like a wave building toward a breaking point. The second that door opened, it was going to hit hard.

Gabriel ran his fingers over the rusted lock mechanisms and sensor panels, his expression grim, voice low.

"At least a dozen humanoid test subjects behind this door. Long-term confinement, they're agitated, and their reflexes are going to be sharp."

Elijah's Affinity stirred quietly around him, his gaze catching the faint black mist leaking from the door's seams, tone serious.

"Heavy genetic alteration residue. These subjects are going to be faster and stronger than any ordinary mutant. No pain response, no fear. And their regeneration's probably extreme."

Orion stood tense, alert to every sound, eyes locked on the door, his voice low and urgent.

"Pais, get behind me. Stay close."

Paisley nodded, stepping dutifully behind him. A pale green light curled around her fingertips, steady and constant, her refined Plant Affinity sense spreading fast through the stone and cracks underground, reaching across the whole buried complex.

A map of the tunnels unfolded in her mind, sharp and clear, and with it, the dense, chaotic rage pulsing from the tomb's corridor — along with several faint, failing signatures, panicked and on the verge of collapse.

"They're surrounded by a nest of serpent mutants. They're in serious trouble."

"We need to reach them fast. We have to get them out."

Gabriel, steady as always, didn't ask a single question. He made the call immediately.

"No more waiting."

"We break through here, clear every test subject inside, push through to the lower levels, and reach them from the inside."

The moment he finished speaking, he raised a hand, Metal Affinity surging around him, a pale gold glow flaring in the dim light, fine metallic particles swirling at his fingertips.

Faced with the door's complex locking mechanism, he reached out directly, his Affinity condensing into a perfect key at his fingertips.

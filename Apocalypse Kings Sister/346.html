Chapter 346

Not far off, Gerald Thompson stood tall, composed as always, a man who rarely showed emotion — but his eyes had gone red and wet, fixed on the girl before him, holding back years of guilt, longing, and overwhelming joy, waiting quietly to one side.

On the other side, Orion, Spencer, Thomas, and Ashley, just finished with their own registration, hurried over to form a gentle wall around the two of them, blocking every curious stare.

Watching his mother cry and his sister freeze up, Orion's chest softened, and he spoke gently.

"Mom, ease up a little. You're scaring her."

"We've been traveling all day to get back here — everyone's starving."

That snapped Diane Thompson back to herself. She loosened her arms at last, though she kept Paisley's hand clasped tight, her fingers trembling, refusing to let go even an inch.

She studied her daughter's face carefully, every feature familiar, years of longing finally settling into place. Tears glittered in her eyes even as she smiled, murmuring over and over.

"Pais. You're home. You're really home..."

No forced drama to it, just the plainest, most honest joy.

Paisley looked down at the warm, rough hand holding hers, feeling the unreserved love and care from the family around her.

From stiff and lost to slowly sinking into the warmth of it, something soft that had been locked away for years stirred loose inside her, a quiet warmth spreading out and chasing away a long, cold loneliness.

This was what real family meant — thinking of nothing but her, accepting her, favoring her, no conditions attached.

"Come on. Let's go home."

Her mother kept her hand tight, voice light and doting.

"The second I heard you were coming back yesterday, I couldn't sleep all night. I've already got a whole spread of your favorite dishes waiting."

Her father stepped closer, his voice warm and rough.

"She's right. Your mother didn't sleep a wink, just hoping you'd come home safe, hoping to see you again soon."

The whole family gathered around Paisley.

For the first time, she understood what family actually felt like — the first time she'd ever had people who were truly hers.

After the brief, warm reunion, she said a quiet goodbye to Elijah, Marcus, and the others.

Before leaving, Elijah lingered a moment, watching her surrounded by her family, something soft in his eyes.

He wanted to say something before he left, but realized he had no standing to, no reason to intrude.

It left a sour taste in his mouth.

Someone had come for Marcus too. He glanced at Paisley once, reluctant, before he went.

Not far off, Rachel, Reid, and the others, just finished with registration themselves, watched the whole warm reunion in silence, something ugly twisting in their chests.

Rachel looked around, left and right, but found no parents waiting for them.

The future stretched ahead blank and uncertain, and worse, she'd lost the Affinity she'd once leaned on — from the celebrated prodigy of her last life to nothing but an ordinary person at rock bottom.

The gap between then and now bred a wild, twisted jealousy in Rachel's chest, resentment taking root and spreading.

Why her?

Why does Paisley get to be this lucky?

Lost for years, and still she comes back to a top family that dotes on her, with talent and power everyone envies.

And here I am, biting my tongue at every turn, living off other people's goodwill just to survive.

A storm of bitterness churned inside her, and a new, vicious scheme began taking root.

The pendant!

I have to get my hands on that storage pendant of hers, whatever it takes.

She was certain the pendant was the one real variable in this second life.

If she could get it, she could rebuild her foundation, awaken again, maybe even surpass her last life entirely.

For now, Chase was the perfect tool to use.

She'd already noticed it, right there at the airfield.

Chase's roots in the Aetheris base ran deep too.

If she kept him close, she could lean on his standing to find her footing, then look for her chance to take the pendant and climb back to the top.

With that in mind, she buried the darkness in her eyes, smoothed out every trace of hostility, and leaned into Chase, playing fragile and helpless.

Paisley's group drove off in their luxury cars, while Chase and the three Lennox siblings climbed into the car Damon had arranged, following close behind.

Chapter 305

Meanwhile, elsewhere in the base.

The group followed the aide through a series of covered walkways and manicured grounds — the part of Stillwater reserved for its core personnel.

Clean, quiet, heavily guarded. Nothing like the ruin outside the walls, or the cramped slums deeper in the city.

The villa sat in the safest, quietest corner of the upper district — a two-story private house with its own courtyard, fully stocked, beds made up with fresh linens, every supply they could need already waiting.

The front door opened onto bright, open rooms, simply but tastefully furnished, the whole place radiating comfort.

The aide spoke, deferential.

"This is our highest-tier housing, reserved for guests like yourselves. If you need anything, there's a comm unit in the courtyard. We'll respond immediately."

"I'll leave you to settle in. Please excuse me."

He bowed slightly and let himself out, pulling the gate shut behind him.

The courtyard went quiet, the noise of the base shut out behind the walls.

Ashley sank into the couch with a long breath, relief all over her face.

"We finally made it somewhere decent. This base is strict as hell, but I'll give them this — the hospitality's actually good."

Thomas looked around, his expression thoughtful.

"The hospitality's a front. The surveillance is real. This director's sharper than he lets on. He's being generous, sure, but he's also keeping us right where he can see us."

Spencer nodded along.

"Three days before we leave. Not much time. That lab's chewed up more than one elite team already — some dead, some comatose, and nobody knows why. We need to be ready before we go in. I'm not ending up a vegetable."

Orion stood by the window, eyes drifting over the shadows posted around the perimeter, voice flat.

"Hidden watchers everywhere. We're being monitored around the clock. No real privacy here."

Nobody looked particularly worried. They'd already seen through the setup, and none of it scared them.

Elijah turned to Paisley, his voice gentler.

"You accepted that mission fast. You already suspected the connection to the lab?"

She nodded, stroking the fox's soft fur, her eyes distant.

"The mission's practically identical."

"Same methods, same goal, in both facilities — forcing mutations, studying Affinities, using live subjects. Someone's running this across the whole country. And since we've already stumbled onto it, we need to see it through."

Trovie's main questline had already gone live. Uncovering the truth behind the apocalypse, rooting out whoever was engineering these mutations — it was both a reward waiting to be claimed and a threat they couldn't afford to ignore.

Elijah's expression settled into something more somber. He nodded.

"Agreed. This is something we need to deal with before we head back to Aetheris."

"Three days. Everyone rest up and get ready."

Paisley looked out the window at the orderly rows of buildings, her eyes cool and sharp.

Whatever truth was buried under that lab, this mission was going to be dangerous. She just hoped it went clean.

Everyone split off to get settled. That's when Spencer, always sharp-eyed, noticed something and brightened.

"This place is actually hooked up to satellite."

"Stillwater's really got its act together."

Since the world had gone dark, every network had been down — no way to reach family, no way to know who was even still alive. A working satellite signal was almost unheard of.

"Let me see if I can reach home."

Spencer pulled out his backup phone, plugged it into an outlet, and waited for it to power on.

After a short boot sequence, the screen lit up, and bars of signal finally appeared.

He didn't wait. He dialed straight into a video call with his family.

The ringtone buzzed through the speaker, steady and patient, carrying a hope that had survived the end of the world.

Paisley slipped away quietly, not wanting to watch.

She found a quiet room upstairs and shut the door behind her.

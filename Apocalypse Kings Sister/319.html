Chapter 319

Outside, the evening breeze drifted by. Inside, warmth lingered. In a world this cold and unforgiving, a moment like this felt rare beyond measure.

Elsewhere, at the same time.

Deep in Stillwater's chaotic, run-down slums.

No orderly buildings here, no clean streets. Just a maze of flimsy, sagging tents, sewage running between them — a world apart from the private villas up top.

One cramped, drafty tent held almost nothing — just two worn-out wooden cots.

On one of them lay Owen, unconscious, his face a sickly pale blue, breath shallow. His old wounds had gone untreated for too long, steadily wearing down what little strength he had left.

The tent flap tore open, and a battered figure stumbled in.

Reid dragged himself through the opening, covered in bruises, clothes torn, his lip split and swollen, a red handprint still visible on his cheek. His arms and legs were scraped and bruised all over, every movement pulling at some fresh injury, pain shuddering through him with each step.

Outside, a crooked makeshift stove burned low, the fire barely holding.

Ryan crouched beside it, quietly stirring a pot of cloudy herbal brew, the firelight catching the exhausted, hollow lines of his face — a weariness far beyond his years.

Inside the tent, on a wobbly, stone-propped table, sat two blocks of stale, blackened hardtack, hard as rock — the only food the three brothers had for the night.

Hearing the commotion, Ryan turned, and the sight of Reid covered in fresh injuries made him go still before he shot to his feet.

"What happened? Did someone hurt you?"

Reid dropped to the ground, fury and humiliation boiling together in his expression, his voice raw as he recounted the afternoon, every word bitter.

"It was Paisley."

"She blew twenty thousand points today without even blinking. She's got more money than she knows what to do with now, and all she had to do was ease up a little and we'd be fine."

"But she didn't."

"She stood there and watched them humiliate me, and then just turned around and walked away."

His voice broke, the movement jarring his injuries, and he hissed in pain through his teeth.

"After she left, that woman went even harder on me. Beat me down for real."

"Fifty slaps, right there in public. My face is still swollen. They nearly broke my leg too."

All his pent-up fury and despair finally broke loose, his eyes bloodshot, his voice dripping venom.

"Paisley's an ungrateful wretch, through and through! Cold, heartless, she'd watch us die and not lift a finger! If she wasn't so cruel, we wouldn't be living like this, and Owen wouldn't still be lying there suffering!"

The fire crackled, the murky herbal brew finally starting to boil, steam rising.

Reid said nothing more. He dragged himself up, wincing, and poured a bowl of scalding water, tipping his head back to drink it down in one go, the heat scraping down his throat, dulling some of the anger simmering inside him.

He grabbed the stale hardtack off the table, tore off a chunk, broke it into pieces, and dropped it into the hot water to soften. Otherwise there was no getting it down — his bruised mouth couldn't handle anything that dry and hard.

Stirring the bowl, he noticed how quiet his brother had gone and looked over.

"Why aren't you saying anything?"

In the dim, flickering firelight, Ryan sat with his eyes down, his long lashes hiding whatever he was feeling. After a long silence, he finally spoke, his voice low and rough, carrying the weight of something he'd finally accepted.

"I saw Paisley too, today."

Reid's hands stilled. He looked up sharply, eyes wide with suspicion.

"She went to the third floor of the critical care hospital."

Ryan's voice stayed low and heavy.

"I didn't know what she was doing up there, not until the whole floor started talking about it."

"She was there to save someone."

"The deputy director — the one who'd been unconscious for days, the one every top doctor in the base had given up on. She cured him in a few minutes."

"One job, and she walked away with thirty thousand points."

Thirty thousand points.

The number crashed down and shattered whatever hope Reid had left.

They worked themselves to the bone every day for a fraction of that, and one move from Paisley had just earned her more than they'd see in years.

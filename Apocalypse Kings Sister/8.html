Chapter 8

Paisley returned to the room she'd been given and locked the door behind her.

She walked over to the window, gazing quietly into the pitch-black, lifeless night sky. Her eyes were calm, unreadable.

She remembered clearly—tonight, the third night after the outbreak began, that strange crimson rain would fall right on schedule.

This red rain was the true dividing line of the apocalypse, the moment everything started to mutate.

The rain carried strange mutagenic agents that would sift through humanity. Some people would awaken all manner of powers.

But most ordinary people—those with weaker wills or frail bodies—would have their minds corroded by the agents, mutating completely into mindless, savage zombies.

Overnight, zombies would overrun the streets, abilities would awaken, and the apocalypse would escalate to a whole new level.

Tonight was destined to be sleepless. The entire world was about to face a catastrophe that would shake it to its core.

After locking the door, Paisley sat on the edge of the bed and looked over the gifts she'd just received.

Elijah's handgun and Orion's diamond-studded combat knife—both would be useful tools for survival once things went south. She kept them close, within easy reach.

As for Thomas' luxury watch, Spencer's jeweled hair clip, and Ashley's large ruby... they might have looked impressive, but in the world now, they were practically worthless.

Without a second thought, Paisley stored them all inside her pendant's spatial storage.

The moment the last item vanished into the space, a crisp, mechanized voice—almost cheerful, like a young girl's—echoed in her mind.

"Ding! High-value keepsake detected. Cross-Dimensional Trading Plaza successfully activated!

"Hello. You are now a Cross-Dimensional Merchant. I am your dedicated system, Trovie."

Paisley went completely still, her mind reeling.

Cross-Dimensional Trading Plaza? A merchant?

What was going on? She couldn't make sense of it.

She'd only stored a few gifts. How did that suddenly trigger something like this... and turn her into some kind of merchant?

This was the apocalypse. Zombies were everywhere. Everyone was just struggling to stay alive. Who had time to buy and sell things?

"Right now, staying alive is all that matters. Who's going to bother trading? What's even the point of being a merchant?"

Trovie's voice softened, as though explaining something obvious.

"Host, just open the plaza interface. You'll understand. It has everything you could possibly imagine—and things you can't. Though, a lot of it is... well, out of your budget for now. And not just because of the price."

Still half in doubt, Paisley pulled up the trading interface that had appeared in her mind.

At first glance, the staggering range of goods was overwhelming. It made her head spin, shattering every assumption she'd held about what counted as "supplies".

The smaller items included milkshakes, soda, burgers, fresh vegetables and fruit—rare luxuries in a world where resources were already growing scarce.

But the larger offerings were where things got surreal: hovercars, military warships, missiles... alongside all sorts of overpowered treasures like lightning-warding talismans, invisibility charms, antidote pills, and ability-enhancing crystals.

This was beyond anything she had ever considered as "supplies".

Yet, the more powerful the item, the more absurd its price—and most were grayed out and locked, labeled, "Merchant tier insufficient. Purchase restricted."

Paisley frowned. "Why can't I buy any of this?"

"Host, you're currently a Level Zero merchant. Your permissions are minimal. Higher-tier items require corresponding merchant levels to unlock. The highest tier is Five."

"Then how do I level up?" she asked without hesitation.

"It's simple: list items in the plaza, complete transactions, earn credits, and you'll gradually advance."

Paisley understood immediately.

She took the two extra sets of clothes she'd gathered while out scavenging earlier and listed them in the plaza for twenty credits each.

"Once someone buys them, the credits will be deposited directly into your account."

Nodding slowly, she also listed her surplus toiletries, tissues, dried rations, and other daily necessities, pricing each one carefully.

Afterward, she stared at her empty credits panel and couldn't help but mutter inwardly, "Leveling up is going to take forever. I don't have a single credit yet. How am I ever supposed to reach Level Five like this?"

Trovie chimed in gently, trying to reassure her.

"Don't worry, Host. Once you save up a hundred credits, you'll advance to Level One. Then you'll unlock cross-dimensional matching—connecting directly with buyers who need what you're selling. Things will move much faster after that."

Paisley fell quiet for a long moment. Then she asked the question that had been weighing on her mind more than any other.

"Am I your first host?"

What she really wanted to know was whether Rachel—the one who'd stolen her pendant in her past life—had ever activated this system or become a cross-dimensional merchant.

If Rachel had possessed an advantage like this back then, there was no way Paisley's bomb would have taken her down in the end.

Trovie's reply was plain and direct.

"I was activated and brought into being by the ruby you stored just now. From start to finish, you have been—and will always be—my only host."

Paisley's thoughts suddenly clicked into place.

No wonder—even when Rachel had stolen the pendant in her last life, the space never grew beyond a hundred square feet.

Rachel had only ever accessed basic storage. She never triggered the system, let alone became a cross-dimensional merchant. Upgrading the space was never in the cards for her.

All Rachel ever got was a lousy hundred-square-foot storage room.

The real opportunity... had always been hers alone.

Paisley focused her mind and asked the system, "My space is only a hundred square feet right now. It's way too small. How do I make it larger?"

"Each time your merchant level rises, the pendant's space will expand and gain new functions. The higher your level, the larger and more advanced the space becomes."

Paisley's eyes brightened instantly.

So that was the space's real secret.

Thank god for second chances.

Just then, a soft chime echoed in her mind.

"Ding! One of your listed items has been sold!

"Twenty points have been added. Your current total stands at twenty."

A faint smile touched her lips, but it quickly faded.

Earning points was painfully slow. She still needed eighty more.

At this rate, it'd take forever.

She was quietly turning over ideas to speed things up when a hurried knock rattled her door.

"Paisley? You in there?"

It was Orion. His voice was tense, edged with worry.

Her instincts prickled—something wasn't right. She pushed off the bed and opened the door.

"What's going on, Orion?"

His eyes swept over her, checking her from head to toe. Only when he saw she was unhurt did his shoulders loosen. He let out a slow breath.

"It's raining hard outside. Weird rain—red, like rust or something. Everyone's gathering in the main hall. You were so quiet up here... I got worried."

His tone softened, layered with real concern.

"This red rain... it doesn't look right. Come downstairs with me. Safer to stick together right now."

Paisley gave a quick nod and followed him out, heading down toward the crowded hall.

The atmosphere downstairs was thick and quiet.

Everyone had gathered, faces uneasy, voices low.

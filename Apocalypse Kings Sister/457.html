Chapter 457

A few teammates crowded around and watched their friend convulse and rapidly change. Their eyes went red, their faces grim, and they felt helpless.

"It's over... With a double poisoning like this, unless there's a special antidote, or a high-level healer, there's no saving him..."

"Two days. The poison's been eating him that long. He's dying..."

Hopeless sighs filled the room, heavy and sad.

Cole clenched his fists until his knuckles went white. His brother-in-arms was about to turn and was hanging on by a thread. His eyes were bloodshot and desperate, with nothing he could do.

He whipped around to look at the Falcon Squad, holding on to one last hope, his voice hoarse and shaking.

"Do you... Does your team have a healer? Please, can you save my teammate? He's young. He can't die like this!"

Elijah looked down at the wounded man on the verge of collapse and total mutation, then turned to Paisley standing quietly at his side. He didn't decide for her. He simply looked at her gently, silently asking what she wanted, leaving the choice entirely to her.

Under everyone's anxious eyes, Paisley slowly stepped out from behind the others.

A quiet, steadying strength came off her.

"Let me look."

Three cool, plain words, and they silenced the whole room's sighs and panic.

She walked slowly to the man on the stretcher.

By now the young man was unconscious, his body convulsing violently, limbs rigid, curling and twisting out of control.

Under his clean skin, black poison lines spread wildly like spiderwebs, crawling from his chest to his neck and jaw, even toward his eyes and forehead.

It was the final sign of turning, lizard venom stacked with deep mental contamination. The toxin had rooted into flesh, the contamination had eaten into his mind. Not even medicine could fix it. It was a death sentence in the apocalypse.

Black blood kept trickling from the corner of his mouth, his breathing was ragged and weak, and every convulsion seemed to pull away another thread of life. Death hung heavy around him.

The Lone Wolf members clenched their fists, eyes red, and turned away, unable to watch. In their hearts they'd already declared their friend dead.

Cole stood nearby, his back stiff, the last thin thread of hope in his eyes, his heart in his throat.

He knew even a legendary squad from Aetheris Base probably couldn't reverse a mutation this far gone. It was a dead end no one could cross.

But they had no other options. They'd have to try anyway. What if?

They couldn't just watch a comrade die.

Paisley knelt and put her fingertips on the young man's neck. His skin was cold and stiff. His veins were choked and dying, the toxin coiled in his organs.

Her eyes narrowed as she quickly traced the injuries back to their source.

A moment later, a clear, warm green light bloomed from her palm.

Not a harsh glare, but a soft, pure glow, full of life. It was Plant Affinity healing and purifying.

Mid Level Seven power flowed gently, warm but strong, carrying a holy force that cleansed all filth, and settled over the young man's entire chest.

******

"Hmmm..."

A faint hum of power sounded.

The young man, who'd been convulsing and twisting wildly, slowly settled, strangely calm.

The savage black lines retreated before the green glow, like snow in the sun, fading and vanishing visibly fast.

The venom and contamination lodged inside his body were peeled away and purified bit by bit.

At the same time, threads of healing power seeped into his damaged organs and withered veins, tenderly mending the flesh rotted by poison and closing his torn wounds.

The room was dead silent. A pin could be heard dropping.

Everyone held their breath, eyes wide, locked on the scene that defied everything they knew. Their minds went blank, and they forgot to react.

The man's blackened lips slowly regained their color.

The burning fever drained away fast, and his skin returned to normal temperature.

The hideous mutation lines vanished without a trace. Even the pooled blood beneath his skin and the dead tissue in his wounds were repaired.

Ten minutes.

The young man who was supposed to die had stopped convulsing. His breathing was even and long, his face peaceful, and the pained knot between his brows slowly eased.

Chapter 4

Paisley was no stranger to scenes like this—the kind that screamed the end of the world. Without flinching, she weaved past the shuffling zombies and slipped into an empty apartment building on the side of the road. She checked each floor quietly before settling on a fairly clean unit on the second floor to lay low for a while.

After locking the windows, barricading the door, and making sure she was sealed off from the dangers outside, she reached for the pendant she always wore close to her heart. Without a second thought, she bit down on her fingertip and let warm blood drip onto its surface.

The moment the blood soaked in, the pendant began to glow softly. Light rippled across it, and just like that, a storage space about the size of a small bedroom quietly unfolded before her.

A faint gleam passed through Paisley's eyes as she stepped inside.

One corner of the space held a clear, shimmering pool of spirit water, glowing faintly and humming with energy.

She knelt, cupped a handful of the water, and drank it straight down. It was cool and sweet, flowing down her throat before blooming into warmth that spread through her entire body. The exhaustion from days on edge melted away, leaving her feeling lighter and alert.

Everything from this space was top-tier—that much was obvious.

A sudden surge of scorching heat burst forth deep inside her core, spreading fast across her whole body. A warm, tingling ache bloomed along every vein.

She was awakening an ability. And not just any ordinary one—it was a Plant Affinity, the rarest kind to surface since the world fell apart.

Plant Affinity came with two gifts: speeding up plant growth and healing wounds. It was incredibly rare, a top-tier support ability that every surviving group fought over.

In her past life, it was Rachel who had awakened Plant Affinity.

With that ability in hand, Rachel had played the saint—healing the injured, boosting crops, winning everyone's trust without breaking a sweat. She became the one people cherished, the one they'd risk their lives to protect.

Meanwhile, Paisley had survived with no ability at all, forced to train herself to fight zombies, always pushed into the deadliest situations.

Rachel, you stole what should've been mine.

Pushing down the anger rising in her chest, Paisley took a steadying breath and scanned the space again.

The spirit water was almost gone—she'd drunk what was left.

And the storage area was only about a hundred square feet, just enough for some basic supplies. Not nearly enough to last long.

A thought crossed her mind.
Could the space level up, like abilities did?

She just didn't know what it would take.

Right now, gathering supplies was her top priority. That was what would give her a real shot at staying alive.

In her last life, Rachel had used this space to live easy, winning everyone over and climbing to the top over Paisley's own downfall.

This time, all of it would be hers.

Cool-headed, Paisley quickly mapped out her next move.

This part of town was lined with supermarkets and convenience stores. The outbreak was still new—most places hadn't been picked clean yet. It was the perfect chance to stock up.

Using memories from her past life, she smoothly avoided several zones crawling with zombies and kicked open the glass door of a corner store with sharp, efficient movements.

The place was empty. Shelves were tossed around, dust already gathering in the silence.

No time to waste. With a sweep of her hand, bottled water, protein bars, instant ramen, bread, and cans lifted from the shelves and neatly stacked themselves into her storage space.

Rice, flour, cooking oil, snacks, candy, dried goods—anything that could keep her going, she took. She cleared out every last bit.

Right after, she moved to the pharmacy next door. Hemostatic gels, antibiotics, bandages, antiseptics, fever reducers, painkillers—she took them all. In times like these, medicine was as good as currency. You could never have too much.

In under half an hour, she'd cleaned out every small shop on the block.

Her hundred-square-foot space was now packed tight with supplies.

Night fell deeper. The clock ticked past eight.

After dark, the streets grew riskier. More zombies wandered out, their low groans rising and falling as they drifted toward the main road.

With low visibility, moving around was twice as dangerous.

Paisley knew when to stop. She turned back toward the second-floor apartment to rest up.

But as she reached the base of the building, she froze.

There in the courtyard sat several brand-new black SUVs, parked like they owned the place.

Someone was here.

Every nerve in her body went taut. She shifted her weight, ready to turn and disappear into the shadows.

The next instant, a shadowy figure stepped in front of her, blocking her path.

"Hold it right there. Who are you? What are you doing out here alone after dark?"

A cold, sharp blade pressed against Paisley's throat, the metal biting into her skin with a silent threat.

Her eyes stayed sharp and steady, showing no fear. Her fingers had already found the dagger at her own waist, ready to strike if needed.

Just then, a tall, straight-backed man emerged slowly from the house.

"Mason. What's going on?"

His voice was low, cool, and carried a calm authority that felt anything but ordinary.

Paisley's instincts tightened. This guy was dangerous—really dangerous.

A one-on-one fight earlier she could've handled, no problem. But this man's presence changed everything.

For now, she'd have to play it careful and wait for a chance to slip away.

Orion Thompson moved closer and took her in—just a slim young girl, hardly a threat. He frowned, his tone edged with impatience.

"Put the knife away, Mason. She's just a kid. What's she gonna do? You're being jumpy."

He walked up to Paisley, his gaze lingering with a hint of something softer, almost gentle.

The thought of his own little sister—missing all these years, always just out of reach—made his chest tighten. His voice softened without him meaning it to.

"Don't be scared. We're not gonna hurt you. Name's Orion. This here's my friend Mason."

He paused, studying her. "What's a girl like you doing out here by yourself?"

Paisley's mind raced.

Orion.

In the later years of the apocalypse, he and Elijah Blackwood built Epoch Enclave—became legends, rulers of their own territory.

Back in her past life, people killed just to get close to him, to have his protection when everything fell apart.

And here he was, right in front of her, on the third damn day of the end of the world.

A plan clicked into place.

If she could stick with him, she'd not only survive—she could make the Lennoxes pay. She could live to see whatever came after all this.

She had to make this work. She had to stay.

Knowing how guarded top survivors could be, Paisley let all her hardness fall away. She lowered her eyes, softened her tone, and let herself sound young, lost, harmless.

"I'm Paisley. I got separated from my friends and ended up here. I'm not... I'm not with the bad ones, I promise."

The name hit Orion like a physical blow.

His whole body went still. A wave of emotion surged behind his eyes—disbelief, wild hope, years of longing all rushing to the surface.

His voice came out rough, barely steady.

"What did you say your name was? Say it again."

Beside him, Mason drew in a sharp breath and stepped forward.

"Orion... Paisley. It could be her. Your sister. The one you've been looking for all this time."

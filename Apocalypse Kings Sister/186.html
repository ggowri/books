Chapter 186

For once, points felt easy to come by.

Paisley didn't hesitate. She looked the Director straight in the eye, her tone level, neither deferential nor defiant.

“All right. I'll try to heal them.”

Then, just as firmly, she set her terms.

“I have two conditions. First, everyone leaves. No one stays in the room while I work. Second, this stays strictly confidential. I don't want anyone else here knowing what I did. I have no interest in that kind of attention.”

Everyone in the room froze, staring at her in surprise.

Most people turned timid and over-careful in front of the Director, hardly daring to raise their voice.

But this young woman stood her ground before him, calm and unbothered, laying out her terms without a trace of hesitation or fear.

That kind of nerve wasn't something most people had.

Surprise flickered across the Director's face, then understanding.

An ordinary young person would have been dazzled senseless by fifty thousand points, falling over themselves to agree to anything.

But she'd taken the offer in stride and still dictated her own terms, holding the room without even trying.

That kind of composure, that kind of presence, wasn't something ordinary people had.

She clearly had the skill to back it up—that was where the confidence came from.

“Done.”

The Director didn't hesitate. He agreed at once, his voice firm, and waved the room out without delay.

“Everyone clears the treatment area now. No lingering. What happens here today is top secret—no discussing it, no repeating it to anyone.”

“Anyone who breaks that answers to military law.”

The medical staff and accompanying officers filed out at once.

Within moments, the wide treatment room stood empty, the doors sealed, cut off from every bit of outside noise and prying eyes.

The silence was absolute. Only the five poisoned, barely-breathing soldiers remained, and Paisley.

Her expression settled into pure focus.

She crossed to the first bed, her eyes falling on Orion, whose face had gone the darkest, whose breath was the weakest of them all.

His face, usually so composed and sharp, was pinched tight now, lips cracked and blue-black. Beneath his skin, dark veins of toxin crawled and spread. His life force was fading, close to gone.

She lifted a hand at once, fingers pale and slender, and pressed them to his wrist. The moment she touched him, she felt the toxin raging wild inside him.

It was cold and vicious, clinging to his meridians like something grown into the bone, eating steadily into his organs—and it devoured anything used against it. No wonder every attempt at healing had failed.

Fortunately, her Plant Affinity countered every kind of toxin and contamination there was.

She steadied her breath, closed her eyes, and let her Plant Affinity—mid Level Six, pure and deep—flow slowly outward.

A warm, clear green light spread from her palm, nothing like the thin, pale glow of ordinary healing. Hers was dense and rich, brimming with life, carrying a purification force strong enough to wrap around his entire body.

******

The green light seeped into him through every pore and meridian, gentle but relentless, working its way toward the black toxin buried deep in his blood.

Thread by thread, the dark corruption was wrapped, pulled loose, and forced out along his meridians.

The contamination that had run rampant through him dissolved, broke apart, and faded away under her purification.

Five minutes in, his breathing began to even out and deepen, the dark bruising draining visibly from his face.

His skin returned to its normal color, and his cracked, blue-tinged lips slowly took on a faint, living pink.

The toxin was gone entirely, and the healing energy went on to mend every meridian it had corroded, every organ it had worn down.

Only once she was sure he was fully out of danger, fully healed, did she let the energy in her palm fade.

She wiped a thin sheen of sweat from her brow, a faint weariness settling over her features from the energy she'd spent.

High-tier purification and healing cost far more than any offensive Affinity ever had.

If her Plant Affinity hadn't already reached Level Six, her reserves deep enough to carry it, she'd never have managed purification this thorough.

She pulled a stool over and sat, and with a thought, pulled a cup of spirit water from her Storage Dimension to drink.

Chapter 173

The instant the jerky hit, the flower's jaws clamped down on the overwhelming heat.

The massive body, mid-attack, mid-coil, froze solid.

A second later, a burst of searing agony exploded through its softest, most vital core.

Fed on rot and poison for so long it had grown immune to acid itself, this thing had never encountered anything like the raw heat of ghost-pepper spice — a weakness it never knew it had.

The brutal heat tore through its system, burning along every channel, and the tender, poison-nurturing core inside began to shrivel and rot, like wildfire raging through its veins.

A deep rumble —

The towering mass of petals convulsed violently!

The massive petals burst apart in an uncontrolled spasm, dark green venom and shredded petal fragments raining down, scorching the ground into blackened craters, smoke hissing up everywhere, the stench of poison thick enough to choke on.

Lost completely to the agony, its massive roots thrashed beneath the ground, tearing through the earth, cracking and collapsing the entire field, crushing the lesser flowers in its uncontrolled fury.

It couldn't gather another ounce of toxic pollen. Its affinity energy spiraled out of control, collapsing in on itself, the once-overwhelming pressure draining away by the second.

High above, Paisley stood steady on her platform of willow branches, eyes cold and clear, watching the Level Six monster thrash below.

Below her, the mutant willow pressed its advantage, thousands of green vines shooting out like unsheathed blades, piercing clean through.

The branches that had just cleared the perimeter surged forward, locking around the flower's thick, twisted roots, tightening layer after layer, cracking the hardened plant flesh, dark green sap oozing out.

Pure, vital plant energy devoured and suppressed the corrupted toxin, dismantling the flower's core essence piece by piece, sealing off any chance of escape or counterattack.

The mutant willow grew visibly stronger with every passing second.

Inside the ward, the group watched in open-mouthed shock, awe written across every face, unable to process what they were seeing.

It can do that too?

"Paisley took down the flower with a bag of spicy jerky. That's insane!"

Ashley's eyes went wide, disbelief plain on her face.

"Guess everything really does have a natural counter. Paisley's a genius — who even thinks of that?"

Spencer murmured, something close to reverence in his voice, fully won over.

The others turned to Paisley, eyes full of outright worship.

Her strength really had reached a terrifying level.

The flower's convulsions grew worse, its massive body withering faster now, the shattered petals hanging limp, the hardened roots turning soft and rotten, its once-overwhelming Level Six presence crashing fast.

It thrashed, desperate to break free of the willow's grip and flee, but the vines only tightened further, the willow's vitality grinding down its poison core, feeding on its life force without pause.

As the last of the heat burned through its core, the massive body locked up, every struggle stopping at once.

The huge flower withered, collapsed, and grayed out at visible speed, the roots sprawled across the field drying and crumbling, every trace of life and poison gone.

Even with most of its body shattered and withered, the core root of the Level Six flower still clung to the earth, its main stem holding on with the faintest trace of life, a last whisper of toxin still lingering beneath the soil.

End-times monsters were stubbornly resilient — as long as the root survived and the crystal core stayed intact, it could eventually feed off the poisoned soil and come back.

It had to be finished, completely.

Paisley's gaze sharpened. The willow lowered its branches, setting her gently back on the ground.

She gripped the Frostbloom Sword, frost coiling along the blade, pale light cutting through the last traces of thin poison mist.

******

She stepped forward, facing down the last thick, hardened stem of the root. Whatever defenses the flower had once carried had collapsed along with its main body — only the stem remained, barely holding on.

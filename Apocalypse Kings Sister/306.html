Chapter 306

She showered and washed her hair, damp strands dripping cool beads of water down her neck, the sharp edge she usually carried softened into something looser, more at ease.

She'd just finished drying her hair when a soft knock came at the door.

Ashley's voice drifted in, small and uncertain.

"Paisley? Can I come in?"

Paisley wrapped the towel around her shoulders and opened the door, her expression mild.

"What's up?"

Ashley stood there gripping her phone tightly, hope and nerves fighting for space in her eyes.

"Mom wants to video call you. Is that okay?"

The question stopped Paisley cold. She went rigid where she stood.

Two lifetimes now, and the word "mom" had never once felt warm. It was something distant, almost foreign to her.

In her first life, twenty years under the Lennox roof, she'd tiptoed through every single day. Her adoptive mother had never once looked at her with anything but cold indifference.

All of that warmth, every bit of it, had gone to Rachel. Spoiled rotten, wrapped in cotton, worshipped like glass that might break. None of it had ever been meant for her.

As a kid, she used to lie awake wondering if something was wrong with her — if she just wasn't good enough, wasn't obedient enough, to deserve being loved.

She'd hoped. Over and over. And every time, she'd been let down.

As she got older, that hope wore down to nothing.

By the time the apocalypse hit, in the last moments of her life, she'd finally seen the Lennoxes for exactly what they were. Whatever thin thread of hope she'd still had left snapped for good.

This time around, she'd made herself hard. No soft spots. No craving for family, no letting anyone get close enough to hurt her again.

That's why she'd walked out of the room earlier.

But now, with this unexpected offer of family sitting right in front of her, she couldn't push down the fear.

She couldn't stop herself from wondering — would her real mother end up the same? Warmth and favoritism reserved for someone else, and nothing but indifference left for her?

She was scared. She didn't dare let herself hope anymore.

Watching Paisley go blank, that flicker of fear crossing her face, something in Ashley softened. She reached out gently.

"Paisley, she really does love you."

"You went missing as a kid. It's been eighteen years, and she never once stopped looking for you. Every single day, she thought about you. She never gave up."

Her eyes started welling up, her voice catching.

"I'm actually dad's war buddy's daughter. Growing up, the family always talked about the Thompson's missing eldest daughter."

Her nose went pink, tears gathering, and she dropped her voice even smaller.

"If… if my being here makes things weird for you, once we're back in Aetheris, I'll move out. I don't want to make you uncomfortable."

A tear slid down her cheek as she said it, and it twisted something in Paisley's chest.

Paisley snapped out of it, looking at the girl in front of her, red-eyed and bending over backward to accommodate her, and the knot in her chest loosened into something gentler.

She reached out and wiped the tear from Ashley's cheek, her voice soft.

"Why are you crying? Who said anything about you leaving?"

"When did I ever say I wanted you out?"

She ruffled Ashley's hair, her tone serious now.

"You're my sister. A real Thompson. You're not going anywhere."

"I'm just scared, that's all. I'm not ready yet. It's not about you."

She looked down, hiding the uncertainty still churning underneath.

"I'm not ready to see them, to talk to them on a call yet. Give me a little time. I'll get there."

She needed time to think this through.

The shadow of her last life still hadn't let go of her.

This family, eighteen years late — did she even want it?

Ashley wiped her eyes and pulled Paisley into a hug, trying to comfort her.

Right then, Thomas's voice called up from downstairs, breaking the quiet.

"Pais, Ashley, come down, lunch is ready."

By the time they got down, everyone was already seated, nobody bringing up the call.

Elijah, sharp as always, caught the flicker of something off in her expression right away.

Chapter 321

His voice carried the kind of natural authority that made people listen.

As his gaze swept the room, cataloging each member, it suddenly stopped.

Their eyes met, and the air seemed to still for a second.

Something shifted behind Gabriel's eyes.

The slight, composed figure who'd pulled him out of that coma yesterday was standing right in front of him now.

He'd woken up, and she'd slipped away before he could even get her name, leaving him with no way to track her down.

He'd wanted to find her — bring her along on this mission, if he could. Having her here would cut their losses significantly. He also wanted to thank her properly.

He hadn't expected to find her standing in this very room.

His gaze lingered on her face for a beat before he pulled his expression back into something composed, settling back into the role of commander.

He looked away, scanning the room again, and moved on to briefing the mission.

"Target for this operation: a secret man-made research facility in the western outskirts. We need to fully investigate the site, recover all research data and toxin samples, and contain any mutant test subjects. If containment isn't possible, kill them."

"Based on prior intel, the facility's internal structure is complex, multiple levels. The toxin inside matches exactly what put our soldiers into comas — it corrodes Affinity and paralyzes the mind. Worse, the test subjects are highly intelligent. The upper body's still human, they can still talk, and their regeneration is extremely fast. They're hard to kill."

"If you encounter one, do not engage alone."

He laid it all out clearly — terrain, enemy capabilities, extraction routes, nothing left unaccounted for.

"Deep inside the facility, there's an unknown high-tier progenitor. That's our biggest threat. Nobody pushes forward alone or goes off-script."

With the briefing finished, Gabriel wrapped it up, voice firm.

"Bottom line — stay sharp, hold formation, watch each other's backs."

He shifted gears.

"We're splitting into two teams. Pick your own partners."

The moment he said it, murmurs broke out across the hall.

Rachel stepped up beside Chase immediately, chin lifted, voice pitched loud and deliberate, dripping with pride.

"Chase here is a peak Level Five Psychic Affinity user. Top-tier combat power, incredible field control. Anyone who wants to make it through this mission clean should team up with us."

Psychic Affinity was already rare and powerful, and a peak Level Five was a height most ordinary fighters could never reach.

Plenty of mid- and low-tier fighters in the room perked up at that, and after a moment's hesitation, a steady stream of them started drifting toward Rachel and Chase. Within seconds, more than half the room had gathered around them.

Rachel surveyed the crowd forming around her, a satisfied, smug glow in her eyes, her lips curling upward.

So what if Paisley and her group were strong? When it came to connections, popularity, and pulling a team together, Paisley would never measure up to her.

A knot of resentment twisted in her gut, thinking back to yesterday, something dark churning beneath the surface.

Yesterday, Paisley's group had clearly had the means to help, and they'd chosen not to — just driven off without her, leaving her and Chase to walk the whole night back to the base on foot. Her feet still ached like they were full of lead, every step a struggle.

She wasn't about to forget that.

She and Paisley were past the point of reconciliation now.

Sooner or later, Paisley would be caught alone and vulnerable, and when that happened, Rachel intended to make sure she never got back up.

On the other side of the room, Elijah, Orion, and Paisley stood together, composed, their presence cutting right through the crowd. Four people broke off from the group and crossed over to them.

It was the captain from the gate the day before.

He spoke, earnest and direct.

"The four of us would like to team up with you."

He'd seen firsthand what these three could do against the dead. He knew exactly how strong they were — sticking with them was the safest bet by far.

Rachel watched the exchange and scoffed.

Gabriel's gaze swept over both teams, tallying numbers, and made the call.

"Teams are set. Here's how it's going to work."

His eyes landed on the group of seven standing with Paisley.

"This team's light a member. I'll join this one and move with you for the mission."

Chapter 353

Elijah went on to lay out the rest of the group's strength as well.

"The Thompson siblings who came back with me — every one of them's Level Six.

Between them they cover Metal, Plant, Fire, and Earth. And Pais has two — Storage and Plant, which is almost unheard of. She always seems to have another surprise waiting."

******

His father's face lit with astonishment he couldn't hide.

"I never imagined all of you would turn out to be Level Six. What a stroke of luck."

"With you all in the mix now, it's about time the balance in this base shifted."

Just as they were discussing what came next and how to position the younger generation, the landline rang sharply, breaking the quiet of the hall.

His father picked up, and Walter Coleman's warm, hearty voice came through the line, inviting him sincerely.

"Chief, the Colemans are putting together a small dinner tonight, and we'd love to have your whole family join us — partly to welcome the kids home safe, and partly to talk through what's next."

His father agreed at once with a smile.

"Of course. We'll be there shortly."

He hung up and looked over at Elijah, his expression turning serious again.

"Mr. Thompson's dependable, our best ally. With all his children turning out to be top-tier fighters now, our odds just got better. Tonight's dinner is a perfect chance to tie our people together and align on what's next."

With the Blackwoods weakened and morale shaky, most of their old allies had already defected, leaving only the Thompsons, the Colemans, and a handful of others still standing firm.

In a situation this precarious, every solid alliance was a lifeline for turning things around.

His father understood exactly what he meant, a knowing smile crossing his face before his tone shifted, turning more serious.

"Well then. Since we're on the subject, there's something I need to tell you too."

"Over twenty years ago, your grandfather and the Thompson patriarch arranged a betrothal between you two. The girl was the Thompsons' daughter — the one they just found again. Paisley."

He looked at his son steadily.

"Now that you've fallen for her, and our two families need each other more than ever, tonight's the perfect moment to bring up the engagement. Lock it in, and it ties our families even closer together."

Elijah blinked, surprise flickering across his usually composed face, then shook his head, a stubborn edge in his voice.

"An engagement? Dad, why have you never mentioned this before?"

"I do like her. But bringing up an engagement out of nowhere right now might not be a good idea at all."

He understood his own heart perfectly, every word sincere.

"What I want is for her to care about me because she wants to — not because some old contract ties her to me."

"She's not like anyone else I've ever met. She's strong-willed, independent, sharp — not someone you can manipulate or push around.

Dad, I know what I'm doing. Please don't complicate this."

His father froze, caught off guard entirely. He'd never expected his steady, obedient son to dig in his heels like this.

After a moment, he gave in, a note of rueful resignation in his voice.

"Fine. We'll do it your way. Just don't come crying to me if you regret it."

"There's no shortage of fine young men in this city — good families, real strength. Without a betrothal, without a title, what makes you so sure she'll choose you in the end?"

Elijah looked up, something in his eyes clearer and more resolute than his father had ever seen.

"Then I'd rather she never feel forced into it at all."

"Liking her is my own business. It shouldn't become a reason to pressure her."

"She deserves to live free and easy, without a worry in the world. What I feel for her was never meant to tie her down — it's meant to keep her safe, make her happy."

Looking at the pure, unwavering conviction in his son's eyes, his father gave up trying to argue entirely, waving a hand in surrender.

"All right, all right. You young people and your romances — it's your call. I'll stay out of it."

With that settled, father and son got themselves ready and set off for the Thompson house.

Meanwhile, in the Quinn family's inner council chamber —

the massive room sat dim and tense, more than a dozen of the Quinn family's core lieutenants gathered, every face grim.

They'd gotten word the moment Elijah's group returned safely and had pulled their registration records overnight. When the screen lit up with one Level Six reading after another, the whole room drew a collective, stunned breath.

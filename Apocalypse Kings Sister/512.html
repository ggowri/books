Chapter 512

"That seems like the best idea we've got.

It's a long river. We can't take it all in one trip.

Paisley's space is huge, but it can't be big enough to hold a whole river…"

"And I've never heard of a Storage Affinity user who can take a river…"

His sentence was still hanging in the air, unfinished.

Paisley looked up calmly and thought a command. In the next instant, the sound of rushing water disappeared.

The entire underground river, which had been flowing calmly a moment ago, shimmering, vanished together with the pebbles in its bed. Only a dry, empty bedrock floor was left.

Everyone went silent.

They stood frozen, looking at each other, eyes wide, minds blank.

Orion's confident words stuck in his throat. His mouth hung open and wouldn't close.

Elijah's tall frame stiffened. His eyes were locked on the spot where the river had been, shock churning in them.

He'd always known Paisley's storage dimension wasn't ordinary, but he'd never imagined it could swallow a whole living river.

Ashley gasped, hurried to the empty riverbed, turned around, and looked at Paisley in awe.

"Paisley! How big is your storage dimension? You took a whole river like it was nothing! This is insane!"

Spencer stood there staring at the bare ground, waves of shock crashing through him.

Ordinary Storage Affinity users could usually only store lifeless things, and in limited volume. Who could take in flowing water, riverbed and all?

Paisley lifted a hand and covered her mouth, letting out a small burp.

It was as if swallowing a whole river had been a huge tonic.

Even she hadn't expected it. A pleasant surprise, and she loved it.

Trovie really was reliable. When she got back, she'd give Trovie an extra lollipop.

Ashley's eyes moved to the fruit trees all around. She walked a circle around the apple trees, then looked at Paisley, hopeful.

"If only we could take all these fruit trees back to the base too!

Paisley, could you put these fruit trees right into your storage dimension?"

Thomas laughed and quickly cut in.

"That's a pipe dream. Nobody's storage dimension can do that. Don't put Pais on the spot…"

Again, his sentence was never finished.

Paisley stepped forward and waved a hand.

A faint tremor rumbled. More than fifty fruit trees heavy with fruit, along with the rich soil beneath them, vanished from the spot.

The thriving orchard was now just flat stone.

A second shock hit everyone, and they were speechless again.

Orion took a deep breath, thoughts racing.

Whose Storage Affinity could be this powerful?

Taking living water, uprooting a whole orchard and moving it. It was far beyond the limit of any Storage Affinity, and far beyond anything they understood.

Only Paisley knew it all came down to Trovie's special power.

Trovie could do almost anything. But this was a secret she'd keep in her heart forever and never tell anyone.

Elijah came back to himself and looked at Paisley with open admiration.

"Thank goodness you're here. We solved the base's water crisis this fast because of you."

Ashley rushed to Paisley's side, eyes shining.

"Paisley! Mission accomplished. Can we head back and turn it in now? This trip was amazing!"

"Thank goodness I have you with me."

Paisley said calmly, "Let's get ready to head back."

They turned back through the passage toward the cave exit.

A little later, gray daylight shone in from the mouth of the cave, mixed with the smell of earth after the Crimson Rain.

They'd just stepped out when noisy shouting and arguing hit their ears.

On a fairly flat stretch of rock outside the cave, two teams were facing off.

On one side was an ordinary base scavenging team of five. They wore ragged clothes, their protective gear torn in several places. They looked tired and had small wounds.

They had no strong backing. They were ordinary Affinity users who'd labored for ages and finally found a small basket of fruit and a sack of potatoes in a crack in the rocks.

It was food for several days.

Now they were guarding the basket tightly, pale-faced, but stubbornly refusing to back down.

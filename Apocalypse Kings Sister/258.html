Chapter 258

"Now isn't the time to be afraid. This world doesn't go easy on anyone just because they're weak."

"Strength is the only thing that matters now. Keep begging and backing down, and you'll always be at someone's mercy. You have to learn to fight for yourselves."

Gentle, but sharp enough to cut, the words landed hard in every one of them.

After a moment of silence, one girl rose, shaking, but certain.

The one who'd just been assaulted.

Hair tangled, face streaked with tears, her whole body shaking, eyes full of shame, pain, and hatred.

Months of being trampled on, all of it breaking loose at once.

She bit down on her trembling lip, tears streaming, and reached for the blood-stained blade on the floor.

The hilt was ice cold, but it gave her courage anyway.

She gripped it hard, raised it with everything she had, and brought it down on the helpless man.

"I HATE YOU—!"

The scream tore out of her, years of grief finally breaking free.

THUD!

Blood splattered the floor again.

The man never had a chance to fight back. He dropped dead, paying at last for everything he'd done.

With the weight of it finally lifted, the girl's legs gave out and she sank to the floor, sobbing into her hands.

The crying was both grief and relief, a rebirth out of the dark.

The other girls watched, and the fear that had ground them down began to lift, replaced by something closer to rage.

They stopped shaking. Stopped shrinking back. One by one, they straightened up, and in each other's eyes they found the same resolve.

No more hesitation. They walked toward the frozen man, steady and sure.

******

For weeks they'd been caged, violated, their dignity ground into the floor, living every day in fear, given no choice but to endure the worst this world had to offer.

Now the chains were broken, and their tormentor was helpless. Finally, a chance to settle the score.

Some grabbed an iron rod, some a broken plank, some the bloody blade, hands shaking but eyes steady.

Together, they turned on him.

Clumsy, maybe, but every strike carried the full weight of everything they'd suffered.

Dull impacts filled the room, and the last of their tormentors stopped breathing.

Every crime, paid for in full.

There was no wild celebration when it was over. Just a heavy, quiet release.

They weren't lambs for the slaughter anymore. They'd torn the darkness apart with their own hands.

Paisley watched quietly, then turned to leave.

Whatever came next, no one could walk it for them.

Salvation in this world wasn't something handed down by someone else. It was waking up in the worst of it, daring to fight back, daring to pick up a weapon.

They'd done it.

In her head, Trovie asked, puzzled.

[Host, you're just leaving? They clearly want to say something to you. They've been watching you this whole time.]

Paisley didn't slow down, her tone unwavering.

[In this world, you learn to save yourself.] [I'm not some saint. I can protect them for a moment. Not for the rest of their lives.] [I can't take them all with me.]

She moved on quickly.

[Where's the food storage? Take me there.] [The upstairs room only had a bit of ration bars and bottled water. There's no way that's all the food in this place.] [A man running an operation like this must have a bigger stockpile somewhere.]

Trovie scanned at once.

[Found it! There's a huge storeroom next to the first-floor kitchen, packed with food!]

Paisley hurried downstairs, and ran into a guard the moment she reached the first-floor hallway.

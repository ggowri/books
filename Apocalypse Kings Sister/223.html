Chapter 223

The minds of the undead had already been locked down by a presence far stronger than hers, something just as binding as what the old man had used beneath the lab, impossible to shake loose.

There was no other way to break the horde's control. The only answer was killing whoever stood behind it.

Paisley steadied her breathing and pushed her psychic power out again, full strength, countless signals pulling together in an instant, locking onto the exact location of the one controlling them.

"Kitty, scale up."

The little white cat leapt without hesitation, its body surging several times larger, fur fluffed out and gleaming, posture shifting into something fierce and powerful.

Paisley swung herself up onto its back.

The cat released a burst of energy, frost sweeping out ahead, locking the oncoming undead in ice, clearing a path.

Across the dark wasteland, the Frostbloom Sword caught the light, glowing pale and steady like a lantern in the night, cutting through the darkness and marking the way.

In just three minutes, the two of them covered a thousand yards, tearing straight through the horde to reach the top of a hill.

Wind howled at the summit, the view stretching wide open. A man in black, his face hidden behind a strange mask, stood there alone.

In his hand, he held an old bronze bell. It was clear he'd been using it to control the horde from a distance.

The masked man's eyes went wide with shock the instant he saw Paisley and the oversized cat racing toward him. His voice cracked with disbelief.

"This... how is this possible... how did you find me?"

His face twisted with panic, the words cutting off before he could finish.

Paisley's gaze stayed flat and cold. She raised her sword and swung, a flash of light cutting through the dark.

One clean strike, straight through his throat.

Blood sprayed hot across the air.

She'd killed plenty of undead before, but this was her first time taking a human life since the world fell apart.

******

The warm, slick feel of it hit her first, the stench of blood flooding her senses, a wave of nausea rolling through her.

She pushed the feeling down and bent to search his pockets fast, pulling out a pouch full of crystal cores and a token stamped with an odd symbol.

Could be tied to whoever he was reporting to. Worth keeping.

Then she picked up the old bronze bell and gave it a light shake.

The clear chime carried out across the field. Below, the once-disciplined, fearless horde lost every trace of control at once, scattering into total chaos, swarming in every direction.

The squad fighting for their lives below caught the shift immediately, relief washing through them, confidence surging back.

"Ms. Lennox did it! She took out whoever was controlling them!"

"They've got no one running the show now! Every kill counts!"

The group rallied, pushing through exhaustion to cut down the scattered undead.

Without a controller, the horde was nothing but scattered, easy kills.

Morale soared. They kept at it, and before long the last stragglers had either fled or fallen.

The smoke cleared slowly. Everyone stood bent over, hands on their knees, gasping, blood-soaked and battered, strength and Affinity both pushed to their limit, but every face carried the relief of having made it out alive.

They were still alive. That was what mattered.

The group took a short rest, patched up their wounds, and set out again for base.

Back at the base leadership's office,

Rachel and Chase, seeing the others hadn't returned, had already decided they weren't coming back.

Perfect timing for the two of them to play the heroes.

They made sure to look appropriately wrecked.

They'd faked a handful of shallow wounds on themselves, gone pale, forced their breathing ragged, playing the part of survivors who'd barely escaped with their lives.

Chase knocked weakly on the director's door.

"Reporting in, sir. The aquarium mission failed. The squad was overwhelmed by high-tier mutants. Everyone else... didn't make it."

Rachel stood beside him, eyes rimmed red, forcing a choked sob as she chimed in right on cue.

"The two of us only made it out because of my precognition and Chase's mental Affinity. We barely survived.

Everyone else is still down there. They're never coming back."

The two of them played off each other perfectly, building themselves up as the survivors who'd clawed their way back through sheer force of will. Once this settled, surely the base's leadership would reward them for it.

The senior staff in the room looked visibly shaken, grief settling heavy over the office, the air thick and suffocating.

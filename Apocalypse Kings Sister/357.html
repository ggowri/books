Chapter 357

Paisley relaxed a little, letting the fluffy white fox and the soft white cat slip out of her Storage Dimension, settling obediently onto the carpet and the edge of the table, not far from her.

She fed them bits here and there.

Ashley sat right beside her, eyes shining as she stared at the two little creatures, delighted, feeding them bits of dried meat now and then.

She turned to Paisley, hopeful.

"Pais, I love them so much — they're so fluffy and cute! Think you could catch me one of these someday?"

Paisley watched her well-behaved little pets eat, expression softening, and thought it over for a moment.

"If the chance comes up."

That didn't discourage Ashley at all — if anything, she smiled wider, plucking up a glistening piece of braised pork rib and setting it carefully in Paisley's bowl.

"That's fine, I can wait!

Pais, try this — it's one of Mom's specialties, my favorite dish of hers."

A trace of nostalgia touched her eyes.

"This is the one thing I missed most, the whole time we were away."

Paisley looked down at the glossy, well-seasoned rib and felt a quiet warmth as she took a bite.

The meat fell right off the bone, salty and sweet in equal measure, a rich, homey flavor she'd never known in either of her lives — the taste that belonged only to family.

She nodded seriously, her voice soft.

"This is really good. Mom's an amazing cook."

It was the first time in her life she'd eaten a home-cooked meal made by her own mother.

One simple bite, and it carried all the family warmth she'd once only dreamed of.

Her mother sat nearby, watching her eat so contentedly, seeing her daughter, finally found again, sitting right beside her — a sudden ache rose behind her nose, her eyes going hot.

She reached over quickly to add another rib to Paisley's bowl, her voice gentle and thick with emotion.

"If you like it, have more. I'll make it again tomorrow."

Her eyes reddened fully as she spoke, years of buried ache impossible to hide any longer.

The thought of her daughter wandering for over a decade, unloved, never once tasting a meal her own mother made, left a raw ache in her chest.

Paisley picked up on it at once, setting her chopsticks down to gently rub her mother's back, her voice soft and soothing.

"Mom, don't be sad. I'm okay.

I'm home now. I can have it every day from here on out."

The gentle words settled over her mother like sunlight, easing a tension she'd carried for years.

Beside them, Mrs. Blackwood watched the whole scene quietly, moved by the warmth of it, her own eyes reddening as something she'd been carrying for days finally surfaced.

She let out a tired breath, her voice heavy with exhaustion.

"It's wonderful, seeing your family whole again like this."

Then her expression clouded over, her voice rough with worry she couldn't quite hide.

"Not like mine. We're barely holding together.

My husband's been poisoned, bedridden for days now. The city's best research institute has tried everything, and they still haven't found a cure."

"The poison itself is strange — clearly someone did this deliberately, meaning to kill him outright."

******

Despair and helplessness filled her eyes.

"We still haven't found the source, or any way to cure it. No one knows... how much longer he can hold on."

Days of relentless worry had worn down even this composed, dignified woman, leaving her visibly fragile.

Paisley looked up at her, the softness in her eyes giving way to something calm and serious, and asked at once:

"Mr. Blackwood's poisoned? What kind of poison?"

She wasn't sure if her Plant Affinity could cure something like this.

But even if it was some rare, incurable poison, her system shop carried a Universal Antidote Pill for just three hundred points — something that could cure any poison in the world.

Mrs. Blackwood shook her head.

"I don't know. It must be something especially stubborn, or it wouldn't have lingered this long."

Paisley, who understood the current state of the base well enough by now, knew Mr. Blackwood simply couldn't die.

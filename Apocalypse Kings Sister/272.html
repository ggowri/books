Chapter 272

Everyone burst out laughing, and the courtyard filled with easy, cheerful noise.

Soon they'd cleaned the fish and paired them with fresh vegetables picked from the greenhouses, setting a full fish feast across the wooden table.

The braised fish gleamed a rich red, the sweet-and-sour fish was bright and tempting, and the sour cabbage fish was savory and fragrant. Stir-fried greens and cold-tossed vegetables rounded it out.

It was the most satisfying, abundant meal any of them had eaten since the apocalypse began.

The mood at the table was relaxed and warm. As they ate, talk turned to their route ahead.

Thomas set down his fork, his expression turning serious.

"I scouted the area earlier. There's no danger nearby for now, and it's safe.

The dozen or so greenhouses here are all intact, and the produce inside is fresh and untouched by the fallout. Safe to eat, and safe to stockpile."

He made a suggestion.

"Why don't we spend this afternoon harvesting everything, and head out first thing tomorrow morning?"

Elijah gave a small nod. "Works for me. After lunch, everyone pitches in."

Nobody objected. Heads nodded all around.

The vegetables on the table had all been picked and cooked that same day, fresh and crisp and sweet, the fish rich and savory. Every bite tasted purely natural, worlds apart from ration bars and instant ramen.

After lunch, the whole group threw themselves into harvesting.

The dozen greenhouses held every kind of produce imaginable.

Bright green lettuce, glossy bok choy, plump Shanghai cabbage, crisp cucumbers, round tomatoes, long zucchini, tender string beans, full bell peppers — row after row of seasonal vegetables thriving, bursting with life.

Paisley's eyes lit up as she scanned the fields. On impulse, she reached out and tried pulling the harvested produce straight into her storage space, and to her surprise, it worked.

Within a few minutes, half the fruit and vegetables from the greenhouses had already vanished into her storage.

Her storage space kept things fresh.

Whatever she pulled out, whenever she pulled it out, it would look exactly as it had the moment she'd put it away.

The instant the bulk of the produce disappeared into her storage, Trovie's chime rang out in her head.

[Large quantity of fresh produce detected!]

[Bonus points earned: 8,000!]

[Current total points: 510,060!]

Paisley's eyes brightened slightly.

She'd only meant to stock up on fresh vegetables, scarce as they were in the apocalypse. She hadn't expected a bonus on top of it.

After the collapse, fresh produce had become nearly impossible to find. Sweet, juicy fruit was worth its weight in gold, and most people couldn't get so much as a taste of it.

Now her storage space overflowed with every kind of fruit imaginable, sweet and fragrant. In this world, that made it priceless.

Within minutes they'd stripped every greenhouse bare, every last bit harvested and stored.

Ashley stared at the storage space now packed with supplies and couldn't help but cheer.

"This is amazing! We won't run out of fresh produce for at least two weeks! No more eating instant food every single day!"

Orion laughed too, sounding genuinely moved.

"This stop was worth it. Finding this much uncontaminated fresh supply is rare. If only we had some obvious way to store it all."

Paisley said nothing.

Only Elijah and Orion knew that what she'd just collected would stay fresh.

The fewer people who knew, the better.

The two of them had covered for her without being asked. The others had no idea.

******

Elijah's gaze swept over the neatly sorted piles of fruit and vegetables. His voice came low and even as he laid out the plan.

"We'll split the produce evenly."

He gave the group a serious warning.

"Tomorrow we leave this place and head out of the city.

The outskirts are rough terrain with plenty of cover, which means more places for trouble to hide. Stay sharp on the road ahead."

Night fell, and the courtyard dimmed under cool lamplight. They made camp there, cooking up a rich pot of fish soup loaded with fresh vegetables. Everyone ate their fill and then some.

The next morning, under the first gray light of dawn, they finished breakfast, packed up, and set out.

The cars pulled away from the farmhouse and rolled toward the outskirts.

The road ahead stretched out in ruin — collapsed buildings choked with weeds, streets dead silent, not a soul in sight. Only the occasional low-level zombie shuffled along aimlessly, slow and witless.

Chapter 174

Paisley's wrist turned, sharp and clean, and the built-up force of the sword finally released.

Shhk —

A crisp, cutting sound rang out through the field as the Frostbloom Sword's innate ice-breaking power sliced clean through the stem's last hardened shell.

The thick plant core split open, fine cracks spreading instantly through the whole stem, the last flicker of life freezing solid and shattering under the cold edge of the blade.

She drove the sword tip down into the soil, following the withered root system straight to its deepest core, and with a light flick of her wrist, pulled out a dark green crystal core, radiating a faint, dying trace of poison, fully intact.

The moment the core left the root, the last trace of life drained out of the stem, and it crumbled to dust on the spot.

The Level Six mutant carnivorous flower — ruler of the entire amusement park — was finally, completely dead.

With the source of its power gone, the thick purple-black mist thinned and vanished, the sharp rotten smell clearing from the air, the cracked earth slowly settling, and the poisoned plants around the field shedding their twisted colors, returning to something close to ordinary growth.

The oppressive, deathly weight over the park lifted entirely, clean air rolling back in, the whole field settling into quiet calm.

Nearby, the all-purpose ward shimmered and faded, the barrier protecting the group finally dissolving.

The group slowly came back to themselves, staring at the transformed, harmless field in front of them, at the heavy Level Six crystal core in Paisley's hand, still too stunned to speak.

After a few seconds of silence, the pent-up emotion broke loose, and everyone erupted into cheers at once, relief and joy flooding through them.

"It's dead! That thing is actually dead!"

Mason let out a long breath, voice loud with relief, the tension finally draining out of him.

"We're not going to die — Paisley saved us!"

Madison's eyes shone, the despair from before completely gone, replaced with pure joy.

Thomas looked at the restored plant life, still half in disbelief at what he'd just witnessed, voice quiet with awe.

"Who would've guessed that monster would go down to a bag of spicy jerky. Paisley's a genius — who even thinks of that?"

"I'm going to be telling this story for the rest of my life."

Spencer looked at Paisley, calm and composed as ever, admiration plain on his face.

"Pais, you're incredible.

If it weren't for you, we'd all be dead today.

You keep pulling miracles out of nowhere and keeping us alive."

Ashley ran up to her side, eyes shining with worship.

"Paisley, you're amazing!

Only you could think of something this weird to turn the fight around!"

Every word was genuine, nothing forced.

They'd all just stared death in the face. They knew exactly how terrifying that flower had been.

Which made Paisley's win all the more incredible to them.

In that moment, their faith in her hit its absolute peak.

This girl, who looked so slight and distant on the surface, had just torn a path through certain death with nothing but her own will — and carried every one of them through it alive.

While they stood there marveling, a few seconds passed, and the mutant willow finished devouring every last trace of the lesser flowers' roots, venom, cores, and affinity energy.

Thick, pure monster essence poured into its core, nourishing every branch and vein.

Hum —

A ripple of energy spread out, pure, vivid green light bursting from its trunk.

The willow's presence surged, climbing fast, breaking straight through its Level Four peak bottleneck and settling into mid Level Six.

The moment it broke through, the massive tree's branches shrank fast, condensing, shedding bulk, growing smaller and more refined by the second.

In moments, the once towering spirit tree had shrunk down into a small, delicate willow sapling, branches slender and supple, leaves a vivid, tender green, radiating a faint, pure aura of Plant Affinity.

It leapt lightly, riding a gust of wind, and landed neatly on Paisley's shoulder, its thin branches trailing down, brushing against her hair, gentle and affectionate.

Paisley tilted her head, eyes soft, and gently stroked the tender new leaves resting on her shoulder.

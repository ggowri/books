Chapter 154

"Let's head out too."

Everyone moved fast, loading into the SUVs.

Two vehicles total.

Thomas drove the lead car. Paisley took the passenger seat, loose and relaxed, a fruit lollipop in her mouth, Kitty curled in her arms.

Kitty had a tiny lollipop of her own clamped between her teeth, cheeks puffed, perfectly content.

In the back seat, Ashley leaned forward, voice sugary.

"Paisley, I want a lollipop too!"

Days on the road together had worn off her early shyness completely. She'd long since figured out Paisley's real temperament — distant on the surface, warm underneath, and easy to push a little.

Paisley's mouth curved, soft. Shielded by her bag, she pulled three more lollipops from her Storage Dimension, unseen, and passed them to Ashley, Spencer, and Madison.

"Thanks, Paisley!"

Ashley's eyes crinkled with delight.

Madison took hers with a quiet, gracious thanks.

Thomas kept his hands on the wheel, eyes forward, voice casual.

"Paisley, unwrap mine for me."

Days of fighting side by side had worn the distance between them down to nothing. He was easy with her now, no walls left.

In the second car, Mason drove. Spencer took the passenger seat. Marcus sat alone in the back.

His eyes stayed locked on the car ahead.

Inside it sat the woman who'd saved his life. The woman he kept tucked away in his heart — Paisley.

These past few days, he'd tried more than once to get close, to talk to her. Something always got in the way.

Just this morning, he'd meant to take the seat beside her in the first car. The others had laughed him straight into the second one instead.

But Marcus didn't let it get to him.

As long as Paisley stayed unmarried, he still had a chance. Time was on his side. One day, he'd close the distance.

The two SUVs rolled steadily down the empty, ruined streets, heading for the mall in the eastern district.

No one noticed the black car trailing far behind, keeping a careful distance, silent.

Inside it sat the four Lennox siblings and Kelly.

Every one of them was running the same ugly math.

Once Paisley was dead, they'd take the pendant.

The road ahead was uncertain. The intent behind them was not.

The cars left the main road, left the base's protected zone, and sank deeper into the wasteland of the eastern district.

The pavement here was cracked and sunken, weeds choking every shoulder, the whole stretch stripped of life — a world away from the order and security inside the base.

Collapsed buildings lined the road in broken rows. Abandoned cars sat tilted and stacked. Dust hung thick in the air over a landscape gone utterly still.

Every so often, a sluggish low-tier zombie wandered into view, sensed the cars, and turned to chase, stiff and aimless — the wasteland's own particular brand of decay.

The car didn't even slow down. It just drove straight through.

Paisley's focus sharpened, her Level Five Psychic Affinity spreading out like an invisible net, blanketing a thousand yards in every direction.

Every blade of grass, every flicker of movement within that radius came through clear as day.

Red dots lit up across her mind, dense and scattered.

Inside the mall — the stairwells, the escalators, the dead corners of every shop — one Level Five lurked, along with seven or eight Level Four mutant zombies, and a swarm of lower-tier ones crowded in tight.

Perfect.

Good practice for everyone.

What caught her attention, though, was the mall itself. The shelves were intact. Everyday goods, stockpiled food — barely touched.

A fully stocked mall this late into the apocalypse was almost unheard of.

Must have been the Level Five wolf's doing, keeping scavengers away.

Lucky. She'd been meaning to stock up on supplies anyway, and here was a full mall, right on schedule.

Ten minutes later, both SUVs rolled to a stop outside the mall's ruined front entrance.

The doors opened. Everyone climbed out, and the cold, dead air of the place hit them instantly — cracked walls stained black with grime, every window shattered, trash everywhere, wind sighing through the gaps with the particular stench of rot that only zombies left behind.

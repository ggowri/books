Chapter 352

"The three-way balance at this base is already cracking. Undercurrents everywhere.

The Quinns are ambitious, quietly absorbing every minor faction they can, chipping away at our resources and connections, pushing harder every day."

"The main Lennox family's deep-rooted and vicious. They play neutral on the surface but they're quietly profiting off every conflict, waiting for their moment. Never underestimate them."

"Word got out that I'd been poisoned, and half the families that used to follow us jumped ship to the Quinns or the Lennoxes. We look solid from the outside, but we're hollow underneath, right on the edge of a fall."

His tone was heavy, summing up the brutal game being played at the top.

"If the Blackwoods ever truly fall, it won't just be us. Every family tied to us — the Thompsons included — gets swept away with us, wiped out entirely."

"The Quinns are ambitious, absorbing every small faction they can, eating into our resources and connections bit by bit, pushing harder by the day."

"The main Lennox family runs deep and plays dirty. They stay neutral on paper, but they're quietly profiting the whole time, waiting to grow. Never easy to deal with."

"Once word got out that I'd been poisoned, half the families loyal to us defected to the Quinns or the Lennoxes. We look steady from outside, but we're hollow at the core, teetering on the edge."

With that, he looked up at the son he was so proud of, exhaustion and helplessness weighing on his face, his voice quiet.

"Good thing you're back. This body of mine — I don't think it'll hold out much longer."

Elijah's chest tightened. His gaze settled heavily on his father's pale, sickly face, worry plain in his voice.

"Father, what poison is this? Is the research institute really unable to cure it?"

His tone was firm, every word claiming responsibility outright.

"From here on, leave all the base business and faction politics to me. Just rest, Father. Focus on getting well."

His father didn't press further on the poison or a cure, setting that worry aside for now. A flicker of relief crossed his face as he turned to his composed son and asked instead:

"After all that time away, where do you stand now?"

"Level Six Lightning Affinity."

Elijah answered plainly, unhurried.

With that, he stepped out of the hall and into the open courtyard.

A flick of his fingers, and lightning cracked through the air, silver-violet bolts writhing and crackling, raw power sweeping the whole courtyard, overwhelming in its reach.

In that instant, the plants trembled, the air itself seemed to freeze, and the sheer force of it made plain exactly what a top-tier Affinity could do.

His father watched, thoroughly satisfied.

No wonder he'd always been proud of this son.

Back inside, Elijah recounted the whole eventful journey home.

He laid it out clearly — the high-level mutant beasts, the ambushes, the desperate shortages — and through all of it, one name came up again and again: Paisley.

She'd broken every deadlock, healed every wound, given up her own supplies when they ran dry. Time after time, at the edge of death, she'd turned the tide alone and kept him safe.

Listening to his son's unbroken praise, his father understood everything in an instant — he was far too sharp not to.

His cold, unbothered son had gone and fallen for someone.

Why else would he care this much about anyone else?

It was rare enough hearing him praise someone this much at all.

A girl who'd caught his eye couldn't be ordinary.

A faint, knowing smile crossed his father's face, equal parts amused and understanding.

"Paisley Lennox? I've never met her, never even heard the name. I never thought I'd hear my son praise a girl this highly."

"Whose daughter is she?"

Elijah answered openly, with no hesitation at all, his eyes sincere and sure.

"Father, she's incredible. She saved my life more than once out there."

"Honestly, without her, I'd probably have died out there — torn apart by a beast or killed by some ambush. I wouldn't have made it back at all."

He admitted his feelings plainly, without hesitation.

"I really do like her, Father."

"She's the Thompson family's long-lost daughter."

Admitting it had never been hard — not since meeting her. The feeling had only grown clearer and warmer, nothing left to hide.

"In my eyes, there's no one in this whole base who can match her. No one better."

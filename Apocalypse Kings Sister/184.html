Chapter 184

“We have a three-hour window. Miss it, and no healer alive could bring them back.”

“Our best chance is a high-tier healer with purification abilities—someone who can strip the toxin away layer by layer and cleanse the contamination.”

High-tier healers were already the rarest Affinity type in this world, and a true purification specialist was rarer still—which made the shortage feel unbearable right now.

The Director's face went darker still. His voice shot up, cracking with panic.

“Nothing can happen to them! Two of the men down there are heirs of the Blackwood and Thompson families, straight out of Aetheris. Their standing is not something we can afford to lose.”

“If anything happens to them on our watch, the whole base pays for it. None of us can carry that.”

There was no room left for delay. He barked the order.

“Send word now! Every registered healer in Rivergate, any tier, mission or no mission—get them here. Whatever it takes, I want these men alive.”

In the crowd, the senior officer whose wife Paisley's squad had once saved straightened at once, nodded, and turned without wasting a second, hurrying off to pull every healer he could find.

Panic rippled through the whole area, the air so heavy it was hard to breathe.

Everyone left was either a doctor or a soldier. No one spoke. Every heart in the room hung somewhere up in the air.

Paisley stood at the edge of the crowd, her eyes dark, a war raging inside her.

She carried high-tier purification and healing power. This strange, corrosive toxin—she had absolute confidence she could clear it completely.

But if she stepped forward now, in front of everyone, and showed what she could do, the whole base—maybe every power with an interest in this place—would fix their attention on her. Staying low-profile after that would be impossible. Trouble, scrutiny, scheming would follow without end.

But her eyes kept returning to Orion on the stretcher, his breath faint, his face a sickly blue, and to Elijah Blackwood beside him, just as unconscious, just as close to death. She couldn't stop worrying about them.

Orion was the brother who'd watched over her quietly, taken her side without question, since the day she came back to this life.

Elijah had been just as careful with her, just as attentive, sincere in every way, never once doubting her. In her heart, he mattered every bit as much as Orion did.

Of everyone since her return, they were among the few who cared for her without an angle, without a price.

And both of them were part of this world's future, its best hope of surviving.

They weren't supposed to die here.

Why were they hurt this badly?

Had her coming back changed something? Set off some butterfly effect she hadn't accounted for?

If so, that made this her responsibility.

They could not die.

Paisley's fingers curled tight, the struggle inside her refusing to settle.

Three hours left. Maybe… maybe the base's healers could still pull off a miracle. Maybe they could still save them.

She'd wait a little longer. Unless there was truly no other option, she didn't want to expose herself.

Time crawled by, each second its own small agony.

Half an hour slipped past.

The senior officer came hurrying back, a whole procession of healers trailing behind him—every registered healing or purification Affinity user Rivergate had on its books, low-tier and mid-tier alike.

No one wasted time. They moved in at once, taking turns working on the five wounded soldiers.

Pale white, soft green, warm yellow—one healing glow after another lit up as streams of restorative energy poured into the wounded. Every antidote and purifying solution they had went into the effort too, everyone straining to hold the toxin back, clear the contamination, repair the damage.

But the same strange thing kept happening, over and over. The instant any healing glow touched a wound, some eerie black mist swallowed it whole, leaving no effect at all.

Worse, every time they tried, the dark bruising on the soldiers' faces deepened, and their breathing grew weaker.

One healer after another stopped, faces pale, foreheads beaded with sweat, shaking their heads as they stepped back.

“It's no use… this toxin is wrong somehow. It eats our Affinity whole. Healing does nothing against it.”

“I can't purify any of this contamination. If anything, I'm making it spread faster.”

“This is past anything I can handle. I've never seen a toxin this aggressive in my life.”

The last healer, the highest-tier among them, finally ran dry. He pulled his hands back, face bloodless, and stared at the scanner readings still climbing worse, shaking his head in despair.

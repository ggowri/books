Chapter 273

Three hours passed, and the group had grown used to the dead quiet stretching endlessly on both sides.

Then, far ahead, a massive evacuation convoy suddenly came into view, its scale impressive even from a distance.

Rows of military vehicles led the way, flanked by soldiers in crisp uniforms standing tall, rifles raised, escorting a huge column of civilians in orderly retreat.

No wonder the road had been so empty. The city and its outskirts had already been evacuated, organized and shepherded out in waves by the military.

These soldiers were staking their own lives to carve out a path to survival for ordinary people.

Suddenly the convoy ground to a halt, brakes shrieking.

The crowd erupted at once, restless and impatient, a wave of irritation sweeping through the whole line. Complaints and shouting rose up in layers, one after another.

"Why have we stopped? What's the holdup!"

"We've barely covered any ground all day! At this rate, when are we even supposed to reach the Stillwater heritage base?"

That was how Paisley learned the convoy's final destination was Stillwater Base.

With the line stalled, the crowd's anger only grew louder, the cursing never letting up.

Paisley's eyes narrowed slightly as she scanned the road ahead, and she made her decision at once.

"Everyone out. There's a horde blocking the way up front."

The group climbed out without hesitation. Paisley waved a hand, and the SUVs vanished into her storage.

Elijah's expression turned cold, his voice flat.

"Go take a look up front."

They moved quickly through the restless crowd, pushing to the very front of the line, and the next second, everyone's breath caught at once.

The road ahead was a wall of black, zombies packed shoulder to shoulder as far as the eye could see, blocking every lane.

The stench of rot hit like a gust, snarls and shrieks rising into a deafening roar, decayed bodies crushing forward in waves, their sheer number enough to make anyone's scalp crawl.

The soldiers up front numbered barely fifty, already firing. Rounds tore through the horde and bodies kept dropping, but the swarm behind them was endless, impossible to clear, impossible to hold back.

Several soldiers bled from gashes on their arms and shoulders, their suits torn by claws, faces pale and breathing ragged, hands trembling around their rifles as the line began to buckle.

But the civilians sheltered behind them showed no gratitude, no shame — only self-interest, growing more restless by the second as the line stayed stuck.

Shoving, cursing, shouting split the air.

In the crowd, the middle-aged man who'd been stirring up the loudest complaints was the worst of them all.

Emboldened by the crowd at his back, he stormed up to the front line and shoved a wounded soldier hard, snarling,

"Why are you blocking the way!

Move it!

If we're late getting to the base, can you even afford the consequences!"

"Do you know who I am? Get out of my way."

The soldier, already running on empty with a throbbing wound, staggered under the shove and collapsed to the ground.

That was what finally broke Paisley.

She'd been scanning the crowd for the zombie controlling the horde when she caught the scene out of the corner of her eye. Cold fury sparked in her chest, and she was already gone, a blur too fast to follow.

Before the man could open his mouth again, Paisley's foot snapped out, clean and brutal, sending him flying several yards back to slam into the ground, his chest seizing, too winded to even get up.

The crowd went dead silent.

Paisley looked down at him coldly, her voice cutting clean through the noise.

"You think you're something? What gives you the right to lay hands on the soldiers risking their lives to protect you?"

"If it weren't for them holding this line with their own blood, someone like you — too busy hiding in the back and running your mouth — would already be zombie food, not even bones left."

"Nobody owes you a thing in this world. No one is obligated to die for your sake."

"Except the soldiers who are doing exactly that, for nothing in return."

Her gaze swept the crowd, her tone hard and unyielding.

"If you've got so much energy to waste, put it to use. Pick up a weapon and help kill these things! Stop hiding back here playing the victim while you insult the people protecting you!"

With that, she pushed off the ground, light as air, and landed on top of a military SUV, standing tall and sharp, looking down over a thousand survivors. Her voice, clear and carrying, cut through the gunfire and the shrieking without effort.

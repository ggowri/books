Chapter 15

Orion was thrilled. "You really do think of everything, Paisley."

Meanwhile, Trovie's squeaky voice kept going off in Paisley's head, way too excited to keep it down.

[Estimated points for bulk trades are way up!

[The more you stock up, Master, the more points you'll make when we sell! It's a guaranteed win! Keep going, don't stop!]

Paisley knew exactly what that meant. Everyday stuff that seemed ordinary in the apocalypse was rare in other dimensions where supplies were tight, so it would sell in no time.

So she went ahead and raided the store's stockroom too, clearing out every last box of backup inventory.

In just over ten minutes, she'd nearly emptied the entire supermarket.

The whole time, chime after chime went off in her head.

[Large-scale stockpiling detected. Inventory is now sufficient for listing.

[Batch of small orders sold! +20 points!

[+20 points!

[+20 points!]

The points kept pouring in, and her 40 points shot up to an even 100 in no time.

Then, a bright chime rang out. [Congratulations, Master! You're now a Level One cross-dimensional trader!

[Storage Dimension upgraded! From 100 square feet to 1,000 square feet!

[Congratulations! Your first cross-dimensional trader has been unlocked!]

Paisley lit up. Her points had finally broken 100, which unlocked direct cross-dimensional matching.

She felt her cramped 100-square-foot space shudder, its edges loosening. Then, it expanded several times over into a big, open room, and everything inside sorted itself neatly into place. What she'd stored so far barely filled a tenth of it.

She'd been worried the storage might not be big enough, but now she could probably fit the entire mall inside with room to spare.

She silently told Trovie, [Connect me with a trader later, when I've got time.]

Orion looked around the empty store and finally relaxed. "That's enough food and medicine to keep the whole team going for a long time," he said softly.

"Want to check out the second floor? They've got clothes and outdoor gear up there, and that stuff will come in handy too."

Paisley nodded. "Sure."

They were just turning toward the stairs when a faint, creepy dragging sound drifted down from above. A zombie's low, guttural growl came with it, and the sound made their skin crawl.

The main doors were shut tight, and the first floor was clear, so the noise had to be coming from upstairs.

Orion's face hardened, and he pulled Paisley behind him, gripping his machete. "Careful," he murmured. "Something's up there, probably a mutant."

The noise in the stairwell was getting closer. Something heavy was being dragged across the floor. Rough, raspy growls echoed through the empty mall, and the hair on their arms stood up.

They'd already taken care of the ordinary zombies on the first floor, and no low-level zombie could make a sound this menacing.

Every muscle in Orion's body went tight. His ability stirred, and a faint ripple of energy surrounded him.

He stepped in front of Paisley to shield her and slowly eased them both back, his eyes locked on the dark bend in the stairwell. He didn't dare relax for a second.

"Stay behind me," he said in a low, tense voice. "Be careful."

Paisley, though, was completely calm. After three years of fighting in her past life, she could tell what level a zombie was just from its sound and smell.

This wasn't a Level One zombie. This was a Level Two speed-type mutant, rare this early in the apocalypse.

It was way faster, stronger, and tougher than a Level One, and it was great at sneak attacks. This soon after the red rain, it was one of the nastiest mutants around, several times more dangerous than an ordinary zombie.

But Paisley had killed much higher-level zombies in the later years of her past life, so this one was easy. Honestly, if her body could keep up, she could take on zombies way stronger than this.

"Relax, Orion," she said softly, tugging on his sleeve. "It's just a Level Two speed mutant. It's tricky, but I can handle it."

The words had barely left her mouth when a pitch-black blur shot out from around the corner of the stairwell, almost too fast to see. It came straight at their throats with a rush of air, reeking of rot!

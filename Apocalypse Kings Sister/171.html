Chapter 171

Every victory, every escape from disaster — it had always come down to Paisley leading the way.

Right now, she was their only hope.

Ashley's eyes were red, voice breaking with panic.

"Paisley, what do we do? It's about to attack again, we should run! Nothing matters more than staying alive — we can't beat this thing!"

"She's right, Paisley! Fighting this head-on is suicide!"

Spencer backed her up, exhaustion and helplessness plain on his face.

"This thing's too strong. We're not a match for it. We need to retreat, now."

Thomas, Mason, Marcus, and Madison all nodded along, retreat suddenly the only thing that made sense.

Just then, a dry, unsettling rustle rose from behind them.

Everyone spun around — and sank straight into a deeper despair.

Their escape route was gone.

The field behind them had filled with lesser mutant carnivorous flowers, blocking every path back.

Rows of waist-high mutant blooms opened their petals, fangs bared, venom dripping, closing in tighter with every second.

These smaller flowers carried the same poison, the same vicious nature, vines tangling together into an impenetrable cage of thorns, boxing the group in completely.

No way forward, no way back. A true dead end.

A fresh wave of poison seeds tore through the air, blotting out the sky, thick purple-black mist rolling in, sickly sweet and rotten, saturating every breath they took.

Death stopped feeling like a distant threat. Raw, bone-deep terror crawled through every limb, gripping each heart tight.

The last flicker of hope they'd had — a way out — was gone, and every defense in their minds collapsed at once, fear sinking in deep.

Ashley shook uncontrollably, pressed hard against Paisley's side, fingers white with how hard she was gripping, teeth chattering.

This is it. We're actually going to die here. No way out...

Spencer, always composed, had sweat soaking through his back, chest heaving, breath shallow, terrified of inhaling even a trace of the poison.

The resolve in his eyes had drained away, leaving only helplessness, his thoughts running cold.

No way out. We're dead.

Thomas, usually sharp and calculating, felt his whole body lock up, mind going blank.

Never felt this hopeless before. Are we really going to die here?

Can't even bring my little sister home to meet Grandma and Grandpa, Mom and Dad. I broke my promise.

Even if he wanted to protect her and get her out now, he had nothing left to work with.

Marcus, Madison, and Mason had all hit the same wall — despair total, resolve gone. Both of them squeezed their eyes shut, giving up the fight.

Marcus, always the one to find a way, took one last long look at Paisley and accepted what was coming.

Madison couldn't look at anything far off anymore, tears sliding down her face, regretting — of all things — that she hadn't ended up with Orion.

Mason, who'd never once backed down from anything, felt his limbs go numb and still, waiting for the poison to finish him.

Everyone's breath caught, limbs frozen cold, the weight of death pressing down on every head, each second stretching out like torture.

Through it all, Paisley stood exactly where she was, calm.

She knew clearly — panic now meant everyone died.

She hadn't expected this mission to turn out to be this brutal.

They were in this mess because of her call.

No one blamed her for it. But she was still responsible for getting them all out alive.

Paisley's voice came fast, urgent, in her mind.

[Trovie, I need an all-purpose ward. How many points?]

Trovie's voice answered immediately.

[Host, five thousand points.]

Paisley's brow furrowed, a flash of irritation crossing her mind.

[Trovie, are you seriously price-gouging me right now? That's steep.]

[It's a permanent one, Host!]

Trovie explained earnestly.

[Worth every point — one-time purchase, lasts forever, blocks all poison, physical, and affinity damage!]

The shriek of incoming seeds grew louder, the poison closing in fast, seconds from swallowing the whole group whole.

Paisley couldn't risk it, couldn't gamble everyone's lives on anything else. The cost stung, but there was no other option.

She gritted her teeth and gave the order.

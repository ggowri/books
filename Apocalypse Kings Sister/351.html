Chapter 351

"The Thompsons are just the Blackwoods' lapdogs, surviving on scraps — not worth a second thought. Once our family takes the Blackwoods down, the Thompsons go with them, wiped off the map entirely."

"And then Paisley's whole fate will be whatever you say it is."

Rachel's eyes lit with sudden delight, elation surging through her.

She'd only ever known her family had some standing in Aetheris — she'd never known the main Lennox line was one of the three top powers outright!

None of this had happened last time. This time, her family's resources turned out to be far beyond anything she'd expected — her comeback practically handed to her.

She grabbed her mother's sleeve at once, eyes gleaming with hunger, her voice pleading and sweet.

"Mom, I need the pendant Paisley wears around her neck. Please, help me get it."

"I've lost my Affinity completely. I'm nothing now — I can't make it in this world without something.

That pendant might be my only chance to awaken again, to change my fate for good."

Her mother looked at her daughter's haggard, helpless face, and thinking of Paisley only deepened her hatred. She patted Rachel's hand firmly, her voice soft but certain.

"Don't worry. Leave it to me. I'll make sure you get what you want."

"When your father gets home tonight, I'll talk it through with him, get the family's connections working on this. We'll get you that pendant."

Reid, standing silent nearby, ignored all of it entirely, his face grim, worry edging into his voice.

"Mom, Owen's still unconscious and shows no sign of waking. We can't just let him lie there forever. What are we going to do? We can't just ignore him."

At that, her mother's expression sobered, the venom clearing from her eyes for a moment.

"I'll call the base's top research institute right now, get your brother sent over for treatment. They have the best equipment and expertise in the whole base. They'll fix him up, get him awake again soon enough."

Ryan said nothing the whole time, but dark clouds gathered behind his eyes. He refused to stay ordinary. He wanted an Affinity too.

If no one was going to help him, he'd find his own way.

The whole journey of hunger, humiliation, and hardship had already reshaped him into someone else entirely.

******

There was no room for softness in this world — only the ruthless and the self-serving survived.

A silent calculation took root inside him, cold ambition and resolve hiding behind his unreadable eyes.

At the same time, in the core villa district, at the Blackwood family's own estate —

the whole compound sprawled vast and imposing, walls towering, guards posted thick on every side, every checkpoint in perfect order, far more intimidating than any ordinary estate.

Spotting Elijah from a distance, every guard bowed in perfect unison, their voices solemn.

"Young master."

Elijah walked tall, cool and untouched by the long journey, his stride steady as he crossed the courtyard and entered the grand main hall.

Inside, his father rose from his seat and crossed the room quickly.

His eyes swept over his son, taking in how steady and composed he looked, approval plain on his face.

His father's expression eased, warm with relief.

"Good. You look well — better than I expected, honestly."

"That was a rough stretch out there. Well done, wrapping up the mission clean."

No sooner had he finished than a tightness gripped his chest, and he broke into a violent coughing fit he couldn't control.

The coughing wracked his whole body. He pressed a hand to his lips, back hunching, color visibly draining from his face into a sickly pallor.

The poison in his body had already settled deep into his organs, eating away at him day and night. He'd only ever kept it hidden, unwilling to worry his family or his men, and more importantly, unwilling to let anyone outside spot a crack in the Blackwoods' armor.

Elijah's expression darkened at once, and he moved quickly to steady his father, worry and a cold edge churning in his eyes.

Once the coughing eased, his father waved a hand to signal it was nothing, then let every trace of warmth drain from his face, turning grave as he laid out exactly how bad things stood across the base.

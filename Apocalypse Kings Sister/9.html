Chapter 9

The girls were all squeezed onto one sofa, while the guys took the other. Only Elijah had pulled a dining chair over by the front door, sitting upright, his gaze fixed on the entrance, alert to any movement outside.

When Paisley came downstairs, everyone looked up.

Ashley was the first to brighten, waving her over with a warm smile. "Over here, Paisley! There's space!"

Thomas quickly chimed in, "Paisley, sit with us!"

Not wanting to be left out, Spencer shifted over. "Plenty of room here. Come on."

Watching his younger siblings practically fight over her, Orion let out a tired sigh. "Alright, enough. You're not kids anymore. Let Paisley sit where she wants."

They quieted down, though disappointment lingered on their faces. Seizing the chance, Paisley made her way to Ashley and settled beside her.

Just then, Mason—slouched on the adjacent couch—let out a low groan. He looked pale, sweat beading on his forehead. "Everything hurts... I'm burning up. Feels like a bad fever."

Madison immediately stood, rummaged through her backpack, and pulled out a digital thermometer. She handed it to him without a word.

A minute later, she checked the reading: 103.6°F. Her expression tightened. "That's high," she murmured, already reaching for the emergency med kit.

She fished out fever reducers and a bottle of water, helping Mason take them carefully. "We don't have a doctor here. If this doesn't break soon..."

She didn't finish, but the unspoken words hung heavy in the air. In a world gone mad, a fever this high could be a death sentence.

Paisley knew better. This wasn't illness; it was the awakening. Her eyes swept the room and landed on Orion beside her. His cheeks were flushed, his breathing shallow. Heat seemed to radiate from him.

"Orion," she said, keeping her voice steady, "you don't look so good either. Do you feel feverish?"

Madison was already moving, thermometer in hand. A moment later, she frowned. "103.1°F. You're running hot too."

She shook her head, confusion lining her words. "One person, maybe coincidence. But two, at the same time? Something's not right."

As she measured out another dose for Orion, she added quietly, "At least we stocked up on meds. Let's hope it's enough."

Orion blinked slowly, as if only now registering the heat coursing through him. Weakness weighed on his limbs, but he forced his voice to remain calm. "I'll be fine. Don't worry."

Just as worry began to coil tightly around the group, Paisley's voice cut through, calm and clear. "It's not a normal fever. They're awakening the ability."

The room froze. Every pair of eyes locked onto her, wide with shock.

Elijah—silent and watchful until now—turned his head sharply. His gaze pinned her, intense and searching. "How do you know that?"

Of course he'd question it. Survivors like him trust nothing easily.

She kept her face neutral, her tone casual, almost dismissive. "Just a guess. I read a ton of apocalyptic fiction back in the day. Always the same—weird rain, then high fever, then... powers or worse."

She shrugged lightly, offering nothing more. She could never tell them the truth: that she'd lived this before, that she knew the crimson rain was only the beginning.

Elijah's eyes darkened, lingering on her. He didn't look convinced, but he didn't press further. Stranger things had happened since the world ended. Maybe fiction wasn't so far off after all.

"So if it is an awakening," Madison asked, her voice hopeful, "the fever is... part of it?"

Paisley nodded. "Survive it, and you'll awaken an ability. Fail, and you'll mutate outright. To zombies."

A cold dread settled back over the room, heavier than before.

At first, they'd thought it was just odd—unsettling, maybe. But now, hearing it confirmed, the weight of death settled over everyone in the room.

Just then, the rain outside surged louder. It hammered against the windows in heavy sheets, streaking the glass with a deep, unsettling red that stained the whole night. Something about it felt wrong, eerie.

Inside, Elijah's skin was burning up with fever.

Mason was trembling uncontrollably, his face shifting between pale and flushed. He gasped for air, fading in and out of consciousness.

Orion wasn't much better. Cold sweat coated his forehead, his lips were cracked, and his body radiated heat like he was trapped in an oven.

Barely two minutes passed before Kelly suddenly clutched her head, gasping sharply from the pain. "My head hurts so bad. I'm burning up all over."

Within minutes, most people in the hall were running high fevers, nausea sweeping through them.

Only a handful remained unaffected—Paisley, Ashley, Thomas, Spencer, Madison, and a few others.

Ashley watched those around her crumple in pain, panic tightening in her chest. She instinctively shifted closer to Paisley, voice dropping to a whisper.

"Don't be scared, Paisley. I've got your back."

Paisley glanced at her. Even terrified, Ashley was offering protection. Maybe she wasn't anything like Rachel.

This felt genuine.

"People react differently," Paisley said calmly. "Some were never meant to be more than ordinary."

In her past life, Reid and Ryan hadn't awakened their abilities early either. It was only after she'd risked everything to find that age-old sacred fungus that they'd unlocked their potential.

This time, she wasn't doing the hard work just for them to take the credit.

She was the only one who knew where the fungus grew.

Without me, Reid, Ryan... this time you'll stay ordinary. Powerless.

A sharp, pain-filled cry cut through the hall from the corner.

Everyone turned.

It was Daniel—the other guy who'd been guarding outside the villa and followed them in later. Paisley had barely noticed him before.

He'd stayed standing by the wall, never sitting to rest. Now his face was dark purple, eyes bloodshot, body twisting and jerking violently.

"Something's wrong—is he mutating?" Elijah snapped to his feet, demeanor turning icy. He moved instinctively, shielding the girls behind him.

The words had barely left his mouth when—

"Crack."

The man's neck twisted at an unnatural angle. His skin grayed, eyes glazing over completely. A low, rasping growl—that unmistakable zombie sound—escaped his throat.

In under three seconds, a living man had become one of them.

"Grraah!"

The newly turned zombie jerked its head, crimson eyes locking onto the living in the hall. It lurched forward, movements stiff and aggressive.

Chaos erupted.

The girls scrambled into the corner. Those struggling with fever had no strength to move—they could only watch as the zombie closed in.

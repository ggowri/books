Chapter 347

Once they were truly inside the Aetheris base, its scale became obvious — nothing like the cramped, makeshift feel of the regional bases. Every corner here spoke of order and top-tier design.

******

The base's territory was vast, swallowing the entire downtown villa district within its reach. Its outer walls rose dozens of yards high, alloy and fortified, layers of defenses stacked behind them, impenetrable.

Inside, the roads ran wide and smooth, the layout meticulous, with hospitals, training grounds, supply depots, and garrisons all fully equipped. Barely a trace of the apocalypse's usual decay showed here.

Tens of millions of survivors called this base home, its stockpiles deep, its systems orderly, its ranks thick with high-level fighters — the largest, most organized, and safest haven in the whole wrecked world, the place every survivor dreamed of reaching.

The black car glided smoothly down the base's main road, not a single jolt.

The air inside was warm and easy, Paisley's hand still held firmly in her mother's, the gentle warmth of it grounding and steady.

Her mother wouldn't let go, glancing down at her every so often, pure delight and relief plain on her face.

Her father, in the front passenger seat, turned around to patiently walk the kids through the base's core rules and zoning, helping them get their bearings.

"Contribution points are the base's only currency. Supplies, housing upgrades, resource requests, access to the training zones, official missions — everything runs on points."

"Crystal cores are the main way to earn points, the hardest currency there is.

The higher the core's level, the more points it's worth — and even unredeemed, a high-level core is valuable for advancing your own Affinity or crafting gear."

He paused, then went on.

"We got lucky. Our place isn't far from the Blackwoods', and it fell right inside the base's core protection zone. We've stayed in the same house this whole time, no trouble at all."

He laid out the base's zoning clearly, point by point.

"The base splits into three tiers, strictly divided by individual strength, family standing, and contribution to the base."

"First is the core villa district.

This is the safest, best-supplied zone in the base, home to established top families, lineages of high-level Affinity users, and core military personnel.

Living here means either top-tier strength or deep family roots, enough to supply the base with real combat power and resources. Security and supplies here are the best there is."

"Second is the high-rise residential district.

Home to Affinity users and survivors who contribute steadily.

They have fixed assignments, steady output, enough to claim housing and daily supplies, live comfortably without the raw survival pressure at the bottom — though resources and security here fall a step below the villa district."

"The outermost ring is the slums. Ordinary survivors live there — no Affinity, no standing, no steady output.

This world's dangerous, and ordinary people can't protect themselves — they end up leaning on powerful users and factions just to survive, scraping by on odd jobs. Cramped, under-resourced, poorly guarded. It's the most chaotic part of the whole base."

The hierarchy said it all — in this new world, strength was the only real foundation.

Paisley listened quietly, taking it all in. In her last life, she'd come to this same base with the Lennoxes, and without an Affinity, she'd lived in a tent in the slums.

Even though the Lennoxes could easily have brought her into the villa district with them, they'd shut her out anyway.

This time, she'd found her real family. In this base of millions, everything had turned out differently.

The car turned onto the quiet, elegant road through the villa district, lush greenery lining both sides, courtyards neat and orderly — a world away from the chaos outside, calm and settled.

Within minutes, the car pulled up in front of an elegant three-story villa, wrapped in greenery.

Inside, the courtyard was spotless, the house lit warm and gold, not a trace of apocalypse bleakness — just the quiet warmth of a lived-in home.

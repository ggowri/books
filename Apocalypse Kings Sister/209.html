Chapter 209

The other had been a selfish coward who only cared about running for her life.

The difference couldn't have been clearer. They were worlds apart.

Elijah put his anger aside, pulled up the virtual mission map, and tapped the screen, his deep voice pulling everyone back to the task.

"Let's rest for a minute, then get back to the mission. There's an elevator up ahead on the right that goes straight down to the basement. The classified files and equipment are most likely down there."

Everyone quickly gathered around to look at the map, and once they saw the numbers, their hearts sank.

It clearly showed that the abandoned aquarium had 30 floors above ground and, to their shock, 15 floors underground.

The deeper the floor, the more chaotic the magnetic field and the more beasts there were. The danger level was unknown.

The elevator shaft was deep and pitch-black, and cold, damp air kept pouring out of it. The eerie, dead silence made their skin crawl, like countless unknown horrors were hiding down below.

They all stepped into the elevator.

Paisley followed Trovie's directions and pressed the button for Basement Level 15. It was the very bottom floor, and it was where the files and equipment were.

The elevator doors slowly slid shut, and it started to drop.

The deeper they went, the colder and stuffier the air got, and a bone-deep chill spread through their whole bodies.

A crushing sense of dread kept growing, and every team member felt their nerve slipping. They were all tense and couldn't stop being afraid.

In the dim, flickering light of the elevator, Orion stood behind Paisley, silently guarding her, ready to block any danger that came her way.

Elijah stood on her other side, tall and stern, with lightning faintly crackling around him. His eyes scanned every dark corner as he kept her protected.

Between the two of them, Paisley was in the safest spot in the elevator.

Every second of the drop was torture. Only a few minutes passed, but it felt like a whole century.

After five long minutes, the strong sense of weightlessness suddenly faded. The elevator jolted and finally stopped.

With a low ding, the elevator broke the dead silence, and the doors slowly slid open.

To their surprise, the basement lights were still on, barely. The pale, flickering light just managed to light up the entrance, but it only made everything around it look creepier.

Outside the elevator doors, huge clumps of dark green seaweed swayed slowly in the draft from a vent. A thick, sharp, corrosive stench hit them right in the nose and made their stomachs turn.

This was no ordinary water weed. Its stalks were thick and sturdy, its surface was slimy and coated in clear mucus, and its leaves had a strange, dark green sheen.

Wisps of toxic mist kept drifting out from between the leaves, full of a corrosive poison that would hurt anyone who touched it.

It was a Level Five mutant seaweed that had taken over the entrance to Basement Level 15. It was so thickly tangled that it sealed off the whole elevator exit, blocking the only way forward.

Elijah stared at the thick wall of seaweed, his face grim. "That's Level Five mutant seaweed. If we don't take it out, we'll never get in there.

"And everything around this level is probably open sea."

The danger they were facing was much worse than they'd imagined.

Orion's eyes turned sharp. Scorching flames burst to life in his palm, lighting up the dark, creepy basement and chasing away some of the bone-deep chill and dead silence. "I'll clear the way," he said steadily.

His fire poured out of his palm in a rolling wave of flames and swept straight toward the thick mutant seaweed.

The roaring fire licked across the dark green leaves, and they sizzled as they burned. The sharp smell of scorched plants mixed with the corrosive stench and spread fast through the air.

The Level Five mutant seaweed was full of corrosive toxins, but fire was its natural weakness. The surface leaves curled up and turned to charcoal the second the flames touched them, and the wall of seaweed burned away layer by layer right before their eyes.

But the weird thing was, as the burned seaweed on the surface withered and fell away, new shoots kept sprouting from deep inside to fill the gaps, one after another. It showed no sign of shrinking at all.

Orion frowned and turned up the fire in his palm until the flames roared even higher. But they still only cleared the surface plants and couldn't wipe it out for good.

"It's no good. It won't burn out," he said, his eyes growing more serious. "We have to kill it at the root."

Elijah stepped half a pace forward and looked over the whole spreading patch of seaweed, his eyes dark and cold.

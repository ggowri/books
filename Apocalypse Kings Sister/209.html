Worlds apart, no contest at all.

Elijah let the last of his coldness fade, pulled up the virtual mission map, and tapped it, his voice pulling everyone's attention back.

“Take a minute. We keep moving. There's an elevator down to the basement just past the next right turn—the data and equipment are most likely down there.”

******

The team gathered around the map, and every one of them felt their stomach drop at what they saw.

The abandoned aquarium had thirty floors above ground, and fifteen more below it.

The deeper the level, the more chaotic the field grew, and the denser the mutant population, danger ratings entirely unknown.

The elevator shaft dropped away black and deep, cold, damp air rising from it, a dead stillness that crawled up the skin, as if something unknowable waited at the bottom.

They stepped into the elevator.

Following Trovie's cue, Paisley steadied herself and pressed the button for Basement Level Fifteen—the very bottom, where the data and equipment waited.

The doors closed, and the elevator began its descent.

The deeper they went, the colder and more stifling the air grew, a bone-deep chill spreading through every one of them.

A crushing dread built with every floor, leaving everyone rigid, unable to shake the fear.

In the dim, flickering elevator light, Orion stood behind Paisley, silent, every line of him braced to put himself between her and whatever came.

Elijah stood at her other side, lightning flickering faintly at his fingertips, eyes sweeping every dark corner, guarding her just as close.

Between the two of them, she stood in the safest place in the elevator.

Every second of the drop dragged. A few minutes stretched out like a century.

After five long minutes, the weightless drop cut off, the elevator shuddering to a final stop.

Ding—

A low chime broke the silence as the doors slid open.

Against all expectation, the basement lights still flickered, weak and white, barely lighting the entrance—if anything, it made the place look worse.

Beyond the doors, dark green weeds swayed in some unseen draft, a thick, corrosive stench rolling in hard enough to turn anyone's stomach.

This was nothing like ordinary water weeds—thick, sturdy roots, a slick, mucus-coated surface, leaves gleaming an unnatural, murky green.

A faint toxic haze drifted out from between the leaves, corrosive enough that a single touch would do damage.

A Level Five mutant seaweed had claimed the entrance to Basement Fifteen, tangled thick enough to seal off the elevator exit completely, blocking the only way forward.

“That's Level Five mutant seaweed. We're not getting through without clearing it.”

“This whole level's probably surrounded by open water.”

Elijah studied the dense wall of weeds, his face grim.

Whatever was down here was worse than anything they'd planned for.

Orion's eyes hardened. Flame roared to life in his palm, blazing bright enough to light the whole dark basement, pushing back the cold and the stillness both.

His voice stayed steady.

“I'll clear the way.”

Fire poured from his hand in a rolling wave, crashing straight into the mass of mutant weeds.

Flame licked across the dark green leaves, crackling and hissing, the smell of scorched plant matter mixing with the corrosive reek already thick in the air.

Toxic as it was, the Level Five seaweed couldn't stand against fire's natural edge over it, the leaves curling and charring on contact, the wall of green burning away layer by layer.

But strangely, as fast as the burnt weeds fell away, new shoots kept pushing up from underneath to fill the gap, with no sign of the wall thinning at all.

Orion's brow furrowed. He pushed more fire into his hand, the flames climbing higher, but still only burned through the surface layer, never reaching the root of it.

“No good. It won't burn out.”

His voice came low, something graver settling behind his eyes.

“We need to cut this off at the root.”

Elijah stepped forward half a pace, his gaze sweeping the whole spreading mass, cold and considering.

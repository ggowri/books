Chapter 301

Guard towers lined the wall at even intervals, firing slits cut beneath each one. Sentries stood at full attention, rifles ready, eyes sweeping the dead ground beyond the perimeter. Nothing moved out there without someone noticing.

Compared to the ruin and chaos everywhere else, a base this size, this organized, this tightly run, said everything about the people in charge of Stillwater.

The closer they got, the calmer everything felt.

The road into the base had been cleared and graded. No corpses rotting in the ditches, no debris scattered across the lanes. Survivor convoys moved along it at a steady clip, tired but orderly, nobody running, nobody screaming.

The SUV rolled up to the main gate and stopped.

A wide checkpoint plaza stretched out in front of the entrance, lanes marked off, everyone funneled through one at a time. No exceptions. Every survivor, every vehicle, got registered, scanned for Affinity, and checked for infection before they were let anywhere near the gate.

The guards worked fast and clean, no wasted motion, no slacking. Elijah's group moved through registration, the body scan, and the Affinity check without a hitch, and were waved through.

A staff member from the welcome desk caught up with them just inside the gate to run through the rules.

At Stillwater, contribution points were the only currency that mattered. Food, housing, tasks, anything you wanted, it all came down to points.

The rule was simple: the base didn't carry dead weight. Everyone who lived here owed it a daily contribution.

Housing came in three tiers, each with its own daily rate. The slums ran five points a day. A single-room bungalow was fifteen. A private villa, with real security and real comfort, cost thirty.

If you came up short, you either handed over supplies to cover the gap or picked up a task from the board.

The clerk looked them over, clocked the way they carried themselves, and pulled up their files. Affinity users, every one of them. His tone eased off a notch.

"You're all Affinity users, so you get first pick. Take a task off the board to earn your points, or just hand over goods instead."

Elijah's expression didn't change. He pulled out his old officer's ID and set it on the counter, voice level.

"I need to speak with whoever runs this base. It's important."

The clerk glanced at the ID, and whatever patience he'd been holding onto evaporated. Contempt crept into his face.

He didn't know who Elijah was, and he didn't care what rank he'd held before the world ended. As far as he was concerned, the man was a relic clinging to a title that meant nothing anymore.

"We're past all that now. This is Stillwater. You follow our rules here."

"I don't care what you used to be. You're in my base now, so you'll play by my rules."

He kept going, voice climbing, smug and cutting.

"Our director's got a thousand things to deal with. You think he's got time for some outsider nobody's ever heard of?"

"He's being generous just letting refugees like you in the gate. Count yourself lucky and don't go looking for special treatment."

Every word dripped with contempt, not a trace of respect for who he might actually be talking to.

Paisley's eyes went cold. She wasn't in the mood to argue. A thin, invisible thread of psychic force shot out and landed square in the clerk's mind.

A high, ringing whine split through his skull. Pain tore through him from every direction at once. His legs buckled and he hit the ground, hands clamped over his head, face white and twisted.

"My head— my head hurts so bad!"

The pain let up just enough for him to look up, eyes wide with fear and fury.

"You attacked a base official! In public! I'll have every one of you thrown out!"

He started shouting for backup.

"Security! Get over here! Throw them all out!"

Guards posted around the plaza snapped into motion, rifles raised, forming a ring around Elijah's group. The barrels all pointed inward. The air went taut.

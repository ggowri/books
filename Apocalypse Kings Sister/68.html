Chapter 68

Paisley didn't hesitate. [Trovie, connect me right now.]

Trovie answered right away. [Got it! Opening an emergency channel... Connected!]

Princess Aurelia sat on the other side of the screen in a gorgeous royal gown. Her face was bright and lovely, and she could barely hide how happy she was as she looked at Paisley.

"I finally get to see you again! I've completely mastered your ice-making method, and I've already made ice and popsicles. You've solved a huge problem for me."

Paisley stayed calm and casual.

"You're too kind, Princess. That was all you. Making ice in just half a day is seriously impressive.

"Besides, it was a fair trade. We both got what we needed, so you don't have to thank me."

The princess's smile faded, and her face clouded with worry.

"Actually, the reason I've been so desperate to reach you is that I need to make another trade."

Paisley thought of the princess's gold and jumped right in. "What do you need, Princess?"

The princess looked troubled.

"I'll be honest with you. A plague just broke out in my kingdom. Countless people have fallen sick and gone delirious, and the whole court is panicking.

"None of the royal physicians know what to do. Nobody can find a way to stop it.

"You're my only hope. Name your price."

Paisley thought it over.

"Tell me the patients' symptoms, and I'll help you find a fix. My price is 3,300 pounds of gold."

The princess's hopes soared. Only 3,300 pounds of gold? Paisley was being way too modest.

She answered eagerly.

"The patients run high fevers that won't break. They cough, struggle to breathe, and get chest pains, plus vomiting, diarrhea, and swollen bruises all over their bodies.

"It spreads incredibly fast, and young and old alike catch it easily. Even the strongest people don't last more than three to five days.

"The royal physicians have tried every remedy and tonic they know, and nothing works. If anything, the patients just get worse."

Paisley listened quietly, and by the time the princess finished, she already knew what to do.

With a single thought, she told Trovie to put together a complete plan to fight the plague. It had to work with medieval medicine, hit the disease hard, and have zero side effects.

It wasn't just one remedy. There were three herbal treatments, one each for mild, severe, and critical cases, plus a full containment plan. That covered disinfecting the whole city, quarantining the sick, boiling drinking water, drying clothes in the sun, and sealing off infected areas.

It was simple, easy to follow, and entirely doable on a large scale with medieval manpower.

The exact herb ratios, how long to simmer each remedy, how often to take it, nursing dos and don'ts, and disinfection steps all appeared on Aurelia's screen right away.

Paisley walked her through it. "Give the treatments based on how sick each person is. Mild cases should lose their fever in three days, and severe cases should stabilize in five.

"Combine that with a full quarantine and disinfection, and you'll have the outbreak under control in seven days. Within two weeks, the plague should be completely wiped out, and it won't come back."

As the princess read through the clear, thorough plan, the tension finally drained out of her body. Her eyes welled up, and her heart overflowed with gratitude.

This plan was a hundred times better than the royal physicians' random remedies. It was logical and aimed right at the disease.

"Thank you so much, my friend!" The princess sank into a deep, formal curtsy.

"Words can't repay what you've done for me. I'll never forget this."

She placed the 3,300 pounds of gold in the trading slot. Then, without wasting another second, she grabbed the plan and hurried off.

Soon, Paisley received the gold.

[The gold's been stored in your Storage Dimension. You can exchange it for points anytime!]

Trovie answered right away. [Rare valuables detected: 3,300 pounds of gold.

[Converting at the cross-dimensional exchange rate... Done!

[Points earned: 30,000! Total points: 51,730!]

Earning points like this was way too slow.

Chapter 160

The whole floor was clothing and home goods — brand-name apparel, linens, shoes, accessories — shelves intact, no burn marks, no tears.

The mall's seal must have held out well, keeping the weather and the dead from ever getting in.

Rows of fresh seasonal clothing hung untouched. Winter coats, soft sweaters, windbreakers, work gear — every size, every style, not a speck of dust or mildew.

The home textiles section was just as rich.

Cotton sheet sets, down comforters, blankets, pillows — all sealed and stacked, soft to the touch.

Waterproof mattress covers, blackout curtains, house slippers, pajamas — a full set of home essentials, more than anyone could want.

Clean, warm bedding was its own kind of currency now. Winters hit hard, and plenty of affinity users had survived the fighting only to lose to the cold.

A set like this was rarer than almost anything else out there.

Nothing useful got left behind.

Paisley scanned the floor and opened her storage without hesitation.

Take it!

Every rack of clothing, every coat, every pair of shoes — gone, shelves bare in seconds.

Take it!

Bedding, blankets, sheet sets, pillows — sorted and stored in her living-supplies section.

Take it!

Curtains, mattress covers, small home items — all gone.

She moved fast. Wherever her eyes landed, the stock vanished.

She left a little behind this time, thinking of the others.

In just a few minutes, the second floor was stripped, save for a few scattered pieces.

Trovie spun through the air, marveling.

[Host is unstoppable! So many clothes!]

Paisley gave the floor one last look, satisfied.

"Next floor."

She headed up to the third floor, ready to clear out the electronics next.

Power and signal were gone in the apocalypse, so tech seemed useless for now — but Paisley knew better. Once the base stabilized and the grid came back, this gear would matter again.

The third floor opened into a clean electronics section, just as untouched as the ones below.

Only a few low-tier zombies shuffled between the counters, sluggish and slow, like the dead zone here had dulled whatever power they had left.

Paisley didn't bother with her sword. A flick of her fingers sent thin vines snaking out, wrapping around their necks and draining the life out of them, quick and clean.

With the floor cleared, the full layout showed itself.

Every counter held branded electronics, glass cases spotless.

Brand-new phones, tablets, earbuds, smartwatches, lined up in every model and color, not a scratch on any of them.

The next section was packed with gaming laptops, work laptops, desktop rigs, mechanical keyboards, gaming mice, high-res monitors — fully stocked.

Beyond that, outdoor-ready tech: power banks, solar chargers, portable projectors, walkie-talkies, night-vision goggles, high-def drones — exactly the kind of gear field teams and base security needed most.

Walkie-talkies and night-vision gear, especially, were almost impossible to find — perfect for team missions, night watch, long-range contact.

A flicker of satisfaction crossed Paisley's eyes.

The base was still small now, but once it grew — security systems, surveillance, comms teams — this gear would be exactly what they needed.

She didn't wait. She opened her storage and started pulling.

Take it!

Every phone, tablet, wearable — gone, shelves bare.

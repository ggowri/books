Chapter 340

Level Six cores were rare on their own, and a purely elemental Fire core suited most front-line fighters. The moment bidding opened, paddles shot up all over the floor in fierce competition.

Fire Affinity users bid against each other, the price climbing steadily.

Paisley raised her paddle calmly, ready to bid for it.

Beside her, Orion frowned and spoke up quietly.

"Pais, something's off about this core. There's hidden impurity, mixed attributes — it's not worth much practically. No need to bid just because everyone else is."

His eye was sharp enough to spot at a glance that the core wasn't worth much.

It wasn't worth spending that many points on.

Paisley shook her head slightly.

"Orion, I have a use for it."

Beside her, Elijah asked nothing further, his gaze fond as he raised his own paddle, voice cool and clear.

"Ten thousand points."

The whole hall went quiet instantly.

The heated bidding war cooled off at once; no one dared raise it any further.

The scene of Elijah snubbing Mr. Quinn and forcing him to pay compensation for Paisley was still fresh in everyone's mind.

No one wanted to cross a man of such unknown depth and power, least of all over a core that wasn't even that useful.

The auctioneer blinked, then recovered and called it loudly.

"Ten thousand, going once!"

"Ten thousand, going twice!"

"Ten thousand, going three times!"

"Sold! Congratulations to the gentleman for the Level Six Fire Affinity core!"

The gavel cracked down, and Paisley blinked, faintly confused.

She had more than enough points in her own account to have won that bid herself. She hadn't needed Elijah to step in.

He was being far too good to her, honestly.

He thought of everything, took her side without question, asked for nothing back, kept pulling her behind him and handing her whatever she wanted.

That weight of devotion, so heavy and warm, left a strange ache in her chest.

The auction moved on, item after rare item going up on stage, the room bidding fiercely over every one.

Time slipped by, and soon the final showcase items of the evening came up.

When a radiant, luminous sacred fungus was set on display, the whole hall erupted.

Its stem was full and healthy, wreathed in a faint white glow, every marking intact, every year of its age plain to see. Even through the shield, a warm, gentle energy radiated off it.

The auctioneer's voice rose, electric with excitement.

"Our grand finale — a sacred fungus! One of the rarest treasures left in this world. Brewed and consumed, it can cleanse and rebuild the body from the marrow out, and with luck, awaken an Affinity outright! For those who already have one, it strengthens the foundation and speeds advancement!"

"Starting bid: ten thousand points. Minimum increment: one thousand!"

The hall exploded. Bids rang out one after another, the energy in the room at a fever pitch.

In this world, an Affinity was everything. Countless ordinary people, weak and powerless, were fighting just to survive. A chance — even a gamble — at awakening one was a once-in-a-lifetime temptation.

"Twenty thousand!"

"Thirty thousand!"

"Fifty thousand!"

"Seventy thousand!"

...

The price shot up, doubling within seconds, the crowd caught up in a feverish bidding war.

******

Just as the bidding reached a fever pitch, Damon spoke up from the front row, his tone leaving no room for doubt.

"Ninety thousand."

The number silenced most of the room at once.

Everyone understood — if Mr. Quinn wanted something, with the Quinn family's resources behind him, no ordinary bidder stood a chance. One by one, people fell silent.

Damon's mouth curved into a satisfied smile, certain of victory, already counting the fungus as his.

But the next second, a cool, detached voice shattered the stalemate.

Elijah raised his hand without hurry.

"One hundred thousand."

Damon's expression froze. He whipped around to glare at the fifth row, fury boiling in his eyes, barely holding back a curse.

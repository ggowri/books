Chapter 333

She'd almost said she didn't need it, that she had plenty of points and could buy whatever she wanted herself.

But the words died on her tongue when she remembered the trick watches she'd picked up on a whim while shopping.

On impulse, she drew four watches from her Storage Dimension, each with a different pattern, understated but clearly well made, and set them neatly on the table.

They looked like simple, tasteful accessories at a glance, but there was more to them than that.

"Orion, Thomas, Spencer, Elijah — these are for you. Pick whichever one you like."

Spencer's eyes lit up immediately, and he grabbed one with a clean, understated pattern, turning it over in his hands.

Ashley couldn't wait to explain, her voice both eager and serious.

"Paisley picked these out just for you, and they're not just accessories!

There's a hidden mechanism inside — ten specially forged needles, sharp enough to punch through almost anything. You can coat them with sedatives or poison too. These could save your life."

That caught all of them off guard for a second, then warmth crept into their eyes.

In the apocalypse, money and supplies came second. A concealed weapon that could save your life in a pinch was the real treasure.

Clearly she'd put real thought into these.

Orion picked up a black watch with a subdued pattern, running his thumb over the smooth dial, something warm in his eyes.

"Pais, you really didn't have to. This is too much."

Elijah chose a matte, cool-toned one with a bamboo-leaf engraving, perfectly suited to his sharp, reserved look, the faintest smile at his lips.

"I love it. Thank you."

Thomas took the last watch and fastened it carefully around his wrist, unable to stop admiring it.

"Pais, I really love it. Thank you!"

Paisley ducked her head, a little embarrassed.

She used to put this much care into gifts for the Lennoxes too.

But every time, they'd either sneered at the gift or just thrown it away.

Not once had it ever mattered to anyone.

This was the first time anyone had ever been this genuinely happy with something she made.

Not far off, Marcus Whitfield sat gripping his water glass tightly, working hard to hold his feelings in check.

His thick lashes hid his eyes from view.

Meanwhile —

on the second floor of the base hospital,

the stark white room was bitterly cold, the only sound the monotonous beep of machines, oppressive in its silence.

Rachel Lennox lay with her eyes shut, still comatose, her face a sickly mix of blue and white, a faint dark tinge around her lips. The poison still clung to her like a shroud, her heart rate and Affinity readings dropping steadily no matter how many times the doctors examined her.

Beside her, Chase Sullivan was in much the same state, locked in a deep coma, motionless. The two of them lay side by side, breath shallow, with no sign of waking.

Reid sat in the chair beside the bed, his expression dark enough to be frightening, resentment and malice brimming in his eyes.

He was hunched over his phone, fingers flying across the screen, hammering out a post for the base's public forum, determined to drag Paisley's name through the mud.

He twisted every fact, left out anything that might cast Rachel badly, and painted Paisley as cold-hearted, letting people die when she had the cure, ungrateful for everything the Lennoxes had done raising her.

Every line was designed to wound, every sentence meant to stir up hatred.

Once he'd finished, he hit post, his eyes dark with spite and calculation.

"Paisley, let's see how you keep your footing on this base once everyone sees who you really are."

The post went live across the network. He set the phone face-down on the table and settled in to wait, eager for the moment the whole base would turn on her in disgust.

Just then, the heavy door swung open from outside.

A tall, severe figure stepped in, dressed in a sharp dark uniform with formal epaulets, carrying the unmistakable weight of someone used to command.

His features were hard, his expression flat, somewhere in his mid-thirties — clearly someone with real power, not a man to cross.

The oppressive air in the room went rigid; even the beep of the machines seemed to soften.

Something told Reid at once that this man was no one ordinary. He wiped the malice off his face, shot to his feet, and plastered on an obsequious smile, hand already extended.

"Hello, sir! May I ask who you are? Is there something I can help with?"

The man ignored the offered hand entirely, his gaze sliding past Reid to the unconscious figure on the bed, his voice low and utterly without warmth.

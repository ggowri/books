Chapter 314

The critical care hospital sat in the core of the base, quiet and secure, reserved exclusively for wounded elites. Ordinary survivors had no business setting foot there.

After passing through several security checks, she stepped into the first-floor lobby of the hospital wing.

Across the room, a thin, upright figure was pushing a heavy mop along the long corridor floor, head down, methodical.

He wore a faded blue cleaning uniform, the fabric worn thin, sleeves rolled up to reveal thin, clean wrists. His movements were careful and steady, every inch of floor scrubbed to a shine, his focus total.

It was Ryan Lennox, the third Lennox brother.

Paisley paused, glancing at him a second longer than she meant to.

Grunt labor at the bottom of society was exhausting, mind-numbing work, but his effort didn't look phoned in at all. No trace of the bitterness Reid carried either. It said something — he was handling this better than Reid, holding himself together where his brother couldn't.

Compared to Reid, who'd let his resentment curdle into something ugly, Ryan had always been the quietest of the three brothers, the one who endured without complaint.

If she hadn't already seen what he was really capable of, she might have believed this harmless act.

In her last life, nobody in that family had been innocent when she died.

Now here he was, reduced to the same lowest rung as his brother, mopping floors for scraps of points, scraping by day to day. A fitting consequence, as far as she was concerned.

Sensing someone nearby, Ryan paused and slowly looked up.

The moment his eyes landed on Paisley, he went rigid, his grip tightening on the mop handle, shock and humiliation flashing across his face, his color draining.

He clearly hadn't expected to run into her here, of all places — a hospital this tightly secured.

A few days, and she'd already changed, somehow more composed, more untouchable than before.

Why was she even here?

Paisley didn't look at him twice. She moved past him like a stranger, following her escort to the elevator, heading up to the third floor.

Ryan watched the numbers climb, confusion settling deeper.

Third floor patients were never ordinary people.

What was she doing up there?

It didn't matter. They had nothing to do with each other now. Whatever trouble she got into wasn't his problem.

Paisley had no idea what was running through his head.

She followed the escort to a private room at the end of the hall.

Inside, a handsome, composed young man lay unconscious in the bed.

The escort explained.

"This is our top combat asset — a Level Six Metal Affinity user, deputy director of the base. He went unconscious after scouting the research facility. None of our doctors or healers have been able to do anything for him."

Paisley checked his pulse and breathing carefully, her expression tightening. The toxin in his system matched the one from the underwater facility exactly.

"I'll see what I can do."

She turned to the escort.

"I need complete quiet while I work. Please wait outside."

The escort hesitated, still unconvinced, but there wasn't much else to try at this point.

Something about her composure made him hold his tongue. He bowed and stepped out, pulling the door shut behind him, waiting in the hall.

The room went completely still.

Paisley stepped up to the bed, lifting her hand. A clean, pale light flowed from her palm, settling gently over the man's forehead.

Her Plant Affinity had reached mid Level Six now, her healing power stronger than ever.

In the next instant, the dark veins spreading under his skin stirred violently, the cold poison thrashing against the light trying to purge it, fighting back with everything it had.

But Paisley's purifying energy was too pure, too overwhelming, like sunlight cutting through shadow. It pushed deep into his veins, his blood, his bones, breaking apart the stubborn poison that had resisted every attempt to treat it.

His once-faint breathing steadied, deepening. The furrow between his brows smoothed out. The gray pallor drained from his face, visibly, by the second.

In under three minutes, it was done.

The toxin that had stumped every top doctor in the base, that had kept their best fighter trapped in a coma — completely purged. Gone.

The man, who had lain motionless for days, let out a small flutter of his lashes.

Then, slowly, he opened his eyes.

His gaze was clear, the death-like haze of the coma gone entirely, a sharp, composed presence settling back into place — Stillwater's Level Six Metal Affinity user, awake.

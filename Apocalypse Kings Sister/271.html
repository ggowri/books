Chapter 271

Why did Paisley get to cling to Elijah Blackwood so shamelessly, while she, after years of loving him, could only watch from the sidelines?

Jealousy spread through her chest like weeds overtaking a garden. Given the chance, she would kill Paisley Lennox. She and Elijah were the ones who belonged together.

Hold it in.

After a brief silence, everyone settled, slipping back into their roles.

Mason Coleman and Thomas Thompson took point on watch, weapons in hand, sweeping the courtyard and the surrounding hills for stragglers and hidden threats.

Madison Hughes and Orion Thompson headed inside, found the kitchen, and began sorting through the leftover cookware and seasonings to get lunch started.

The rest of them finally relaxed. Paisley's gaze drifted to the clear water of the fish pond, watching the fish and shrimp dart back and forth, her eyes bright with sudden interest.

******

Paisley turned to Spencer beside her, eyes crinkling with a grin.

"Spencer, let's have a fishing contest. Whoever catches a fish first wins. I've never actually caught one before."

Spencer nodded at once, happy to take her up on it, the corner of his mouth lifting.

"My hands have been itching for this."

Ashley perked up immediately, bouncing over with her hand already raised.

"Count me in too! I want to play!"

Paisley pulled three fishing rods from her storage space.

The three of them found their spots and sat down. For a while the courtyard held nothing but the soft rustle of wind through the grass, quiet and easy.

Spencer had fished before. Within moments his line went taut, and with one sharp flick of his wrist he pulled a fat, gleaming crucian carp out of the water, droplets scattering across the grass as it flopped.

Not long after, Ashley landed one too, letting out a small delighted squeal, eyes curving with pride.

Paisley's line, though, sat perfectly still in the calm water. Not a single nibble.

She crouched there patiently while the other two reeled in catch after catch, and she had nothing to show for it.

Even the little white cat at her feet grew anxious, rubbing its small body against her leg over and over. Its soft meows came one after another, as if it were just as worried as she was.

Ashley bit back a laugh and turned to look at Paisley's hopeless expression, her voice bright and teasing.

"Pais, so there's actually something you're bad at!"

Paisley propped up her rod with forced composure, stubbornly insisting,

"This spot must just be unlucky. It's not that I'm bad at it. I refuse to believe it. I'm catching one if it kills me."

The words had barely left her mouth when Spencer's rod jerked again, and a heavy fish broke the surface, scales flashing in the sun, closer to three and a half pounds of firm, plump meat.

He hefted it with a grin.

"This one's perfect for fish soup at lunch. Everyone could use the nourishment."

Just then Elijah stepped out of the house, his eyes finding the girl by the pond right away, and he walked straight over to her side.

His voice came low and warm.

"Let me help."

The cat at her feet seemed to understand every word. It meowed twice more, tail flicking, rubbing insistently against Elijah's pant leg, clearly hoping for its share of fresh fish too.

Elijah took the rod from Paisley's hands. His technique was practiced, and within seconds the line jerked hard. He flicked his wrist, and a plump grass carp landed cleanly on the hook.

He didn't pause. He cast again, and again, pulling in fish after fish at an almost unbelievable pace, nearly instant each time.

Watching everyone else haul in a full catch while she had nothing, Paisley sighed and huffed.

"I'm done fishing."

Elijah turned to look at her, warmth pooling softly in his eyes, his voice indulgent.

"Everything I catch is yours."

Ashley chimed in right away.

"Pais, mine too! All of it!"

Spencer laughed and backed her up.

"I caught five. Except for the one for lunch soup, the rest are all yours."

Paisley looked at the bucket brimming with fish, warmth spreading through her chest, eyes curving into a smile.

"Well then, I won't be polite about it."

She glanced down at the little white cat staring up at her with pleading eyes.

"Guess we've got a whole fish pond now. This one braised, this one sweet and sour, this one for sour cabbage fish. The rest are all for my cat. My ears are worn out from all that meowing."

Chapter 310

When did the base get a player like this?

How had he not heard about her?

Paisley pulled up her account panel and finished the transfer, quick and clean.

Twenty thousand points gone in an instant, the transaction complete.

The shopkeeper handed over the neatly wrapped gift bags, his tone practically glowing.

"Everything's all packed up for you. Please come back anytime!"

A flicker of light passed over Paisley's fingertips, and the weighty bags vanished into thin air, tucked away into her storage.

Plenty of survivors had already awakened Storage Affinity by now. Nobody batted an eye. She didn't need to hide it anymore.

Ashley couldn't wait, running her fingers along the cold, sharp steel of her new blade, delight written all over her face. She looked up at Paisley, grinning.

"You're the best, Paisley! Thank you so much, I love it!"

The four of them had just turned to leave when a sharp, grating voice erupted from somewhere nearby.

A crowd was already drifting toward the commotion. A woman, immaculately dressed, her makeup heavy and precise, stood with her hands on her hips, chin raised, radiating entitlement. Her voice cut through the street.

"Disgusting! Some broke janitor thinks he can touch me? Look what you did to my clothes!"

"Either pay me two hundred points, or slap yourself fifty times right here, right now!"

"Pick one, or I'll call security and have them throw you out to rot with the dead."

Heads turned. The man being targeted wore a faded, water-stained cleaning uniform, a worn rag clutched in one hand, a battered plastic bucket at his side. His shoulders were hunched, and he kept bowing, apologizing over and over, his voice thin with exhaustion.

"I'm so sorry, ma'am. I didn't mean to."

"I was mopping the walkway, and some water splashed onto your clothes. It wasn't intentional at all."

"Please, it's just a little water. It'll wash right out. Fifty slaps is really too much to ask."

When Paisley got a clear look at the man's gaunt, haggard face, she went still.

It had only been a few days, and she'd barely recognized him.

Reid Lennox.

In her last life, Reid had been the Lennox family's second son — the one who'd awakened a Fire Affinity thanks to the sacred fungus she'd found. Arrogant, full of himself, always carrying that air of entitlement. Nothing like the broken man in front of her now, caked in dust, bowing his head.

He'd grown thin, hollowed out, every trace of his old arrogance stripped away, leaving behind nothing but exhaustion and desperation. The cheap uniform hung off his frame — a world away from the flashy, overconfident Fire Affinity user he'd once been.

Had he finally learned to bow his head?

But looking into his eyes, at the resentment and bitterness still simmering beneath the surface, Paisley felt nothing. Just indifference.

The woman sneered at his groveling, like she'd just heard the funniest thing all week, her contempt sharpening as she pressed closer.

"Too much? You're a nobody without an Affinity, and you think you get to talk to me about fairness?"

"This outfit's a custom base commission — two hundred points, easy. Pay up and I'll let it go."

"Can't afford it? Then get on with the slapping. I don't have all day."

The crowd watched, nobody stepping in to help. The woman's clothes, her attitude — she clearly had backing, and nobody wanted to pick a fight with that.

Reid's back went rigid, his face draining of color, his hand clenched white-knuckled around the broom handle, shame crawling up his neck.

He genuinely couldn't pay.

Working the lowest-tier cleaning jobs, a full day's labor earned him twenty points — barely enough for the cheapest hardtack and water, enough to keep him alive and nothing more.

The three of them had fallen hard since everything collapsed.

Rachel had cut them off completely, living it up for herself, not sparing them a second thought. Whatever family loyalty they'd once had was long gone.

Owen, the eldest, was a Level Four Lightning Affinity user, the only one of the three brothers who'd awakened anything. He'd taken a serious injury on a high-risk mission and had been bedridden ever since, desperate for points to cover treatment.

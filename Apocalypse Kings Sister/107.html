Chapter 107

"The extra ten covers the extra food," she said flatly.

"Thank you!"

Jasper's eyes lit up. He hurried to take the plate and fork, holding the steaming food like it was treasure. He was so excited that he was shaking.

After days of chewing on cold, hard food and going hungry, that rich, savory smell finally made everything feel okay.

He found a spot off to the side and started wolfing it down. Every bite was rich and full of flavor, better than anything he'd eaten since the apocalypse began.

The soft mashed potatoes soaked up the thick, savory sauce, and the meat was tender without being greasy. The fresh herbs cut through the richness, and as the warm food went down, it warmed him from the inside out and melted away all his exhaustion.

Rachel stood frozen next to him. She watched him trade away the 60 crystal cores she'd risked her life for, and he hadn't even saved her one bite of the feast.

She was shaking with rage, and her face went from white to red.

She'd risked getting torn apart by zombies to save up those cores, and he'd just taken them like they were nothing.

"Mr. Quinn!" Rachel hissed through her teeth, low and furious.

"What gives you the right to steal my crystal cores? Those were mine!"

Jasper's mouth was stuffed with food, and he was clearly loving every bite. He just glanced at her, not the least bit guilty.

"You're the one who didn't want to pay.

"I paid for it, so obviously it's mine.

"You're the one who wanted to stick with me, so whatever crystal cores you get belong to me too!

"What, you have a problem with that?

"Then get the fuck out. Nobody's going to baby you.

"You still think you're some pampered little princess?"

He was warm, full, and perfectly happy, and he couldn't care less how Rachel felt.

In the apocalypse, resources came first. If something was right there for the taking, he'd be stupid not to take it.

That shut Rachel up. Her chest heaved. She was furious and hurt, eaten up by resentment and regret.

But if she left him now, she'd be dead for sure in a world this dangerous.

I picked the wrong guy. That's on me.

She regretted it so much. Why had she ever stuck with this useless idiot?

She'd never met anyone this classless in her life.

Now, she had nothing left.

She hadn't even gotten one bite of hot food, and all her hard work had been for nothing.

Rachel glared at Paisley.

This is all that bitch's fault.

She fought the urge to stare at the food and miserably chewed on the rock-hard hardtack in her hand.

One day, she'd make sure Paisley died a horrible death.

Every last one of them is going straight to hell!

The others eating nearby watched the whole thing in silence. Nobody said a word, and their eyes were cold.

Rachel had chosen this path herself, and she had nobody else to blame.

Not far away, Owen and his brothers watched the whole thing through their car window, smirking with pure satisfaction.

Rachel had it coming.

This was karma, plain and simple.

They didn't feel an ounce of sympathy for her anymore!

After they finished eating, everyone got back on the road.

At four in the afternoon, they reached the outskirts of Rivergate.

Rivergate was a city built on the water.

Before the apocalypse, it had been a world-famous tourist spot and a major trade hub.

Canals ran everywhere, little bridges arched over the water, and rivers and lakes wrapped around the whole city. It had been a beautiful, charming place.

Even after everything the apocalypse had done to it, it was still easy to tell from far away how amazing it used to be.

Waterfront shops, scenic lookout towers, and riverside walkways stretched on and on. But all the life had drained out of them long ago, leaving only silence and decay.

A wide ring of waterways surrounded the whole city, and every road in or out had collapsed or caved in. The only way into the city now was by boat.

Elijah parked the car in a safe spot and looked out at the city surrounded by water.

"Rivergate's waterways are complicated, and there's too much we don't know. It's going to be dark soon, so we shouldn't go in blind right now."

Everyone nodded. They only had about two hours of daylight left, and once night fell, there was no telling what might be lurking in the water.

The outskirts of Rivergate, on the other hand, were packed with shops and houses. It used to be a busy waterfront shopping district and neighborhood, so there had to be plenty of supplies left.

Paisley looked around and sent out her psychic senses at the same time. A fine net of awareness spread out and covered the whole area within 1,000 yards.

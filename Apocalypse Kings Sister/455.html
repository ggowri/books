Chapter 455

Behind them, the swarm's shrieks were already at the first-floor lobby, and a stinking gale chased at their heels. Death trailed them step for step.

They ran flat out, upward, without a pause.

Second floor, third, fourth, flashing past. The stairwell was dead quiet, with scuff marks from crawling beasts everywhere. The higher they climbed, the fainter the sweet, metallic smell grew, and the pressure eased a little.

Nobody relaxed. They gritted their teeth and charged up to the top, the fifth floor.

But when Mason kicked open the stairwell's iron door and stepped into the open top floor, everyone froze, every muscle tight.

The wide, empty storage floor held a crowd of living people.

Over ten of them, all in neat black tactical uniforms, with excellent gear, rifles loaded, cold and fierce. They were base Affinity users.

They were clearly hiding here, driven up by the lizards. Now they stared down at the Falcon Squad with cold, hostile eyes.

The two teams stared at each other, and the air seemed to set solid.

The strangers were watchful and measuring, and their hostility didn't hide. Their fingers stayed on triggers and Affinity points, ready to strike.

One wave ended and another began.

The fifth-floor storage area was wide, with piles of old crates scattered about, throwing half the floor into shadow.

Cold daylight slanted through the dusty windows and cut the two teams' silhouettes out sharply.

On one side, the Falcon Squad, scrambling, sweating, fresh from a bloody fight. On the other, a team that had been holed up here for a while, in tight formation, ready to fire.

When their eyes met, the air went tight and hard to breathe.

All the strangers were tense. Every face showed the hard wariness of people who'd been through combat. Their fingers pressed the triggers, Affinity flickered in their palms, and their hostility was sharp. If the Falcon Squad so much as twitched, gunfire would start.

The Falcon Squad dared not relax either. They quickly sidestepped into defensive formation, backs to the stairwell, watching the strangers ahead and the lizard horde closing in below. They were trapped between two threats.

They had barely escaped a beast tide only to run into a high-level Affinity team of unknown strength. They had no way forward, no way back.

The standoff lasted a few seconds. The one to break it was the strangers' leader.

He was tall, in a spotless black combat uniform, with a cold, sharp face. The calm, hard look in his eyes spoke of long time spent in command. His presence was steady and heavy, clearly a veteran high-level captain.

He looked down on the squad, his eyes finally settling on the steady-looking Elijah. His voice was low and hard, probing.

"Were you chased up here by the lizard swarm too?"

Not a greeting or a pleasantry, just an exact read of the situation.

Elijah stayed calm, not backing off under his bearing. His voice was level.

"The whole mall is a lizard nest. The lower floors are completely sealed off. There was nowhere to go but up."

"We didn't know anyone was up here. Sorry to intrude."

The man's eyes darkened. He took in the blood on the squad's clothes and the beast stench still clinging to them, glanced at the faintly shaking floor and the dense crawling sounds beneath, and the hostility in his eyes eased, replaced by a heavier look.

"We've been trapped here for two days."

He laid out their situation plainly, breaking the dead-end standoff.

"The lizards have us stuck too. The moment we try to break out, the whole colony wakes up, and swarms seal every stairway down. We tried to break out and took heavy losses. We can't get through."

"All my people are hurt in some way."

At those words, the Falcon Squad understood.

No wonder they were so hostile. They'd been trapped too long, nerves frayed, and they treated every stranger as a threat by instinct.

Like the Falcon Squad, they'd been driven into a corner by a sudden beast tide as prey.

And they'd been stuck here even longer.

******

Elijah took the opening, softened his edge, and spoke honestly, reaching out first.

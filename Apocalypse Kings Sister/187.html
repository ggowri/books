Chapter 187

The cool, sweet water went down easy, and warm, abundant energy swept through her at once, filling back in everything she'd spent.

After a short rest, with most of her strength back, she didn't linger. She moved to the next bed and began working on Elijah and the four other unconscious soldiers.

Her focus held steady, her expression calm.

Rich green light rose from her palm again, a steady flood of Plant Affinity pouring into each soldier in turn, gentle and relentless at once, repairing every inch of tissue and meridian the toxin had touched.

Layer after layer, the clear green light spread on…

Outside the treatment room, the senior staff, officers, and medical workers stood frozen, holding their breath, no one making a sound.

Every heart hung somewhere up high, caught between dread and hope, waiting on the outcome.

Each passing second dragged on, unbearably slow.

No one dared to speak.

The Director stood at the front, hands clasped behind his back, his usually composed face drawn tight, brow furrowed without easing.

His eyes kept drifting to the sealed door, his thoughts a tangled mess.

Right now, every hope he had rested on one young woman.

The base's best doctors had already run out of options.

The officer whose wife she'd once saved stood with his hand curled tight, knuckles white.

Could she really bring all five of them back?

A poison their best healer couldn't even touch.

The medical staff stood with their hands at their sides, faces grave.

They knew better than anyone how critical these five were. All of them had one foot through death's door already, the toxin buried in their bones and organs, and there was no known cure for it.

Time crept past—ten minutes, twenty, an hour…

The room stayed utterly still, no sound, no movement, the quiet itself enough to set nerves on edge.

Outside, the mood grew heavier by the minute. Hope dimmed in more than a few eyes, despair creeping quietly in.

“It's been this long with nothing. You don't think… they've already…”

Someone's voice dropped low, drained of all strength.

“That poison beat every healer we have. It's too strong, too strange—we never should have pinned all our hope on one girl.”

“It's over… it's over… they can't already be gone, can they… we shouldn't have put this on her shoulders in the first place…”

“It's been a full hour and still nothing. Let's just go in.”

The murmurs rose and fell, thick with despair, settling over the crowd like a weight.

Every eye stayed fixed on the sealed door.

Then, faintly, a soft green glow began seeping out through the crack beneath it, warm and full of life.

The murmuring cut off at once. People leaned in together, straining to see through the gap.

The next moment, the door opened from the inside.

Paisley stepped out, a faint tiredness in her eyes, a thin sheen of sweat at her temple, her steps unhurried, her gaze perfectly clear.

She looked up at the staff all staring at her, and the words that came out landed like a thunderclap.

“The toxin's gone. The contamination's clear. I repaired the damage to their meridians and organs. They're fine.”

“They're out of danger. They could wake up any time now.”

Just two sentences, said so lightly, and yet they hit like thunder.

Dead silence fell, so complete you could hear a pin drop.

Shock froze on every face, pupils shrinking, disbelief written across the room.

For several long seconds, nobody moved. Then the silence broke all at once into a chorus of sharp, disbelieving breaths.

“They're—they're alive?! All five of them? Is this real? Tell me I'm not dreaming.”

“That poison beat our best healer cold, and she just... cleared it? All of it? This can't be a trick, right?”

People pushed past her, hurrying in to check for themselves.

The five men, whose faces had been dark and breath faint, now looked entirely normal, breathing deep and even. Still asleep, but clearly out of danger, nothing like the dying men they'd been hours ago.

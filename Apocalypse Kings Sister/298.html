Chapter 298

Before they could pile on more guilt, Paisley spoke, her voice cool and precise, cutting straight through their performance.

"Are we close, exactly?"

Her gaze stayed flat and unbothered, her presence pressing down over the whole scene.

"Don't try that guilt-trip routine on me. I don't buy it.

"The cars are mine. The supplies are mine. I could smash the cars right now and it still wouldn't be any of your business."

******

Their carefully arranged expressions of misery cracked instantly. None of them had expected her to be this unmoved.

In the stunned silence, Paisley casually laid out her price.

"If you want a ride, fine. Five hundred Level Three crystal cores. Fair trade.

"Don't say I didn't give you the chance."

The words landed, and Chase's pupils contracted, his face falling fast.

He glanced down at the handful of mismatched, scattered cores in his storage pouch, a sinking feeling settling in. Five hundred Level Three cores was practically an impossible sum for him right now.

Rachel went rigid, both hands clamping down protectively over her bag, panic and desperate reluctance flashing across her face.

Everything she had — scraped together from playing every angle she could — barely amounted to a hundred Level Two cores, not even a tenth of what was asked, and this was her only safety net in this world. She wasn't about to hand it over easily.

The rest of the group clutched their own pouches too, faces twisted with reluctance. None of them wanted to empty their savings for one ride. Walking seemed better than that, and deep down, most of them were still hoping for a free ride anyway.

Seeing their stingy, self-serving reluctance laid bare, a cold, mocking smile tugged at the corner of Paisley's mouth.

"If you're not willing to pay, then get out of the way. We're leaving."

Rachel wasn't ready to let go of her only shot at survival. She swallowed her embarrassment and forced her expression back into something soft and pitiful, stepping forward to beg, trying to lean on old ties and sympathy.

"Paisley, we grew up together. Can't you cut us some slack? I really can't take another step. We go back so far, you can't just let me suffer like this..."

"Move."

A low, commanding voice cut her off mid-sentence.

Orion stepped forward, his tall frame settling firmly in front of Paisley, a chilling pressure sweeping over the group, his eyes cold and sharp.

"Don't block the road."

That phony saint from the Lennox family, acting up again.

Every time he thought about how she used to torment his sister, he wanted to deck her.

How did she even have the nerve to come begging now?

The force of his presence sent Rachel stumbling back several steps, her fragile act shattering into pure, flustered desperation, though she didn't dare push closer.

This man was an apocalypse powerhouse. Not someone she could afford to provoke.

Elijah stepped up right behind him, his eyes cold as a frozen lake, his voice flat.

"The vehicles and supplies are only enough for our own group. There's no extra room. Find transportation somewhere else."

Rejected again, Rachel's composure finally snapped, her voice rising in outraged indignation.

"Paisley, how can you be so heartless! It's barely any trouble for you, and you're just going to watch us walk the whole way to the base? How could you do that? We're family, after all..."

Paisley raised her eyes, cool and clear, and let out a mocking laugh.

"Family? You think you count as family?"

"I have no obligation to help any of you in this world. I don't owe you anything."

Rachel's face twisted dark with something vicious, and she pulled out the one card she'd always relied on to control Paisley.

She knew Paisley too well, knew how much she valued family, how much their parents' opinion mattered to her. She was certain it would still work this time.

"Aren't you afraid of what Mom and Dad will think, acting this cold-blooded?"

Rachel gritted her teeth, her voice sharpening into a threat.

"I'll be in touch with them again soon. Aren't you worried they'll be completely disappointed in you? Isn't their opinion the one thing you actually care about?"

Every time before, the moment she invoked their adoptive parents, Paisley had always backed down, always given in.

But this time, Paisley just looked at her coldly, her expression perfectly even, nothing but indifference in her eyes.

"The moment I decided to cut ties with all of you, I stopped being part of the Lennox family."

Her voice stayed flat, shattering every hope Rachel had left.

"I made my peace with never speaking to them again a long time ago. You really think, after everything, that I still care what they think of me?"

"I've already found my real birth parents. I don't have the patience to deal with the Lennox family anymore, and I'm done playing along."

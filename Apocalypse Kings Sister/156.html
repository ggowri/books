Chapter 156

"If it bothers you that much, two hundred Level Three crystal cores per token. Pay me back whenever you can."

If she didn't set a price, they'd never feel right using them.

The whole group exhaled at once, the guilt draining out of them.

Worth it. So worth it.

Everyone knew — storage, portability, preservation, all in one — a device like this was priceless. Crystal cores couldn't even buy it on the open market.

Two hundred Level Three cores barely scratched the surface of what it was worth.

They all understood exactly what Paisley was doing. Cutting them a deal. Treating them like family.

For anyone else, she wouldn't have offered this at all.

No one hesitated. Everyone nodded.

"Worth it! So worth it!"

Spencer spoke up first, practically giddy.

"Two hundred Level Three cores for a permanent storage token? That's not a deal, that's robbery — in our favor."

"Thanks, Paisley!"

Ashley gripped her token tight, eyes bright.

"I want one! Thank you! I love this thing already — I'll pay you back the second this mission's done!"

Madison and Mason nodded along, grateful.

With a hundred square feet each, missions, supply runs, crystal core hauls — everything just got easier. No more hauling, no more spoilage. Exactly what they needed most, right now.

Marcus looked down at the cool metal token, something in his eyes growing warmer.

Paisley seemed distant, but underneath it, she was thoughtful. Generous.

How could he not fall for someone like that?

"No problem."

He spoke up, serious.

"I'll pay you back soon."

Every one of them felt the same mix of gratitude and curiosity.

Paisley kept surprising them, over and over, like she had an endless supply of them.

But however mysterious she was, she was still their teammate.

They'd keep her secrets.

Paisley checked the time — nine in the morning — and gave the final instructions.

"Alright. Decide for yourselves whether to bond your tokens."

"Once that's settled, pair up and start moving."

"Stay safe. If anything goes wrong, use the walkie-talkies right away."

With that, she said nothing more, reaching out to push open the mall's rusted, weathered door.

Cold air rolled out to meet her, thick with decay. She stepped through first, calm, into the dark and dangerous mall.

******

Past the door, the space opened up fast, and the low growls of zombies rose from every direction.

Clusters of Level Two and Three mutant zombies shuffled across the lobby, flesh rotted and patchy, movements stiff.

They caught the scent of the living and surged forward all at once, jamming the path ahead.

Paisley's expression didn't shift. A flash of cold light, and the Frostbloom Sword settled into her palm.

Level Six Plant Affinity wrapped around the blade, frost flickering along the edge, the chill cutting sharp.

Facing the swarm, she moved light and fast, every strike clean, no wasted motion.

Squelch. Squelch.

Heads rolled with every pass of the blade, black blood spattering the floor.

Against the surge of her power now, these low-tier zombies didn't stand a chance. She cut through them like cutting vegetables.

Kitty trailed behind her, small white body bouncing from kill to kill.

Every time a zombie dropped, she pounced, cracking the skull open with a precise paw strike, pulling out the crystal core, and tucking it away with careful focus.

The mall looked ruined on the outside — rust and grime, broken edges. Inside, though, the structure held firm, shelves and fixtures somehow preserved, untouched by looting or destruction.

The first floor opened into a massive grocery supermarket — daily goods, snacks, instant food, rice and oil, everything lined up in rows, in quantities that seemed almost absurd.

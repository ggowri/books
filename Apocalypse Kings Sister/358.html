Chapter 358

If he did, Elijah might not manage alone, and the Thompsons' fate was tied to the Blackwoods' either way.

No one was allowed to hurt the people she cared about.

Besides, the Blackwoods weren't bad people, and Elijah had been genuinely good to her this whole time.

"Mrs. Blackwood, don't worry. I might be able to help."

The moment she said it, the heavy gloom in the room shattered, replaced by a sudden, stunned silence.

Mrs. Blackwood's head snapped up, her tired eyes going wide, sorrow replaced instantly by startled hope.

She stared at the young girl in front of her, breath catching, disbelief written across every feature.

Over the past weeks, nearly every notable doctor and Affinity user in the base had been brought in, every one of them shaking their heads, calling the poison beyond their reach, watching Mr. Blackwood waste away a little more each day.

And now, after all the experts had come up empty, this young girl had broken through that hopelessness with just a casual "I might be able to help."

"Pais... what did you just say?"

Mrs. Blackwood's voice trembled, as if she were grasping at a lifeline that might vanish any second, hope and deep hesitation both churning in her eyes.

"I know you have a good heart, that you don't want to see us suffer like this. But this poison's so strange, and even the best doctors couldn't crack it. Can you really...?"

She didn't finish, but the doubt in her voice said enough.

It wasn't that she doubted Paisley's abilities exactly — the situation had simply been hopeless for so long that no one dared hope anymore.

She was afraid of being let down again.

Her mother, sitting nearby, frowned sharply too, the warmth in her face giving way to open astonishment.

She leaned forward slightly, studying Paisley's calm face, disbelief plain in her eyes.

As close as her family was to the Blackwoods, she knew exactly how dire Nathaniel's condition really was.

Her husband's biggest worry these past weeks had been exactly that.

The strange poison had been eating away at him day by day, and if anything, he'd looked worse today than before.

Pais was so young — however capable she was, how could she possibly cure something like this?

"Pais, I know your heart's in the right place, that you want to help."

Her mother's voice carried real worry, concern plain in her eyes.

"But the Chief's poison is a different kind of problem entirely. Every Affinity doctor, every herbalist in the base has tried and come up with nothing. Please don't push yourself too hard..."

Both women wore the same expression — shock layered over doubt and anxiety.

As far as anyone knew, the poison was simply incurable, with no hope of a turnaround. What Paisley had just said sounded like pure fantasy.

Ashley stepped forward at once, certainty shining in her clear eyes, fully on her sister's side without a shred of doubt, her bright voice cutting through the hesitation in the room.

"Mom, Mrs. Blackwood — I believe in Pais."

"If she says she can do it, she can. You should trust her too."

Time and again on their journey, Paisley had pulled off miracles, finding a way through even the worst situations.

Ashley believed every single word out of her sister's mouth, without question.

She looked at the two women, still uncertain, her voice firm and clear.

"Pais never talks big for no reason. If she says there's a way, there's a way. She'd never make a promise she wasn't sure she could keep."

Ever since she'd known her —

others might dismiss it as youthful bravado, but Ashley had always known — her sister's abilities went far beyond what anyone else could imagine.

Her faith in Paisley never wavered, not even slightly.

Ashley's words were like a dying flame flickering back to life inside Mrs. Blackwood's chest.

Her throat worked, swallowing down the doubt, her voice rough.

"Pais... are you really sure about this?"

Her mother's gaze sharpened too, worry still there but a flicker of hope stirred by Ashley's confidence.

They'd both watched Nathaniel deteriorate with their own eyes, worn down by it, and even a one-in-ten-thousand chance was something worth grasping at.

Facing their conflicted, anxious looks, Paisley stayed perfectly calm, nodding slowly, her dark eyes clear and certain, carrying a quiet reassurance.

Chapter 5

That was all it took to shatter Orion's calm.

The icy composure in his gaze shattered instantly. His eyes roiled with a tangled mess of stunned elation, bittersweet ache, and the scorching rush of reclaiming what he'd thought lost forever.

Years of searching, countless dead ends, sleepless nights wondering if she was even alive—it all collapsed into this single moment.

He looked down, his eyes tracing every detail of her face, hesitant, almost afraid to believe it.

The young features, the delicate line of her jaw... she looked so much like their mother.

It was her.

After all these years, it was really her.

When the world ended, he'd believed he would never see her again.

But here she was—his little sister, found in the ruins of everything.

Orion was a man of ice and control, never one to show what he felt. Now, though, his voice shook. His fingers trembled slightly, and he breathed carefully, as if the sight before him might dissolve like a desperate dream.

"Paisley..."

The name left his lips, low and tender, almost breaking on the way out. Something glinted in his eyes—a sheen of wetness he wouldn't let fall.

"I'm Orion. Your brother. I finally found you."

Nearby, Mason had lowered his blade, standing silent in disbelief. He'd served beside Orion for years. He knew better than anyone the sleepless nights, the dead ends, the relentless search for this one lost girl.

Paisley felt a stir inside. Her vision blurred just enough as she looked up, hesitant. "Brother?"

He seemed so raw, so real. Could it be true?

She remembered the whispers from her past life—Rachel saying she'd take the place of the Thompson daughter. Was this what they meant?

Had he been searching for her all along?

Seeing her so thin and uncertain sent a sharp ache through Orion's chest. She was just a kid when the apocalypse happened—wandering alone through chaos, surviving who knows what.

All the cold intensity he carried melted away. He softened his posture and slowly reached out, resting a gentle hand on her shoulder. His touch was feather-light, afraid she might bolt.

"I'm your brother," he said, each word deliberate. "You don't have to be afraid anymore. I'm here now. I'll keep you safe.

"No one will hurt you again. I won't leave you behind."

Paisley dropped her gaze. Behind her long lashes, a flicker of resolve passed through.

Orion's appearance was the first real gift since she'd been given a second chance—and the strongest ally she could've hoped for.

Last time, she died alone in the ruins, forgotten.

Now, she had a brother who looked like he could move mountains.

Maybe fate is finally paying me back.

The Lennox brothers could keep Rachel and treat her like trash.

Paisley would hold onto this man—a power player in this new broken world—and climb her way to the top.

Everyone who'd ever betrayed her, looked down on her, owed her—she'd make them see her. Fear her. Pay for it all.

"Brother," she whispered again, letting her voice go small and soft. She played the part perfectly: the sweet, trusting girl with her guard down. "Are you really my brother?"

The question hit Orion right in the chest. His heart swelled, joy and grief tangling tight inside. Before he could second-guess it, he pulled her into a careful hug.

"Yes. I am."

His voice was thick, earnest. The smile on his face was tired but real. "You're the Thompson daughter who went missing years ago. And I finally found you."

He eased back, hands still on her arms. "Let's get inside. From now on, you stick with me. As long as I'm here, no one touches you. I promise."

Paisley nodded, eyes still lowered.

But inside, her thoughts were clear and sharp. This was her chance—her golden ticket. Orion was top-tier in this apocalyptic game, and she wasn't letting go.

She fell into step beside him, head slightly bowed, moving quietly.

Orion glanced back and saw her hesitation, the unease in her posture. It cut deep.

He stopped, reached over, and wrapped her cold, small hand in his. His palm was warm, solid, safe.

"Don't be scared," he said, slowing his words. "I've got you.

"You don't have to doubt who you are. That pendant you're wearing—it's a family piece. Passed down to the daughters in our line for generations. You've had it since you were a baby.

"The moment I saw it, I knew."

He took a steadying breath. "Besides me, you have two other brothers. They're out there looking for you, too.

"We got a lead a week back, saying you might be in Aetheris. We drove straight to Marisport, then heard you'd come to Southspire for that academic decathlon. We didn't stop—just pushed through."

His voice grew heavy. "Then everything fell apart. Roads gone, cities overrun... I thought I'd lost you for good. But here you are. Guess someone's watching out."

Paisley listened, quiet on the outside.

Inside, her mind was racing.

They'd crossed states for her. Chased clues from one city to the next, all the way into the apocalypse.

They never stopped looking.

It turned out she hadn't been abandoned after all. There really were people in this world who would cross states, drive through ruins, and risk everything just to find her.

Having lived through two lifetimes, she thought she'd grown used to the coldness and betrayal—to having no one who cared, no one on her side. Her heart, long hardened by resentment, felt like stone, numb to everything.

But now, that icy shell suddenly cracked. A wave of warmth, fierce and real, spread through her chest, soothing old wounds she'd thought would never heal.

Tears welled up without warning, hot against her skin as they trailed down her cheeks. This time, they weren't tears of hurt. They were something else—something that felt like coming home after a long, cold night.

Orion kept her hand firmly in his and led her into the main hall of the safe house.

The space was open and well-kept, surprisingly orderly for the end of the world. From the kitchen came the steady sounds of a fire being lit and pans shifting—three girls were already moving about, preparing what passed for dinner these days.

In the center of the room, three guys sat leaning forward on the couch, talking in low tones about routes, supplies, whatever came next. But it was the man seated between them who commanded the room.

He wore a tailored tactical uniform, dark and sharp. His shoulders were broad, his posture straight—everything about him spoke of authority and control.

His features were all angles and intensity, with a gaze that could cut through steel. He looked severe, untouchable, the kind of person you instinctively didn't want to cross.

When the door opened, all four of them looked up at once.

Orion couldn't hold back. Still holding her hand, he stepped forward, his voice clear and bright with a joy she hadn't heard in years. "Everyone—look. I found her. I found our sister."

The room froze for half a second, then erupted.

The quiet focus shattered into disbelief, then wild, overwhelming relief. They were on their feet in an instant, closing in with eyes she could barely meet—eyes full of something like awe, and a protectiveness that made her breath catch.

Thomas Thompson reached her first. Steady as he seemed, his voice wavered when he spoke. "Is it really you? Our little sister—after all this time?"

Right behind him, Spencer Thompson's eyes were already glistening. "Thank God," he breathed, shaking his head slightly. "After all these years... we finally found you."

His gaze softened as it rested on her face, taking in the gentle curve of her brows, the quiet calm in her eyes. A faint, tender smile touched his lips. "And you're even more beautiful than we imagined."

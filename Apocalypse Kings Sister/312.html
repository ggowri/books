Chapter 312

It had only been a few days, and he'd already forgotten.

Paisley wasn't the obedient, accommodating girl she used to be.

His composure fully broke. He dropped the act and switched tactics fast, his voice softening, aiming for something closer to wounded nostalgia.

"Paisley, we grew up together. We're family, whatever else happened."

"Back then, sure, maybe my temper wasn't great, but I never actually wronged you. When you got picked on as a kid, I was the one who stood up for you. We have history."

"I know you might still have some resentment, but it's the end of the world now. There's no point dwelling on the small stuff."

His voice stayed earnest, his eyes sincere, working hard to drag her back into the old, false sense of family obligation.

"I'm really out of options here. If I can't come up with the two hundred points, I'll have to slap myself fifty times in front of everyone, or get thrown out of the base."

"Getting kicked out, fine, that's on me. But my brother's still waiting on treatment. His life's counting on me. I can't afford to mess this up."

"Paisley, for old times' sake, help me out just this once. I swear I'll pay you back double."

The crowd shifted, a few softer-hearted bystanders murmuring for her to go easy on him.

But the cold in Paisley's eyes hadn't thawed at all.

She knew exactly what the Lennoxes were. She knew exactly how hollow Reid's words were.

A lifetime of being drained, exploited, looked down on — that had burned off any remaining sentiment long ago. When she'd died, they hadn't shed a single tear. They'd said she deserved it.

This life, she'd already severed every tie to that family. She wasn't about to let some manufactured guilt drag her back in.

"First, I cut all ties with the Lennoxes a long time ago. There's no family here to speak of."

"Second, whatever they did for me as a kid, the years of being used and taken from more than settled that debt."

"Third, this mess is one you made yourself. It has nothing to do with me. I'm not paying for your mistake."

Her words came out cold and absolute, leaving no room to argue, shattering whatever hope Reid had left.

After two flat refusals, whatever pleading tone he'd managed cracked entirely, and panic took over.

Looking at her — polished, wealthy, drowning in resources — and then at himself, filthy, humiliated, barely scraping by, the gap between them crushed whatever dignity he had left.

Every bit of his old pride shattered. He dropped all pretense, stepped forward, and folded into something small and desperate.

His voice shook, eyes rimmed red, every bit of his old confidence gone, nothing left but raw begging.

"Paisley, I was wrong. I really was wrong."

"I was arrogant before, cruel, I didn't appreciate what I had, I know I owe you. Please, just forgive me this once."

"I can't get thrown out. My brother's counting on me. I'm the only thing holding him together."

"I really can't take fifty slaps in front of everyone. I'd never be able to show my face here again, and I might end up in the hospital myself, unable to work at all, and he'd lose his only chance."

"Two hundred points is nothing to you. But to me, it's the only thing standing between me and surviving!"

He bent further, his whole body folding in on itself, voice cracking, nearly sobbing.

"Please, Paisley. Just this once. I'm apologizing. I'm admitting I was wrong. Please."

The crowd quieted, watching the man beg, a few murmurs of sympathy rising.

But Paisley's eyes stayed cold as ice, not a flicker of give in them.

She looked at him, groveling, drowning in regret, and felt nothing. No pity. No softness. Just indifference.

In her last life, the Lennoxes had drained her dry and tossed her aside without a second thought, and when Rachel shoved her into that crowd of the dead, not one of them had shown her any mercy.

Whatever Reid was going through now was just the consequence of the arrogance and cruelty he used to deal out so freely. He'd chosen this path. He could live with where it led.

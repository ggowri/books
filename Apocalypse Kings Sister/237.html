Chapter 237

Orion stood among the branches with his back straight, unable to hide how proud he was. As he looked at Paisley, who still looked perfectly calm, his heart swelled with warmth.

His little sister always shone brightest when things were at their worst. She was so strong that no one could match her.

Elijah stood on top of the thick branches, his eyes deep as he gazed at Paisley, cool and tall, up in the air.

The mountain wind tugged at her clothes. She looked calm and composed, like a savior walking out of a hopeless situation.

The admiration in his eyes grew even deeper. She kept her cool in a crisis and had unbelievable power, and she was so dazzling that he couldn't look away anymore.

Meanwhile, on the other side, Rachel and the three Lennox brothers, whose parachutes were just as damaged, were plunging fast and out of control.

They'd just watched Paisley raise her hand and make giant trees appear in midair to save the others, and a desperate will to live flared up in their eyes. They struggled and screamed for help toward her.

"Paisley, save me..."

"Paisley! Save us!"

"Pais! Please, save us!"

"Paisley Lennox! Don't just let us die! Please, save..."

Their voices were hoarse and terrified, full of panic and begging, and they put all their hope of surviving on her.

Paisley even saved strangers, so there was no way she wouldn't save them. She'd cared about them more than anything before. They were sure she couldn't be heartless enough to just watch them die.

But up in the air, Paisley looked down at them.

Her eyes didn't show a flicker of emotion, only a bone-deep coldness, as she quietly watched the four of them fall, struggling and screaming for help.

All the times they'd taken Rachel's side, mistreated her, given her the cold shoulder, and schemed against her in her past life came rushing back.

She didn't feel a shred of pity, only indifference.

Not kicking them while they were down and finishing them off right now was already the biggest mercy she could give.

And there was no way in hell she was going to save them.

Paisley calmly looked away without giving the falling four another glance. She waved her hand lightly, and the vines gently lowered the others to the ground.

Rachel, Owen, Reid, and Ryan ended up falling straight into a body of water below.

They hit the lake one after another with heavy splashes, and the freezing water swallowed all four of them.

The sudden weightlessness and the icy water caught the four of them off guard. They choked on several mouthfuls of lake water and couldn't stop coughing.

The lake barely softened their fall, which was the only reason they survived. But their whole bodies ached, and their skin was covered in tiny scrapes. Their soaked clothes clung to them, and the cold wind left them shaking and purple in the face, absolutely miserable.

The four of them flailed and struggled in the freezing lake, using up every bit of strength they had before they finally dragged their soaked, heavy bodies to the shore and collapsed on the cold, wet bank.

They were covered in mud, their wounds stung, and the wind cut right through them. That was their reality now.

On a clearing not far away, though, it was a whole different story.

Paisley's group had landed safely. They were sitting on stools around a table, taking their time enjoying a hot lunch.

The smell of cooked meat and veggies filled the air, and warm main dishes and fancy sides covered the table. There were even ice-cold Cokes to wash it all down.

It was peaceful, warm, and cozy.

The sight stung the four who'd just crawled out of the lake.

All their supplies, bags, clothes, and rations had been left on the plane, and they'd long since burned up with the wreck.

Now, they had nothing at all.

They had no dry clothes to change into and not a single bite of hot food. They were cold and starving, and even the driest, hardest hardtack from the base had become a luxury they could only dream of.

On one side, people had food, comfort, full bellies, and peace. On the other, people were soaked, cold, starving, and miserable.

The difference between them couldn't have been crueler.

Suddenly, Reid's stomach growled loudly, and his face turned bright red. "Owen, let's go ask them for some food," he said, his voice hoarse from hunger and cold. "I really can't take it anymore, and I feel awful all over."

Next to him, Ryan stared hungrily at the delicious food in the distance, swallowing over and over, his voice weak and bitter. "I'm starving too... That food smells so good. I can't even remember the last time I had a real meal."

Chapter 237

Orion stood among the branches, back straight, pride and awe written plain across his face as he looked at his sister, her expression still calm. Warmth surged through his chest.

His little sister, always finding a way to shine brightest when things looked worst. Unmatched, no question about it.

Elijah stood among the dense branches, gaze heavy, looking out toward the cool, composed figure hanging in the air.

Mountain wind stirred the hem of her clothes, her posture calm and unhurried, as though she'd walked straight out of disaster as its answer.

The admiration in his eyes only deepened. Her composure under pressure, her devastating strength, everything about her made it impossible to look away.

Meanwhile, on the other side, Rachel and the three Lennox brothers, their parachutes just as damaged, were plummeting out of control.

Having just watched Paisley raise her hand and conjure towering trees out of nothing to save the others, raw desperation ignited in them, and they screamed toward her direction with everything they had.

"Paisley, save me..."

"Paisley! Save us!"

"Pais! Please, I'm begging you!"

"Paisley! Don't let us die! We're..."

Their voices came out hoarse with terror, pure panic and pleading, every last shred of hope pinned on her.

Paisley saved strangers without a second thought. Surely she wouldn't let them fall.

She used to care about them more than anyone.

They were certain she wouldn't be cruel enough to watch them die.

But high above, Paisley looked down.

Nothing stirred in her eyes. Only a bone-deep coldness as she watched the four of them plummet, screaming and flailing.

Every memory came flooding back, the favoritism, the cruelty, the cold indifference, the scheming, all of it from her last life.

Nothing in her stirred with pity. Only indifference.

Not finishing them off herself was already the greatest mercy she had left to give.

Expecting her to step in and save them now was never going to happen.

Paisley's gaze drifted away, not sparing the four of them another glance. She lifted a hand in a light sweep, and vines caught the others gently, lowering them safely to the ground.

Rachel, Owen, Reid, and Ryan plunged straight down into a lake below.

"SPLASH— SPLASH—"

Several heavy impacts cracked through the air in quick succession, the icy water swallowing all four of them whole.

The sudden weightlessness combined with the freezing water caught them completely off guard. They choked on mouthfuls of lake water, coughing violently, over and over.

The water cushioned their fall just enough to save their lives, but left them battered and scraped raw, clothes soaked through and clinging to their skin, the cold wind biting deep enough to turn their lips blue, shivering uncontrollably, about as wretched as it got.

The four of them thrashed and struggled in the freezing water, burning through every last bit of strength before finally dragging their soaked, heavy bodies to shore, collapsing in an exhausted heap on the wet bank.

Mud-caked, covered in stinging cuts, freezing in the wind: that was their reality now.

Not far off, on a stretch of dry ground, the scene looked entirely different.

Paisley's group had landed safely and settled comfortably around a table, already enjoying a hot, steaming lunch.

The smell of meat and vegetables drifted through the air, warm entrées and carefully arranged side dishes covering the table, even a few ice-cold Cokes to wash it all down.

Peaceful, warm, and completely at ease.

The sight cut deep for the four who'd just dragged themselves out of the lake.

All their supplies, their bags, their clothes, their rations, everything had gone down with the wrecked plane, burned to nothing.

They had absolutely nothing left.

No dry clothes to change into, not a single hot meal, cold and starving, even the driest, hardest piece of base-issued hardtack now felt like an impossible luxury.

One side sat warm, fed, and at ease.

The other sat soaked, starving, freezing, and utterly wretched.

The gap between them couldn't have been crueler.

A loud, sudden growl tore from Reid's stomach. His face flushed red with embarrassment, cold and weak all over, his voice coming out rough and strained.

"Owen, let's go ask them for some food. I can't take this anymore. My whole body hurts."

******

Beside him, Ryan stared at the fragrant food in the distance, throat working helplessly, eyes full of longing, voice weak and bitter.

"I'm starving too... that food smells incredible. I can't even remember the last time I had a real meal."

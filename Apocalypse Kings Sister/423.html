Chapter 423

"Make up for it?"

She gave a cold laugh, bone-chilling.

"How are you going to do that?

When I was eleven and had a fever that wouldn't break, you locked me in a dark room because I was a burden. You let me burn until I passed out, and no one came.

Or when Rachel and her friends bullied me in high school and you stood by and watched. You didn't ask a single question while I was beaten and trampled."

"Or after high school, when you stopped giving me a cent. I went hungry and freezing with no clothes and no food, while you ate and dressed well and laughed together. You never gave me a scrap of warmth."

Each question rang out, and each stripped away their fake masks.

"You say you raised me."

Her eyes cooled further, and for the first time a faint red crept in, years of hurt finally showing.

"After Rachel came back, I had to mop, do laundry, cook, and clean the whole house. And I still never got a hot meal or warm clothes.

What you gave me wasn't care. It was cruelty. It was neglect. It was leaving me to survive or die alone."

"In my hardest, worst, loneliest years, you stood by and watched, just because I wasn't your real daughter.

When I was bullied, shut out, beaten, you still watched. You even joined in, looked down on me, and kept me at a distance."

"Now I've finally found my real parents and my real family, and you show up to talk about family, making up for it, and gratitude for raising me?"

"That's laughable."

Every word came out like blood.

Years of things she'd never said aloud poured out, and the Thompsons listened, aching and furious.

Diane's face darkened. She stepped in front of Paisley, her heart breaking, and her scolding was sharp.

"You two have some nerve!

How much Paisley suffered in your house, you know better than anyone!

You abandoned her, froze her out, and abused her, and now you have the face to come here and claim her as your daughter? We haven't even come after you. How dare you?"

Ashley's eyes were red. She clenched her fists and snapped, "Exactly! You didn't fix anything before. Now Paisley has a family, and you show up playing loving parents. You just want to use her. It's disgusting!"

Thomas, Spencer, and Orion all stood, lining up at Paisley's side, their eyes hard and their presence at full force. They stood shoulder to shoulder, united against the couple.

Gerald's face was iron, a chill rolling off him, his words cold and final.

"Paisley suffered in your house, and you never once acted like parents.

Now she's with us, safe and fine. She doesn't need your fake affection or your late apology."

Their masks were torn off in front of everyone. Gordon and Helen's smiles collapsed, their faces going red and white, humiliated and angry.

Helen raised her voice in a panic, trying to guilt her again.

"Paisley! No matter what, we raised you! If we hadn't taken you in, you wouldn't even be alive!

You can't forget where you came from. You can't be this cold!"

"Taking someone in isn't the same as treating them well, and staying alive isn't a gift."

Paisley's eyes were cold, her aura rising and sweeping the room.

"You took me in to look good. Once you had what you wanted, you threw me away. I owe you nothing for that fake upbringing."

"From the moment you stood by and watched me suffer again and again, whatever bond we had was gone."

Gordon thought of the task they'd been given. When soft words failed, he straightened his face and tried to bully her with a stern look.

"Paisley!

Don't be so willful!

Once a parent, always a parent. However strong you've become, you can't forget where you came from.

Cut ties?

Come back to the Lennoxes with us and we'll let it go. Otherwise people will say you're ungrateful and heartless!"

That threat finally enraged the whole family.

Orion stepped forward, his Metal Affinity flickering around him, sharp as a blade.

"You think you have the right to talk about gratitude and morals?

When people were bullying Pais, where were your morals?

Now you want to tie her down with a reputation? Dream on."

"Get out of here right now!"

Gerald's voice was cold, firm, ordering them out.

"If you keep making trouble and guilt-tripping her, don't blame me for what happens."

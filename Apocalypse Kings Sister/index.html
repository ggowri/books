Chapter 1

By the third year of the apocalypse, the world was on the verge of a new era. Paisley Lennox would not live to see it.

She had clawed her way out of more bloodbaths than she could count.

Hundreds of battles. Countless brushes with death. Her body was a map of scars. No ability. No elemental spark. No psychic gift. She'd survived on grit alone.

Everyone in the settlement called the Lennoxes the luckiest family alive: three brothers, all with top-tier abilities.

But no one knew that the Lennox family owed its foothold in the apocalypse to one person: Paisley, who had bled for every inch of it.

She’d survived countless zombie hordes, only to be betrayed and murdered by the very people she’d trusted with her life.

Now she stood in a half-collapsed building, the wind driving the stench of rot into her face. Ahead, a dense horde of zombies shuffled closer, step by step, sealing off the narrow hallway—her only way out.

Then—a hard shove between her shoulder blades. Sudden. Vicious. Calculated. She never saw it coming.

Before she could recover, Paisley pitched forward into the thick press of reaching, rotting bodies.

Cold, rotting fingers shredded her jacket. Festering skin scraped her throat, and icy hands closed around her limbs. Death was closing in, one freezing touch at a time. She whipped her head around, pupils blown wide, blood turning to ice in her veins.

Rachel. Rachel Lennox. She had pushed her.

Rachel. The Lennoxes' precious biological daughter, the sister who'd always seemed so fragile, who clung to Paisley's shadow with wide, timid eyes. The girl Paisley had shielded for three long years, who called her Sis in that soft, grateful voice.

She'd been standing right behind her.

And behind Rachel, fanned out in a loose, protective half-circle, stood the three brothers—the men she'd fought for, bled for, and foolishly believed were her family.

Owen Lennox, the eldest, whose control over lightning made him the pillar of the Lennox family. How many times had Paisley thrown herself in front of a lunging zombie to buy him the split second he needed to unleash his power?

Reid Lennox, the second brother—quiet, intense, a master of devastating fire. She’d gone hungry so he could have the better rations, and scavenged the deadliest ruins for parts to keep his gear running.

Ryan Lennox, the youngest, whose water ability outshone everyone on the team. Arrogant and wild—yet she’d always doted on him, covered for every reckless stunt, and let him claim credit for fights she’d won.

These three powerful men—who could have cleared the hallway with a single strike of lightning, a burst of fire, or a surge of water—just stood in the dust-streaked light. Watching. Faces cold. Detached. As the zombies dragged her under. No pity. No guilt. Only bone-deep indifference, as if she were less than a stranger—just debris finally swept aside.

The zombies’ guttural moans filled her ears. Something wet and jagged raked across her shoulder, and pain bloomed—hot and immediate. But the physical agony was nothing compared to the betrayal.

"Why?"

The word tore from her throat, raw with disbelief. She stretched out a bloodied, trembling hand toward the brothers she’d once risked everything to protect.

"Owen! Reid, please... somebody... Ryan, it’s me! Help me!"

They didn't even look at her.

All three brothers had eyes only for Rachel.

They kept her sheltered in their midst.

Owen’s voice was flat and final. "We need to move. The charges are set."

Reid nodded eagerly. "That’s the last big nest. Blow it, and the cleanup’s done. Our place at the base is locked in."

At those words, Rachel—sheltered at the center of her brothers’ protective circle—ducked her head, tears glistening, the tip of her nose turning pink. When she spoke, her voice was a fragile whisper, thick with what sounded like guilt. Her voice shook. Her eyes didn’t.

"But... Sis is still in there. I just... slipped. I didn’t mean to bump into her. Shouldn’t we at least try to get her out?"

She sounded so eager to save Paisley. But her eyes, half-hidden behind her lashes, held not a flicker of remorse. Only a small, secret delight.

Before anyone could respond, Ryan cut in, his voice dripping with contempt. Every word was a poisoned blade, plunging straight into Paisley’s heart.

"Save her? Please. She’s adopted. Not real family. Just some stray we picked up."

"That ungrateful little snake! We took her in, and this is how she repays us?"

"The commander liked what he saw, and she had the nerve to say no. Serves her right."

"The Lennoxes fed her, clothed her, and kept her alive for three years. Without us, she’d have been zombie food in a week. How did she think she’d made it this far?"

"Let her die here. At least she’ll be good for something. It’s the most useful she’ll ever be, and she should be grateful."

Ryan’s expression softened as he turned to Rachel. "And once she’s dead, you can finally take her place... as the Thompson heiress."

Ryan’s words landed casually, almost weightlessly, yet each one ground three years of her love and trust to dust.

Three years fighting side by side. Three years giving them everything. She’d bled holding off zombies so they could run. She’d risked her life scavenging, taken on the deadliest missions, and shouldered the blame whenever things went wrong. She’d given them the best food, the safest shelter, the most precious ability crystals. All of it, to them.

The family she thought she’d found. The people she’d guarded with her life. None of it was real. It had all been one long, calculated lie. To them, she’d never been family. Just a stray they’d pitied, a tool to be used and sold off the moment the price was right.

The Thompson heiress... Did that mean her real family had been looking for her all along?

"Boom—"

The charges went off. The building shook as a scorching blast swept down the hallway and slammed into her.

The zombies tore into her flesh. Pain lanced through her. Blood soaked through her ragged clothes. Her vision dimmed, streaked with red and black.

Paisley lay crumpled in the horde, watching three familiar backs shrink into the distance. The last warmth in her eyes died, replaced by a cold, hard stare. Her jaw clenched. Her fingers dug into the dirt. Hatred burned in her chest.

This isn't fair.

It can't end like this.

Years of loyalty. Years of putting her life on the line. And this was her reward? Betrayal. A setup. A death no one would even mourn.

Why did the liars and leeches get to walk away unscathed while she was left here to be torn apart?

With the last of her strength, Paisley dragged her hand to the detonator strapped to her chest and pressed it.

Rachel and the brothers didn’t even have time to scream before the fireball swallowed them whole.

Paisley gritted her teeth and glared at the spot where they’d stood until darkness swallowed her whole.

If I get another life...

I’m going to make Rachel and the Lennoxes pay. Every one of them. A hundred times over. In blood.

Fire surged skyward. The blast roared across the ruins, and the whole nest went up with it.

*****

"Smack!"

A sharp slap cracked across Paisley’s face, leaving her cheek stinging.

Owen glared down at her, furious.

"What the hell were you thinking, leaving Rachel out there alone? You know what’s out there. Was this some jealous little stunt? That’s enough."

Reid drove his boot into the back of her knee, and her leg buckled. She hit the floor hard. "On your knees," he said. "Apologize to her. Now."

Ryan jerked his chin at the jade pendant around her neck. "Take it off. Give it to Rachel. Call it an apology."

Rachel hovered behind them, sniffling, one hand tugging at Owen’s sleeve.

"Guys, stop. It’s my fault, I shouldn’t have wandered off. Please don’t be mad at Sis."

The shouting, the fake tears—it all buzzed in Paisley’s ears. But her cheek burned. That was real.

Her head spun. Then everything clicked.

I didn't die.

I'm back.

It was the third day of the apocalypse. She’d been given a second chance. And this time, they would pay.

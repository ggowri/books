Chapter 387

Diane stared, stunned, as her wounds vanished entirely in no time at all, no pain left, not even a mark, momentarily forgetting her grievance entirely, eyes wide with shock and delight.

"Pais, this... did you awaken a Healing Affinity?"

Plant Affinity didn't automatically come with healing ability, nor did it automatically come with purification.

Paisley nodded gently, looking up at Diane, her tone warm but insistent.

"Mom, my Plant Affinity can heal injuries. So tell me honestly, is there anywhere else that hurts? What actually happened at the supply depot?"

Diane lowered her head, running her fingers over the back of her now-smooth, unblemished hand. The sharp sting of the scrape had vanished completely, not even a faint scar or redness left behind, her skin perfectly restored.

The shock in her eyes hadn't faded yet, replaced by disbelieving delight.

She'd known Paisley's Plant Affinity was impressive, but she'd never imagined it could heal, too, a true miracle touch.

The grievance and bitterness she'd been holding onto eased considerably under this sudden burst of joy, though her eyes still stung, her nose prickling faintly.

Under Paisley's gentle but unwavering gaze, Diane finally couldn't hold it in any longer. She let out a quiet sigh and slowly recounted what had happened at the supply depot.

"I really didn't want to tell you. You finally made it home, and I didn't want you worrying about petty family business."

Diane wiped at the corner of her eye, her voice carrying a faint grievance and helplessness.

"You already know about the tension between the senior branch and us, the junior branch."

"This morning I went to the base's public depot to collect our monthly supplies. I waited in line for ages, and right when it was finally our turn, I ran into Brenda Thompson, your uncle's wife."

At the name, Diane's brow furrowed with frustration.

"She recently awakened a Water Affinity. Not very high level, just Level Three, but among ordinary people, that already counts as impressive."

Supplies were scarce in the apocalypse, and everyone fought over every scrap. Anyone with Affinity power tended to throw their weight around without restraint.

As long as things didn't get too out of hand, the security officers usually looked the other way.

Brenda had always enjoyed picking on her, and now that she had an Affinity, she'd grown even more arrogant, with no regard for anyone else at all.

"She cut right in front of me in line, took our family's rice, flour, and vegetables. When I spoke up, said supplies were allocated by household, that everyone gets their fair share, she wouldn't listen. She used her Affinity to slash at me with a water blade, shoved me."

Diane lifted her arms and legs, pointing at the spots that had just been healed, her voice full of grievance.

"I'm just an ordinary person. I wasn't fast enough getting out of the way, and the water cut my skin. She shoved me down, and I scraped myself up pretty badly on my hands and feet."

"She was so smug about it too, said it's an Affinity user's world now, that ordinary people like us should step aside, that supplies should go to awakened people first, that I shouldn't push my luck, and if I talked back again, she'd really teach me a lesson."

"I couldn't win against her, and I didn't dare fight back. I just had to watch her take our supplies and walk away empty-handed."

Diane sighed softly, grabbing Paisley's hand quickly, afraid she'd act rashly and cause trouble, her voice gentle and placating.

"Mom was thinking, forget it, it's just some supplies, not worth holding a grudge over. She only has Level Three Water Affinity, but she's still an Affinity user, and I was scared you'd get hurt standing up for me."

But even with all her efforts to let it go, the memory of being bullied and humiliated in public still showed in Diane's eyes.

Paisley listened quietly, all the warmth in her gaze fading, replaced by a cold, bone-deep chill.

Level Three Water Affinity?

That pathetic level of power, and she still had the nerve to throw her weight around at the base, bully an elder, steal supplies meant to keep ordinary people alive, completely out of line.

She reached out and patted the back of Diane's hand gently, her tone soft but carrying an unmistakable edge of steel.

"Mom, letting it slide doesn't always bring peace. The apocalypse was never a world where the strong get to prey on the weak. Anyone who dares bully us pays for it."

"I'm here now. I won't let you suffer another moment of unfair treatment."

"Come on. Let's go pull the surveillance footage first, then head to your uncle's house to settle this."

This clearly wasn't the first time this had happened.

It was just that Mom had been putting up with it. She'd been afraid.

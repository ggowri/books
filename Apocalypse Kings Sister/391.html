Chapter 391

Now that her mother could hold her own, she wouldn't have to swallow every slight anymore, at the very least, no one would get away with bullying her so easily.

Diane's eyes grew warm, and she said quietly, with real gratitude.

"Thank you, Pais."

It was her daughter who'd given her back her dignity and her strength in this bleak, hopeless apocalypse.

Paisley was practically her little guardian angel!

Since the apocalypse began, with people growing colder and reality turning more brutal by the day, Diane had felt her sense of belonging in this family slipping further and further away.

Watching everyone around her shine, her outstanding sons, her dazzling, powerful daughter, she'd grown more and more insecure, feeling like the most ordinary, most useless one of them all, the gap between her and the rest of the family only widening, leaving her more timid by the day.

But now, with real, tangible power flowing through her palm, the confidence she'd lost for so many years was finally, slowly, coming back.

Paisley let the warmth in her eyes settle into something sharper, her tone crisp and decisive.

"Mom, get ready. Let's go get your justice."

Diane nodded firmly, the last trace of timidity gone from her once-gentle eyes, replaced by a sharp edge.

"Let's go."

Time and time again, the bullying, the stolen supplies, the public humiliation. Every time she'd held back, every time she'd backed down. And what had it gotten her? Not peace, but worse treatment each time.

Today, every debt, old and new, would be settled at once.

The senior branch's standalone villa wasn't far from their own home, located in a prime section of the base.

Grandpa Thompson had always lived with the senior branch, his favoritism toward them baked into his bones since forever, whenever the senior branch caused trouble, he always covered for them, right or wrong.

Within a few minutes' walk, the two of them arrived outside the villa's iron gate.

Standing before the familiar, cold front gate, the deep-rooted timidity in Diane still flickered faintly beneath the surface.

Gentle and soft-spoken by nature, she'd spent her whole life yielding, avoiding conflict, never standing her ground, even facing the greatest injustice, her first instinct was always to retreat.

Her fingers tightened unconsciously, her steps faltering slightly, a part of her wanting to simply turn around and leave.

Paisley caught her hesitation immediately. She reached over, gently steadying her mother's arm, her voice clear and calm, every word certain.

"Mom, don't be afraid. I'm right here."

"Stand tall. Knock with confidence."

She looked up, her gaze steady and encouraging.

"Don't forget, you've never been alone. You have Dad behind you, three capable sons, and me."

"All of us are standing with you."

The words struck straight at the softest part of Diane's heart, scattering the last traces of her hesitation entirely.

That's right, she wasn't suffering alone. Her whole family had her back.

Diane took a deep breath, every trace of hesitation gone from her eyes, and raised her hand, knocking firmly on the villa's front door.

Knock, knock, knock.

The sound of knocking faded, and almost immediately, a sharp, arrogant woman's voice rang out from inside, dripping with impatience.

"Who is it? Knocking this early, how annoying!"

It was Brenda's voice.

The iron gate swung open abruptly. Brenda stood there in clean, comfortable loungewear, still wearing the satisfied smugness of someone who'd recently stolen supplies. Seeing Diane standing outside, she froze for a second before her face twisted into open mockery.

"Oh? It's you? What, can't let it go, came all the way here?"

She crossed her arms, looking down at Diane with the arrogant posture of someone riding high on Level Three Water Affinity, her domineering attitude on full display.

In her eyes, Diane was nothing but a pushover, an ordinary person with no way to fight back, incapable of causing any real trouble even showing up at her door.

Her gaze shifted to Paisley standing nearby, cool and composed, strikingly beautiful. A flicker of wariness crossed Brenda's eyes, though she kept up her tough front.

"What, brought your daughter along to back you up? This base runs on strength. I took those supplies fair and square, why should I give them back?"

Footsteps sounded from inside the house. Grandpa Thompson walked out, leaning on his cane. Without even asking what had happened, he immediately scowled and barked.

"What's all this noise about this early in the morning, second daughter-in-law?

We're family. It's just a little bit of supplies, what's there to fuss over?

Go home, now. Stop making a scene at the front door!"

He didn't even know what had happened, yet his bias kicked in automatically, blaming the wrong side without a second thought, as always.

In the past, hearing this, Diane would have swallowed her grievance in silence and turned to leave.

But today, she stood tall, spine straight, not a trace of her usual timidity left.

Before Paisley could say a word, Diane stepped forward, a faint golden Affinity glow flickering quietly at her palm, her voice calm but carrying real weight.

Chapter 213

After several more exchanges, the mutant finally understood.

It realized the weapon in Paisley's hand could actually kill it, and it had no interest in dragging the fight out any longer.

Its half-regrown fingers slammed down on a switch at its side, and black mist boiled up around its body.

The next second, its form dissolved into the darkness of the tunnel, riding the chaotic magnetic currents to vanish without a trace, leaving behind nothing but a pool of sticky poison and a lingering sense of dread.

The others rushed to the poisoned teammate and hauled him upright.

His face had gone ink-black, lips a deep bruised purple, body wracked with convulsions, breath down to almost nothing. The poison had already eaten into his organs.

Someone watched him fading and his voice cracked with panic:

"He's poisoned too deep! He's not going to make it, what do we do?"

Another teammate dug out the last of their antiseptic from a pocket and fed it to him, murmuring:

"Hang in there, man.

You're going to make it.

We'll get the base's best healer on you the second we're back.

They fixed up the Captain when he was critical before, didn't they?"

But the medicine did nothing against a poison that strong. No effect at all.

Hopelessness crushed the poisoned man completely. He gasped for air, eyelids too heavy to lift, and used his last ounce of strength to force out a few final words:

"I'm... not going to make it... please, go back... tell my wife and kids... to keep living... I'm sorry I couldn't... I couldn't stay with them..."

As the words left him, a wave of black blood surged up his throat and spilled out, reeking and thick. His body went limp, breath fading further, close to gone for good.

Elijah couldn't stand to watch. He gave the order, voice heavy:

"There's nothing we can do for him right now. Ask him if there's anything he wants said. Let him go without regret."

"I asked our handler yesterday, and all he sent us was Chase Sullivan and Rachel Lennox to stir up trouble, no real healer at all. I've never met a more useless commander."

The news only deepened everyone's despair.

All they could do was watch a teammate die.

That was when Paisley finished scanning the dark tunnel, confirmed the mutant was really gone, and walked over to crouch beside the dying man.

After checking him over, she spoke, calm and even:

"Carry him to that clear spot over there. I'll treat him."

The tunnel went utterly silent. Every face turned toward her, frozen in shock.

Elijah and Orion stared at her too, unable to process what she'd just said.

They'd traveled with Paisley long enough to know her.

She was level-headed, careful, and never spoke without being sure. She didn't make promises she couldn't keep.

If she said she could treat him, it meant she actually could.

Both men's minds were reeling.

Paisley had healing powers too, on top of everything else.

How much more was she hiding?

Their respect for her deepened on the spot.

The dying man's teammate was still frozen, staring at her, eyes full of disbelief, too stunned to move.

"You can heal? You can actually save him? How sure are you?"

"He has a wife and kids waiting for him."

"I'm not asking for a miracle. Just get him back to base alive, and I'll beg that mystery healer on my knees if I have to."

...

******

Paisley had expected doubt. Her expression stayed calm, though something graver crept into it as she spoke:

"Keep questioning me if you want, but you don't have another option right now.

Every second we waste, he loses more of his window to be saved. There isn't much time left.

Whether you trust me or not, whether you let me treat him, that's your call."

The poisoned man, slumped in the others' arms, was already slipping from consciousness, the voices around him muffled and far away. Only his will to survive kept the last of his awareness alive.

He forced his heavy eyelids open. His blue lips trembled, convulsions weaker now, chest barely rising, each breath sharp with pain and the taste of blood.

The poison had already eaten into every organ he had. Any longer, and he was dead for certain.

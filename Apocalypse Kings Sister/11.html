Chapter 11

Orion Thompson had only a small backpack slung over one shoulder. The moment he came downstairs, his eyes fell on Paisley Lennox, sitting alone on the couch, empty-handed.

He thought back to last night, when he'd found Paisley all alone with nothing to her name. He couldn't help picturing all those years she must have spent on her own, drifting from place to place and barely getting by. It broke his heart.

He strode over and softened his voice. "Don't be sad, Paisley. Once we get to the big mall, they'll have everything.

"Clothes, jewelry, everyday stuff. Take as much as you like. Apocalypse or not, my sister gets nothing but the best.

"And don't worry about carrying it all. I'm here, and I'll carry every last thing for you."

Paisley's heart warmed a little. It had been years since anyone had cared about her like this, openly and with nothing held back.

Ever since Rachel Lennox came home at 18, all the love, patience, and tenderness in that family had gone straight to her.

Just to hold on to that flimsy idea of family, Paisley had backed down again and again, giving Rachel her things, her chances, everything.

The Lennoxes had always thrown the fact that they'd raised her in her face, guilt-tripping her into giving in and giving up.

Back then, she'd been way too naive. She'd thought that even if they weren't blood, growing up under their roof had to count for something. She'd wanted a little love from them so badly that she'd swallowed every insult and kept giving in.

Even this trip to Southspire had only happened because the whole family wanted to cheer Rachel on at her violin competition.

No one seemed to remember that Paisley played the violin far better than Rachel ever had.

The family was terrified she'd steal Rachel's spotlight. They hadn't just banned her from competing. They'd made her tag along the whole time and babysit Rachel.

Now, when she looked back, she couldn't believe how stupid she'd been.

The Lennoxes had never seen her as family. From start to finish, she'd been an outsider, a prop to make Rachel look good.

But Orion, standing right in front of her, was different. The way he cared for her, fully and without a second thought, was what real family felt like.

She'd already given up on trusting anyone. Her plan had been to keep the Storage Dimension to herself and never tell a soul.

But now, she'd changed her mind.

They had a long road ahead of them, scavenging for supplies all the way back to Aetheris, and the Storage Dimension could really help. Orion and the others had been good to her, so of course she'd pay them back.

After thinking it over, she looked up at Orion and said softly, "Thank you, Orion.

"Actually, I awakened an ability last night too. It's a Storage Dimension. All my stuff is already in there, so I didn't need to pack."

Then, Paisley showed him. One thought, and the solid wood stool in front of her vanished into thin air, pulled into the dimension. Another thought, and the stool dropped back into place, perfectly intact.

She was upfront with him. "The catch is, it's only about 100 square feet right now. That's way too small to hold much."

Orion was quick to reassure her, his face lighting up. "That's fine. It's already amazing.

"It can hold at least a few days' worth of food and emergency meds for all of us. That takes care of most of our problems."

A thought hit him, and his face turned serious. "Pais, a Storage Dimension is insanely rare and insanely powerful. No one else can know about it.

"The fewer people who know, the better. Otherwise, people are going to want it for themselves, and that means trouble.

"Come on, come upstairs with me. We need to tell Elijah."

The two of them headed up to the second floor, where Elijah Blackwood was still packing. As the team's captain, he kept all their rare supplies.

Orion quickly filled him in on Paisley's Storage Dimension and had her show him on the spot.

With a thought, Paisley pulled the chair beside her into the dimension, then brought it right back out, quick and clean.

Elijah was always calm and composed, never one to let his feelings show.

Last night, people had turned into zombies right in front of him, and he'd fought them off up close. His face hadn't so much as twitched. Now, though, he looked completely stunned.

His eyes went wide. For a split second, his usual composure slipped, and he took half a step forward without realizing it. He stared at the empty spot on the floor where the chair had just been, unable to believe it.

A Storage Dimension!

It was the rarest, most sought-after support ability in the whole apocalypse, and Paisley, of all people, had one.

With a Storage Dimension, a person could keep supplies, weapons, and medicine on them at all times, out of sight. In a world gone to hell, that was the ultimate trump card.

Elijah was quiet for a long moment before he got his shock under control. When he finally spoke, his voice was a little lower than usual, and dead serious.

"Keep it hidden. Other than the three of us, nobody else can know.

"If word gets out, you won't be the only one in danger. Our whole team will have a target on its back."

Chapter 183

The moment she stepped out through the hall's main doors, the evening wind caught her, and at the same instant, commotion erupted near the base's main gate, the mood turning tense.

Five or six military SUVs rolled to a stop, their bodies scored with scratches and dark bloodstains, battered almost past recognition.

The doors opened, and uniformed figures climbed out one after another.

Soldiers who were usually a formidable presence now looked utterly wrecked.

Their combat gear hung in torn strips, soaked through with dried blood. New wounds crossed old ones. Their faces were paper-white, lips drained of color, breath ragged and uneven. Several swayed just trying to stand, every inch of them worn down by exhaustion and injury.

Every one of them carried injuries—some with arms twisted and swollen, others with shoulders torn to raw flesh. None of them were in good shape.

Medical staff who'd been standing by rushed in immediately, stretchers lined up in neat rows, their faces tense as they checked each soldier's injuries and began emergency treatment in practiced order.

A few guards hurried over to hold back the gathering crowd, and the mood turned heavy and close.

The attending physician finished a fast check of the wounded, his brow locked tight, his words clipped and urgent.

“These injuries are severe. There's an unknown toxin eating through their tissue, and standard medication can't suppress or clear it. We need emergency specialized treatment right now!”

Even as he spoke, the stretchers were already being wheeled into the makeshift hospital.

The crowd at the gate kept growing, hushed voices trading worried speculation.

“How'd they end up this hurt? They're supposed to be the base's best fighters!”

“Did the scouting mission go wrong? Did they run into a high-tier beast swarm?”

“Look at that color in their faces—they're poisoned, and badly. Unknown toxins like that are the worst. Ordinary medicine won't touch it.”

“This is bad. Toxin damage this severe needs a high-tier healer to purge it, or they'll be dealing with the aftereffects for life.”

“But Rivergate doesn't have a single high-tier healer.”

“If the base's strongest, most reliable squad came back looking like this, whatever's out there just got a whole lot worse…”

The murmurs piled on top of one another, fear and unease creeping through the crowd until the whole gate sat under a heavy, suffocating weight.

******

Paisley had already started to turn and walk away.

This was Rivergate's internal affair, a matter for its own garrison. It had little to do with her, and she saw no reason to get involved.

But just then, her gaze caught on the last stretcher in the line, and stopped there.

The uniform on him was soaked in blood, his frame too slight for the weight it carried. Even half-conscious and drained, his spine stayed rigid, some soldier's instinct too deep to let go even now.

It was Orion.

He was hurt. Badly.

The brother who always stood steady and sure, who'd planted himself between her and every danger since she'd come back to this life—he lay still on the stretcher now, his face frost-pale, lips cracked and faintly blue. His breath barely stirred. Blood had soaked outward across his chest in dark, spreading rings.

In an instant, every bit of her earlier detachment fell away, and her chest clenched tight.

Paisley couldn't stay uninvolved anymore. Worry flooded her eyes, and her feet moved before she decided to, falling in behind the stretcher as they carried him into the makeshift hospital.

Just then, the Director arrived in a rush with a cluster of senior staff, his steps hurried, his face tight. The moment he reached them, the questions came fast.

“What happened? How did the entire squad end up like this?”

“What's their condition? I want everything thrown at saving them!”

The tension in the room stretched taut. The attending physician held the freshly printed scan results, his fingers trembling slightly, his expression as grim as it could get.

“Sir! All five unconscious soldiers are deeply poisoned, radiation contamination levels far past critical.”

“The toxin is highly corrosive. It's already spread through their bloodstream into their organs, their meridians, their minds. Standard healing agents and basic purification do nothing—worse, they actually speed the toxin's spread.”

He drew a breath and gave the worst of it, his voice hoarse and heavy.

“If we can't fully purge the toxin and the contamination in time, it will eat through their nervous system and leave their minds permanently damaged. They'll never wake up again—they'll be left in a vegetative state. Worst case, organ failure. They won't survive the night.”

“Sir, every second counts right now.”

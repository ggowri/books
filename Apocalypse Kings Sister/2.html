Chapter 2

The pendant against her chest felt warm. It was the only thing her birth parents had left her. She hadn't known it then, but it held a secret—a Storage Dimension, capable of stockpiling supplies.

It was the same day in her last life, too. Rachel had run off on purpose and set the whole scene up to make her take the fall.

Back then, Paisley had been naïve. Stupid, even. They'd forced her to take the slaps, kneel and apologize, and in the end, hand over the pendant.

With that very pendant—the one holding a hidden Storage Dimension—Rachel had stockpiled resources, won people over, and become everyone's favorite.

Only now, after dying a miserable death in her past life, did Paisley see them for what they really were.

The three Lennox brothers were self-righteous hypocrites who couldn't tell right from wrong. And Rachel, who played the fragile victim, was nothing but a vicious fake. She was the one who'd personally shoved Paisley into hell.

When Ryan reached out to snatch the pendant, Paisley sidestepped smoothly, kicked his legs out from under him, and got back to her feet like it was nothing. She brushed off her clothes, her gaze cold and sharp. That old, weak, people-pleasing look was gone.

"The pendant stays with me. No one's taking it.

"Rachel said herself she wandered off. It had nothing to do with me.

"She's the one who made everyone worry for nothing. She should apologize."

Paisley's eyes swept over the four of them. All the softness was gone, replaced by a hatred that went bone-deep.

Last time around, she'd given them everything—only to be betrayed and left to die. Since fate had given her a second chance, she was going to settle every score. One by one.

Seeing Paisley—always so obedient—talk back and even fight back in front of everyone, the Lennox brothers' faces darkened.

Rachel's tears fell like they were free.

She'd been reborn too.

And she was determined to get that pendant.

In her past life, it was because of this pendant that she'd won everyone over.

It belonged to her.

Paisley was just a stepping stone.

"If Rachel wants the pendant, consider it a favor. Don't push your luck," Ryan spat, scrambling up. He was still burning from that kick. Seeing Rachel cry so pitifully only made him angrier. "The Lennox family raised you for twenty years. Was all that just wasted on you?"

He went for the pendant again, this time with force. How dare she disobey? How dare she kick him? She needed to be taught a lesson.

He'd only gone down earlier because he wasn't ready.

Paisley blocked his grab without hesitation and struck back.

Another kick landed hard.

Ryan flew back ten feet.

Three years of life-or-death combat in the apocalypse had sharpened her moves. They were brutal, efficient.

But the brothers hadn't awakened any abilities yet. They were just rich kids, no match for her now.

Everyone stared, mouths hanging open.

The other rich kids and chaperones trapped here with them watched in stunned silence.

They'd all come for a competition. Then the world ended, and they'd gotten stuck.

Who would've thought the quiet, delicate girl could kick a grown guy that far?

The living room fell dead silent. You could've heard a pin drop.

Ryan twitched on the floor, pain radiating through his chest. He braced himself but couldn't get up. The arrogance was gone, replaced by humiliation and rage.

He never imagined Paisley—always so quiet, so obedient—would dare fight back. And hurt this much.

Owen and Reid turned pale, then red with anger. They glared at Paisley, shock and fury warring in their eyes.

To them, Paisley was a charity case. Someone who owed the Lennox family everything. She was supposed to take it, to obey. But now she stood there, cold and sharp, like a completely different person.

Since when did Paisley learn to fight like that?

Rachel's crying cut off abruptly. A flash of shock—and something darker—passed behind her eyes.

What was wrong with Paisley?

This wasn't how it was supposed to go.

She was reborn. She knew how this life would unfold. She was meant to do better, be better.

And Paisley was meant to be her stepping stone.

But now... Paisley was slipping out of her control.

Panicking, she cried even more pitifully, her voice weak and trembling as she choked out, "Paisley... why did you have to go this far... hurting our brother Ryan like that... Just apologize to him, and we can all move on."

Every word sounded like an attempt to smooth things over, yet each one subtly shifted the blame onto Paisley.

The wealthy socialites watching nearby began murmuring among themselves, casting disdainful glances toward Paisley while clearly taking Rachel and Ryan's side.

They whispered that Paisley was heartless and ungrateful—that she'd left Rachel in danger, refused to take responsibility, and now had publicly assaulted Ryan. She didn't know how good she had it, they said.

Owen, stung by the whispers and humiliated in front of the crowd, glared coldly and snapped, "Paisley, get down on your knees and apologize!"

Reid stepped forward, his tone harsh and demanding. "Apologize to Rachel and Ryan right now, or you're out of here."

Both brothers were certain Paisley would obey like she always had.

In their eyes, she had grown up relying on the Lennox family. In the chaos of the apocalypse, being cast out meant certain death—she had no other place to go.

She would have no choice but to lower her head, admit she was wrong, and quietly give in, just like before.

This was just a lesson to put her back in her place.

But the Paisley standing before them now was no longer the pushover who'd bend to their threats.

A cold, mocking smile touched her lips. There was no fear in her eyes—only icy defiance.

"Get down on my knees and apologize?" she repeated softly, her voice cool and steady. "What exactly did I do wrong?"

She looked up. Her eyes swept first to Rachel, whose face was streaked with tears and set in that wounded, wronged look, then to the two domineering brothers. Her voice stayed steady, unflinching as she spoke.

"Rachel was the one who ran off alone and put herself in danger. You two demanded I apologize without even hearing the whole story. And Ryan—he attacked me first and tried to take the pendant.

"From beginning to end, I wasn't the one in the wrong."

She paused, letting the words hang in the air.

"The Lennox family's so-called 'kindness' all these years was nothing but a chain—a way to control me, to keep me indebted, and to take everything I had.

"Why should I keep humiliating myself, kneeling and begging for forgiveness over and over?"

Owen's face flushed with rage. "How dare you talk back like that, Paisley!"

Reid took another step forward and reached out, intending to force her down by the shoulder like he'd done so many times before.

Chapter 178

But everyone in that room knew exactly whose effort had made this possible — whose effort had kept them all alive.

Without Paisley, none of them would be sitting here dividing up a reward. They'd all be dead.

Madison spoke up first, firm about it.

"I don't agree with an even split.

Paisley carried this mission. If not for her, we'd all have died in that flower field. Splitting it evenly isn't fair to her."

"Exactly."

Mason backed her up immediately, earnest.

"Paisley's the one who pushed to take the Level Six mission in the first place. She cleared the path for us the whole way, handed out storage tokens so we could carry supplies — she took the most risk and did the most work, start to finish.

She should get a bigger share."

Marcus nodded along too.

"Agreed. An even split ignores what she actually brought to this. It's not fair to her."

Ashley blinked up at Paisley, completely sincere.

"Paisley did more than any of us. She should get the biggest share of this."

Thomas nodded, calm.

"We already cleared plenty of crystal cores along the way just clearing zombies. The real credit for this mission goes to Paisley. She should get more."

Every single one of them agreed — Paisley deserved a bigger cut.

Thomas turned to her.

"Paisley, what do you think?"

Paisley

Facing their genuine insistence, something warm settled in her chest, her expression softening, the corner of her mouth curving faintly. Her voice came out calm but firm.

"No need. Split it evenly.

We're all in this together, risking our lives side by side. We'll be teaming up long-term — I can't keep getting special treatment every time."

She looked around at them, eyes clear and open, a touch of helplessness in her voice.

"Just split it evenly.

If I get treated differently every time, I'll start feeling too awkward to keep working missions with you all."

"Besides — we've all faced death together. No need to split hairs over this."

Seeing her stand firm, they let it go and agreed to the even split.

Good people, all of them.

Her mind drifted, unbidden, to her past life. Something tightened in her chest.

In her last life, no matter how hard she fought, how many people she protected, how much danger she took on herself — everyone had just assumed it was owed to them.

She was always the one on the front line, facing every threat first. And when it came time to divide the spoils, she was always the one who got the least, the one everyone forgot.

Looking back, none of it had been worth it.

Lucky for her, this time around, she had people who genuinely cared, who knew gratitude, who showed up for each other.

******

Her thoughts drifted back, and her mouth curved into something colder.

Today's two rounds of psychic-controlled ambushes had cost Rachel's group plenty.

This had only been a warning.

Next time, she wouldn't hold back.

Soon, Spencer divided up all the rewards evenly among the seven of them, routing it straight to everyone's wristband accounts.

The moment the rewards landed, the other six exchanged a quick, knowing glance.

Each of them pulled two hundred Level Two crystal cores and one hundred Level Three crystal cores from their own share and transferred it to Paisley.

They still owed her a hundred Level Three cores from before.

Paisley's account jumped by a full twelve hundred Level Two crystal cores and six hundred Level Three crystal cores.

With that done, everyone headed off to rest.

Soon, the room was empty except for Paisley.

She looked down at the massive pile of crystal cores, a faint smile curving her lips.

A good day's haul, all around.

A thought, and she slipped into her Storage Dimension.

Paisley called out.

[Trovie, tally up everything from today. Give me the full count.]

A second later, Trovie's voice exploded in her mind, high-pitched and thrilled, rattling off numbers at top speed.

[Host! Host! Full accounting complete!]

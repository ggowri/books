Chapter 91

The second floor was full of bedrooms.

Paisley kept walking until she reached the biggest room. She gently pushed the door open and found a fancy, luxurious bedroom.

She searched the whole room but didn't find a thing.

Just as she was starting to wonder, Trovie's bright, confident voice rang out in her head.

[Master, there's a hidden cellar! All the gold is stashed down there, about six feet to your left!]

Paisley did as it said right away. She took a big step to the left and looked down at the floor.

The floor was covered in old cement tiles, all flat and solid except for one stone tile in the middle that stuck up just a little. Its edges were too clean and neat, and it didn't match the others at all. Someone had obviously hidden something there.

She bent down and pressed lightly on the raised tile with her fingertip.

With a sharp click, the heavy stone slab slid inward and slowly opened up.

A pitch-black opening appeared, so dark she couldn't see the bottom. Cool, earthy air mixed with a heavy metallic smell rushed up at her. A secret underground room had been hiding right under her feet.

In the middle of the floor sat two bulging burlap sacks, stuffed so full that they looked like they'd burst. Just looking at them, she could tell they were heavy.

She climbed down the steps and untied one of the sacks.

A dazzling golden glow lit up the whole room.

It was packed with heavy gold bars, stacked layer after layer.

Trovie squealed. [Master, we're rich! This bag alone weighs at least 55 pounds!]

Paisley untied the second sack.

There weren't any gold bars in this one. Instead, it was stuffed with all kinds of gold jewelry, thick chunks of gold, and gold-plated ornaments. It was a random mix, but every piece was pure gold, heavy, gleaming, and worth a fortune.

Trovie's voice cracked with excitement.

[Master, we hit the jackpot! This jewelry's worth even more than the gold bars. We can trade it in for a ton of points!]

Paisley smiled. With a single thought, she swept both heavy sacks of gold into her Storage Dimension.

[Nice work, Trovie. We only found this gold because of you.]

Trovie bounced around happily in her head.

[Master's rich! Two whole sacks of gold! Can Trovie get a popsicle as a reward?]

The little white cat nuzzled her neck and let out soft, pleading mews. It kept rubbing its little head against her jaw, like it was saying it wanted a popsicle too.

Paisley laughed at how sweet and clingy it was and ruffled its fur. Then, with a single thought, she took two sweet popsicles out of the mini fridge in her Storage Dimension.

Inside the dimension, Trovie hugged its popsicle and munched away happily, swaying back and forth, perfectly content.

Just then, Trovie's voice suddenly turned dead serious.

[Master! There's another hidden basement nearby! Someone in there is about to die!]

Paisley frowned slightly and sent out her senses without thinking.

But she couldn't sense anyone alive.

The person must be so close to death that she couldn't pick them up.

Still, everyone's fate was their own.

In the apocalypse, human life was cheap, and there were dying people everywhere. She was no saint, and she'd never been one to hand out kindness to just anyone. There was no way she could save them all.

When Trovie saw she wasn't moving, it spun around in a panic and begged her.

[Master, you have to save him! He's going to be super important in the future. He can't die!]

Paisley hesitated.

She'd always known Trovie had a special gift for sniffing out and appraising treasure. But she'd never imagined it could also judge how much a person was worth and predict the future.

If Trovie was this desperate and kept insisting this guy mattered, he was definitely no random stranger.

She stopped hesitating. With Trovie giving directions, she left the gold room and went deeper down the underground passage. Soon, she found another sealed basement, even more hidden and easy to miss.

She pushed open the heavy iron door, and dim, damp air rushed out at her.

Sure enough, a young man in his twenties was curled up inside the cramped, run-down basement.

He was wearing a filthy, torn-up white lab coat. He was tall, but now he was slumped against the wall, too weak to sit up. His wrists and ankles were locked to the wall with thick, cold chains that dug deep into his skin.

He was covered in old and new wounds, and the dried blood had turned black. His clothes were in tatters, his skin was torn up, and he was barely breathing. His head hung limp, like he might not last another minute.

Chapter 451

The mood in the car was still tense.

They all knew that avoiding the obvious ambush didn't mean they were safe.

The Quinns' power ran deep, spread everywhere, and their scheming was cold and careful. They'd set their sights on the squad, and they wouldn't give up easily.

No one could say whether they'd guess the new route and set a trap in Larkspur, waiting for the squad to walk into it.

More than that, the grain depot mission had never been just an ordinary supply run.

A wager between two factions sat behind it, tied to resource shares, the bases' reputations, and future influence. It would shape both sides' standing and power at every survivor base from here on.

Win, and their faction's strength surges and they pull ahead of the rival. Lose, and resources dry up, reputation tanks, and they're stuck on the defensive for good.

The convoy left the edge of the Ridgemont ruins and turned onto an open, deserted main road.

No one had repaired the road in ages. Cracks ran everywhere, and weeds shoved through the gaps. The road that once carried streams of traffic was now bleak and empty.

Just after they left the pass, two black Jeeps appeared ahead.

They were parked steadily at the roadside, clean and well equipped, clearly an organized team of base Affinity users.

Seeing the speeding car, they showed no hostility. They slowed, pulled over, and raised a hand to flag them down, looking friendly enough.

A window rolled down. A sharp-looking young man in camouflage leaned out and smiled, speaking politely.

"Hi there, fellow team. Where are you coming from, and where are you headed?"

Then he got to the point, his eyes eager.

"By the way, do you know the way to any government grain reserves nearby? We've come all this way and can't pin down the location."

No harm in a polite word. Mason eased the car to a stop at the roadside and waited for the captain's answer.

Elijah looked calm, his eyes steady and unreadable. He answered in an even tone.

"We're just passing through, headed to Larkspur. We don't know about any grain depot. Is there one nearby?"

They all knew what was going on.

In the apocalypse, ownerless reserves went to whoever was strong and capable. Whoever found them first and controlled them took the resources for their own base.

Grain and supplies were always the main prize every faction fought over.

Don't flash your wealth, don't give away secrets. That was the basic rule of survival.

They'd already emptied the depots, so telling these people the truth was out of the question.

Sure enough, after hearing Elijah's airtight answer, the young man glanced into their car, saw no supplies, and looked clearly let down.

The warmth in his face cooled. He started to hesitate and stumble over his words, and wouldn't offer another useful detail.

He was afraid they'd follow him and grab the supplies.

"I see... Okay, thanks anyway."

He brushed them off, wrapped it up, rolled up the window, and started the car. The two Jeeps kicked up dust and tore toward another turnoff in a rush, clearly desperate to find the depots before anyone else did.

The convoy set out again, pushing toward Larkspur.

The closer they got, the denser the traffic of Affinity teams on the road.

Base plates of every kind, different uniforms, Affinity auras of every flavor, all over the place.

Some teams sped along in a rush. Some stopped at the roadside to argue over routes. Some probed each other, tension running under the surface.

Everyone had the same goal. The grain stored in the reserves.

It was enough food to sustain a large base for a whole year, with oats, flour, and oil galore.

In a world short on supplies and starving for food, it was the kind of fat prize that could make big bases tear off their masks and fight.

An hour later, the SUV drove deep into the main district of Larkspur.

Next to the ruined, scorched Ridgemont wasteland, it was a different world.

This had once been the busiest core city in the region. Skyscrapers stood in rows, shopping districts packed together, and even after the apocalypse tore it apart, it couldn't hide its former glory.

The streets were wide and orderly. Giant billboards stood faded and weathered along the road. Rows of shops lined the sides, and the buildings, grand and imposing, were barely damaged.

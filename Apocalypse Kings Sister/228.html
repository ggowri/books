Chapter 228

"Colonel Blackwood, what makes you say that? Can you find out where the person behind this is?" the commanding officer asked.

With a bolt of lightning, Elijah knocked back a mutant sea beast that was trying to climb the wall.

"Does the base have any psychics? Anyone except Chase is fine.

"Psychics have stronger senses, and they're the only ones who can pinpoint whoever's behind this."

The commanding officer thought it over. "Chase is the strongest psychic at the base.

"Every other psychic here is Level Four at most, so they probably aren't strong enough..."

Just then, a clear, cool, steady female voice suddenly rang out. "I've already found where the controller is."

Paisley slowly pulled back her psychic power, her eyes sharp as a blade, and locked onto a spot more than 1,000 yards to the left. "I can find this person."

She turned to Elijah and the core team members and spoke fast. "But I need to circle around through the reefs on the left and hit them from behind, and I need you to cover me."

As soon as she finished, the commanding officer and several high-level combat Affinity users next to him looked doubtful. They clearly disapproved and had serious concerns.

"Are you sure a girl like you can find the controller's location?"

"Don't tell me you're a psychic?"

"How did you even figure out where he is? We don't believe you, so we're obviously not going to back you up."

The commanding officer at the head of the group looked grim and warned her sternly, clearly worried.

"Young lady, look at the situation we're in. The whole base is hanging by a thread, so this is no time for jokes.

"This isn't a game, and nobody has time to play make-believe with you. One mistake, and the whole defense line will collapse!"

One after another, they spoke up, and every word was full of doubt and worry about Paisley.

In their eyes, Paisley was too young to have strong psychic power. What she'd just said was way too far-fetched, so she had to be showing off and joking around.

The mood turned tense and awkward, and most of them didn't believe Paisley.

"I believe her," Elijah said with a cold, stern face, without any hesitation. He trusted Paisley's judgment, no questions asked. Those three words rang out loud and clear, full of absolute trust.

He knew Paisley's strength and character too well. Every time they'd been backed into a corner, she'd found the key to getting out, and she'd never been wrong.

She'd never say something she wasn't sure of. He was willing to trust her strength and her judgment.

Orion stepped half a pace forward and stood firmly at Paisley's side, defending her. "Paisley never jokes around. If she says she can find them, then she can.

"You don't need to doubt her. Just give it everything you've got and follow the plan!"

With the two of them standing up for her, the others didn't dare say another word, even though they still had doubts.

Elijah ignored all the noise around him and gave the order on the spot. "From now on, I'm in command, and I'll take responsibility for whatever happens.

"Everyone, listen up! Focus all firepower and powers on the beast horde straight ahead! Give Paisley full cover!"

Orion hurried forward, looking worried. "Paisley, I'm coming with you! I can protect you."

"No need," Paisley said, shaking her head, her voice calm and clear.

"If too many of us go, they'll spot us and know we're coming. Hold the front line and keep the sea beasts from breaking off to cut me off. That's the best way you can protect me."

Orion knew how carefully she thought things through, and he knew what she could do. Even though he was uneasy, all he could do was push it down. "Paisley, be careful."

"Mm-hm." Paisley nodded slightly, her eyes clear and steady, without a trace of fear.

Without another word, she leaned down and lay flat on the little white cat's back.

The cat crouched to gather its strength, then pushed off hard and leaped into the air. Its strong, snow-white body flew over the high wall and cut through the bloody sea wind.

Under perfect cover from the roaring cannons and the flames lighting up the sky, Paisley and the cat sped off as fast as they could toward the dark reefs near the shore on the left.

The water on the left was dark and damp. Huge reefs stood in layers, slick and black from years of waves, and they were full of cracks and crevices. It was the perfect place to hide.

The bitter, salty sea wind whistled between the reefs, carrying fine, cold spray and slapping against the rocks with a loud roar. It perfectly covered the sound of the little white cat landing.

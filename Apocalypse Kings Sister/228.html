Chapter 228

"What makes you say that, Colonel? Can you actually find this person?"

Elijah struck down a mutant sea beast attempting to scale the wall with a bolt of lightning.

"Does this base have any psychic-Affinity users? Anyone but Chase Sullivan."

"Psychic users have stronger perception. They're the only ones who can locate whoever's controlling this."

The commander offered, thinking it over.

"Our strongest psychic here is Chase Sullivan."

"Everyone else tops out at Level Four. Their range probably isn't enough..."

That was when a clear, cool, steady voice cut through.

"I've already found the location."

Paisley withdrew her psychic power slowly, her gaze sharp as a blade, locking onto a point roughly a thousand yards to the left.

"I can bring them down."

She turned to Elijah and the core members of the squad, speaking fast.

"I need to loop around through the reefs on the left side and come at them from behind. I'll need you to cover me."

The moment the words left her, the commander and several high-tier combat specialists nearby showed open doubt, their faces set with disapproval and concern.

"You're telling me a girl your age can actually pinpoint the controller's location?"

"Are you saying you're a psychic Affinity user yourself?"

"How exactly did you find this location? We can't just take your word for it. We're not risking a maneuver on this."

******

The commander's expression turned stern, his tone edged with a warning.

"Young lady, with the whole base hanging by a thread right now, this is not the time for jokes.

This isn't a game, and no one has the patience to indulge you. One mistake and the entire line collapses."

One after another, they voiced the same doubt, the same concern aimed at Paisley.

To them, she was too young to have psychic power this strong. What she'd just said sounded absurd, like she was fishing for attention or making a reckless joke.

The mood turned tense, most of them clearly unconvinced.

"I believe her."

Elijah's voice cut through, flat and certain, no hesitation in it at all.

Four words, carrying the full weight of his trust.

He knew Paisley's strength and her judgment too well by now. Every time they'd faced a dead end, she'd found the exact way through, without fail.

She never spoke without being sure.

He was willing to stake everything on her judgment.

Orion stepped forward, placing himself solidly at her side.

"Paisley doesn't joke around. If she says she can find them, she can.

You don't need to question it. Just cover her and follow the plan."

Seeing the two of them stand firm, the others held their doubts and said nothing more.

Elijah ignored the murmurs around him and gave the order, voice hard.

"From this point on, I'm giving the orders. I'll take responsibility for whatever happens."

"Listen up!

Every unit, every Affinity, focus fire straight ahead on the horde!

Full cover for Paisley!"

Orion stepped forward, worry plain on his face.

"Paisley, I'm coming with you. I can protect you."

"No need."

Paisley shook her head, her voice calm and clear.

"More people means more risk of being spotted, and that defeats the point. Hold the front line and keep the beasts from circling back on me. That's the best protection you can give me."

Orion knew how carefully she thought things through, and he knew her strength. Still uneasy, he swallowed it down and spoke, voice heavy.

"Paisley, be careful out there."

"Mm-hm."

Paisley gave a small nod, her eyes clear and steady, no trace of fear in them.

She said nothing more, swinging herself onto the little white cat's back.

The cat gathered its strength and launched off the wall, its white, powerful frame clearing the top in one bound, cutting through the bloody sea air.

Using the cover of the artillery fire and bright flashes from the front line, the two of them moved at full speed, heading for the dark, deep reefs off to the left.

The water on that side sat dark and still, massive reef formations rising in jagged clusters, worn smooth and black by years of tide, riddled with gaps and crevices, perfect cover for anyone hiding there.

The salty, bitter wind howled through the reefs, carrying fine, cold sea spray, slapping against the rocks with a steady, crashing rhythm that perfectly masked the sound of the cat's light landing.

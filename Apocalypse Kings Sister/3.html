Chapter 3

But before his hand could land, Paisley shifted lightly to the side. In one swift motion, she caught his wrist and twisted it hard.

A faint crack sounded, followed by Reid's sharp gasp of pain. His arm was locked in place, sweat beading on his forehead as he was forced to bend forward, nearly losing his balance.

The crowd fell silent.

All the murmurs and accusations died instantly. Everyone stared, shocked, no one daring to speak now.

First Ryan, sent flying with a single kick. Now Reid, subdued with one hand.

The quiet, compliant girl they thought they knew was gone—replaced by someone fearless, someone who refused to be controlled.

Paisley stared down at Reid, who was pinned under her control. Her gaze was icy, devoid of any warmth.

"You really can't stand me, can you? Well, don't worry—you won't have to kick me out.

"The moment you took her side and tried to steal my pendant, you stopped being family to me."

With that, she released him and gave a sharp shove.

Reid stumbled backward, nearly losing his footing before catching himself against the wall. He looked utterly pathetic.

Paisley raised her eyes and swept a cold glance over the four members of the Lennox family. Her voice rang clear through the living room.

"From today on, I, Paisley, am done with the Lennox family—and every single one of you."

Rachel's fake wounded expression froze, panic flickering deeper in her eyes. Her worst fear was coming true: Paisley was slipping out of her control. No longer weak, no longer easy to manipulate.

I have to get that pendant today. It's my ticket to surviving the apocalypse. It belongs to me.

If Paisley discovered its secret and started using the space to stockpile supplies, it would all be over for her.

Driven by growing obsession, Rachel dropped her sweet, fragile act entirely. She lunged forward, hand shooting out to snatch the pendant from Paisley's neck, her eyes blazing with unmistakable greed.

Paisley had been watching her closely.

The instant Rachel rushed at her, Paisley lifted her foot and delivered a swift, precise kick to Rachel's calf.

It wasn't overly hard, but it was firm and ruthless.

"Crack."

Rachel cried out and crumpled to the floor in pain.

Seeing this, the three Lennox brothers snapped into action, moving to shield Rachel.

Ryan pushed through his own pain and struggled to his feet, his face dark with anger.

"Paisley, how dare you hurt Rachel like that? She just wants the pendant—hand it over now! Someone as kind-hearted as her deserves it more than you ever could."

Owen's voice was icy as he added pressure. "You think you can hurt Rachel and get away with it? Don't make this harder than it has to be. Give us the pendant now, and we'll let today slide."

The three of them stood united, openly siding with Rachel and demanding the pendant.

The small crowd that had gathered fell completely silent. Any earlier whispers died away—everyone could now see the truth behind the drama.

This wasn't about Paisley being ungrateful. It was about Rachel's greed and the Lennox brothers throwing their weight around to back her up, no matter what.

Paisley watched Rachel sobbing on the floor, her own eyes cold and unfeeling. A chill ran through her—how had she never seen their true colors before?

I was fooled for three years... and it cost me my life.

Now that she was back, she wouldn't rest until she'd dragged the whole Lennox family down with her.

She looked down at them, her voice low and final.

"You're all really showing your true colors today. Since you can't stand the sight of me... consider this me saving you the trouble."

Paisley raised her head, her clear voice carrying through the hall once more.

"Let everyone here be my witness. Starting today, I, Paisley, am cutting all ties with the Lennox family. My life, my death, my choices—none of it has anything to do with any of you from now on."

Before anyone could respond, she turned and pushed through the front door, stepping out into the dim evening.

A heavy silence settled over the room. People exchanged uneasy glances.

No one had expected Paisley—once so quiet and dependent on the Lennox family—to have the guts to burn her bridges like this. To walk away from her three powerful adoptive brothers at the brink of the apocalypse...

Without their protection, in a world overrun by zombies and something far worse—human nature—her chances looked slim.

And with looks like hers, she'd have a target on her back the moment she was on her own.

Rachel stayed where she was, watching Paisley disappear from view. All pretense of innocence melted from her eyes, replaced by pure venom and bitter frustration.

She could barely contain the urge to run after Paisley, to end her right then and take what was hers.

But the apocalypse had barely begun, and the place was still packed. She couldn't make a move openly, so she forced down the gnawing greed and the urge to kill rising in her chest.

Right on cue, she slipped back into that fragile, self-blaming act. Her eyes reddened, and her voice wavered.

"Owen, Reid, Ryan... it's almost dark. The streets are crawling with zombies. It's too dangerous for her to be out there alone.

"This is all my fault. If it weren't for me, she wouldn't have stormed off like that.

"Maybe... maybe we should go find her and bring her back?"

Reid snorted, his tone dripping with certainty.

"You're too soft-hearted. After what she did to you, how can you still worry about her?

"Don't sweat it. I'll get that pendant for you, no question.

"It's getting dark—no way Paisley actually stays out there. I give her five minutes tops before she comes crawling back, begging for forgiveness."

Ryan winced slightly, a hand pressed to his still-aching chest. A mocking smirk spread across his face.

"With that spineless attitude of hers? If she actually has the guts not to come back, I'll be impressed. But let's be real—half an hour from now, she'll be on her knees handing that pendant over with both hands."

Only Owen stayed quiet. His eyes darkened as he stared at the still-open door, silent and unreadable.

Everyone in the lobby paused, glancing at their watches almost on reflex. They settled in, waiting like they were watching a show unfold.

Nobody believed Paisley would last five minutes.

One minute passed. No sign of her.

Two minutes. Outside, aside from the distant, garbled groans of zombies, the street remained empty.

Three minutes. The mood in the room began to shift. The earlier confidence faded, replaced by a low, uneasy tension.

Four minutes. Reid's smirk stiffened, his confidence visibly cracking.

Thirty minutes later, she still hadn't returned.

Paisley was really gone.

She'd actually walked out—and hadn't looked back.

The lobby fell dead quiet. All eyes turned toward the three Lennox brothers, whose faces had gone stone-cold under the silent, heavy weight of everyone's stares. There was no mistaking the secondhand embarrassment and quiet ridicule hanging in the air.

The smirk had long vanished from Ryan's face, replaced by a flush of humiliation and disbelief. His expression shifted between pale and flushed, ugly and strained.

Reid stood frozen, his earlier certainty completely shattered. He couldn't wrap his head around it—the same Paisley they'd pushed around for years, the one who never fought back, had actually left for good.

Beside them, Rachel's fingers clenched tightly around the fabric of her skirt, her nails digging into her palms. Panic and jealousy churned violently inside her.

She left. Now how was she supposed to get the pendant back?

This wasn't how it was supposed to go.

Paisley was meant to be weak, obedient, always under their control. That pendant belonged to her.

But now...

Paisley had walked away without a second glance, cold and done with all of them.

*****

Out on the street, Paisley pushed all of them from her mind. She needed to get stronger, and fast. Payback would come later.

Right now, the streets were swarming with zombies. She had to find somewhere safe to hole up for the night and figure out how to activate the pendant's hidden Storage Dimension.

The apocalypse had only just begun. Stocking up supplies was her top priority.

The longer this went on, the scarcer food would get—and the more valuable it would become.

She stepped out of the hotel lobby for the last time. Behind her, the heavy metal door clanged shut, cutting off all the fake concern and the noise for good.

It was only the third day since the outbreak, but the city already looked nothing like its former self. Everything was in ruins.

The streets lay dead and silent, covered in a thick blanket of dried leaves and dust. Here and there, zombies dragged their stiff, shuffling bodies along the sidewalks, their low, raspy moans carrying in the cold evening wind. The sound felt hollow, lonely—the kind of chill that seeped deep into your bones.

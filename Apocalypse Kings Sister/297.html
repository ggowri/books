Chapter 297

"Thomas, Spencer, you're both doing great."

Orion and Elijah opened their mouths, then said nothing.

Breakfast was a full spread — fluffy white buns, crisp golden fried dough sticks, and a steaming bowl of oatmeal, easy on the stomach.

Everyone took their seats and dug in.

Elijah finished his last bite calmly, looked up at the group, and laid out the plan in a steady, clear voice.

"We leave right after breakfast. Head straight for Stillwater Base, no unnecessary stops."

Everyone nodded, energized, ready to move at a moment's notice.

A little while later, Paisley held the docile little fox in her arms, pinching off bits of a steamed bun and feeding it patiently.

The fox nestled against her, nibbling happily, its red fur soft and fluffy, the picture of contentment.

The group packed up their supplies and headed toward the vehicles waiting outside, ready to set off.

Just then, a burst of chaotic, weak footsteps rustled through the weeds outside the villa, paired with heavy, labored breathing.

Everyone froze and turned toward the sound.

The moment they saw who it was, surprise flickered across every face.

Walking toward them was Rachel's group.

Rachel looked nothing like her usual polished, composed self, her state utterly wretched.

Every one of them was covered in dust and grime, hair disheveled, faces haggard, eyes bloodshot and heavy, legs stiff and swollen, each step unsteady and shaky.

Clearly they'd walked through the entire night without rest, bodies completely spent.

Paisley raised an eyebrow.

No way.

The world really was this small.

What a coincidence.

She'd torn up their tires on a whim yesterday, and now she couldn't help wondering if they'd actually been foolish enough to walk the entire night to get here.

Just the thought of it was enough to make her ridiculously happy.

Chase spotted Paisley immediately, his exhausted face lighting up at once, as if he'd found salvation in the middle of disaster, and hurried over.

He pushed through on wobbly legs, gasping for breath, his voice carrying the relief of a man who'd survived something terrible, though his tone stayed entitled.

"Finally, living people! We nearly died out there on the road!"

He looked straight at the group, clearly ready to depart, his gaze landing on the pristine SUVs, and asked outright for help, with zero shame.

"You're headed out, aren't you?

Must be Stillwater Base, right?

Give us a ride, would you? Take us along!"

He said it as if it were obvious they owed him, conveniently forgetting that he'd once abandoned his post as a coward back at the sea research lab.

Rachel dragged her exhausted body forward too, her expression carefully arranged into fake fragility, her voice soft and pitiful.

"Paisley, we really can't walk anymore. The whole road's crawling with zombies, every step dangerous. We barely made it this far."

"For old times' sake, can't you just give us a lift? I really can't go on, and we go back a long way. You can't just leave us to suffer."

The rest of their group chimed in too, faces drawn with exhaustion and false pity, each one pleading in turn, though their tone stayed entitled underneath it all.

"Yeah, it's not even out of your way."

"We're all survivors here, we should help each other out. You can't just watch us die out here, can you?"

Listening to their twisted, shameless pleading, Ashley's brows shot up, visibly annoyed, and she stepped forward to push back.

"Who even are you people? I don't know you. Why should we help you?"

"You already walked all night, didn't you? Clearly you can still walk. Might as well keep going together to the base."

The girl's words landed sharp and clear, striking right at the heart of it, leaving the whole group stunned into silence.

Chase's expression darkened slightly. Swallowing his embarrassment, he forced out a stubborn excuse.

"Last night was a special situation, we didn't have a choice.

Out here, running into each other at all is practically fate. You've got two or three cars, give us one.

We're all headed to the same place, Stillwater Base. Just give us a lift. No need to be so stingy about it."

"Stingy?"

Paisley finally spoke, her voice cool and light, carrying a cold edge sharp enough to cut through the noise instantly.

She stood tall in the morning light, the docile fox cradled in her arms, her expression distant and composed as she swept a cool glance over the pathetic, self-serving group in front of her.

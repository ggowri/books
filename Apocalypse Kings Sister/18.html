Chapter 18

Paisley could tell right away that this was the mall's main storeroom. A lot of the mall had been trashed, but this place had been well protected.

The left side was piled high with pantry staples. Vacuum-sealed bags of oats and flour sat in neat stacks next to cases of peanut, corn, and olive oil. There were also bulk bags of salt, white sugar, and brown sugar, plus every spice and seasoning imaginable.

The right side was split into two big sections. One was full of brand-new household goods, from tissues, toilet paper, and wet wipes to toiletry kits, laundry detergent, soap, trash bags, and disposables. Every box was still sealed.

The other section held seasonal clothes in every size and style, including windbreakers, warm hoodies, light down jackets, tough work pants, and comfy loungewear. There was also plenty of new underwear, warm shoes, and socks. In the apocalypse, clothes like these were as practical as it got, and hard to come by.

Later in the apocalypse, stuff like this would be worth more than gold, and there was enough here to last everyone a long time.

With a single thought, Paisley made it all disappear, from the food and spices to the household goods, clothes, shoes, and socks. Everything landed in her dimension and sorted itself into place.

She'd just finished when Trovie let out another ear-splitting squeal.

[Master, there's a ton of gold in here too! Seriously, so much gold! Go farther in, quick!]

Paisley's eyes lit up, and she headed straight for the very back of the room.

A massive combination safe stood against the far wall, 33 feet tall and made of thick alloy. It was the most secure spot in the whole storeroom.

Without a moment's hesitation, Paisley pulled out her pistol, aimed at the lock, and pulled the trigger.

The gunshot boomed through the room, and the lock blew apart.

She grabbed the heavy door and pulled, and it slowly swung open.

The safe was packed with row after row of solid gold bars, stacked perfectly and gleaming. There were so many that it was impossible to guess what they were worth.

On top of the gold bars were three smooth, old-fashioned pendants. Their size, color, and texture were almost identical to the Storage Dimension pendant around her neck, so they could easily pass for the real thing.

A sly smile crossed Paisley's face.

She could use one as a decoy to fool Rachel. It would throw her off the scent and hide the fact that Paisley already knew the real pendant's secret.

Trovie was so excited it could barely talk straight.

[We hit the jackpot, Master! You're gonna be loaded!

[Put this gold on the Trading Plaza, and it'll turn into a mountain of points! A few more hauls like this, and you'll be a Level Two trader in no time!]

Paisley waved her hand, and the giant safe vanished into her dimension, gold bars, fake pendants, and all.

Then, she picked out the pendant that matched hers best and hung it around her neck.

With one thought, the real Storage Dimension pendant changed shape. It turned into a smooth, clear bracelet that fit snugly around her wrist.

With that done, she hurried back to Orion. "Okay, we can go now."

Orion nodded and opened the storeroom door. They headed out together, planning to go back to the first floor and meet up with the others.

But they'd only taken a few steps when a shrill scream came from somewhere above them and ripped through the silent mall. It was a scream of pure panic, and it made their stomachs drop.

Orion stopped in his tracks, instantly alert.

"That's not the second floor," Paisley said. "It's coming from the third."

In her past life, she'd heard every kind of scream for help there was, and she could pinpoint where a sound came from.

Orion frowned, and after a moment, his head won out over his heart. "Let's head back down and regroup with the others first," he said. "It's not worth the risk."

In the apocalypse, people could be more dangerous than zombies, and there were unknown threats around every corner. Rushing in to save a stranger would only put them in danger.

Paisley nodded. "Yeah. There's no point playing the hero in the apocalypse."

What had happened to her in her past life was proof enough.

If even her own family had betrayed her, why would she trust a stranger?

Without another word, they turned to head back the way they'd come.

But they'd only gone a few steps when a woman came stumbling down the stairs from the floor above.

She was wearing a mall employee uniform. Her hair was a mess, her face was streaked with tears, and her clothes were torn and covered in dust. Her legs gave out, and she dropped to her knees in front of them.

"Help me... please... save me..." She sounded hoarse and terrified, and she kept glancing back over her shoulder.

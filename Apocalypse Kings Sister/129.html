Chapter 129

Marcus kept his eyes on Paisley, dead serious. "I'll protect you too!"

Paisley's eyes welled up. "Just look out for yourselves. We're all going to be okay."

For the family who loved her, if it really came down to the wire, she'd have no choice but to show her hand.

As for everyone else...

She glanced at the three Lennox brothers. They had nothing to do with her anymore.

Who knew what they were cooking up with Rachel this time?

The ship was about to sink, and the horde was ready to tear them apart. Death was waiting no matter which way they turned, and there was no way out.

Then, at the very last second, the sharp, steady roar of engines rang out from the misty water in the distance. It cut through the dead silence and the crashing waves, loud and urgent!

Five armed patrol boats sped through the waves at full throttle. They burst through the mist and raced toward the ship, the Rivergate Patrol's insignia clearly printed on their sides.

The officers on board were well armed and well trained. They looked serious, and they moved fast.

They got into position in no time. High-level affinities and heavy weapons fired at once, laying down a dense wall of firepower that slammed right into the horde of sea beasts!

Explosions tore through the water, and the river boiled and churned. One after another, the sea beasts near the ship were blown apart, driven back, or sent sinking to the bottom.

The fierce, nonstop attack blasted a wide path through the horde and forced the army of sea beasts to back off.

The Rivergate Patrol had shown up just in time and saved everyone from certain death.

It wasn't until everyone was standing safely on shore that they could finally breathe again.

As the team's leader, Elijah walked up to the patrol's commanding officer and held out his hand.

"Thank you. Seriously, thank you for showing up when you did. If it weren't for you, our whole team would be at the bottom of that river right now."

The patrol leader wore a crisp military uniform. He stood ramrod straight, with sharp eyes and a stern face, every bit the careful, steady soldier.

He shook Elijah's hand briefly, his grip firm but controlled, and kept things strictly professional. "Just doing our job. No need to thank us."

After he let go, the commander looked the group over one by one, sizing each of them up.

"So, what brings you all the way to Rivergate? Ever since the apocalypse started, these waterways have been crawling with mutant beasts. Almost no outsiders dare to come out here anymore.

"Or should I say, hardly anyone makes it to Rivergate alive."

His eyes narrowed, and his voice turned suspicious.

"You all look calm, and you held out until we got there, so you're clearly powerful ability users. Don't tell me you came all the way to Rivergate for a little sightseeing.

"So tell me the truth. What are you really doing here?"

Elijah's face turned serious as he took his ID out of an inside pocket and handed it over.

"Elijah Blackwood, Colonel of Aetheris's Fourth Regiment," he said firmly.

"I need to see whoever's in charge of Rivergate. I have something top secret to report in person."

The commander's face tensed, and he quickly took the ID and checked it carefully.

The embossed seal was crisp, and the serial number was in the proper format. Every detail proved the ID was real.

A colonel this young, from a regiment that reports straight to Aetheris, here on classified business? This guy is no joke.

All his suspicion vanished, and he turned careful and respectful. He gave Elijah a serious nod.

"Please wait here a moment. I'll report this to my superiors right away."

With that, he quickly assigned two of his officers to stay behind and look after the group. Then, he left with Elijah for headquarters to brief the higher-ups.

Patrol officers led the rest of Paisley's group to a lounge inside the Rivergate base so they could rest.

Everyone sat down and grabbed the cups of hot coffee on the table.

From what Paisley had seen on the way in, Rivergate was nothing like the other chaotic, run-down places.

Everything here was orderly, and the streets were neat. There were no refugees rioting and no killing or looting in the streets. High walls and guard posts were everywhere, and soldiers patrolled in shifts day and night.

The military was clearly taking good care of this city. It still felt as safe as it had before the apocalypse.

Chapter 313

"No."

Two words, light and flat, but absolute.

"This is on you. Are you seriously going to tell me you didn't walk into her on purpose?"

"Everyone can see what you were trying to pull. Nobody here's stupid."

"Your situation isn't mine to fix. You put yourself here."

With that, she didn't spare him another look. She stepped past him and turned to go.

Reid's whole body went slack, like the air had been knocked out of him, his legs nearly giving way.

Watching her walk away without a backward glance, the last spark of hope in his eyes died out, leaving nothing but regret.

The crowd's whispers stuck to him like needles, humiliation burning through him.

Paisley shook off the whole encounter, though the weight of it still sat heavy in her chest, killing any desire to keep shopping.

She turned to Ashley and the others, her voice even.

"Go ahead without me. I'm going to walk for a bit."

Ashley, picking up on her mood, didn't press. She just nodded.

"Okay. Call me if you need anything, I'll come running."

"Mm."

Paisley nodded and turned, walking alone down the wide, orderly street.

The base buzzed around her, alive and busy, but the irritation sitting in her chest didn't ease up at all.

She let the signs guide her, eventually ending up at the base's mission hall.

The hall was packed, Affinity users and ordinary survivors alike moving through, picking up tasks, cashing in points. Loud, but orderly.

Paisley scanned the board posted along the wall, her eyes skimming over row after row of listings until one caught her attention.

[Level Seven High-Risk Mission: Successfully treat the base's critically ill soldiers]

[Reward: 30,000 contribution points]

She studied the brief, and something sharpened behind her eyes.

According to the listing, this group of soldiers had all collapsed after a mission, poisoned by something no one could identify. The base's entire medical staff was stumped — the symptoms didn't match any known undead toxin or mutant venom.

A theory formed instantly. She needed to confirm whether this toxin matched the one from the lab under the aquarium Elijah had investigated before.

If the toxins matched, it would confirm beyond doubt that the two facilities were connected — run by the same dark organization — and bring them that much closer to understanding what had really caused the apocalypse.

And thirty thousand points was a generous reward on top of it.

Without hesitating, Paisley walked straight to the mission desk.

"I'll take this Level Seven detox mission."

The clerk looked up and gave her a once-over.

The girl in front of him was strikingly beautiful, slender, young — nothing about her looked like someone capable of curing a Level Seven mystery toxin and saving critically ill soldiers.

Skepticism flickered across his face as he spoke up, cautious.

"You sure about this? This is a Level Seven high-risk mission. Dozens of elite soldiers' lives are on the line. This isn't something to take lightly."

"Our best doctors are stumped. You're young — do you really think you can pull this off?"

"If you take this and can't deliver, you could be held legally responsible for delaying treatment."

Paisley's expression stayed calm, her voice steady, no hesitation in it at all.

"Thanks for the warning. I know what I'm doing."

"Just register me."

Seeing how composed and certain she was — nothing like reckless overconfidence — the clerk didn't push further. He processed the registration and set up her access.

Once it was done, he waved over a medical escort.

"Take her to the base's critical care hospital."

"Yes, sir."

The escort stepped forward, polite.

"This way, please."

Paisley nodded and followed, moving through a dedicated corridor behind the mission hall, straight toward the base's highest-tier hospital.

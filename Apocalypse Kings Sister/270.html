Chapter 270

Meanwhile Ashley and Orion each wore a storage ring, small and unassuming, yet capable of holding everything they'd found.

Why does everyone else have one of those, and not me?

Envy, jealousy, and self-pity knotted together in her chest, bitter enough to taste.

I want one of those so badly.

If I had one, I'd never have to worry about running out of anything again.

Caught up in it, Kelly's eyes drifted sideways, landing on Madison's slender fingers.

Her pupils shrank.

Madison had a storage ring too, a colorful one.

Kelly's resentment spiked.

The others, fine. But Madison? What gives her the right?

Madison came from a third-rate family in Aetheris, unremarkable, her family's business propped up by favors from others, including Kelly's own.

Back in school, Kelly had looked down on her, never thought she was worth being friends with.

Being kind to her had always been performance, a way to look generous by comparison.

Now, after everything had fallen apart, the girl she'd once looked down on was doing better than her, liked by everyone in this group.

And now she's got a storage ring too.

The gap between them filled her with something close to hatred, her nails biting into her palms.

No. This isn't fair.

That ring should be mine.

I'll find a way. I'll take it from her eventually.

As they climbed into the car, Orion noticed Elijah's pale face, concern mixing with confusion.

"What happened to the captain? Is he hurt?"

Paisley kept it simple.

"Nothing to worry about. Small fever. He'll be fine after some rest."

She left out the details.

"We've rested enough. Let's move. We'll find somewhere to stop for lunch."

No one argued, and they all climbed in.

Paisley started the car, and they pulled out onto the empty road outside the city.

Ten minutes later, they reached a quiet, hidden farmhouse.

Far from the chaos and zombies of the city, it had survived untouched, clean and orderly.

A few intact greenhouses stood outside, bursting with green, ripe seasonal produce inside.

Everywhere else, the world had rotted into silence and blood.

Here, somehow, the vegetables thrived untouched, leaves full and green, not a trace of decay.

A clear pond sat beside the house, fish and shrimp visible beneath the rippling surface.

"We'll stop here."

Paisley said.

Everyone's eyes lit up, no objections.

At the sight of fresh produce and live fish, the day's exhaustion melted away.

A chance to eat well and relax, rare enough in this world to feel like a gift.

Days of fighting had worn everyone down.

Looking at the garden and the pond now, most of that exhaustion lifted, replaced by something closer to peace.

Paisley got out first and carried Elijah, still feverish and half-conscious, straight into the house, settling him onto the living room couch and tucking a blanket around him.

The others exchanged glances behind her, and fell silent for a good five seconds.

Everyone knew Elijah, cool and distant, never let anyone get this close, not even asleep. With Paisley, he was almost docile.

Something between the two of them felt different now. Without a word, everyone looked away and carried on like they hadn't noticed a thing.

Only Kelly stood apart, hands clenched at her sides, bitterness rising in her chest all over again.

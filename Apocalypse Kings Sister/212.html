Chapter 212

"Paisley, you had this thing the whole time? Why didn't you use it?"

Paisley gripped the cold metal of the gun, raised it toward the thrashing mutant, and answered without much interest at all:

"Nothing we ran into before was worth wasting the charge on."

Orion: "..."

Elijah: "..."

Everyone else in that tunnel was fighting for their lives. Paisley looked like she was waiting for a bus. It was, frankly, unsettling how strong she was.

After a beat of silence, Elijah shook off the shock and got his head back in the fight.

"Pais, go ahead. He won't last much longer."

The humanoid mutant was still shrieking, the sound wave grinding everyone down, its tendrils cinching tighter around the poisoned man. His face had gone a deeper shade of blue, his convulsions wilder, his breath nearly gone.

Paisley's gaze sharpened. All the idle calm drained out of it.

She lifted her arm, aimed, and squeezed the trigger.

A beam of pure, brilliant blue tore through the air, cutting straight through the chaos of sound waves, driving right at the mutant's skull.

******

Sensing the threat at the last second, the creature's hollow eyes flashed with sudden fury, its shriek cutting off mid-breath.

Its mutated body moved with unnatural speed. It threw up one long, venom-slick arm, trying to block the beam barehanded, as if it could stop a shot strong enough to kill a high-tier beast.

The beam detonated on contact.

There was no cushioning it. Hardened flesh, bone, and poisoned sinew meant nothing against that kind of destructive force.

A sharp, wet crack rang out as the creature's entire arm was obliterated, flesh scattering into the air and dissolving into nothing.

Whatever defenses it was proud of meant nothing in front of that gun.

Agony rolled through its body. It staggered back several feet, the sound-wave attack cutting off, the pressure crushing the tunnel suddenly gone.

The pain in everyone's skulls vanished. The weakness in their limbs faded. They sucked in ragged breaths, staring at what had just happened with eyes wide from shock.

After a stunned pause, someone snapped out of it and shouted, teeth gritted:

"Keep firing! Kill it! Don't give it a chance to recover, go!"

Every eye locked onto the blue-lit gun in Paisley's hand, thick with envy, hunger, and awe.

No one had guessed something that small could hit that hard — one shot, and it had taken off an entire arm from a high-tier mutant.

If only they'd known. When Elijah had mentioned these in the group chat before, they should have bought in instead of balking at the price.

Could they still get their hands on one?

If they made it out alive, they were definitely asking again.

Just then, something horrifying began to happen.

The ragged wound at the creature's shoulder started writhing, dark-teal flesh crawling and multiplying. Broken bone, torn skin, poisoned sinew knitted back together in seconds, visible to the naked eye.

Within moments, a whole new arm had formed, skin still that same sickly blue-black, fingers tipped with venom, as good as new.

The tunnel went dead silent. Everyone felt the blood drain from their faces.

That kind of regeneration. An entire severed limb, rebuilt in seconds.

They went pale, trembling, unable to stop shaking.

The thing already had a brutal sonic attack. Add regeneration like that, and could this monster even be killed?

Despair crashed over them all over again.

Only Paisley stayed unmoved, calm in a way that bordered on frightening.

She raised the gun again, firing one shot after another, each beam punching clean through the creature's torso, chest, shoulders, tendrils — every vital point it had.

Every wound rotted and vanished in an instant, and every time, the flesh grew back, healed over.

Except the healing was getting slower each time.

Paisley caught the pattern. It wasn't infinite regeneration after all. If she just kept—

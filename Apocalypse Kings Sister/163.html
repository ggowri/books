Chapter 163

Why is it this clean?

There's nothing left at all?

They all turned to look at Paisley, suspicious.

Something felt off.

******

"Paisley, we've been looking for you!"

Ashley ran up, sticking close, glancing around curiously.

"The third and fourth floors are spotless — what did this place even sell?"

Thomas looked around the empty, polished counters, brow raised.

"Not a trace of stock anywhere. You think someone already cleared this place out?"

None of them guessed the truth — that Paisley alone had emptied the whole treasure vault, minutes ago.

Paisley felt a flicker of guilt and rubbed her nose, not bothering to explain.

Spencer didn't linger on the oddity. He stepped forward, looking at her with open admiration.

"Paisley, you okay?

We cleared the strays outside and swung by the basement — the Level Five wolf-man was already dead. That was you, wasn't it?"

Paisley nodded, easy.

"Yeah. I got it."

"Paisley's amazing!

That wolf-man was brutal, been here forever, and you took it down that fast. First mission, done, just like that!"

Spencer's admiration was genuine.

He added, "I already stuffed the body in my storage token. We can turn it in at the base for the reward."

Paisley blinked, remembering she still had to actually report the mission for points.

"Good thing you thought of it."

Thomas's eyes lit up, glancing toward the fifth floor.

"I remember the map — fifth floor's the food court and movie theater. Worth a look, might find something to eat."

"It's about noon anyway. I'm starving."

Everyone agreed instantly, the mission's fatigue forgotten, replaced with anticipation.

"Let's go check it out!"

The group headed up the stairs to the fifth floor, Kitty padding along at their heels.

The fifth floor hit them with a wave of something almost cozy, a sharp contrast to the cold brutality downstairs.

One side held a full row of food stalls — a milkshake stand, a coffee shop, a kettle corn stand, a hot dog roller, a dessert counter — all of it clean, equipment intact, no rust.

The ingredients behind the counters — syrups, milk powder, coffee beans, pre-cooked meats — all sealed and still usable.

The other side was a full arcade — claw machines, dance games, basketball shooters, racing games, screens intact, everything working.

A small theater and lounge seating sat in the corner, a leftover slice of peace.

Everyone's eyes lit up at the sight of the food and games.

"There's even a milkshake machine!"

Ashley bolted toward the stand, delighted.

"The mix is all still here! We can make our own!"

Everyone got into it fast.

Spencer cleaned off the hot dog roller and loaded it with sealed sausages, the machine warming up with a sizzle, the smell of grilled meat spreading through the floor.

Thomas fiddled with the kettle corn stand, pouring in kernels and butter syrup. Kettle corn burst out moments later, golden and fragrant.

Ashley dragged Paisley over to help mix milkshakes and brew coffee.

Warm milk and black tea, a splash of syrup, tapioca pearls and jelly bits — smooth, sweet milkshakes coming together one after another. Fresh-ground coffee beans brewed into something rich and dark.

They gathered around the stall, trading off tasks, laughing, snacking on crisp kettle corn, greasy hot dogs, sweet milkshakes, and strong coffee — a warm little moment that pushed back against everything the apocalypse had thrown at them.

After eating, the group ran off to the arcade, hitting the dance machines and claw games, laughing like they hadn't laughed in ages, their voices filling the whole floor.

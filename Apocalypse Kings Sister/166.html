Chapter 166

Reid and Ryan shot Rachel looks of frustration and disgust.

"Last time it was us she threw under the bus. Now it's Owen.

Rachel, how selfish can you get?

Do we mean anything to you at all?"

"If Owen hadn't dodged in time, he'd be dead right now!"

Kelly, still shaken, glared at her own bruised and bloodied arms, voice sharp with blame.

"I knew teaming up with you people would end badly. I never should've agreed to this.

Almost got myself killed for nothing."

The car filled with accusations, everyone talking over each other, the fragile alliance crumbling fast.

Watching the anger build, Rachel let her face crumple, tears spilling down pale cheeks right on cue, voice breaking.

She played small, helpless, looking between her three angry brothers and Kelly's disgust, shaking her head, voice trembling.

"I'm sorry, I'm sorry, this is all my fault, I really didn't mean it!

When that thing lunged at us I just froze, my mind went blank, it was pure instinct — I'd never hurt you on purpose, I'd never put anyone's life at risk!"

She wiped at her tears, shoulders trembling, playing every bit the victim.

"You all know how hard it is to survive right now, how much we need each other. Why would I ever sabotage that?

What would I even gain?

I was terrified too. My hands are still shaking."

She turned to Owen, voice softening further, eyes full of manufactured remorse, apologizing through tears, word by word.

"Owen, I know I messed up. I panicked and put you in danger, I almost got you hurt.

Be angry, yell at me, I won't argue.

But we're family. I never once wanted to hurt you. Please, just this once, forgive me for panicking."

Then, as if the weight of it finally broke her composure, she added, voice thick.

"Every time something went wrong before, I always said it wasn't Paisley's fault — that it was an accident, my own clumsiness.

But you three always decided it was her fault anyway... so why is this suddenly different?"

That one line, meant to sound like weakness, hit the three brothers like a stone dropped into still water.

All three of them froze, something cold opening up in their chests as old memories surfaced.

Every time Rachel and Paisley clashed, Rachel always wore that same look — teary, wounded, barely holding back tears.

She never once directly blamed Paisley. Just vague, soft deflections, always the same line.

It's not her fault. She's just clumsy.

Back then, all they ever saw was the glimmer of tears in Rachel's eyes, the fragile act — and they assumed, without question, that Paisley was the aggressive, cruel one.

And Rachel was the one who suffered, too kind-hearted to expose her sister's wrongdoing.

Every time, they'd turned on Paisley without a second thought — called her petty, ungrateful, heartless toward her own sister.

But now, thinking back over every detail.

What if — what if they'd had it backwards from the start?

What if every conflict, every rift, had never been Paisley pushing too hard — and had always been Rachel quietly manipulating things from the shadows?

All those years, Paisley had silently absorbed their misunderstanding, their harsh judgment, their cold shoulders, carrying blame that was never hers to carry — alone.

The real cause of it all had never been the quiet, withdrawn Paisley.

It had been this fragile, pitiful-looking girl in front of them.

The scale of it, the guilt, crashed down all at once, gripping their chests, stealing the air from their lungs.

Chapter 214

The only hope left in front of him was the girl standing there.

He used the last scrap of strength in his body to lift a finger, forcing out a broken, barely-there whisper, pure survival instinct:

"I... agree... please... save me..."

The moment the words left him, he went completely slack, head lolling, eyes half-shut, slipping under, just a thread of breath left in him.

No one hesitated after that. They snapped into motion, carefully lifting him and carrying him to a clean, level patch of ground, laying him down gently.

"Hurry! Save him, please!"

"We believe in you! Please, save him!"

The others stepped back, clearing space, eyes locked on Paisley, hearts caught somewhere between dread and hope.

Everyone held their breath, afraid to make a sound, afraid to throw off whatever she was about to do.

Elijah and Orion took up position facing outward, backs to the treatment area, keeping watch over the dark tunnel without needing to say a word to each other.

They sealed off every outside distraction, giving Paisley total quiet to work in.

Paisley wasted no more time. She dropped to a crouch.

She lifted her pale, slender fingers and pressed them lightly to the man's burning, blackened forehead.

A thread of warm, pure green energy flowed from her fingertips, gentle but piercing, sinking in through the point between his brows and racing through his body.

Most healing Affinities could only patch wounds, close cuts. Paisley's was different. Hers carried the pure essence of Plant Affinity, built to cleanse contamination and poison and repair damaged organs from the inside.

The moment the green energy entered him, the cold black poison raging through his system reacted like it had met its natural enemy. The dark current eating through his organs began to dissolve on the spot.

Everyone watched, plain as day, as the deep blue-black bruising on his face slowly faded, lightening by the second.

Paisley's expression stayed calm, the energy at her fingertips flowing steady, gentle yet absolute as it moved through his channels, clearing the scattered poison piece by piece.

The convulsions wracking his body eased. His rigid limbs relaxed. His shallow, ragged breathing slowed into something long and even, and the life that had nearly slipped away was pulled steadily back.

Three minutes later, Paisley withdrew her hand. The last thread of pure energy cleared away the remaining poison.

Wisps of black-gray toxic mist rose off his skin, dissolving instantly into a foul-smelling haze before vanishing completely.

The moment the last of the poison cleared, the man drew in a deep breath. His eyes opened, the dull gray of near-death gone from them, color returning to his face. Not a trace of poison remained.

The toxin had been completely cleansed.

The teammates who'd watched the whole thing stood frozen, shock rolling through them in waves that refused to settle.

"How is that even possible... that's high-tier mutant venom! There's no known cure for it. She actually did it."

"No, seriously, even the base's top healer could only hold it back, never clear it out completely. She wiped it out in minutes!"

"She's a high-tier Purifier!"

"This is unbelievable... how much more does she have hidden away?"

"Is she really the mystery healer everyone keeps talking about?"

...

The man who'd been pulled back from death pushed himself upright, body light and free of pain for the first time in what felt like forever. His eyes went red. He stood, bowed his head low toward Paisley, and said, voice breaking:

"Ms. Lennox, thank you. Thank you so much. You didn't just save my life, you saved my whole family. I will never forget this."

The rest of the team echoed him, voices thick with awe and genuine gratitude:

"Thank you, thank you for saving him, for saving all of us!"

Elijah and Orion's gazes landed together on the cool, gentle figure in front of them, the shock in their eyes deepening into pure, helpless admiration.

Where others saw a dead end, she walked through it without effort. Where no healer could break a poison, she cleared it like it was nothing.

Paisley Lennox always found a way to pull a miracle out of the worst situation.

Paisley glanced at the comm device on her wrist. Already one in the afternoon. Time had flown.

"It's past noon. Let's get some food in us, recover, and then keep moving."

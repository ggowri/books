Chapter 323

Paisley nearly laughed at that. This wasn't some drama where people competed to look good. Ten yards wasn't exactly a death-defying leap. The whole display felt unnecessary.

She genuinely didn't understand why a routine field drop needed this much ceremony.

She released her Plant Affinity without another word.

In an instant, the ground beyond the hatch shook violently.

Countless roots tore up from beneath the earth, vines and shoots shooting upward at a staggering pace, branching and spreading with incredible speed.

In the span of a breath, thick, resilient dark-green vines wove together, layer over layer, climbing straight up to the hatch, stopping right at Paisley's feet.

A dense, solid platform of woven vine-wood formed, wide and sturdy enough to easily hold all of them.

Everyone left in the cabin stared, eyes wide with shock.

Elijah hadn't expected it — in just a few days, Paisley's Plant Affinity had grown this powerful.

Gabriel paused mid-step, a flicker of genuine surprise crossing his usually composed face, his gaze fixed on the sprawling mass of vine.

Her Plant Affinity was this strong.

That explained a few things.

On the ground below, Rachel and Chase's group, barely settled from their own landing, froze completely, disbelief written across every face.

A moment ago, that Level Five Plant Affinity user growing a three-yard branch had seemed impressive. Next to Paisley conjuring a stable, ten-yard-tall platform on command, it wasn't even close. Firefly against the moon.

Orion stood behind her, eyes shining with open pride, unable to keep the smile off his face.

His little sister, outdoing everyone without even trying.

A Level Five Plant Affinity user showing off in front of the group was nothing compared to what Paisley could do without even thinking about it.

His sister really was the strongest.

Paisley's expression stayed calm, unbothered by the shock, envy, and awe aimed her way.

She stepped onto the platform and walked down, easy and unhurried, like she was crossing flat ground.

Gabriel, Elijah, and Orion followed close behind.

Once all six were safely on the ground, the sprawling mass of vines slowly receded, shrinking back to nothing.

With everyone down, Gabriel quickly took headcount and reorganized the team, settling into formation.

Everyone shifted into combat readiness, rifles up, expressions grim.

The forest ahead stretched dense and ancient, the canopy blotting out the sky, a heavy black mist hanging between the trunks. No wind, no insects, nothing. The silence pressed down, oppressive and wrong.

A faint, acrid smell hung in the air — blood mixed with something chemical, sharp and cold, the kind of scent that crawled under your skin.

According to classified base intel, a long-abandoned human experimentation facility sat buried beneath this stretch of forest, fully sealed off from the outside world, its presence almost entirely unknown.

The facility had two separate entry points. One sat deep in the forest, a purpose-built, heavily fortified aboveground structure, protected by thick steel doors and layers of security.

The other lay beneath the forest floor, hidden under a centuries-old tomb, its tunnels winding down into the facility's lower levels — almost nobody knew it existed.

"We're splitting into two teams."

Gabriel's orders came quick and clean, his gaze sweeping the group as he assigned roles.

"I'll take one team to the surface facility entrance, break through the outer defenses, push in from the front."

"Chase, you take your team through the tomb entrance below, loop around from the lower levels. The underground layout's complicated, traps everywhere, high concentration of mutants. Stay careful."

Two entry points, one open, one hidden, converging on the facility from opposite directions.

"Understood!"

The group answered in unison.

Both teams split off immediately, moving in opposite directions, advancing deeper into the forest.

Gabriel's team moved with steady, measured steps, heading straight for the pale, boxy structure visible deep in the trees.

The building was built from reinforced alloy and blast-proof concrete, sealed on every side, no windows, no gaps — just one heavy, encrypted steel door, covered in old locks and sensors, cold and silent, radiating lockdown.

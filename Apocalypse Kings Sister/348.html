Chapter 348

Upstairs, Diane led her by the hand straight to a south-facing bedroom and pushed the door open.

"Pais, this room's been ready for you for years. Your dad and I set it up a long time ago."

The moment the door swung open, Paisley's steps faltered, a sudden sting of tears rising in her eyes.

The room was bright and spacious, every detail tasteful, every choice clearly made with her in mind, warm and carefully put together.

A soft, cloud-like bed, a cushioned window seat, an elegant vanity — every piece spoke of care.

What struck her hardest was the closet and display cabinets, packed full of clothes and keepsakes.

From a toddler's soft little dresses to an elegant young woman's gowns, every size for every age, growing with the years, all brand new and carefully stored.

The glass display cases beside them were full of years of birthday and holiday gifts — fine jewelry, little trinkets, treasured keepsakes, all arranged with care.

Her mother's voice was gentle, carrying years of buried ache and stubborn hope.

"We never stopped looking for you, not for a single year. Every birthday, we got you a present. Every age, we had clothes ready ahead of time."

"We kept hoping — hoping one day my girl would come home safe, put these clothes on with her own hands, open all these presents we'd been saving up."

Her eyes reddened slightly as she ran a hand gently over the bedding.

"I put this room together myself, every piece of it. Every single day, I hoped you'd come back.

Even when everyone else said to give up hope, I never did. You were never some abandoned child, Pais. Not for one moment did we forget you."

Such plain words, but they carried over a decade of unbroken longing.

Across two lifetimes of drifting alone, Paisley had grown up lonely, wronged, unloved, long used to a life with no one in her corner.

She'd told herself a hundred times that she must have been abandoned — why else would no one have come looking, for all those years?

But standing in this room, surrounded by years of quiet waiting made real, she finally believed it: she had never been unwanted.

Someone had been waiting for her this whole time, never giving up. She hadn't been thrown away after all.

******

A warm ache rose sharp behind her nose, warmth sweeping through her whole body, smoothing over every bit of cold loneliness from both her lives.

Paisley lowered her eyes, lashes trembling, a thin mist of tears rising.

After a long silence, she lifted her head, eyes still damp, her voice rough with years of solitude, gentle but sincere.

"Thank you, Mom."

Three simple words, barely a whisper, yet heavier than anything.

In that moment, every year of wandering across two lives finally found somewhere to land, every buried hurt and loneliness gently soothed.

This time around, she finally had a home.

Her mother's whole body went still with shock.

She'd waited over a decade to hear that word.

From the day her daughter disappeared, through every sleepless night of searching, every quiet cry in the dark — it had all finally come back to her.

The girl in front of her had long since outgrown the childish softness she remembered, grown into someone composed and resilient, no longer the little thing who used to cling to her for comfort.

Years of pent-up emotion broke loose at last, tears spilling freely down her face.

She stepped forward and pulled Paisley into a careful, cherishing embrace, her voice breaking.

"My good girl... my Pais..."

Mother and daughter held each other close, the room bathed in warm light, brimming with love that had taken far too long to arrive.

By the door, Gerald, Orion, and Ashley stood quietly, watching the scene, their eyes reddening too, though gentle, relieved smiles touched their mouths.

The weight on Gerald's chest finally lifted for good.

His daughter had really come home.

Everything else could wait.

He believed that one day, Paisley would truly let the rest of the family in too.

He turned to Orion, his tone turning serious.

"Orion. Come to the study with me."

Father and son stepped into the study, the door closing softly behind them, the air inside turning heavy at once.

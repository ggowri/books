Chapter 191

The two groups, side by side, couldn't have looked more different.

Rachel's crew looked worn down, listless, their clothes filthy and torn, fresh scrapes and bruises on every one of them—some with bleeding arms, others with swollen, reddened shoulders. They looked like wreckage.

Each of them clutched a blackened lump of hardtack.

Paisley's group, by contrast, looked clean and bright-eyed, every one of them.

The contrast couldn't have been sharper.

Paisley's expression didn't so much as flicker. She walked straight past without a glance, close enough to hear someone's stomach growling.

They'd brought this on themselves, every bit of it.

Ignored outright, Rachel's group froze where they stood, faces flushing pale and red by turns, humiliated. But they'd learned their lesson, and none of them dared say a word.

The resentment in their eyes, the shame, the hate—none of it stayed hidden.

“Wow, they look rough.”

Ashley said it with open relish.

Spencer cut in, his voice cold.

“They had it coming.”

He and Thomas had slipped in last night while they slept and taught them a lesson personally.

“Trash like that shouldn't even get to exist in this world.”

Thomas agreed.

“Exactly. Honestly, I think they've got it too easy. It only gets worse from here. We should find a way to get them kicked out of the base.”

Mason Coleman passed along what he'd heard that day.

“Heard they've cut ties with Dominic Harrington.”

“Correction—Harrington dumped them. Said they were too weak and too much trouble.”

Ashley laughed.

“So we don't have to deal with them tagging along anywhere anymore. About time. That's what you get for messing with Paisley.”

“We won't even have to lift a finger. With that kind of strength, they'll die out there on their own.”

Paisley's expression stayed unmoved.

Her own grudges, she'd settle herself. That was the only way it meant anything.

She wasn't some pushover anymore.

But everyone else seemed to have forgotten that.

Still, their unconditional loyalty warmed something in her chest.

Talking the whole way, the group reached the base hospital.

In the ward, Orion, Elijah, and the rest of the soldiers were already awake.

******

A night's rest, on top of what Paisley's Affinity had already repaired, had the five of them fully back to normal, every trace of the toxin gone.

The five were Rivergate's top fighting force, close to the ceiling of what the base could field.

More importantly, Orion and Elijah both came from Aetheris's top families, names that carried serious weight and connections in those circles. Their standing mattered enormously.

Elijah, especially, had come to Rivergate on official assignment, carrying real responsibility. Their safety mattered to the whole base, even to the system above it—this touched on humanity's future.

Word that all five had woken, fully healed, reached the Director immediately.

He'd been on edge all night, and the relief hit him hard. Without wasting a moment, he gathered the senior staff and medical team and went to the ward himself.

They moved quickly but respectfully, quieting their steps the moment they reached the door.

The Director went in first, and seeing all five sitting up, steady and recovered, the strain finally left his face.

His eyes settled on Orion and Elijah, his manner careful, deferential.

“Mr. Blackwood, Mr. Thompson, I heard you've all woken and recovered. How are you feeling? Anything still bothering you?”

He knew exactly what these two meant—names from the inner circle of Aetheris, untouchable in their own right. If something had happened to them under his watch, no small base's director could have absorbed the fallout.

The weight on his chest finally lifted now that he'd seen them whole, and he let out a long breath.

Elijah gave a small nod, calm and courteous.

“We're fine. Thank you for your concern.”

Orion nodded as well, his voice level and cool.

“Thank you. We've recovered fully.”

Both sat straight-backed on their beds, their bearing cool and refined even fresh off a near-fatal injury—the unmistakable poise of Aetheris's elite, steady enough that it left the staff in the room even more deferential.

Chapter 203

He watched the two of them saunter over, his voice cutting and flat, stripped of all warmth.

“Chase. Get your psychic senses out now, scan the area, confirm whether anything's hiding nearby.”

At the same time, the team's scanning equipment came online, blue light sweeping across the ruined aquarium, floor by floor, reading the structure for any movement inside.

His gaze shifted to Rachel, playing fragile as ever, his voice cold with the full weight of command.

“Ms. Lennox, you foresaw the mission objective's location. Point us to exactly where the data and equipment are hidden.”

Every eye in the group turned on her.

Some looked hopeful, some wary, more than a few openly skeptical.

She'd gotten herself onto a high-risk mission on nothing more than a vague claim of foresight, and everyone was waiting to see whether that claim was real.

Chase might be arrogant, but his Level Five psychic power, at least, was proven.

Rachel's chest tightened, her fingers curling quietly, panic rising underneath.

Her foresight was nothing but scraps of blurry memory from her last life. She had no real idea where anything was.

She vaguely remembered there was something valuable hidden in the aquarium, but not which room, not which direction.

But with every eye on her, waiting on an answer, there was no room left to back down.

Show weakness now, and she'd get nothing out of this at all.

She shoved the panic down fast, lowered her eyes to look timid and fragile, bit her lip lightly, and pointed toward the dark tunnel at the deepest end of the building.

“I—I just felt it. The equipment and the data are in the deep-sea performance hall, the one furthest in. That room feels the darkest, the strangest. That's where it all has to be.”

They were all going to die here anyway. Whether she got it right or wrong, no one would ever know the truth.

Something sharp and cold hid behind her eyes as she said it.

Beside her, Chase played along at once.

He glanced up, lazy, feigning depth as he sent out a thread of psychic force for show, then backed her up without missing a beat.

“I ran a sweep myself. The deepest area really does have the most activity—danger and energy signatures, all tangled together. That's the target. She's right.”

With a Level Five psychic backing her up, most of the ordinary team members let their doubts go without a second thought.

But standing at the front, Paisley's eyes carried a thin, cold edge of mockery.

Her own psychic reach was blocked by whatever field covered this place, unable to pick out detail, but she could still read the currents of air and energy clearly enough.

The deep-sea hall at the far end carried none of the clean, regular energy signature equipment would give off. Instead it reeked of mutant beast hostility, thick with the stink of blood.

This wasn't foresight. This was a guess, maybe worse—leading everyone straight down the deadliest path on purpose.

Elijah and Orion caught on just as fast, their brows tightening, something cold passing through their eyes.

The scanning equipment confirmed it a moment later, the readout plain on the screen: the deepest hall showed dense high-risk biological signatures, with no mechanical trace of any stored equipment or data.

That confirmed it. Rachel was lying, Chase was covering for her, and the two of them hadn't come here to help at all—they'd come to sabotage.

But with the whole team assembled and the mission underway, this wasn't the moment to start a fight within their own ranks.

Elijah swallowed down the cold fury churning in him, kept his face unreadable, and gave the order in a level voice.

“Everyone check your gear. We split into three teams.”

“One team per vehicle. Move independently, scout carefully as you go, and stay on guard.”

“My team takes the left corridor into the building. Chase, you take the central main passage. The rest take the right side route.”

He'd thought it through carefully.

With the team this divided, no telling who meant well and who didn't, forcing everyone to stick together would just leave them open to being undermined from the inside.

Splitting up was the safest option available right now.

At the order, the team moved fast, checking their gear.

Chase's eyes flashed with a quick, calculating smirk, and he agreed without hesitation.

“Fine by me.”

Exactly what he wanted.

Splitting up meant he was finally out from under anyone's eye, free to sabotage however he liked. This time, they'd all die in here.

He pulled Rachel in close against him, every bit of it for show.

Beneath her sleeve, Rachel's fingers curled, a hidden gleam of satisfaction pressed down at the corner of her mouth.

This time, Paisley had to die.

The pendant would be hers.

Paisley shot Rachel one cool glance, no illusions in her at all. She was up to something again, obviously.

Who actually died today was still very much up for debate.

Elijah's gaze swept the group one last time, his voice grave.

“Everyone, stay in contact. No reckless moves, no breaking off alone.”

“If anyone finds a lead on the equipment or the data, report it on the team channel immediately and hold position. Do not touch anything yourselves.”

Chase let out an impatient snort, waving a hand.

“Yeah, yeah. Could you talk any more?”

With that, he led Rachel and four others off, swaggering straight into the dark main passage, swallowed by shadow within seconds.

On the other side, Paisley followed Elijah, falling in with Orion and the rest toward the left corridor.

Once inside, cold, dead silence swallowed them, a damp, rotting sea wind the only thing moving, the building so thick it even swallowed the sound of the wind outside—quiet enough to make anyone's skin crawl.

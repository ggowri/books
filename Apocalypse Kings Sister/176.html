Chapter 176

They kept sobbing out thanks, voices shaking, pure relief and gratitude pouring out.

Paisley and the others calmed them down, confirming everyone had only minor injuries — just days without water, mostly.

Thomas handed each of them a bottle.

Water they'd just picked up from the mall, as it happened.

Once everyone had recovered enough, the group escorted them back toward the base.

Getting ready to head out, Paisley stopped mid-step, the warmth in her expression cooling fast.

Her senses, sharp as ever, caught a handful of signatures tucked away in a nearby street corner, carefully suppressed, still stubbornly following.

Owen's group, again.

Lucky for them, they'd survived the Level Five zombie and tracked her this far anyway.

Paisley's gaze went cool, nothing in it but indifference.

She didn't bother turning to look. She let her Psychic Affinity drift out quietly and locked onto a massive mutant ancient tree standing near the corner.

A single precise command.

Rumble —

The ancient tree shuddered violently, its thick branches whipping forward with brutal force, hard dead limbs cutting through the air straight toward the spot where Owen's group hid.

Hidden behind a broken wall, Rachel's group had no time to react.

They'd been so focused on watching Paisley, they never considered the trees around them could turn on them without warning.

"Watch out!" Reid shouted, scrambling to throw up a fire barrier, but the thick, hard branch slammed into it with the full brutal force of plant affinity behind it.

The fire wall shattered, sparks flying, and a snapped branch caught Reid's arm, tearing open a bloody gash. He grunted and stumbled back.

Rachel's face went white with fear, her carefully done makeup smeared with dust, hair whipped loose by the force of the wind.

She threw up a water barrier in a panic, but it dissolved like paper against the ancient tree's brute force, torn apart by the branches in an instant.

A thumb-thick branch whipped across her forearm, pain flaring hot through her skin, the limb swelling red almost instantly.

She stumbled and fell hard onto the rubble, her expensive jacket torn wide open, every trace of her usual poise gone.

Kelly screamed, curling up and covering her head, a branch catching her back anyway, a bruise already blooming.

Owen and Ryan scattered in a panic, but the tree's branches seemed to track them, lashing out from every direction, debris flying everywhere, every escape route sealed off.

Ryan tripped over a root and went down hard, scraping his palms raw on the jagged rocks.

The whole group scrambled, screaming and stumbling, utterly wrecked.

Furious and terrified in equal measure, Rachel clutched her throbbing, swollen arm, eyes flashing with venom.

"Damn it! This has to be Paisley. She can control mutant trees remotely?"

She spat the words through gritted teeth, cold sweat soaking her back, covered head to toe in dust.

This only cemented her suspicion — Paisley had to have awakened a storage space, and that was why her plant affinity had come online too.

Kelly, shaking all over, whimpered beside her.

"Rachel, this is too dangerous. We need to stop messing with her."

Rachel's fists clenched, eyes drifting toward the base, unwilling to give up with the pendant so close — but the tree was still attacking.

A second wave was coming.

Back at the park entrance, Spencer and the others turned toward the commotion in the distance, catching sight of the chaos.

"Is that Rachel's group?

They actually followed us here."

Thomas frowned.

Paisley glanced at the distant scene, fingers twitching, and let her Psychic Affinity press down again.

The ancient tree swung its trunk hard, slamming into the wall Rachel was hiding behind, cracks spiderwebbing across the surface.

Rachel's heart pounded with fear. She didn't dare linger any longer, dragging her battered group into a stumbling retreat down a nearby alley.

This had only been a warning.

Next time, she wouldn't be so lenient.

Paisley's mouth curved faintly, and she withdrew her Psychic Affinity.

The willow on her shoulder swayed its leaves lazily, and Kitty let out a slow yawn.

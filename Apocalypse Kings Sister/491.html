Chapter 491

"Mr. Thompson, Mrs. Thompson, this way! You're up!"

The clerk flipped open his logbook and counted out winter coats, coal, and ration bars, chatting warmly the whole time. His smile was genuine.

"We've all heard about your Paisley. Who at the base hasn't?"

"She's a hero around here now. All that food she brought back saved everyone. When you get home, tell her I said hi. Tell her every one of us admires her."

"She's our hero!"

Warmth spread through Diane's chest. "I'll tell her," she said, smiling.

The clerk handed over a thick bundle of supplies, more than generous, and tucked in two extra packs of fuel blocks.

"The Freeze keeps getting worse, so take these. They'll keep your place a little warmer. Falcon Squad pulled off something huge. Their families deserve extra."

Gerald took the heavy bundle in both hands and looked out the window at the snow whirling down.

Who would have guessed it? A few days, and everything had turned upside down.

People used to fawn over the senior branch and snub the junior one. Now they could all see what Paisley and the kids had done.

Diane hugged the bundle, unable to stop smiling. "Let's tell Paisley everything people said about her when we get home," she murmured.

Gerald beamed with pride. "I've never been this popular in my life. Thank goodness our daughter came home."

"It's all because of her that people are being this nice to us."

"Pais is our little lucky charm," Diane said. "Life's gotten so much better since she came back."

******

They had just hauled their bundles toward the warehouse door when a raised, angry voice behind them made them stop.

They turned. Dennis Thompson and Brenda Thompson were standing at the distribution counter.

The pile in front of them was pitiful. One thin, worn coat, one small bag of cheap ration bars, a tiny share of coal. Next to what Gerald and Diane had just carried out, the gap was obvious.

Brenda's face had gone rigid, her lips pressed thin. Dennis scowled and snapped at the clerk.

"Do you know who we are? You'd cut our supplies like this? You want to keep your job here or not?"

The clerk looked up. The old deference was gone from his eyes. He sounded flat and unbothered.

"Big talk. You think you're still who you used to be?"

"I'm following base rules. Blame your own daughter, Chloe. She ambushed our people. That's a serious crime. Family allotments were cut by order of the leadership."

"If you don't want it, leave it all here. At least we're still feeding you. The base is being generous."

Dennis opened his mouth and found nothing to say.

Diane watched quietly, and old memories rose up.

Back when supplies were tight, Brenda had swaggered through every distribution, leaning on Chloe's powers and Grant Quinn's backing. She'd shoved the junior branch aside and grabbed the coats and food that were theirs.

Diane had swallowed it every time.

The tide had turned. Paisley had done it all by herself and won the family its dignity back.

Diane felt it, but she had no wish to gloat. Kicking someone when they were down wasn't in her nature.

She tugged lightly at Gerald's sleeve. "Let's go home. Our girl should be up by now."

Gerald nodded, and they left the warehouse and walked off through the snow.

Brenda watched them go, fists clenched, nails digging into her palms. Rage choked her, and she turned all of it on Dennis.

"Why is it so little?" she hissed. "Why does Gerald's family have such good luck?"

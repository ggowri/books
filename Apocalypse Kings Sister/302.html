Chapter 302

The moment the threat closed in, Orion, Thomas, and Spencer moved as one, shouldering in front of Paisley, Ashley, and Madison. They stood tall, braced, ready for whatever came next.

Right as the standoff reached its breaking point, a deep voice cut through the crowd from behind, carrying enough authority that nobody questioned it.

"What's going on here? What's all the yelling?"

The crowd parted. A man strode out, Stillwater's dust still on his jacket, a captain's insignia on his shoulder.

He looked like he'd just come off a mission, grit and the faint smell of gunpowder still clinging to him, eyes sharp, bearing steady.

The second he saw Elijah's face, the hard edge in his expression melted into real, open relief. He crossed the distance fast.

"It's you! I just got back from an op outside the walls — I figured you'd be arriving this morning, but I didn't think I'd walk straight into you like this."

His gaze swept over the leveled rifles, then the clerk curled up on the ground. His jaw tightened.

"What happened here? Somebody explain. Now."

Before anyone else could speak, he turned to Elijah, his whole posture shifting to something close to reverence.

"Colonel Blackwood. Weren't you looking for whoever's running this base? I'll take you there myself."

The crowd froze.

It clicked for them all at once — this was the captain who'd led the mass evacuation a few days back, the one who'd pulled hundreds of survivors out alive.

He turned on his heel and fixed his glare on the pale, shaking clerk, his voice gone cold.

"What exactly happened here? A front-desk clerk pulled armed security on honored guests?"

The clerk flinched, scrambling for an excuse.

"Captain! I didn't know they were — were with you! They wouldn't follow the rules, they attacked me with their Affinity in front of everyone, I was just handling an outsider by the book!"

"Why'd they go after you and not anyone else?"

"Rules?"

The captain let out a short, furious laugh.

"You don't get to talk to me about rules."

"If Colonel Blackwood and his people hadn't stepped in, those twelve hundred survivors trapped outside the city would all be dead. Every last one of them."

His voice carried, loud and hard, and heads turned all down the line — people waiting to get through the gate, guards still holding position.

"They're heroes. To this entire base."

The second he said it, the crowd started murmuring agreement.

A weathered middle-aged man pushed forward, eyes wet with gratitude.

"He's right! They saved my life! I was there that day — I watched them kill the lead zombie myself. We wouldn't have made it without them!"

"Me too!"

Another survivor, younger, his eyes red, chimed in.

"My whole family's alive because of them! If not for them, we'd all be gone!"

More and more people who'd been there that day stepped forward, each one adding their own thanks.

In the space of a few minutes, the whole mood flipped.

The outsiders who'd been treated like troublemakers were suddenly the people who'd saved a thousand lives.

The clerk, on the other hand, had gone the color of paper. He was shaking so hard he nearly collapsed.

He'd mocked that old officer's ID, sneered at this group, and it turned out they were the reason a thousand people were still breathing.

The security team that had their rifles raised a minute ago stood frozen, hands trembling on their weapons, faces pale. They lowered their guns and backed off, not willing to stand their ground anymore.

The captain turned a cold look on the clerk, voice flat and final.

"A front-desk worker, throwing his weight around, sneering at the people who saved this base, humiliating them in public. That's a disgrace to everything we stand for. You're relieved of duty, effective now. Every point you've earned is forfeited. Three months on labor detail. Think it over."

"Y— yes sir. Thank you for not having me thrown out…"

The clerk sagged against the ground, voice shaking apart. It was over. Barely.

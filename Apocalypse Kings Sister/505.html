Chapter 505

Evelyn nodded.

"It's rare to taste a soup this rich and nourishing. I feel so much better."

Everyone at the table had felt the change in themselves. The lively table went quiet for a heartbeat, and then gasps rose from every side.

Thomas couldn't stop looking at the soup, his breathing soft.

"Just one bowl of soup clears out exhaustion and helps us break through? What are this fungus and this broth?"

Everyone's eyes turned to Paisley, full of awe and admiration.

Diane said softly, "Paisley, this stuff of yours is incredible."

Then she stopped, a flicker of shock crossing her face, and pressed a hand to her chest.

"Wait… I think I just awakened an affinity."

A cool, clear energy rippled out from inside her.

Her fingertip moved. A few drops of soup spilled on the table floated up, gathered into round, clear beads, and hung in midair, trembling.

A faint blue glow wrapped around the droplets. Soft mist spread around her, and the air filled with a thin, damp haze.

Diane could hardly believe it. She focused again, and the water in the glass on the table slipped free of its rim and turned into thin threads, curling around her fingers like little snakes.

Her Water Affinity was gentle and long-lasting. It had no violent shock to it, only pure warmth.

Everyone at the table put down their bowls and forks. Every eye went to Diane, and a murmur ran around the room.

Gerald's eyes went wide with surprise and delight.

"Honey! You really awakened an affinity? And it's Water, too!"

Thomas shouted.

"This is amazing! In the apocalypse, it's so hard for ordinary people to awaken. Most never get the chance!"

Ashley's eyes shone.

"Water Affinity?! The base is desperate for Water Affinity users. You can store clean water, irrigate, and in a fight you can attack and defend both!"

General Blackwood watched the water beads drift in the air and spoke slowly, a little surprised.

"I never expected someone to awaken at this stage. It's very rare, and the soup deserves the credit."

Evelyn was just as amazed.

"Paisley, this soup is a treasure."

Diane watched the threads curl around her fingers, thrilled and a little nervous. She let go gently, and the beads dripped back onto the table.

She turned to Paisley, her eyes full of gratitude.

"Paisley, thanks to your soup. I never imagined I'd become an Affinity user someday."

Paisley's lips curved. "No problem, Mom. You already have a strong foundation. The soup just helped bring out the affinity seed hidden inside you."

Elijah watched, moved. Whatever Paisley casually took out could bring such huge fortune to the people around her. He felt lucky to be at her side.

Orion came back to himself and joked.

"Our girl really knows how to do things."

The storm still screamed outside, and the house was full of laughter.

******

At the same time, in another worn-down villa, the cold was brutal, and icy drafts cut through every gap in the windows.

Rachel's group stumbled through the door in a heap.

Their coats were soaked through. Torn fabric showed scratches of different depths, and dried dark blood clung to the ragged cloth.

They'd come back from the mission empty-handed, not a single warm supply collected, and the mission was ruled a failure.

The base had taken what little contribution points they had left as a fine. They'd fought a swarm of mutant snow rats for nothing and gotten only wounds.

Ryan was the worst off.

His left leg had a huge chunk of flesh torn out by rat teeth. He was drained, swaying, and could barely stay on his feet with Owen holding him up.

He wanted to go to the base hospital, but their contribution points were gone and they couldn't afford the cost. They could only grit their teeth, buy a few boxes of cheap antibiotics, and tough it out at home, letting the medicine slowly close the wounds.

******

They sat around the cold wooden table. Rachel's eyes were red, and she looked utterly wronged.

She laid it on thick for Gordon and Helen, accusing Paisley of being cold-blooded and heartless, of watching them be surrounded by rats and refusing to lift a finger.

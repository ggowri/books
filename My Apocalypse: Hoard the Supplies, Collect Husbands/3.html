Chapter 3 Blackie

"Don't worry. He looks fierce, but he's actually gentle—just a bit stubborn," Logan said, leading her two steps closer before taking the leash from a nearby soldier.

The Doberman didn't move, remaining seated calmly.

"His name's Blackie. He's only six months old," Logan added.

"Six months? He's already this big?" Cecilia's voice betrayed her surprise.

Logan nodded, patting Blackie's large head.

"He was an only child in his litter, born bigger than the rest. A natural-born warrior, you could say," he joked lightly.

"Blackie, down!"

At his command, Blackie lay down, revealing a scar over a foot long on his back. No fur had grown over the pink, scabbed flesh, making it look quite gruesome.

Logan explained, "He's smart, perfect for military service. We started training him on missions when he was five months old, but he got seriously injured fighting criminals a couple of days later. That's why he retired.

"As a decorated veteran, he deserves a good home to spend the rest of his life in. So, he's not up for auction."

He stroked Blackie's head, a hint of regret in his voice. "This guy saved an entire team of us."

After talking for a while longer, Cecilia decided to adopt Blackie.

At six months old, he still had plenty of growing to do. Smart, obedient, and imposing... he'd be the perfect companion.

Though his injury might affect his right front leg's mobility in the future and could flare up on rainy days, requiring careful care, lifelong medication, and expensive medical bills, none of that mattered.

Once the apocalypse came and he mutated, his enhanced healing abilities would completely heal the old wound.

It was clear that Logan truly loved dogs. After Cecilia made her decision, he took her to a nearby reception room to chat for an hour—covering Blackie's diet, care requirements for his injury, and scheduling future check-ins.

He even asked about her family situation, frowning slightly when he learned she was a student.

"I'm a senior in college. I have no classes left, just an internship. I can definitely take care of Blackie," she assured him.

After a few more questions, Logan said the final decision rested with Blackie.

He led her back to the dog's cage.

Blackie lay quietly inside. When Cecilia approached, he only lifted his eyelids slightly, showing little reaction.

"He's more intuitive than most dogs. Sometimes we swear he's almost human," Logan said, opening the cage door.

At the sound, Blackie stood up, his black eyes fixing firmly on Cecilia.

In her hand, she held what Logan had said was Blackie's favorite treat—a donut.

Cecilia approached slowly, nervous despite her love for dogs. His size was intimidating.

Blackie ignored her at first, staring quietly at her before glancing at Logan, refusing the treat.

Just as Cecilia feared he didn't like her, he finally lowered his head and gently took the donut from her hand.

Excitement surged through her, making her want to jump for joy.

Logan let out a relieved breath beside her.

"If he hadn't taken that from you today, I couldn't in good conscience let you adopt him. This dog's picky—only gives his trust to people he approves of."

With Blackie's approval, Logan didn't hesitate any longer.

Blackie had rejected four previous adopters, so they were relieved he'd accepted her.

Looking at the dog, Cecilia couldn't help but smile.

Who would've thought such a young dog could be so discerning?

After finishing the paperwork, she took Blackie home, along with a crate of toys, a crate of treats, two bags of dog food, and other supplies. These were all gifts from the soldiers, a token of gratitude for his service.

Then, Cecilia stopped at a pet store on the way to buy a dog bed.

Leading Blackie inside, she pointed to several options, half-joking that he could choose.

She didn't expect him to understand, but to her surprise, he glanced at her, walked to a dark blue bed, placed his paw on it, and looked back. His expression was as if to say, "This one."

Cecilia was amazed. This dog was something else.

She had three months to bond with Blackie. Although Cecilia wasn't sure if she'd succeed, she knew one thing—dogs were simple creatures, far simpler than humans.

Treat them well, and they'd return that loyalty tenfold.

To her surprise, an unexpected guest waited at her front door when she arrived.

He had long legs in skinny jeans, a white shirt unbuttoned at the top three buttons, revealing a toned chest. His handsome face, the kind adored by young fans, wore an anxious expression.

"Cici, why did you block my number?" It was Alexander.

If Cecilia remembered correctly, he was supposed to talk to her about their engagement today.

The marriage alliance between the Barnes and Summers families had been arranged by her grandfather before his death, sealed with an exchange of tokens.

The Barneses had returned the antique ring her grandfather had given them, claiming it was a favorite piece of her grandmother's and that they felt unworthy to keep it, so it would serve as their engagement token.

What a load of nonsense.

They'd had the so-called premium ring appraised, only to find it was a dyed antique, practically worthless.

Little did they know, it contained a spatial storage ability—priceless for surviving the upcoming apocalypse.

And once blood-activated, the "dyed" stone would transform into a genuine high-quality ruby.

In contrast, the Summers family's "token" to the Barneses was real—shares and funds worth a fortune.

And it was the Barneses who'd demanded that specific token.

Cecilia was so stupid in her last life, believing their lies and giving them half the shares and funds her parents had left her.

She'd even trusted their promise. "Once you and Alexander marry, we'll be family. It'll all be yours again. We'll manage it for you while you're in school."

How naive she'd been.

Worse, after the apocalypse, she discovered Alexander was having an affair with her childhood best friend, Rebecca Lewis, all along.

Now, as Cecilia saw him standing there, old grievances and new anger welled up inside her.

She was tempted to let Blackie attack him, but she knew she couldn't.

Not yet. The apocalypse hadn't arrived.

But when it did... she'd make them wish they were dead.

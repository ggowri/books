Chapter 32 Acid Rain

Cecilia had dealt with plenty of sprains and injuries during the apocalypse, so she'd basically become a makeshift medic. Just by feeling around, she could usually tell if something was dislocated or broken.

Luckily, Blackie didn't have any fractures or dislocations. His paw was just sprained. He must have hit the billboard at a weird angle and twisted it on impact.

After the exam, Cecilia felt a wave of relief. About an hour later, once the weather outside calmed down a bit, she bundled up and went out to grab the solar charger she'd ordered.

She glared at the little package when she got back inside and smacked it a couple of times. "This is your fault. You and my stupid brain are the reason Blackie got hurt."

She pulled out her radio and tuned in to the local news, treating Blackie's paw while she listened. Blackie sat there like a perfect patient and let her work.

The radio announcer was warning everyone about the severe weather. Several companies had already switched to remote work, and some factories had even shut down for a few days. A few experts claimed the storm would blow over soon.

Then she switched to a different station that had a younger crowd. The hosts were talking about some "doomsday theory" that was spreading online.

Cecilia finished wrapping Blackie's paw in gauze and gave him some Magic Spring water to drink. She looked at his now softball-sized bandaged paw and felt satisfied with her work.

Meanwhile, the radio hosts were still going on about the viral doomsday prediction.

"So this user Bobby somehow predicted exactly how this storm would develop," one host said. "He said we'd get nationwide rain for about a week, and then high winds would kick in. I didn't believe it at first, but when those winds hit the whole country today, I had nothing to say."

The other host jumped in. "The scariest part is that Bobby's saying acid rain is coming next, and he's telling people to stock up on supplies. That's what really freaks me out. His post has like 200 thousand likes now, and it's still climbing."

"You know what? I'm going to the store after this. I don't care if it sounds crazy. This weather is insane."

"Same. I'm hitting up the grocery store right after my shift."

Hearing this, Cecilia grabbed her phone and searched for "Bobby" and "doomsday." She found his Twitter account pretty quickly, but when she tried to open the post everyone was talking about, it had already been deleted.

She remembered Bobby from her past life. He'd been the one who spread the apocalypse theory online and convinced tons of people to stockpile supplies. Cecilia had stocked up too because of his warnings.

After everything went to hell, someone said they knew Bobby personally. Apparently, he was a wealthy amateur meteorologist with a whole setup of expensive weather monitoring equipment at home.

She never did find out what happened to Bobby after that.

The wind howled all night long. Cecilia held Blackie close as she drifted in and out of sleep, never quite settling into a deep rest.

Blackie seemed to pick up on her unease and stayed curled up beside her without moving around much.

When she woke up the next morning around seven, she glanced outside and saw nothing but gray darkness. Normally it would be light out by now. Even on cloudy days, it shouldn't be this dark.

She walked over to the floor-to-ceiling windows in the living room and looked out. The wind had died down and the rain had lightened up quite a bit, but thick clouds covered the sky. It looked more like five in the morning than seven.

Cecilia grabbed a piece of paper, cracked the window open, and stuck it outside. After she finished breakfast and came back to check, she noticed the paper had slight corrosion marks on it.

Just as she suspected—acid rain. The apocalypse had officially started.

That day, she saw tons of posts online. Some people said the rain felt off somehow. Others mentioned that the vegetables in their yards had wilted overnight. A few people said their outdoor dogs looked sick all of a sudden.

The words "acid rain" and "apocalypse" were being searched constantly. The platforms kept blocking those keywords, but people just kept using them anyway, posting them in comments and all over social media.

Pretty soon, an official statement came out warning citizens that due to urban pollution and other factors, the rainwater currently falling did contain small amounts of acid. They asked people to limit going outside and said they were working on a solution.

That word "small amounts" was pretty vague though, and it set off a ton of debate online. People were all talking about how bad this acid rain was.

Plenty of people pushed back against the panic.

They said the rain was slightly corrosive, but it wasn't that bad and this was nowhere close to an apocalypse. No need to worry, just remember to carry an umbrella when you go out.

Some so-called experts even came forward saying the rain was only mildly acidic and posed no real harm to humans. They told everyone to calm down and just go about their normal lives.

After all, it had been raining heavily across the country for over a week now, and the fact that it was letting up was actually a good sign. Some people even predicted the rain would stop completely in a few days.

Cecilia could only shake her head at all of it. The acid rain wasn't going to stop, and as time went on, it would only get more corrosive.

Sure, the acid rain would eventually help people awaken their superpowers later on, but that was cold comfort.

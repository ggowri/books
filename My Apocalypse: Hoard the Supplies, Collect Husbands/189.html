Chapter 189 The Secret of Mutation


In fact, the military had already been puzzled by this very issue.

They had successfully produced mutated crops. Those crops had all mutated after being soaked in acid rain. More precisely, the ones that survived the soaking process mutated, and the resulting mutated grain had proved remarkably stable.

Animals, however, were a different matter.

They had soaked animals in acid rain as well, yet most of them had failed to mutate. Some had simply died.

That made the mutated military dogs, along with certain mutated pet dogs and cats found among civilians, all the more baffling.

In some cases, even the owners themselves had no idea whether their animals had ever come into contact with acid rain.

But Cecilia knew something the military did not.

Animals were actually easier to mutate than plants.

Any animal that had been exposed to acid rain could mutate. The difference lay only in timing and degree.

The longer the exposure, the stronger the mutation.

There was, however, one crucial condition. The animal had to remain within the wider post apocalyptic environment and changing climate. It absolutely could not be shut away in a cramped laboratory. If it was, not only would it fail to mutate, it would most likely die instead.

Now Cecilia explained her theory about animal mutation to Colonel Reed.

"Colonel Reed, my Blackie mutated, and so did Jet. I've also seen another mutated dog. I've spent a good deal of time thinking about why these animals mutate. In fact, Jet's mutation was something I deliberately guided."

Hearing all this, Thomas found her reasoning highly convincing. He even turned to look carefully at Jet, who was still standing nearby in a weak and pitiful state.

Then he looked back at Cecilia seriously. "Miss Summers, I do think what you're saying makes a great deal of sense, and this discovery would indeed be useful to the military. But... at present, we still can't fully confirm that this is the correct method for triggering animal mutation. It will need to be tested."

Cecilia nodded lightly. "Of course. You can report it to the old general and have them run the tests. Just remember this. Once an animal has been exposed to acid rain, it must not be confined to a laboratory. It has to be allowed to live as it naturally would. If it's a dog, let it run across the ground. If it's a bird, let it fly in the sky. If it's a cat, let it leap and climb. Don't imprison them like prisoners. They need to merge with the ecosystem and the climate around them."

She then went on to explain in more detail why she had reached that conclusion.

She spoke about how, before the Ice Stage had fully arrived, she had already noticed Blackie's coat becoming thicker and Jet's feathers growing fuller, and how those changes had first led her to her suspicions.

She also explained why she had prepared so many supplies for winter, saying it was because she had noticed the changes in the animals around her.

Although Thomas found her reasoning persuasive, he still could not help thinking that if it turned out to be wrong, the old general might still whip him bloody for bringing it forward. So he grinned and said, "Miss Summers, testing this will take time. Meanwhile, the old general will have to put a great deal of effort into settling today's matter over the next few days."

Cecilia looked at him and thought that, for all his straightforwardness, he certainly knew how to bargain for a favour.

Smiling, she said, "Colonel Reed, I understand what you mean. The government is short of industrial machine tools for production lines right now, isn't it? As for the deal we discussed last time, the one percent commission, I can give up another third of that."

Thomas held up three fingers. "Half."

Cecilia rolled her eyes inwardly. Thomas really did have a knack for being both likable and infuriating at once.

"Fine. Half it is."

Thomas was delighted.

Another advantage pocketed.

Cecilia only had to look at his face to know exactly what he was thinking.

But Cecilia was not the sort to let him take advantage so easily.

"Oh, by the way, I'm planning to use the sixty fifth floor to conduct research on mutated organisms and see whether they can be applied to medicinal materials. I mentioned it briefly to your old general last time. He seemed very interested, though we haven't yet settled how I would cooperate with the military when the time comes."

The instant the words left her mouth, Thomas felt his right eyelid twitch.

He looked at Cecilia's smiling face and felt a faint chill run down his spine.

Why did he have the uneasy feeling that the extra share he had just squeezed out of her would be clawed back later?

Still, that was a problem for another day.

Hearing that Cecilia wanted to research mutated organisms, and knowing that the old general had already taken a batch of medicine from her to use on the front lines against mutated creatures, Thomas was in an excellent mood.

The old general had said it worked astonishingly well.

So Thomas made a grand gesture and ordered his men to move the bodies of the mutated cockroaches they had exterminated a few days ago, several hundred pounds of them, up to the sixty fifth floor for Cecilia.

Cecilia knew at once that this would allow the Space to level up again.

But then she heard that they were mutated cockroach corpses.

And Thomas added that they were the winged kind, some as long as a forearm, even the smaller ones still the size of an outstretched hand.

At that point, Cecilia found that she could not feel especially pleased about it.

Leaving that aside, Thomas needed to hurry off and deal with the aftermath of the incident.

He also said he would send people to clear away the bodies and bloodstains outside Cecilia's door.

Meanwhile, downstairs, Nathan and Frank had heard the gunshots and were still joking among themselves.

Mabel, meanwhile, was privately convinced that Cecilia was about to suffer badly.

Her eyes had been fixed on the sixty sixth floor the whole time, and she had even been counting how long Cecilia had been upstairs.

As she listened to the shots and counted the minutes, she could not help imagining that those bullets might well be tearing into Cecilia herself.

The more she thought it, the more her whole body trembled with excitement.

She could barely resist rushing upstairs to see Cecilia's misery with her own eyes.

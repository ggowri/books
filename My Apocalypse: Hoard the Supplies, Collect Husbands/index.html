Chapter 1 Reborn

A sharp jolt pulled Cecilia Summers from sleep.

Panic, thick as tar, clung to her—memories of being strung up, blood dripping from her veins, screaming in vain.

She thrashed wildly, grabbing the first object within reach and hurling it. The crash echoed, jolting her into awareness of her surroundings.

Disbelief washed over her. This was her bathroom, the one she'd had before the apocalypse.

Ten years she'd survived in the ruins, ten years since she'd fled Heldon City... How could she be here again?

"Never mind, I'll find someone like you..." A familiar melody drifted in, faint but clear.

Cecilia glanced at the sink and spotted her phone, its pink case emblazoned with a photo of her and Alexander Barnes.

That phone should have died years ago. And she'd been bleeding out, her flesh carved up for the raiders who'd caught her.

The phantom pain of a blade slicing her skin lingered, along with the cold dread of life seeping drop by drop from her fingertips.

Alexander.

The name curdled her blood. Just the sight of his face made her stomach churn.

Trembling, Cecilia reached for the phone. Her fingers fumbled, as if she'd forgotten how to hold such a delicate object.

The screen lit up, displaying January 26th, 5023, three full months before the world ended.

Wait. I'm reborn?

The word settled in her chest, a mix of shock and searing resolve.

She swiped to silence the music, then stood and slowly turned to face the mirror.

Her long hair fell over her shoulders, framing her bright, alert eyes, high nose, and smooth, unmarked face. The only blemish was a faint scar on her thigh, from a bike accident when she was ten.

After the apocalypse, Cecilia had shaved her head herself to avoid men's predatory glances in the lawless wastes. But it hadn't been enough. Those men were crazed by hunger and desperation.

In the end, she had no choice but to scar her own face. Three jagged lines ran from her chin to her brow, and the pain was searing enough to make her scream.

Suddenly, a thought struck her.

She pulled on a robe and rummaged through the vanity drawer, finding it in seconds—an ancient silver ring inset with a large, vivid emerald.

Alexander had given it to her, but it had never truly been his. It was a Summers family heirloom, her grandfather's gift to the Barneses as a token of friendship. It belonged to her.

Grabbing a fruit knife from the kitchen, she pricked her finger. A single drop of blood fell onto the emerald, which absorbed it instantly.

The gem's green hue shifted, deepening into a rich, crimson red.

At the same time, a vast space bloomed in her mind—roughly the size of three football fields, its edges shrouded in thick white mist.

Testing her newfound connection, Cecilia focused on a mug on the vanity. The ceramic cup vanished, reappearing in the corner of the mental space, its cheerful smiley face unbroken.

Satisfaction warmed her.

She discovered the ring's secret in her last life too. But once Alexander took it from her, he kept all its power for himself, giving her nothing.

Worse, he was the one who handed her over to the raiders, who bled her dry.

The phone chirped again, shattering Cecilia's thoughts. The screen flashed "Alexander."

Her jaw tightened. Just seeing his name made her skin crawl, but she forced herself to calm down.

The Barneses still owed her tens of millions of dollars in stocks and funds.

With the ring back in her hands, she had her safety net. But she needed cash, and lots of it, to stockpile supplies.

Those stocks were hers, and she was going to get them back.

Cecilia typed fast, texting him: [Let's break up. Call off the engagement.]

She hit send, then blocked his number.

She knew Alexander well. They were in the middle of engagement preparations; this would send him into a panic.

And when he got flustered, she could use that to get back every penny the Barnes family owed her.

Turning her attention to more pressing matters, she pulled up her contacts and found the number of a food wholesaler she'd met through a business connection.

She dialed the number.

She placed a bulk order for oats, wheat flour, quinoa, and olive oil.

This wholesaler was someone she'd helped with a business referral before, and his contact had been in her phone ever since.

The wholesaler didn't have a wide variety or large quantity of goods, but his prices were 30% below market, which suited Cecilia perfectly.

Thanks to the big business she'd referred him to earlier, he even threw in two cases of seasonings—paprika, oregano, black pepper, and so on.

Cecilia didn't have much money in her bank account—only around a few hundred thousand dollars, and some of her savings required paperwork to withdraw.

After paying the wholesaler, she turned to online shopping, buying large quantities of daily necessities: sanitary napkins, toilet paper, antiseptic ointment, toothbrushes, shampoo, body wash, and more.

She bought everything at wholesale prices, haggling with the sellers over the phone after selecting the quantities.

Fortunately, there was a site-wide sale—30 credits off every 200 spent—making the total cost very cheap.

By the time she finished shopping, her available funds were almost depleted, leaving only around 100 thousand dollars, but she felt much more at ease.

After a moment's thought, Cecilia decided to focus on making more money and stockpiling supplies next.

For now, the priority was the ring.

Sliding it on her finger, she grabbed her bag and headed out, straight for a jewelry shop near the antique district. She had a gem set into a necklace there in her last life.

"I need Mark Winslow to make a piece of jewelry. It's urgent, and I'll pay whatever it takes," she said, stating her request directly.

The ring was too inconvenient to keep; this Space was her lifeline for surviving the apocalypse.

Mark was the best craftsman here, known for making jewelry that was sturdy, durable, and beautiful.

After examining the ring for a long time, Mark looked up, incredulous. "This ring... It's an antique, and this ruby is extremely valuable. Are you sure you want to take it apart to make a necklace?"

She nodded firmly. "Positive."

Cecilia wanted him to craft the ring into a custom piece of jewelry.

The key was that it had to stay secure during chaos, no matter how intense her movements, and remain completely hidden from sight.

Seeing her unwavering resolve, Mark immediately removed the ruby from the ring. He used the hardest platinum to make a hinged casing, then added several chains to the platinum shell as Cecilia requested.

These chains would keep the pendant tightly against her skin, ensuring it wouldn't fly off during intense activity, be easily spotted, pulled off, or broken.

Cecilia paid extra, and Mark said it would take about five hours.

Mark had a good reputation, and they'd known each other before. But to be cautious, she took a short video of him working on the piece to prevent any foul play. With that, she left temporarily.

Her next stop was the bank, where she withdrew a fixed deposit, giving her more funds.

She then wandered around the nearby antique district until she spotted a shop sign reading "Antique Paintings," and walked straight in.

A young man with orange hair was manning the counter, his bright color clashing sharply with the shop's antique decor.

Looking up from his phone, he asked, "Can I help you, miss?"

Cecilia didn't answer, walking straight to a large landscape painting covering half the wall. She knocked once—solid wood—and again, this time harder. The sound was hollow, echoing.

"I want what's behind here," she said flatly.

The young man froze, then chuckled nervously, "I have no idea what you're talking about."

Cecilia didn't waste time. She transferred 20 thousand dollars to the shop's account. The young man's eyes widened as his phone pinged with the notification.

"A friend sent me. He said you have good stuff. Don't worry, I'll show you my ID. If I leak this, and something happens to your shop, tell your guy to come kill me," Cecilia explained, sliding her ID across the counter.

He picked up her ID, squinting at it. "Twenty-two? You look like a minor."

Cecilia rolled her eyes. At five feet four inches, she was fairly tall, but her baby face often led people to mistake her for a minor.

The young man studied her for a long moment before gritting his teeth. "Fine!"

Then, he locked the shop door, drew the curtains, and pressed a hidden button.

The entire wall flipped around, revealing a small room filled with melee weapons.

"Wow, even better than my friend described," Cecilia exclaimed.

That friend was someone she met after the apocalypse. He told her about how his group found this cache of "good stuff" while scavenging, and bringing it back to their base caused a stir.

She turned to the young man with a smile. "How much for all of it?"

An hour later, Cecilia stood by her car, watching him load four large crates of weapons into the trunk and backseat.

Once he left, she opened one crate and pulled out a curved knife inlaid with a ruby.

The blade was sharp, glinting faintly in the afternoon sun; a closer look revealed thin green streaks running along its surface, making it quite unique.

This was the knife she'd used throughout her last life, the one she'd bought from that friend at a high price.

Finding it here had been her main goal in coming to the shop.

Once, a young man had told her this knife was something special. She'd asked the orange-haired youth about it earlier, and he'd said it had been left by his father, with no known origin.

With this knife and the other weapons in the Space, her chances of surviving the apocalypse had just increased significantly.

Chapter 133 Cannibalism

Cecilia heard frantic squeaking from inside.

No time to worry about that. She looked around. "Blackie!"

Blackie bolted over.

Cecilia spotted the blood streaking across his glossy black fur. Her chest tightened.

She'd raised this dog herself—every kibble, every scrap of meat! Hell, she'd even peeled eggs for him by hand just to keep his coat shiny and gorgeous.

Her rage kicked up another notch.

With Arthur gone, no need to hide the Space anymore. Cecilia yanked out a can of gasoline and doused the rats.

Then flicked her lighter.

The whole area went up in flames.

Countless mice turned into roasted rodents. A huge wave of them dropped dead.

But plenty were still alive. Not even close to wiped out.

And they obeyed the Rat King without question. After a brief hesitation, they regrouped and lunged at Blackie and Cecilia again.

Blackie's face twisted in pain, but he still followed Cecilia's orders—he didn't change size.

Cecilia was terrified of Blackie's ability being exposed. She'd warned him never to shift in front of others without her command.

But right now? No one was watching.

And Cecilia had the perfect idea.

"Get big. Stomp them!"

Blackie's eyes lit up. He exploded in size—bigger than ever before. Ten feet tall.

He bounced across the ground like a wrecking ball, crushing the rats that had been gnawing at him like fleas.

When the numbers thinned, Cecilia called him back. "Shrink!"

They both dove aside.

The surviving rats stumbled around, dazed and disoriented.

Cecilia yanked the grenade pin and hurled it.

BOOM.

Flames shot up. The stench of burnt fur filled the air, mixed with dying shrieks.

Cecilia and Blackie circled the edges, mopping up the mutant rats that weren't quite dead yet. Another twenty down.

After all that, a solid ten minutes had passed.

Cecilia gasped for air, tossing the rat corpses into the Space. Might as well let it absorb their mutant energy—no point wasting it.

That's when she heard noise from the warehouse.

The door slammed open. The stench of blood poured out.

Arthur emerged, drenched head to toe in gore, dragging the Rat King's corpse by one hand.

The thing barely had a scratch—except for two daggers buried deep in its eye sockets.

Blood soaked its white fur.

Inside the warehouse, the leaderless rats had turned on each other—clawing, biting, tearing.

Even the stragglers who'd survived Cecilia's grenade blast ignored her completely. They charged back inside to join the carnage.

Tiny newborn rats stumbled forward on shaky legs.

Wounded rats dragged themselves toward the fray.

Crazier still—the rats locked in the other warehouse started going berserk too. They burst through the door and joined the slaughter.

It was like Cecilia, Arthur, and Blackie didn't even exist. All the rats saw was flesh. Blood. Their own kind.

A brutal massacre unfolded right before Cecilia's eyes.

Even after witnessing countless horrors in her past life, this sent a chill down her spine.

Worse? After taking down their prey, the rats devoured each other on the spot.

Some gorged on several of their own, bellies swelling grotesquely like they'd explode any second.

But they kept eating.

"What the hell are they doing?" Arthur frowned.

"Choosing a new Rat King," Cecilia said coldly.

In her past life, Cecilia hated three mutant creatures above all: rats, cockroaches, and centipedes.

Centipedes could slip through the tiniest cracks. Your kid or lover took a nap, and one crawled into their ear or nose. Some died instantly. Others? The centipede laid eggs inside them. By the time you noticed, baby centipedes burst through their bellies—using human flesh as an incubator.

Cockroaches ate everything. One of the most vicious mutants that actively hunted humans. Big as dinner plates, some grew sucking mouthparts. During the insect plague, Cecilia saw one suck a man's eyeball clean out of his skull.

And rats? They evolved for breeding. Ate anything. Crazy smart. Would do anything to survive. Worst part? They chose their king through cannibalism.

They devoured each other until one emerged victorious. The weak fell first, forced to watch from the sidelines. The strong kept eating, devouring siblings until one—belly nearly bursting—became king.

Within days, that king would birth an army. Hundreds, maybe thousands of rats.

And the corpses of its devoured kin? Food for the next generation.

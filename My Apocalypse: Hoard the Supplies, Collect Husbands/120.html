Chapter 120 Charles Was Dead

Who could've imagined that in just a few months, things would come to this?

Hands shaking, Hailey wiped the blood off her knife and put it away. Golf club in hand, she headed upstairs.

She clenched her fists, psyching herself up.

Get stronger. Don't be a burden to Cecilia and Arthur.

Five, maybe six minutes after she left—

The "dead" Mabel stirred.

She clutched her wounded stomach and pushed herself up, glaring at where Hailey had disappeared. Teeth grinding. Eyes blazing with pure hatred.

Before coming up here, she'd been terrified they'd beat her—or that Cecilia would just kill her on the spot.

So she'd padded her chest and stomach.

Only her side was unprotected—she'd been in such a rush running downstairs that she hadn't covered it.

Hailey's knife had only grazed that small exposed spot on her side. Everything else? Completely untouched.

And Hailey, already panicking, had no idea if she'd actually stabbed Mabel or not after that frantic flurry.

Plus, with all those layers, Hailey couldn't tell the difference between a blade hitting flesh versus fabric.

She saw blood on the knife and assumed she'd done it.

Mabel played dead, held her breath, and fooled her completely.

"Damn you, Hailey. Damn you, Cecilia. Damn the whole sixty-sixth floor! Just you wait! I'll get my revenge. I'll tear you all to pieces!"

Mabel clutched her wounded side and stumbled back downstairs.

What she didn't expect—when she finally got home, tears streaming down her face, desperate for Charles to comfort her—

Charles was already dead. Frozen.

His eyes were wide open, mouth hanging slack, like he'd spent his final moments searching for her, calling her name.

But Mabel hadn't been there.

So even in death, his eyes held reluctance. Longing.

"Charles, Charles! My Charles!"

Mabel dropped to her knees, cradling his head.

She pulled him into her arms, desperate to give him warmth one last time.

Like maybe—just maybe—if she could warm him back up, he'd come back.

But it was over. All of it.

Mabel cried until her voice went raw, until the wound in her side screamed with pain. Only then did she force herself up to treat it.

After bandaging herself, the blood loss made her even colder.

But the apartment—aside from the bed and a few warming items—had nothing left to burn!

"I have to survive. I will survive."

Shaking violently, Mabel circled the room again and again.

She even went back outside, searching the entire floor.

Nothing.

Evan and the others had stripped the place bare!

They'd practically ripped the wooden door off on their way out.

Payback for Mabel taking all the wood from the building first!

"Damn Evan. Damn Cecilia. Damn Hailey. Damn every single one of them!"

Mabel muttered deliriously. Blood loss and cold had left her body weak and fragile.

Even her mind started slipping.

"No. I have to survive. I will survive."

Mabel gritted her teeth and dragged Charles's body to the floor. Then she set her bed on fire.

As the wood caught flame, she finally felt some warmth.

She pulled his corpse close, held him tight, and talked. Kept talking. Like words could make her stronger. Like if she said enough, maybe he'd answer back.

Three days passed like that. Mabel stayed by that bed.

Charles had gone completely stiff by then. His eyes shut forever.

He was a frozen corpse now. Nothing more.

After eating and disinfecting her wound, Mabel felt a bit better.

Except... she'd burned through the entire bed, and there was nothing else left to burn.

She stared numbly around the room. The place was completely empty.

She tried tossing a metal scrap into the dying fire, but it didn't warm up at all.

The fire just got smaller.

She nearly broke down crying.

Then her eyes landed on Charles.

"Honey, don't hate me. Please... I have to live. I have to—so I can get revenge for you."

Her hands shook as she peeled off his clothes. One piece at a time. Fed them to the fire.

But fabric burned fast.

Just two hours, and all of Charles's clothing was gone.

Mabel collapsed by the fire pit, hugging her knees in despair.

God, she didn't want to die.

Not when those bastards were still out there. Not when she still had so much hate left.

Finally, with shaking hands, she reached for the last thing in the room that could burn.

Charles.

Corpses could burn. They could even burn for a long time.

To get it to catch, she poured the last of their olive oil all over him.

Chapter 18 Fugitive

Winter gear and sun protection clothing were also en route.

That night, Cecilia pulled out her laptop and began downloading novels, movies, and TV shows.

Blackie curled up beside her, watching the screen intently. Whenever small animals appeared, he'd perk up, pacing back and forth across the room, ears pricked, nose twitching as if trying to track down their scent in the air.

Amused by his reaction, Cecilia also downloaded dozens of animal documentaries.

It would be a nice way to pass the time with Blackie after the apocalypse.

To her surprise, she soon discovered he loved cartoons, too.

When the characters made exaggerated movements, his eyes would widen in fascination, as if Blackie was struggling to comprehend how such antics were possible.

Naturally, Cecilia began downloading cartoons by the dozen.

By the next day, the anti-corrosion renovations were complete. Cecilia inspected the work thoroughly, even testing it with an acidic solution she'd brought along. Everything checked out, so she paid the final installment.

As the workers packed up to leave, one burly man shot her a dark glare.

Cecilia frowned slightly—she recognized him as one of the men who'd mocked her as "just a young chick" in the corridor a few days earlier.

There was something off about him, she thought, a prickle of unease at the back of her neck.

She'd encountered too many men like him in the apocalypse—untrustworthy, vengeful—to let her guard down.

Having given the workers keys to the penthouse during the renovations, Cecilia decided to change the locks immediately.

She'd be moving in today.

The heating stoves were installed that afternoon.

She'd initially planned to buy just one, but ended up purchasing four—three for the spacious living room and one for the bedroom.

The penthouse boasted a spacious living room. Therefore, Cecilia intended to place her bed near the living room's floor-to-ceiling windows, a vantage point to keep watch on the outside world.

The fourth stove, in the bedroom, was for Blackie—giving him another warm space to roam freely.

With four stoves, they'd have no trouble weathering the upcoming extreme cold.

She moved a few essential items from her old house to the penthouse, officially taking up residence.

Having bought top-of-the-line, zero-formaldehyde materials, Cecilia could settle in right away without worry.

Exhausted from the long day, Cecilia fell asleep as soon as her head hit the pillow.

Sometime later, she was jolted awake by a wet nose nuzzling her cheek.

Blinking her eyes open, she saw Blackie standing over her, his gaze bright and sharp in the moonlight streaming through the window.

His intense stare snapped her fully alert, no trace of sleep left in her.

That was when she heard it—a faint, scraping sound coming from the front door.

Someone was tampering with the lock.

Cecilia slipped on a jacket and crept toward the door, Blackie at her heels, his paws lifted softly to avoid making a sound.

Outside, the intruder fumbled with the lock for a long time, then muttered a curse under his breath. "Why the hell is this door so damn strong? Won't budge an inch," he grumbled.

The burly worker's sinister face flashed in her mind—she'd been targeted.

After failing to open the door with a key, the man switched tactics, trying to pick the lock.

But the door Cecilia had bought was top-of-the-line, modified for extra security; the worker's efforts were futile.

Beside her, Blackie bared his teeth, muscles tensed in an attack stance—ready to pounce the second the door opened.

Cecilia pressed a finger to her lips, gesturing for silence, then slipped into the next room to call the police.

Just as she thought the intruder had given up and left, he returned, armed with a different tool, and resumed picking the lock.

This time, a series of sharp clicks cut through the silence—the first lock had given way.

Cecilia's heart skipped a beat. This worker was surprisingly skilled, far more than a typical construction hand.

After breaking through the first lock, he moved to the second, his movements light and precise—those of a professional thief.

But the moment the door swung open, Blackie launched himself forward, tackling the man to the floor with a heavy thud.

Cecilia flipped on the light switch, flooding the room with brightness.

Sure enough, the intruder was the burly worker who'd glared at her earlier.

Blackie pinned him down effortlessly, his weight keeping the man immobile.

When the police arrived, they arrested the man immediately. A quick background check revealed he was a wanted fugitive—with a 200-thousand-dollar reward for his capture.

"Miss, I don't know if you're lucky or just plain cursed," one of the officers said, shaking his head with a half-smile.

The man glared at her, hatred burning in his eyes. "Didn't expect your dog to be this tough. I'll admit defeat today, but you'd better pray I never get out of jail!"

Cecilia frowned, confused by the intensity of his resentment.

Later, the police filled her in—he'd been on the run for years after committing murder.

Being caught by her meant he'd almost certainly face life behind bars.

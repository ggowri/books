Chapter 188 A Bargain with Thomas

The veteran's eyes shifted, then he clenched his teeth and said, "Colonel Reed, we were only acting under orders from above. If you have something to say, tell your old general to take it up with General Mercer. Even if you kill us all here today, word of our commander's death will still get out. We're only soldiers carrying out orders. Military orders are absolute. We had no choice. If you kill us too, the men under your command will lose heart. They'll think their lives mean nothing to you."

He sounded bold, but in truth he was only looking for a way to save his own skin.

What he meant was simple enough. If they died here, Colonel Barnes's death would still land on Thomas's head.

So killing them would gain him nothing.

They were only soldiers following orders. If Thomas killed them, the men serving under him in future would look at their fate and wonder whether the same might happen to them one day. It would make everyone think he did not value the lives of ordinary soldiers at all.

Thomas lifted his eyelids and looked at the veteran. "Well now. What's your name? Jeremy Barnes actually had someone under him with a brain?"

The veteran gritted his teeth. "Ian Yates."

Bang.

Thomas raised his gun and shot him without hesitation.

The bullet struck him clean between the brows.

Ian died on the spot.

The other two soldiers saw their comrade fall and immediately struggled forward, desperate to throw themselves into the fight.

Thomas's men held them back and rewarded each of them with several heavy punches.

"Just like Barnes's lot. No sense of propriety at all," Thomas said coolly, blowing the smoke from the barrel of his gun. "When answering a superior officer, shouldn't you use proper respect?"

Then he walked over to the two men, who were still struggling and hurling curses, and kicked each of them once.

"You really think I wouldn't kill you? Stop trying to be clever. Behave yourselves. I can see you're loyal enough, just obeying orders from that dog Jeremy Barnes, so I'll let you go back. We're all in the military. We've all got family and friends. There's no need to throw your lives away for a man who only knows how to flatter superiors and claw his way forward through dirty tricks. But if you annoy me, I'll kill you too."

After that, the two soldiers finally fell silent.

They only stared at their dead comrade, jaws clenched tight, tears running soundlessly down their faces.

One of Thomas's own men stepped up beside him and asked, "Are we really letting them go?"

Thomas gave a cold snort. "What, should I kill every last one of them and turn into the King of Hell? Those two left alive are useless anyway. The ones with real nerve and ability were the ones at the front. Once we've dealt with everything here, we'll send them off."

Several soldiers took the two surviving Barnes men away.

Soon, only Cecilia, Arthur, and Thomas remained.

Off to one side, Cecilia let out a quiet breath of relief after watching Thomas's handling of the situation. Her opinion of him rose still further.

She had already gathered Jet into her arms and was gently stroking his head.

Jet nestled against her like a spoiled child, holding up his wing pitifully the whole time.

A moment earlier, while no one was paying attention, Cecilia had pretended to take something from her pocket and slipped out a tiny amount of Magic Spring water she had portioned off earlier, feeding Jet a little of it.

She dared not give him too much in case he decided to put on a public performance of instant healing, but even that small amount had already stopped the bleeding and left him looking far more spirited.

Now that everyone else had gone, Thomas sighed. His face carried a trace of helplessness.

"Miss Summers, you've landed me in a great deal of trouble."

Cecilia smiled faintly. "Colonel Reed, I know someone dying on your territory has caused you trouble, and trouble for the old general as well. But before the apocalypse, the Barnes family was only a minor clan. They had a bit of money in Heldon City, but no real power."

"I don't know how they managed to catch the military's attention after the apocalypse. But... Colonel Reed, I understand your attitude today, and I appreciate your help and protection."

Thomas waved a hand and cut her off. "At a time like this, Miss Summers, let's skip the formalities. Just say what you mean."

Cecilia looked at him steadily. "I repay grudges, and I repay kindness too. I know something that may be extremely valuable to your old general. Something important enough to save more of your soldiers, strengthen your forces, and help your old general gain the upper hand over the other factions..."

The more she spoke, the brighter Thomas's eyes became.

By the end, he grabbed hold of her hand. "Miss Summers, out with it."

Cecilia then quickly and simply explained what she knew about the hidden uses of acid rain.

This was the bargaining chip she intended to offer in return for the old general's help in resolving today's matter.

In truth, in her previous life, the military had eventually discovered that acid rain could trigger mutation in plants and animals.

But as far as Cecilia knew, that did not happen until the Heat Stage had already arrived.

By then, once the snow had melted and the rising temperatures had caused vast amounts of water to evaporate, the acid rain that had once been everywhere seemed to vanish as though it had never existed at all.

Even now, the acid rain lay buried beneath thick snow, and few people would think to study it.

At this point, military research still mostly assumed that animal mutations were caused by changes in climate.

Their direction was not entirely wrong.

The changes in plants, animals, and insects were indeed related to shifts in the climate.

But the true secret lay in the acid rain.

The problem was that their researchers worked mostly in laboratories and had never connected the acid rain with the changing climate, which was why they only uncovered the truth much later, during the Heat Stage.

Chapter 11 Her Life and Her Memories

Even in this life, the two of them were still sneaking around together.

Julia confirmed they'd booked the room for four nights; that evening was only the second.

A flood of revenge scenarios from novels popped into Cecilia's head—live-streaming the confrontation, showing up with a crowd to expose them, tearing Rebecca's clothes off in public to shame her...

But none of these were practical.

The Barnes family was influential; if Cecilia made the scandal public, they'd likely refuse to call off the engagement, withhold her parents' stocks and funds, turn on her completely, and even smear her reputation to save their own.

Desperate to protect their name, they might do something reckless.

Guess beating up Rebecca and humiliating her could wait until the apocalypse.

Once the order collapsed, she could make the pair pay whatever price she wanted.

"That's all I can do for you. I never liked you, but I came because we've both been cheated on," Julia said, rolling her eyes again.

Cecilia reflected on their strained relationship. It stemmed from her reserved nature, her reluctance to complain, and a few instances where she'd refused to accommodate Julia.

A sudden idea struck her, one that would require Julia's help.

Taking a deep breath, she looked at Julia sincerely. "Julia, I need your help. I was so stubborn and difficult before, but I promise I'll never oppose you or the others again."

Julia froze, clearly caught off guard by the apology.

But after a moment's hesitation, she nodded.

Cecilia smiled inwardly.

Perfect.

This time, she was determined to cut ties with Alexander and the entire Barnes family for good.

After parting ways with Julia, Cecilia contacted Alexander's grandmother, Miranda Barnes.

At over eighty, Miranda was still in excellent health. Formidable since her youth, she remained the undisputed authority in the family.

Cecilia suspected Miranda wasn't entirely unaware of Alexander's antics, but she was certain Miranda knew nothing about his affair with Rebecca.

Given her own past, Miranda despised nothing more than infidelity and betrayal.

Years ago, Alexander's father had an affair with a mistress, and Miranda had come close to breaking his legs.

What was more, in her youth, Miranda had been obsessed with Cecilia's grandfather—deeply, hopelessly obsessed.

Using this piece of history might be the key to cutting all ties with the Barnes family for good.

Cecilia told Miranda she wanted to take her for a drive, saying she'd pick the old lady up the next evening. Miranda was overjoyed, saying she'd wear her favorite silk scarf.

Hanging up the phone, Cecilia took a deep breath, silently hoping Miranda wouldn't be so enraged that she'd tear the scarf to pieces.

After a full day of running around, Cecilia felt exhausted when she got home.

Opening the door, she found Blackie sitting upright at the entrance, his head bowed and his eyes rolling upward, staring at her with a distinct air of resentment—as if saying, "Where have you been all day?"

His droopy expression was enough to make anyone laugh.

Cecilia hurried to take out a roasted chicken she'd bought on the way, tearing off two pieces to feed him.

"Blackie, look what I brought you! Come on, let me feed you!"

At the sight of food, Blackie's mood lifted slightly. He took the chicken from her hand in his mouth and trotted off to eat alone.

That evening, she took Blackie for a walk and stopped by a nearby convenience store to buy a pile of fresh vegetables.

Cecilia planned to cook some meals and store them away after her physical training that night—she was actually a skilled cook.

Besides, the 5,000 disposable containers she'd ordered online had all arrived.

Over the next few weeks, Cecilia resolved to cook as much as possible in her free time, aiming to fill all 5,000 containers before the apocalypse, which was less than three months away.

As they walked, they passed a beverage shop.

The sight of the colorful, sweet-smelling drinks made Cecilia slap her forehead in realization.

She'd stocked up on so much food over the past few days that she'd forgotten about sweet beverages—those little joys that boosted happiness.

One cup of something sweet could keep her energized all day!

Right there in the middle of the sidewalk, still holding Blackie's leash, Cecilia pulled out her phone and searched for nearby beverage shops.

For every shop with drinks she liked, she ordered thirty servings—mango smoothies, pineapple milkshakes, strawberry frappés, rose vanilla lattes, mint lemonades, and more.

She didn't miss the shop they were standing in front of, ordering forty drinks of various types.

After paying, she carried the stack of drinks to a quiet spot and tucked them away in the Space.

Cecilia would add these sweet beverages to her shopping list starting tomorrow. With that in mind, her mood lifted considerably.

Blackie had no interest in such sugary things, and Cecilia didn't dare feed him anything unfamiliar.

So, by the time they finished the walk and headed home, Cecilia was holding a large milkshake in one hand and Blackie's leash in the other, feeling utterly content.

That night, she practiced with her knife again, followed by combat skill training to build up her strength.

She also started packing up her belongings—she'd sold the house and agreed with the agent to move out within five days.

Earlier that day, Cecilia had found a reliable renovation company for the new penthouse, and the plans were to reinforce the existing simple decor—upgrading the glass and floors, installing insulation in the walls, and adding two wood-burning stoves.

The stoves would keep the entire house warm, and she planned to place her bed right next to one of them.

She briefly considered installing a radiant floor heating system, but ultimately felt the whole process—having to tear up the entire floor—was just too much hassle.

Still, she might add one if time permitted.

Most importantly, though, were the doors and windows.

When the apocalypse came, coping with extreme weather would be one challenge—but fending off people with ill intentions would be another.

Sturdy doors and windows were non-negotiable.

For the windows, Cecilia opted for bulletproof glass, with 0.8-inch-thick steel railings reinforced on the outside.

For the front door, she chose an ultra-sturdy double-door setup—an outer steel door 0.16 inches thick, and an inner door she hadn't yet selected, as she planned to shop for one in the next few days.

That night, Cecilia packed up her few belongings.

Sitting amidst a pile of odds and ends, she suddenly felt a twinge of sadness at the thought of leaving her old home.

It was the small space her parents had left her; she would never have left if it weren't so unsuitable for post-apocalyptic survival.

Among the things she packed were many items that would be useless in the apocalypse—her elementary school diary, large bow hair clips, the photo album her parents had left her, and the yearbooks signed by her classmates at graduation.

These things couldn't be eaten or drunk, but they were her life and her memories.

In her previous life, Cecilia had had no time to take these small treasures with her, forced to abandon the house and everything in it.

All she'd managed to slip into her pocket was a family photo.

Now, having the time to sort through them made her feel incredibly fortunate.

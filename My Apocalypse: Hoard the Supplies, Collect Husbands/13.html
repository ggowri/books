Chapter 13 The Day Was Packed

Yet Rebecca had gained nothing from the fiasco. She ended up joining Alexander in berating Cecilia, demanding to know her motives and standing up for Alexander.

"What a shame I missed the show," Cecilia sighed.

Figuring the pair had endured quite a scare the night before and wouldn't be up early, Cecilia set them aside for the moment to focus on her own tasks—her usual routine of walking Blackie, jogging, and buying breakfast, followed by a trip to the supermarket to check for any overlooked supplies.

Cecilia had noticed Blackie had an enormous appetite, with a particular fondness for meat.

Logan had warned her this breed was expensive to raise; his muscular build required plenty of protein, and boiling meat for him regularly was best.

Every morning, besides his dog food, she'd boil up beef, chicken breasts, and other meats.

But he wolfed them down as if he'd been starved for days.

Even after two days of this feeding routine, Cecilia noticed he'd lost a little weight.

Left with no choice, she called Logan—only to learn she'd been underfeeding him.

So that morning, after their walk, Cecilia watched as Blackie buried his entire head in his bowl, gobbling down his food. It was clear she'd need to buy far more meat than initially planned.

And once the apocalypse hit, meat would become a scarce commodity. In the early days, no one knew if mutated creatures were edible, and everyone feared them. People would fight fiercely over a single bite of meat, even a single sausage.

Her plan was to wrap up things with Alexander today, arrange for the renovation workers tomorrow, then take Blackie to the suburban slaughterhouses.

She remembered several large ones on the outskirts of the city, processing cattle, sheep, and pigs.

The day was packed.

During the morning, Cecilia oversaw the construction crew's work.

Installing the new doors and windows was relatively straightforward, but the walls required extensive renovations—coating both the interior and exterior with anti-corrosion materials, including the penthouse's rooftop. She also planned to reinforce the floors with additional materials.

Earlier, she'd filed the necessary paperwork with the property management, who hadn't asked many questions, only reminding her not to damage the building's esthetic.

Shortly after ten o'clock, her phone rang—Alexander, his voice seething with anger.

But before he could say more than a few words, Cecilia quickly composed herself and spoke in a trembling, tearful voice, "Alex... I-I sent the wrong room number yesterday. I was so nervous I turned off my phone—I was scared of getting your rejection... When you didn't show up, I thought you'd said no, so I fell asleep. I only saw your messages and Rebecca's this morning."

Silence fell on the other end of the line.

Cecilia was always strong and resilient, rarely showing tears.

That quivering, held-back sob caught Alexander off guard.

He'd been ready to unleash a torrent of insults, but the words died in his throat.

A cold laugh bubbled up in Cecilia's chest—she'd him right where she wanted him. She pressed on, her voice still shaky. "And I read Rebecca's messages. I realize now my earlier idea was reckless. If fate made me send the wrong room number, it means I wasn't meant to rush into this. I think... I'll wait until after our engagement."

Alexander grew anxious at that.

But no matter how much he pleaded or argued, Cecilia clung to two points—"Rebecca talked sense into me" and "this is fate's will"—refusing to budge on the idea of sleeping together.

In the end, Alexander had no choice but to hang up in frustration.

As soon as the call ended, Cecilia could barely contain her laughter.

Curious, Blackie tilted his head, watching her curl up on the floor, doubled over in giggles that made her stomach ache.

It took her ages to calm down.

When she finally looked up, she found Blackie's face right in front of hers.

"You'll never understand how happy I am right now, Blackie," she said softly.

Alexander wouldn't break up with Rebecca over this, but he'd definitely harbor resentment toward her.

And to smooth things over, Rebecca would go out of her way to please Alexander that night—perfect timing for her next plan.

Besides, making the two scoundrels miserable filled her with immense satisfaction.

Once she'd composed herself, it was back to work.

That afternoon, she headed to the building materials market to pick out a security door—solid steel, thick enough to resist most tools.

She had the store reinforce the lock for extra safety, then had it delivered.

Now the penthouse had two layers of protection—a heavy stainless steel outer door and a new security door inside.

If someone knocked, she could open the inner door and speak to them through the security door, keeping herself safe.

By the end of the day, the doors and windows were installed. Tomorrow, she'd oversee the application of the anti-corrosion materials.

At seven o'clock that evening, Cecilia headed to the Barnes Manor to pick up Miranda.

Miranda insisted on asking her to stay for dinner, but Cecilia declined, saying she'd discovered a wonderful new restaurant. Since they were going for a drive, she wanted to treat Miranda to dinner there instead.

Although the Barnes family's bodyguards and Alexander's sister, Sarah Barnes, wanted to come along, Cecilia didn't object.

Chapter 96 The Barns Came

Before her, the man Cecilia had been clutching was already dead—torn apart by the mutated rat.

The creature lunged toward Cecilia, but she kicked it away with a single, brutal motion.

In the stunned silence of the crowd, Cecilia walked over to the man she'd stabbed and pulled her knife free. A cold smile spread across her face as she turned to the others. "So tell me—what's scarier? The mutated rat, or me?"

The crowd scattered in terror.

Everyone felt a bone-deep chill run through them.

Without a flicker of emotion, Cecilia grabbed both troublemakers and hurled them off the building.

Hailey stepped forward, offering to clean up the blood by the entrance.

Cecilia didn't argue—she left the mess to Hailey.

Over the next few days, the group worked together clearing snow from the rooftop. It was backbreaking work, but they finally managed to clear most of it. Given how slowly the snow was falling now, they wouldn't need to repeat the process anytime soon.

No one complained. They all understood the stakes—better to be prepared than sorry.

When they finished that day's work, Arthur suggested they all come to his place for dinner.

After spending these days together, they'd grown closer. With such a massive project behind them, everyone was in good spirits and agreed.

Cecilia and Hailey both brought what food they could spare.

They shared stories about themselves over the meal—it was actually pleasant. Even Blackie got several chunks of meat and sat beside the table, happily chewing away.

Cecilia had to admit, Arthur could really put away food. The man was an absolute bottomless pit—he ate like he hadn't seen food in weeks.

Hailey had gradually opened up over the past few days, finally sharing pieces of her own story.

They were deep in conversation, everyone relaxed and laughing, when the rumble of engines cut through the air outside.

For days, no government vehicles or boats had come through.

The world outside had been dead silent—not even birdsong.

The sudden roar of engines left everyone stunned.

Cecilia and the others rushed to the window, grabbing binoculars to peer down. Three massive SUVs had pulled up outside!

From their floor, they couldn't make out the details, but the vehicles were clearly modified—oversized tires, reinforced bodies, anti-slip chains. Even on the treacherous icy roads, they moved like they were on solid ground.

Cecilia's brow furrowed slightly. She had a sinking feeling about this.

The surviving residents in nearby buildings erupted in excitement. They thought the government had finally come to rescue them!

Someone rushed downstairs and ran toward the SUVs, shouting, "Quick! Take me to the shelter! I need to get to the shelter!"

BANG!

A gunshot cracked through the air.

The residents who'd been moving toward the vehicles froze in terror. Most were ordinary people who'd only seen guns on TV or the internet—they'd never witnessed real firearms or heard actual gunshots.

At that moment, snow began to fall again—fat, heavy flakes drifting down through the wind.

"Nobody move! Stay where you are! Anyone takes a step, and I'll blow their brains out!"

A burly man in a fur coat jumped down from the lead vehicle, a long vertical scar running down his face.

Though he was vicious toward the residents, his demeanor completely changed when he approached the rear passenger door. He became respectful, practically bowing and scraping.

He carefully opened the back door with an ingratiating smile. "Ma'am, please watch your step."

Yes, these vehicles belonged to the Barnes family estate.

And the passenger in the back seat was none other than Miranda Barnes.

Since the apocalypse began, Miranda Barnes had been worried sick about her grandson, Alexander. After all, he was the sole heir of the Barnes family fortune—their future hope and inheritor.

But during the acid rain crisis, the Barnes family had stumbled upon a major opportunity that could elevate them to unprecedented heights. They'd been too busy to spare resources looking for Alexander.

She'd assumed that since Alexander lived in the commercial district, he wouldn't lack for food and supplies.

Later, when the government established shelters, she'd specifically arranged for people to locate Alexander and bring him to safety.

But word came back from the shelter that Alexander had never arrived.

So Miranda had come to this building to search for her grandson.

What she hadn't expected was to find Alexander's corpse the moment she stepped out of the vehicle.

He lay face-up, eyes wide open, surrounded by a pool of frozen blood. His hands and feet had been severed, leaving only stumps. His body had frozen solid with the ice beneath him, like some grotesque sculpture.

"Alex! My grandson!"

The old woman collapsed to her knees, her anguished wails echoing across the empty landscape.

"Who did this? Who turned him into this?"

Miranda struggled to her feet, trembling as she turned to the scarred man. "Find them. Find whoever did this to him!"

The man nodded grimly.

He gestured, and five or six more men emerged from the vehicles—all heavily armed, over six feet tall, and built like tanks. From their presence alone, it was clear they could tear the entire building apart.

"Ma'am, please wait in the car while we... while we remove Alexander and bring him home."

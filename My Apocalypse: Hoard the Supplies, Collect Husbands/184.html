Chapter 184 The Search Begins


The soldiers behind Colonel Barnes followed his lead and cocked their weapons as well.

To everyone's surprise, Colonel Reed did not flinch in the slightest. He simply reached into his pocket and pulled out a grenade.

"Jeremy Barnes, do you really want us all dead together?" he said coldly. "Don't push your luck. Ten years ago, when I was fighting on the front line, you were probably still grovelling at some general's feet. Death doesn't frighten me. I, Thomas, have no wife, no family, just my own life. But your Barnes family..." He let out a mocking laugh. "How many people would die if you went down? Not worth it, is it?"

Sweat instantly broke out across Colonel Barnes's forehead.

He knew Thomas was not someone to trifle with.

Ten years earlier, Auroria had gone to war with neighbouring countries. Thomas had been sent out with the troops then, just an ordinary private. He fought for three years, and when he returned, he was already a captain. One could only imagine how many military honours he must have earned in that time.

Only a few years later, Thomas had risen to the rank of colonel, and he was only in his thirties.

Jeremy, meanwhile, was already in his forties. Even with the Barnes family pulling strings for him, he had only managed to become a colonel as well, and the family treated it as though smoke had risen from their ancestral graves in blessing.

But Thomas was different.

Thomas was an orphan. Everything he had, he had earned on his own.

Within the military, he was known by three simple words.

Mad as hell.

Still, Jeremy could not retreat. Behind him stood the entire Barnes family.

He had been searching for a Space for so long, and this was the first time he had truly felt he might be close.

He also knew Cecilia had an old emerald left to her by her grandfather, which made the whole thing seem even more plausible.

Just as he was wondering what to do next, a young soldier came running up from downstairs.

He was drenched in sweat. Leaning close, he whispered a few words into Thomas's ear.

Jeremy watched Thomas raise an eyebrow. Then, to his complete surprise, Thomas immediately put on a broad smile.

"Well now, old Barnes, let's not keep arguing," Thomas said cheerfully. "If this is an order from your general, then by all means, go on up."

As he spoke, he slipped the grenade back into his pocket, then pressed down the rifle of the soldier beside him.

He even stepped aside with exaggerated politeness and left the stairway clear.

For a moment, Jeremy was dumbfounded.

What was this madman playing at now?

Still, with the path open in front of him, he had no intention of wasting more time tangling with Thomas here. He gave a sharp wave of his hand and led his men upstairs.

Thomas grinned as he watched Jeremy disappear from sight.

The little rascal.

What the young soldier had just reported was that Arthur and Cecilia were already on their way up and should arrive very soon.

Thomas did not actually want to make an outright enemy of Jeremy Barnes or the people backing him, so once he knew Cecilia and Arthur were nearly there, he simply let Jeremy go up first and do the hard work.

After all, Jeremy still had to break through the electric barrier on the sixty fourth floor, and after that, get past the several layers of defences Arthur and Cecilia had put in place.

By the time he was through all that, it was anyone's guess whether Jeremy's men or Cecilia and Arthur would reach the flat first.

What Thomas did not know, however, was that Jeremy had come prepared.

They had already learned that Cecilia and Arthur had set up an electrified door and several more entrances secured with multiple locks.

So they had brought all the right equipment.

The moment they reached the sixty fourth floor, they put on insulated gloves, and one of the specialists quickly neutralised the electric barrier.

Then they moved higher.

Faced with the chained doors ahead, they brought out heavy duty cutters designed specifically for locks.

After a few hard snips, the thick chains hanging across the doors were cut clean through.

The locks themselves were handled by another specialist, who swiftly prised them open.

In no time at all, they had reached the corridor on the sixty sixth floor.

Jeremy looked at the doors in front of him and asked, "Which one is Cecilia' flat?"

A man stepped forward from the back and pointed to the door at the very end.

"That one."

He was a young soldier who had previously been tasked with gathering information on Cecilia from Mabel and others in the shelter so that this operation would go smoothly.

Jeremy let out a cold snort and swept his hand forward.

"Get that door open."

At that moment, Cecilia and Arthur had only just reached the fiftieth floor.

Someone immediately stepped forward with tools and began working on the lock.

But Cecilia's front door was much harder to force than the outer ones, so even opening the first lock took a full minute.

By then, Cecilia and Arthur had only made it to the fifty fifth floor.

Cecilia's pace had clearly slowed. She was breathing hard now, drawing great ragged breaths.

Arthur glanced at her. "I'm faster. I'll go on ahead."

At a time like this, Cecilia was in no mood for false pride. She nodded, then looked at Blackie beside them.

"You too."

Without hesitation, Blackie sprang after Arthur.

Cecilia clenched her teeth as she watched Arthur and Blackie bound upwards in just a few strides. It reassured her slightly, but she still forced herself onward, climbing the stairs as fast as she could.

Meanwhile, after Jeremy's men broke through the first lock, they quickly worked open the second as well.

Jeremy was delighted. He strode forward at once, determined to be first through.

But the moment he took a step, something flew straight at his face and slashed at his head with a paw.

"Ah! What the hell is that?"

Jeremy shouted in alarm.

Behind him, a few of the soldiers had already started firing.

But whatever it was moved far too quickly, and dawn had not yet fully broken. The corridor was still dim, making it impossible to see clearly.

The soldiers immediately began to panic.

But the corridor was cramped, and one experienced soldier quickly shoved the muzzles of the submachine guns down.

"Don't fire! You'll hit our own men!"

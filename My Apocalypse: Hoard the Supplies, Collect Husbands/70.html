Chapter 70 Tackle the Delivery Boxes

Ashley stood at the top of the stairs, her face pale with fury. She stamped her foot in frustration.

Upstairs, Cecilia remained unaware.

She had long since retreated to her apartment, the stairwell door locked securely behind her.

Back in her living room, Cecilia stood before the floor-to-ceiling window, watching the distant boat's lights appear and vanish on the water.
Just like last time.

The world was sinking into a new normal: no power, no running water, no internet. Police and military forces were stretched impossibly thin.

Official efforts would be focused on securing critical supplies like emergency grain reserves, she knew, leaving countless pockets of survivors to fend for themselves in a brutal contest for dwindling resources.

Her earlier confrontation with Ashley had left a sour taste, neither anger nor victory, just a dull residue of tension. Seeking a distraction, Cecilia pulled a tall, frosty glass of homemade iced juice—the real kind, steeped long with lemon and mint.

She took a long sip, the cool sweetness a balm. Better still were the chewy cubed coconut she'd added; each sip yielded a few, bursting with mild sweetness between her teeth.

The simple pleasure instantly lifted her mood.

Finishing her juice, she headed for the spare room to tackle the mountain of delivery boxes.

"It's quite the workout. If only Blackie and Jet could lend a paw," she sighed.

Cecilia stepped into the cleared space and summoned several dozen parcels from her Space. Facing the small hill of cardboard, she couldn't help a wry twist of her lips.

As if understanding, Blackie trotted into the room, nosed through the pile with purpose, and emerged dragging a specific box. It deposited it proudly at her feet, tail whipping the air, eyes bright with expectation.

Cecilia's eyebrows rose. This one had his full attention.

"Alright, let's see what you found, clever boy."

Taking the box, she settled on the floor and tore it open.

A soft laugh escaped her. Of course. It was a case of premium dog food—venison formula, Blackie's absolute favorite.

Under its unwavering, hopeful gaze, she popped a can open right then and there, setting it down for it as a reward.

Jet fluttered over to investigate the boxes, circled twice, but found nothing of avian interest. With a dismissive chirp, he flew to his perch by the tablet, resuming the drama Cecilia had left paused.

Spreading a small cushion on the floor, Cecilia began her task in earnest.

The variety from the distribution center was staggering.

Food and drink were a given.

In the days before the final shutdown, doomsday prep had gone mainstream.

People had ordered everything imaginable online, and it had all piled up, undelivered.

She unpacked crate after crate: several cases of cola and sparkling water, bags of spicy beef jerky and pepperoni sticks—perfect savory snacks for a movie night—and an assortment of other non-perishables.

Then came the miscellaneous items: cans of interior paint, an assemble-it-yourself children's play kitchen, a nylon hammock, and so on.

Seemingly useless now, but with her Recycler, nothing was truly waste.

A real find was a set of three high-end, ruggedized two-way radios, a latest-generation model from a top brand.

Cecilia smiled, turning one over in her hands. One for her, one for Arthur, one for Hailey.

They had no immediate need, but the day would come when they'd need to venture out.

The radios were compact, barely spanning her palm, with a lanyard for wearing around the neck. According to the manual, they had a clear range of nearly two miles.

Next, she uncovered several inflatable rafts.

Probably purchased during the weeks of relentless rain by those with a sudden sense of foreboding.

Cecilia had stocked a rigid inflatable boat for herself, considering it far more practical, but one could never have too many options for mobility.

If she didn't need them, they'd make for valuable trade goods.

The afternoon slipped away in a flurry of unpacking, and amid the clutter, Cecilia stumbled upon something unexpectedly useful.

It was a sidecar assembly—detached from its motorcycle, missing both the engine and the front frame.

Only the passenger compartment and two wheels remained.

Her eyes drifted from the sidecar to Blackie, who was happily gnawing on a new bone she'd pulled from one of the parcels. A slow, mischievous smile spread across Cecilia's face.

A dog's purpose revealed itself in time. With a little modification, this sidecar could become a small cart—perfect for Blackie to pull.

Just then, Blackie paused mid-chew, sensing a familiar, icy stare from his owner.

It froze, the bone forgotten in its jaws.

By six in the evening, Cecilia's energy was spent.

Her body ached as though it might fall apart, and a dull stiffness had settled in her neck.

And yet, she'd only made it through a third of the deliveries.

The remaining packages still waited, piled inside the Space.

She stepped inside to check the day's yield from the Recycler—only to find a foot-soaking basin waiting for her.

Irritation began to rise until she noticed a slip of paper tucked beneath it.

Cecilia picked it up and read the bold print: [Has survival in this cold, tense apocalypse left you worn out? Does the Ice Stage push you toward despair? Try a soak. Remember—staying alive isn't just about food. You need comfort, peace, and a moment for your spirit. So go on... soak those feet.]

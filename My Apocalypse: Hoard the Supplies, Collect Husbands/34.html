Chapter 34 Rebecca?

Once the photos and text hit the internet, everything exploded. The experts who'd been saying the acid rain was harmless suddenly went quiet, and more doomsday theories started flooding social media.

Cecilia looked out her living room window and saw the massive shopping center down the street packed with cars. People were pushing shopping carts loaded with bags and boxes, hauling everything they could carry back to their homes.

Online, raincoat sales were going through the roof, and a lot of stores were already sold out.

Comment sections were full of people ripping into the bloggers who'd claimed the acid rain would stop soon or wasn't dangerous.

Cecilia sighed and took Blackie out for another walk. This time, they stayed out for over an hour, and when she got back, she noticed red spots had appeared on her face.

Her brows furrowed.
Only two days in, and the acid concentration already increased this much?

Clearly, not everything was playing out the same way as her previous life.

Last time, the acid rain had started out weak and only got stronger after about two weeks when some areas started flooding.

But this time, after just two days, the rain was already strong enough to cause skin damage in under an hour.

Her phone started blowing up with notifications. She opened it and saw that every group chat was sharing the same video. Someone had stayed out in the acid rain a bit too long, and their face had literally rotted.

The guy was a vegetable farmer. He'd been watching his crops get destroyed by the rain and wanted to build a shelter over his yard with his wife.

But while they were putting it up, his face got burned.

In the video, the farmer was crying and pointing at the completely withered plants behind him. "All these vegetables are ruined. We used to have a tarp over the yard, but after two days of rain, it corroded right through. One night, and the whole thing was full of holes.

"My wife and I thought we'd take advantage of the lighter rain and put up something thicker while wearing our raincoats. But the wind blew my hood off and I didn't think much of it..."

Then he showed everyone the damage to his face. He leaned into the camera and the rotted skin filled the screen. Raw flesh, some areas oozing black fluid. That was definitely what acid corrosion looked like.

Cecilia stared at the light rain falling outside and sighed quietly. This world was about to descend into chaos.

Sure enough, by afternoon, news reports were coming in from all over about people fighting over food. In some rural areas, there were even reports of people breaking into closed grocery stores to loot supplies.

The cops arrested them and publicly shamed them online.

The government started making official statements, telling everyone not to panic, to stay indoors as much as possible, and assuring people they were working on solutions. They kept warning everyone not to believe rumors.

Cecilia didn't have any family left, so she wasn't in any family group chats.

But the building's residents' group chat was buzzing every day with people bragging about how much stuff they'd stocked up.

Plenty of them were posting photos of their hauls, mountains of food stacked up and looking tempting as hell.

The comments underneath were full of people going "goals!" and "damn, nice!"

The more praise someone got, the more people jumped in to show off their own stockpiles.

The whole chat was practically a party, everyone encouraging each other and competing to see who had the most supplies.

Cecilia looked at all those usernames with apartment numbers attached and felt genuinely worried for them.

When the apocalypse finally hit and society collapsed, these people would be the first ones everyone went after. Easy targets, sitting ducks.

Five days later, the acid rain still hadn't let up. In fact, it was getting stronger.

Now it had reached the point where even umbrellas didn't help anymore.

People who had to go out for supplies were getting creative. Some were using pot lids as shields, others were carrying tables over their heads. But most people just stayed inside.

Most grocery stores had shut down, and plenty of companies gave their employees temporary leave. Factories stopped running too. The entire city had ground to a halt, but really, it wasn't just the city. It was the whole world.

As of today, Cecilia's apartment complex still had running water, electricity, and internet. But she'd seen posts online from people saying their neighborhoods were already losing water, power, and connectivity one by one.

The acid rain was eating through power lines, water pipes, and gas lines. It was only a matter of time before the entire city went dark.

The city tried sending out repair crews, but it was like trying to bail out the ocean with a bucket.

Cecilia had actually watched a utility worker with acid burns eating holes in his hands still trying to fix power lines high up on a pole.

He lost his grip and fell. Luckily, an ambulance got there fast and took him away, but according to posts online, hospitals were running out of beds.

Cecilia checked the school group chat and saw that classes had been canceled and everyone was panicking.

For safety reasons, the school wasn't sending students home. Instead, they were keeping everyone quarantined in the dorms.

Julia told her the school had stockpiled supplies and the cafeteria was still serving meals every day. That gave Cecilia some relief. After all, those were her classmates and teachers.

But she didn't expect to spot a familiar name in the apartment complex group chat—Rebecca.

Rebecca introduced herself in the chat, saying she'd just moved in, and posted a picture of herself in a bikini from some trip.

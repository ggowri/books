Chapter 48 Neighbor

Cecilia recalled her previous life and realized the authorities still didn't have the means to intervene or relocate anyone.

That left only one other possibility.

The people upstairs must have found some empty units and broken in, moving their stuff inside.

Thinking this, Cecilia lightened her steps and shot upward faster.

Passing one floor, she caught voices in the hallway. There were two men talking.

Man A said, "Looks like the apocalypse is really here. The first floor's completely underwater. Did you see that family? The woman was crying her eyes out. But that guy on that floor, he's ruthless. Two slaps and the woman's dazed, following him like a lamb while moving all their stuff."

Man B replied, "That guy's a coward. What's the use of hitting a woman? In the end, she moved most of the stuff while he just stood there smoking. Still, if this really is the end of the world, he better watch his back. Let's make sure we take turns keeping watch on this floor at night."

Man A said, "Sure. We've been best friends for years, and there aren't many people on this floor anyway. We've got to look out for each other."

Man B replied, "Yeah. How's your food supply? I didn't stock up properly before, now..." Then, he sighed.

Man A said, "Me? Between the kid and the dog, I don't even know what to do. Probably won't be two days before that dog gets slaughtered first."

Hearing this, Cecilia snorted silently and tuned out.

Glancing at the floor number—23rd—she made a mental note.

If she ran into these two later, she'd have to be careful.

From their conversation, it was clear they were somewhat alert to the dangers of the apocalypse.

But they hadn't stockpiled much. Even at the very start, they were already panicking.

In that case, it was likely they'd turn desperate later, stealing food and doing things no decent person would.

Cecilia kept climbing, and the building was unusually quiet.

She noticed that on some floors, people had blocked the hallways with random junk—probably realizing what was coming next.

She took a short break midway, thinking to herself that climbing all these stairs was no joke.

When she reached the 58th floor, she suddenly heard someone softly crying.

It was a woman.

Cecilia slowed her steps even more, not wanting the woman to hear her.

Luckily, the woman was too absorbed in her sobs to expect anyone climbing at this hour.

Cecilia peeked cautiously from the stairs and saw that the woman was surrounded by piles of items, sorting through them while crying.

Many of the things looked water-damaged. This must be the family from the first floor that the two men had been talking about.

The woman was small and slight, barely five feet tall. She was fairly attractive, but now, with her hair messy and face unkempt, she looked worn down.

Cecilia frowned. Seeing her handle all that stuff alone, she felt even more contempt for the man of the house.

Just then, a man's furious shout boomed from inside the apartment. "Stop your crying! You're driving me insane! Are you looking for a beating?"

The woman quickly covered her mouth, terrified to make a sound.

But the man wasn't done. He stormed out, swinging fists and kicking, landing blows without any care.

The woman curled on the floor, holding her head, crying quietly, and wailing softly.

The man was drunk, and he didn't seem to know or care how hard he was hitting her.

Minutes later, he finally stopped.

Before retreating back inside, he delivered one final kick. "Useless!"

The woman lay sprawled on the floor, barely moving, too shocked to even get up.

Cecilia ground her teeth in anger. What a piece of trash!

But in the apocalypse, she had seen far worse.

People dismembered in the streets, children sold for survival, women forced into unspeakable work...

Violence was just the lowest form of control.

She shook her head. Other people's family business wasn't her concern, and she had no intention of getting involved. Besides, she couldn't risk exposing herself while scavenging supplies across the hall, so she kept moving briskly upstairs.

Still, she memorized the couple on the 58th floor.

They were trouble.

The man was violent, and the woman was timid. But even a timid woman under enough pressure could turn into an accomplice.

Of course, it was also possible she might fight back.

Time would tell.

Cecilia took advantage of the fact that the woman hadn't gotten up yet and continued climbing.

When she passed her own floor, she noticed a makeshift security door had been installed, sealing off the exit tightly.

She raised an eyebrow, figuring those two girls weren't entirely clueless.

But when Cecilia reached her floor, she froze.

Someone had moved in next door!

The once-empty hallway was now cluttered with half-sorted belongings.

Fitness equipment and boxes of all sizes—some unmarked, some too jumbled to tell what they held—were scattered carelessly. It was as if the new tenant didn't fear theft at all.

On Cecilia's own door, a note was taped: [From your neighbor.]

The handwriting swirled with flourishes. It was definitely from a man.

Cecilia scanned the area once more, then frowned slightly and slipped carefully into her apartment, making sure the new neighbor wasn't watching.

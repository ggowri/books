Chapter 22 Looming

The area was extremely isolated, half-developed at best. No park rangers patrolled here, and even hikers rarely ventured this far.

Cecilia had specifically chosen a steep cliff edge, marked with a sign that read "Dangerous Slope—No Trespassing," just a few yards away.

Once there, she waved her hand again, releasing Harvey from the Space.

Stumbling out, he found himself standing right at the cliff's edge. His face turned as white as a sheet.

"W-Where was I? What was that place? What's going on?" he stammered, his voice trembling.

Cecilia stared at him, her smile sweet yet chilling. "Harvey, do you remember what you did to me?"

Harvey's eyes widened as he was still trapped in panic.

Images of his cruelty in her past life flashed through Cecilia's mind.

He tried to step back, but Cecilia grabbed his arm, shoving a knife into his chest.

He struggled, gasping for air, but she drove the knife in again and again.

When his eyes finally dimmed and lost their light, Cecilia pushed his body over the cliff.

Watching his figure disappear into the abyss below, Cecilia let out a cold laugh. "You were never a good man, even in my past life. Colluding with Alexander and Rebecca... you thought you could use me again? This time, you practically handed me the chance to kill you."

With Harvey gone, her thoughts turned to Rebecca and Alexander. A sinister smile appeared on her face.

When the apocalypse came, she'd make sure they suffered far more than he ever did.

A quick death at the cliff was too merciful for them.

As Cecilia had predicted, no one ever found Harvey's body.

Given his usual carefree, irresponsible reputation, even his parents assumed he'd run off to party somewhere at first.

It wasn't until several days later that they grew worried and reported him missing to the police.

The police launched an investigation but hit a dead end.

Harvey's social circle was far too messy—while pursuing Cecilia, he'd also been seeing four or five other girls, texting them more frequently than he did her.

The police did question Cecilia, but with no evidence linking her to the disappearance, they only took a statement and let her go.

The Space was truly a game-changing weapon.

Days blurred into weeks, and the summer vacation passed in a flurry of morning workouts, shopping, cooking, and stockpiling supplies.

Soon, the start of the school year approached.

Cecilia flexed her arm, feeling the faint outline of muscle—her past-life fighting skills were returning, too.

Blackie, meanwhile, had grown much bigger under her care. His wounds had healed, his fur sleek and shiny, his chest muscles firm and strong.

The dog was full of energy, with a mischievous streak that showed now and then.

Most of the items she'd ordered online had arrived: clothes, generators, solar panels, daily necessities, food, snacks, dog food—everything she could need.

Only a large order of grain remained, set to arrive in the next few days.

Surprisingly, over 10 million dollars still remained in her account.

Even though Cecilia had already stockpiled plenty of grain, food, clothing, and daily necessities, she knew well enough that when it came to these essentials, one could never have too much.

But Cecilia wanted to put the remaining money to better use.

She refused to spend it all on supplies; she planned to do a second round of stockpiling once the apocalypse began, when she might secure even more.

What Cecilia hadn't expected was that the apocalypse would come early.

Cecilia remembered it clearly—the end had started with ten days of rain across the globe.

At first, the rain wasn't acidic and wasn't too heavy, letting people go about their daily lives normally.

But after those ten days, the rain intensified—and acid seeped in.

Those who went out without an umbrella returned with red rashes on their faces.

Some even suffered blistering and rot on their hands.

News reports warned of toxic rain caused by environmental pollution, urging citizens to wear umbrellas or raincoats and avoid exposing their skin at all costs.

What no one anticipated, though, was that the rain's acidity would spike sharply after five more days.

Even umbrellas would corrode and develop holes after just ten minutes in the downpour.

Soon, cars and buildings outside began to erode too.

People were trapped indoors, and society's order collapsed completely.

The apocalypse had truly arrived.

Cecilia called the school to take a leave of absence, claiming illness.

She then reorganized her finances and supplies.

More than enough food and daily necessities were stored away—enough to last a lifetime, even with a hundred Blackies.

Just one problem remained—the shelves and water storage tanks she'd ordered were delayed, set to arrive the next day.

Once she got the tanks, Cecilia needed to stock up on as much clean water as possible. When the acid rain fully hit, most of the world's water sources would be contaminated, making clean water one of the most sought-after resources.

Then there were the vehicles.

Cecilia had her eye on two RVs and two rugged off-road trucks, but the delivery date was set for that Friday—three days away.

Time was running out, and with the apocalypse looming, she couldn't afford to wait even a single day longer.

Chapter 76 They Deserved It

Among these groups, there was one in particular.

It consisted of only about a dozen people. The two most formidable members were twin burly men, and their leader was a man with beady, shifty eyes.

Now it seemed that man was Bryan!

Cecilia had only encountered this group once in her past life.

At that time, they had cornered two young girls.

The girls looked so young that in a normal society, they would still have been innocent students.

But these scumbags had leered and violated them.

No matter how the girls screamed and pleaded, they didn't stop. Instead, they laughed heartily.

Cecilia had been hiding in a nearby garbage truck at that time, her body curled up in fear.

She was terrified of being discovered and dragged out. Especially when she heard these bastards discussing whether to keep the girls as a food reserve for future use after they'd had their way with them, she nearly cried.

It had been just a year into the apocalypse, and cannibalism hadn't become common yet.

But these people had already turned into beasts, willing to do anything for a taste of tender flesh.

So, when Cecilia saw the twin brutes, she was determined not to let any of them leave the mall alive.

It was for the revenge of those two little girls in her past life, for the fear she'd endured back then, for all the people who might be threatened and persecuted by them in the future, and for her own safety.

These people had to die!

With that thought in mind, Cecilia gave the dagger she'd used to kill Bryan a hard flick. Then she walked over to the unconscious twin brutes. With a swift motion, she struck each of them once, putting them out of their misery.

"You got off easy," she murmured, then gave each of the brothers a hard kick.

She thought it a blessing for others that such scumbags had died in the early days of the apocalypse.

Arthur walked over and looked at the woman who had just killed three men without a change in her expression. "Ms. Summers, care to explain why you killed them all?" he asked, though there was admiration in his eyes.

"Because they deserved it," Cecilia replied, then asked playfully, "Mr. Barnes, I noticed you didn't hesitate at all when you pointed your knife at ordinary people. Care to explain why?"

She emphasized the words "ordinary people" to let Arthur know that she was starting to doubt his claim of being a former soldier.

A glint of amusement flashed in Arthur's eyes, and his eyes widened slightly, but his face remained calm. "I saw them harassing women. I thought a little punishment would make them mend their ways.

"But last night when I went out to look for supplies, I heard they'd driven an old man to his death. Keeping scumbags like them alive would be an insult to my identity as a former soldier."

Cecilia hadn't expected Arthur to explain so clearly.

And the fact that he so easily admitted going downstairs to look for supplies last night made her trust him a little more.

This was because his words were like an olive branch, indicating that he considered her one of his own.

At the same time, Cecilia's mind sharpened. She caught on to the phrase "a little punishment" in Arthur's words.

Just as she was about to ask, Arthur preempted her.

"Yes, I killed the bastard downstairs," he said.

Cecilia nodded slightly.

Earlier, she had briefly wondered who could have slipped into the bedroom, killed a sleeping man, and left Phoebe, lying right beside him, completely unaware.

Arthur had crossed her mind as a possibility. At the time, she didn't know him well enough to be sure.

"I approve of your methods, Mr. Barnes," Cecilia said, extending her hand.

Arthur shook it without hesitation.

Nearby, Hailey had been stunned by their ruthless efficiency.

But she quickly collected herself. As she learned that Arthur was the one who had killed the man who assaulted her—and by extension, punished Phoebe—a sharp, vindictive thrill shot through her.

She clenched her fists, pounding them against her still-trembling thighs. She needed to become strong, to not hold the others back. This new world demanded she adapt quickly.

They transported their haul back to the apartment.

True to their earlier agreement, Hailey took only a small share, leaving the majority for Cecilia and Arthur. She even set aside a portion for Blackie.

In the end, Hailey's portion amounted to a few bags of flour, some pasta, several cans and vacuum-sealed meats, a couple cases of water and soda, assorted sundries, and one rigid inflatable boat.

She only accepted the boat after Cecilia and Arthur insisted they split the two evenly.

Hailey harbored no resentment. On the contrary, she was grateful.

Cecilia didn't offer to give up her own share out of pity.

While she had ample supplies and was pleased with Hailey's attitude, a deal was a deal. Their agreement stood.

Once everything was moved, each of them took a rigid inflatable boat and made another trip to the mall.

Chapter 41 The Boss

Cecilia seized the moment and, with a thought, sent the sheep into the pasture inside her Space.

She only hoped the rabbits there would play nicely. At the very least, the grassland should be enough to keep the sheep alive.

It was obvious this livestream studio usually focused on pets.

Inside one of the cages, Cecilia found the body of a dead cat.

She could only shake her head and sigh.

In the apocalypse, people were barely surviving. Animals locked in cages and left unattended never stood a chance.

Across from the cages was a large cabinet, packed from top to bottom with pet supplies.

There was feed for livestock, canned cat food, and dry kibble.

Sadly, none of it mattered. The animals trapped inside the cages could not reach any of it.

Cecilia did not have the luxury of mourning the dead. She took all the pet food along with the entire cabinet and moved on.

Once she had cleared most of the third floor, she headed up to the fourth.

The fourth floor was also a product marketing company, but this one was something else.

The entire floor had been decorated in a rustic, farmhouse style. The walls were painted white to resemble snowdrifts, with bundles of dried corn and strings of red peppers hanging as decoration. Even the floor was designed to look like tire tracks pressed into snow after a winter storm.

Still, that was not the most interesting part.

What really caught her eye were the signs above each office door.

One read "Worst Performer of the Month," clearly reserved for whoever had bombed their sales.

Another said "Top Performer of the Month," meant for the star employee.

There was even an office labeled "Deal Makers," obviously the team in charge of pulling in investors.

Inside the offices, most of the contents were similar. In one room, Cecilia found stacks of potato chips, spicy snacks, and junk food. Another room was filled with canned fruit. One held drinks close to their expiration dates. Yet another was stocked with dark chocolate and other low-calorie snacks.

There were also stone prints, handmade knitted items, and cross-stitch pieces. They were not especially useful, but she collected them anyway.

In one of the rooms, she spotted tanks filled with all kinds of ornamental fish.

With a single thought, Cecilia swept all the fish into her Space.

She did not have a Magic Spring there yet, but when she had time, she planned to dig a small pond and raise a few fish in the spring water to see what kind of changes it might cause.

After the apocalypse, getting fresh seafood had become a pipe dream.

Just as she was thinking that, Cecilia never expected to run into a seafood distributor on the next floor up. Ironically, it was also this floor's worst performer of the month.

From the looks of it, business had been terrible. Nothing had sold.

Which worked out perfectly for Cecilia.

Above the fourth floor, the building was no longer made up of small livestream studios. Instead, it was filled with large product warehouses meant for bulk sales.

There were more than 20 massive commercial freezers, all crammed full of seafood.

Lobster, king crab legs, scallops, clams, and even ready-to-eat items like grilled shrimp skewers, smoked salmon strips, spicy calamari rings, marinated sardines, and crispy fried smelts.

It was a seafood lover's paradise. Everything was there.

Cecilia did not hesitate. With a broad sweep of her hand, she took every freezer and stored them all in her Space.

That was when she remembered. She had reached this floor previously as well.

Back then, the power had been out for ages.

Without electricity, all the seafood in the freezers had spoiled beyond saving.

The entire floor had reeked so badly that it felt like walking into a morgue.

Because of that, they had skipped the fourth floor entirely and rushed straight to the fifth.

Today, she was in luck. The power had only gone out that morning. There was still plenty of cold air left in the freezers, and nothing had gone bad.

Next door, Cecilia also found a company that specialized in frozen desserts and ice cream.

This company was the top performer of the month. With the recent heat finally starting to ease, ice cream sales had actually been through the roof.

The priciest tubs—both imported and local brands—were all lined up in a dozen freezers, and Cecilia swept every single one into her Space.

These were lifesavers during the heatwave, little bundles of joy and comfort.

In the far corner of the floor was what looked like the CEO's office.

When Cecilia arrived, she realized the entire floor belonged to the same enterprise, just split into a few different companies.

What made her chuckle the most was the sign on the CEO's office. It simply read "The Boss."

It was clear and to the point.

"The Boss'" office had a high-security lock and looked far fancier than the other rooms on the floor.

Cecilia fiddled with it for a while before finally getting it open.

For a few moments, she even thought about giving up.

After all, there were still more floors to explore. She couldn't waste too much time here.

But the moment she stepped inside, she knew it had been worth it.

"The Boss'" office hit her with a strong, classic study vibe, a sort of "refined, middle-aged comfort" energy.

Two large white-and-blue ceramic planters sat by the door, each one holding a few lilies.

But with no one around for days, they had all withered.

The furniture was all solid wood, high-end, and eclectic.

There was a mahogany display shelf, a walnut desk, and deep cherrywood chairs. It was an odd mix, but all premium stuff.

Glass figurines and ornaments were scattered around, and even the shelves themselves were carved from rosewood and mahogany.

The walls were covered with framed oil paintings. Cecilia scanned them quickly, spotting serene landscapes, still lifes, and abstract pieces with calming tones, though she didn't pause long enough to take in all the details.

Chapter 121 Easy Prey

"Cecilia, Hailey, Evan... everyone in this building, I'm gonna make you all pay for what happened to my Charles!"

Mabel muttered these words as she worked, her voice dripping with venomous hatred.

This burning desire for revenge was all she had left—the only thing keeping her alive.

She was gonna kill everyone in this building.

Meanwhile, Cecilia had no clue about the danger brewing below.

Hailey came back after about half an hour.

Cecilia heard her door open and close but didn't go check on her.

Some things you had to deal with on your own.

Like killing someone for the first time.

Cecilia could still remember her first kill in her previous life. She'd been terrified.

It was right when the Ice Stage hit.

She'd made it to a shelter by then, surviving on tiny rations of compressed crackers and water.

Everyone got the same. Every day.

Then an old woman figured her age gave her privilege. Had kids at home, she'd say. Always trying to mooch off people nearby.

At first, Cecilia had been kind—she'd give her a piece every time.

But then she overheard the old woman talking to someone nearby.

"That Cecilia's an idiot," she'd said. "She looks like a total pushover. In normal times, she'd probably be some guy's plaything."

The words crushed her. After that, she stopped sharing her food.

That's when the old woman's true nature came out. She started spitting at her, spreading rumors that Cecilia was selling herself to men.

It got so bad Cecilia had to disfigure her own face just to escape the harassment.

But the old woman wasn't done. She'd found her punching bag and kept swinging.

After Cecilia's disfigurement, she switched tactics—mocking her looks constantly, sneering at her in front of everyone.

Finally, one day, Cecilia and the old woman got assigned to the same supply run with the government convoy.

The whole trip, the old woman played the age card—bullying Cecilia, making her do all the work.

On the way back, the government caught the old woman hiding stolen supplies.

Then the performance began.

The old woman blamed everything on Cecilia, repeating all the slander she'd spread before—right in front of the officials.

The crowd, already poisoned by rumors, joined in.

In that moment, Cecilia finally got it.

This world had no use for kindness or patience anymore.

She pulled out her knife. A few strikes, and the old woman was done.

She retrieved the stolen supplies from the corpse and cleared her name.

She'd expected punishment. Instead, the government just made her do a few days of hard labor, then let her go.

But when she came back, everything changed.

The people who'd bullied her started avoiding her.

Those who'd mocked and insulted her freely? They didn't dare anymore.

That was the day she stopped being easy prey.

Now Hailey needed to reach that point too.

******

After all the recent chaos, Cecilia decided to take a few days to just chill at home, watching movies and reading books.

After the blizzard hit, it buried an entire floor overnight.

Cecilia dragged a massive bean bag to the floor-to-ceiling window, tossed on some pillows, and flopped down in full couch-potato mode with a book.

Jet perched on the bean bag beside her, happily watching shows on a tablet she'd placed on the small table nearby.

Blackie had no interest in entertainment. He gnawed absentmindedly on his ball, clearly lost in thought.

Cecilia had a feeling something was on his mind, but she couldn't figure out what.

Thanks to the Space-provided firewood, the room sat at a comfortable forty-something degrees Fahrenheit—practically luxurious in this frozen hellscape.

Cecilia pulled out snacks from the Space, feeding Blackie and Jet while munching away herself.

Before she knew it, it was ten at night.

All afternoon and evening, she'd skipped real meals and just gorged on junk food.

Chips, scallion crackers, cream cakes, spicy strips...

It felt a little sinful, but damn if it wasn't satisfying and stress-relieving.

Before bed, Cecilia played with Blackie for a while, practicing some combat moves together.

She noticed that since evolving, Blackie could not only shift his size but was way faster too.

Cecilia smirked. "Hmph. Lucky bastard with that evolution buff. But whatever—you're still my dog."

She gave his massive head a good scrub and yawned her way to bed.

******

The next morning, Cecilia woke to find the window covered in thick condensation.

The temperature difference—warm inside, freezing outside.

She wiped away the fog and checked the outdoor thermometer.

Negative forty degrees Fahrenheit.

Coastal Antarctica usually hovered between thirty-two and negative four.

Inland Antarctica? Negative forty to negative fifty-eight.

Right now, in this ordinary inland city—Cecilia's city—the temperature matched inland Antarctica.

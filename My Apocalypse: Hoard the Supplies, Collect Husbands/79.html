Chapter 79 Belongs to Everyone

Sure enough, Cecilia heard angry shouts echoing from the stairwell.

"Those bastards on the 66th floor are trying to hoard all the relief supplies!"

"Criminals! Someone call the cops on them!"

"They're gonna let us starve to death!"

The accusations were flying fast and furious.

Cecilia stared at the neighbors gathered outside, their faces twisted with rage like they wanted to break down the door and tear everyone apart.

She raised an eyebrow. "Well, it's barely morning and we've already got a mob at our door. How lovely." Her voice dripped with cold sarcasm.

When Cecilia walked Blackie toward the stairwell entrance, she spotted a familiar face leading the charge—that same woman who'd shown up before with her grandson, trying to threaten her.

The woman's voice rang out loud and aggressive, full of confidence now that she had a crowd backing her up.

It was a far cry from when she'd come up alone to pick a fight with Cecilia.

From the other residents, Cecilia learned the woman's name was Ashley Lyons.

But people didn't call her by name out of respect—they did it because nobody wanted to deal with her tantrums.

Before the world went to hell, if anyone crossed Ashley, she'd camp outside their door for two solid days, screaming insults and spitting on their doorstep.

Someone had called the police on her once, but it didn't do any good. She'd throw herself on the ground, wail about being victimized, and make such a scene that even the cops threw up their hands and called her a lost cause.

After the apocalypse hit, Ashley made herself everyone's problem. She was older and had her grandson with her, so she went around banging on doors and demanding food, as if the world owed her something.

If people didn't hand over their supplies, she'd scream that they were trying to starve her grandson and abuse an elderly woman.

Things got worse after the rescue team came through. Ashley went entirely off the rails. She'd show up at people's apartments and threaten them.

If they didn't give her food and water, she'd tell them they were committing murder, and she'd report them to the police when the government came back.

Most people in the building were young, and they couldn't deal with her constant harassment.

Ashley got lucky, too—she never ran into anyone tough enough to shut her down, so she had the younger residents completely under her thumb.

Right now, she stood in front of Cecilia with a whole group backing her up, chin raised high as she shouted the loudest, "You know this is illegal, right? When rescue comes and we all get out of here, I'm reporting you first thing. You'll go to jail!"

Cecilia almost laughed, looking at this woman stirring everyone up.

Ashley had no idea that Cecilia going to jail wasn't going to happen in this lifetime. The prisons were probably empty by now anyway—no one left to run them.

Arthur and Hailey stepped out into the hallway, too.

The cuts on Hailey's face had scabbed over, making her look fierce and damaged. A few people who recognized her whispered to each other and kept glancing her way.

Still, Hailey just stood there with a blank expression, completely unbothered.

"Who said we're blocking you from going upstairs to get supplies?" Cecilia walked forward and faced the crowd at the door.

Somewhere along the way, their little group of three had shifted, and Cecilia had become the one calling the shots.

Cecilia stepped forward with Arthur and Hailey flanking her on either side.

Ashley jabbed her finger at Cecilia, her voice sharp and accusatory. "You want us to come get it? Then why the hell is your door locked?! The rooftop belongs to everyone, and you blocking it off means you're hogging public space!"

A few people around her muttered in agreement.

Cecilia took one step forward, and Ashley immediately stumbled back two steps.

The moment word spread that the government was distributing supplies, everyone from the lower floors rushed up. Now the entire stairwell was packed wall-to-wall with people.

When Ashley backed up, she stepped right on someone's foot.

The person whose foot got stepped on jerked backward and stepped on the person behind them.

Within seconds, complaints rippled through the crowd.

Someone near the back, clearly fed up with waiting, shouted over the noise. "What are we standing around for? There's way too many people here. We can't all go up! Let's just pick a few people to go grab the stuff and bring it down so we can divide it up!"

The suggestion earned immediate approval from most of the crowd.

The entrance to the rooftop sat directly across from the stairwell landing—a narrow passage with just five or six steps leading up to a single door that could barely fit one person at a time.

If this entire mob tried to squeeze through, that door would probably get ripped right off its hinges.

Cecilia let out a cold laugh and pointed straight at Ashley. "Well, ma'am, since you're screaming the loudest, why don't you be one of the representatives and go up there to haul the supplies down?"

Before Ashley could respond, Cecilia reached for the door like she was actually about to unlock it.

Ashley's face went white when she saw Cecilia, and she stumbled backward.

The crowd behind her erupted in complaints. "Hey, what's going on up there? You can't just back up without warning. You stepped on my foot!"

"Seriously, if you don't want to be in front, move aside. The rest of us actually want to get supplies first."

Ashley was genuinely terrified of Cecilia now.

There was something about that half-smile on Cecilia's face that made Ashley think she'd opened the door just to lure her in and beat her up.

Ashley retorted, "I'm definitely going in, but not by myself."

Besides, there was no way Ashley was going to haul boxes herself. That kind of work was for other people to do, and they'd better deliver everything straight to her door, too.

In the end, five people were chosen to go inside—Ashley, three strong young men, and a young woman whom most people recognized.

Alexander was one of the three men.

He looked even worse now, dehydrated and worn down. But he was tall, and when they'd called for volunteers to carry supplies, Cecilia had seen Rebecca shove him forward. His height made him an obvious choice.

Chapter 214 The Letter

Blaise had no great passions in life, apart from one. He was utterly obsessed with pigeons.

So all of this had clearly been planned in advance. Someone had calculated every step, waiting for exactly this moment to stir up trouble.

Now the identity of Dyson and the others in Tower B had become clear as well. They were mercenaries too, a group called the Black Dragon Syndicate.

What no one could understand was why this lot had happened to turn up in Tower B of all places.

Just as Cecilia was at a loss, there came another knock at the door on the sixty-fourth floor.

She went to answer it and found, standing outside, the little boy she had rescued earlier from Helen Lane, the tall, thin woman who had taken an orphan hostage.

The boy gave Cecilia a sweet smile the moment he saw her.

"Big sister, someone asked me to deliver a letter to a man upstairs called Arthur."

As he spoke, he pulled out an envelope.

Arthur came over, opened it, and took out the sheet inside. After one glance, his brow furrowed deeply.

"Tonight, I need to go out."

Cecilia did not ask further. From what Blaise had said, this seemed to be part of an old grievance between Arthur's organisation and the Black Dragon Syndicate.

Ordinarily, now that the apocalypse had come, such old grudges might have been ignored.

But these people had appeared right under Arthur's nose, and they were clearly keeping watch on both Arthur and Blaise's movements.

"They know too much. We can't afford not to be on guard."

That had been Arthur's exact wording.

What made it all even more baffling was that Colonel Reed had also left at precisely this time.

Was it a coincidence, or did this so-called Black Dragon Syndicate have the means to manipulate people inside the military as well?

Cecilia thought it over again and again, but she could not recall hearing anything about the Black Dragon Syndicate in her previous life.

That probably meant they had never made much of a splash.

She had no wish to get involved, so she simply said that she too needed to go out that evening.

In truth, she was planning to retrieve all the supplies Nathan had left behind.

After all, it was not only Nathan, now dead, who had known about them. His two bodyguards had known as well.

If she waited too long, someone else might beat her to it.

Cecilia went back to sleep for a while, then drank some Magic Spring water and immediately felt refreshed.

By the time the sky had nearly gone dark, she was ready to set off.

But just as she was about to leave and had reached the sixty-fifth floor, Barry, who served under Captain Jackman, arrived.

Not only had he come, he had also brought the mutant cockroach corpses Cecilia had been waiting several days for.

"Ms. Summers, I've brought Blackie a present!"

Barry grinned and waved the chicken drumstick in his hand.

It looked crumpled and rather sorry for itself, as though it had been stuffed inside his coat for several days.

At once, Blackie poked his head out from behind Cecilia, ears pricked high, his icy eyes locked tightly onto the drumstick in Barry's hand.

Cecilia gave Blackie a pat on the head and rolled her eyes.

What was this supposed to mean? Had she been starving him at home? How pathetic could he look?

"Come on, Blackie. Have a drumstick."

Barry crouched down and beckoned to him cheerfully.

Blackie wagged his tail and walked over to Barry, clearly recognising him by now.

But to everyone's surprise, although he was obviously desperate for it, he did not go for the drumstick in Barry's hand.

Instead, he fixed Cecilia with those icy blue eyes, as though asking, Can I eat it?

Barry laughed when he saw it.

"Ms. Summers, you've trained Blackie so well. He won't even take food from someone else without your permission."

Only then did Cecilia suddenly realise something.

It was true. At some point, without her noticing exactly when, Blackie had stopped eating anything that did not come from her.

Earlier that day, Blaise had found Blackie amusing and tried to feed him a chunk of pork shank.

Blackie had not even spared him a glance. He had simply turned round and gone back inside.

By contrast, Snowy, who was staying with them for the time being, had scampered over at once and clung to Blaise's leg begging for food.

At the time, Cecilia had assumed it was just because Blackie was not yet familiar with Blaise.

But now Blackie stood there looking at her, tail wagging furiously, his eyes full of eager urging.

Can I eat it? Can I? Please?

With a sigh, Cecilia took the drumstick from Barry and fed it to Blackie herself.

Blackie devoured the whole thing in a few bites. Once he had finished, he licked his lips with obvious satisfaction.

Then he looked up at Cecilia with longing in his eyes, as if to say, That was excellent. Next time, do bring me a few more.

But apparently he quickly realised that the naked greed in his gaze was damaging his dignity, because he promptly shut his mouth, puffed out his chest, and stood stiffly to one side like a little guard on duty.

Barry reached out and patted Blackie on the head, smiling brightly.

"It makes me happy seeing Blackie getting better and better. Several of the mutant dogs in our unit have already died."

By the end, there was a hint of sorrow in his voice.

Barry seemed to want to stay and chat longer, but Cecilia was pressed for time and did not continue the conversation.

So Barry stopped talking as well and instead called over his men to unload the mutant creature corpses they had brought back from the front line, stacking them on the sixty-fifth floor.

Cecilia took a look. Most of them were mutant cockroach carcasses, though there were also a few sacks filled with mutant rats.

They looked revolting, but the thought that the Space could level up from them left her thoroughly satisfied.

Since there were too many eyes around just now, Cecilia did not immediately put the corpses into the Space. Instead, she said goodbye to Barry and prepared to retrieve Nathan's supplies first, planning to come back later to deal with the mutant creatures.

As she was leaving, Barry hesitated, clearly wanting to say something.

One look at his expression was enough for Cecilia to know that he had something on his mind.

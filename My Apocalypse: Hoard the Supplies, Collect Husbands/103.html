Chapter 103 Mabel's Greed

After a quick huddle, everyone grabbed their gear and headed out to scavenge.

Blackie stayed behind, guarding the stairwell.

With the temperature dropping again these past two days, Blackie's fur had grown thick—thicker than before.

Now horse-sized, his coat was so dense you could shove your whole hand in and not touch skin. Just soft, fluffy fur.

Of course, his appetite had exploded too.

Cecilia did the math: one Blackie ate as much as 60 Jets... or 10 of her.

The thought made her stomach drop.

Sure, the Space had plenty of supplies. The farm produced food. But even that couldn't keep up with this eating machine.

After telling Blackie to hold down the fort, they headed downstairs.

The nearby floors were already stripped clean. They had cleared them out ages ago.

They had to go lower.

By the 40th floor, every door had been pried open. Anything useful? Gone.

"It's the 48th floor," Hailey said.

When they'd passed it earlier, they'd noticed the 48th had installed a security gate at the stairwell—just like the 66th.

They didn't think much of it.

If they could prep for the apocalypse, so could everyone else.

"Let's check lower floors."

But then they realized—everything burnable or useful in the entire building was gone.

Cecilia thought back to that security door on the 48th floor.

Ah. So that's where it all went.

Mabel and her husband must've crammed the hallways full of supplies.

Cecilia smirked. Those two are something else.

Sure, she'd hoarded tons of supplies—even cleared out that entire 12-story building across the street.

But she wasn't stupid. Strip your own building bare overnight and your neighbors start sharpening knives.

On her way down, she'd noticed others were still alive in the building besides the 48th floor.

Brandon Turner—the wife-beater—and his wife Emily. Ashley's daughter-in-law. A few more.

All their doors were barricaded. Signs of life inside.

Some had even cracked their doors open to peek out nervously as Cecilia's group passed by.

What Cecilia didn't expect? Mabel's greed pissed everyone off fast.

Turns out, people had noticed all the firewood in the building was gone.

They'd assumed the 66th floor took it. No one dared complain.

Everyone knew the people on 66 were ruthless. And they had that massive mutant black dog.

Going up there would be suicide.

But today? The 66th floor crew came down looking for firewood and left empty-handed. And they'd been talking about Mabel's security door.

People noticed.

And then they remembered—that brand-new security door blocking the stairwell to Mabel's floor.

"Son of a bitch! Who took all the goddamn furniture? Trying to freeze us to death? MABEL! I thought you were decent!" Brandon roared from the 58th floor.

Doors started opening.

Residents who'd been watching stepped out.

Within minutes, everyone left alive in the building—except Mabel's family—poured into the stairwell.

Cursing. Shouting. They surged upward.

A mob forming fast.

Cecilia and her crew? They stayed out of it. Didn't want any part of that circus.

They figured it hadn't gotten desperate enough yet to raid the living.

"Let's hit Building B," someone suggested.

Mostly offices over there. Fewer residents. Way more furniture.

Better haul, less drama.

They'd been planning to scope out Building B eventually anyway. Mabel just moved up the timeline.

Mabel had always been a social climber.

Before the apocalypse, she'd charmed her bosses, buddied up to all the neighbors.

Every year, she won awards as a model employee.

At home? Doting husband who worshipped the ground she walked on. Did whatever she said, defended her no matter what.

Picture-perfect life.

All thanks to her silver tongue and street smarts.

After the apocalypse, she sweet-talked the residents—who already liked her—into trusting her completely.

They elected her building manager.

Sure, with the 66th floor around, the title didn't mean much.

But she loved it. The respect. The authority.

She'd even made deals: when they got to the government shelter, they'd stick together. She'd still be in charge.

Then the government ships never came back.

And the Ice Stage hit.

That changed everything.

Screw "building manager." Screw "authority." None of it mattered.

She was terrified. And she finally understood—the 66th floor had the right idea all along.

Survival. That was all that mattered now.

So she and her husband made a decision. They sealed off their floor, just like the 66th had done.

Chapter 38 She Had to Move

Then Bryan stormed off, and the crowd dispersed.

While everyone was leaving, Cecilia hurried back to her apartment and grabbed Blackie for a walk.

As they headed out, she couldn't stop thinking about that look Bryan had given Phoebe.

It wasn't just anger. He was planning something, and she had a feeling he was going to be the reason things went sideways in Building A.

Cecilia didn't want to get involved though. This was just the beginning, and things were only going to get worse from here.

Based on everything she'd experienced in her past life, the people who stayed clearheaded and didn't play hero were the ones who survived the longest.

Those two girls, Phoebe and Hailey, seemed like neighbors and looked pretty close. Hopefully, they'd be smart enough to watch their backs.

The property management was actually doing a decent job. They'd pulled out a backup generator to keep the elevator running for now.

Once the elevator shaft got flooded with acid rain though, that would be the end of it. After that, everyone would have to take the stairs.

At least it would be good exercise, but it also meant Cecilia's chances to go outside were getting more limited. She wondered how long the building could even hold onto that generator once things really went to hell.

She took Blackie around the complex a couple of times and checked out some of the nearby shops.

She'd been hoping to find more supplies to stock up on since she could never have too much, but what she found instead was floodwater. The street at the entrance to their complex was already knee-deep.

Cecilia looked up at the rain coming down. It was definitely heavier than before. This area sat on higher ground in the city, so it had stayed dry despite the rain falling nonstop for days now. In the lower-lying neighborhoods, the water had probably reached the second floor by now.

Blackie stood beside her, staring at the floodwater with an almost human-like intelligence in his eyes.

Cecilia reached down and scratched his head, and she could swear his skull felt bigger than before. Maybe it was just her imagination.

The street was completely empty, but she could see plenty of people standing at their windows looking down. Maybe they'd been cooped up inside for too long without power or internet and didn't have much else to do.

Cecilia didn't know if this would be the last time she'd see the street looking normal, but standing there in the eerie silence, she felt a wave of sadness wash over her.

After a bit, she and Blackie walked a couple more loops around the block and she focused on the delivery center across the street.

The place was already abandoned, and the rainwater had risen to the steps. She knew it was almost time to make her move.

She patted Blackie's head and they headed home. The acid rain had definitely gotten more concentrated.

Even though they'd only been out for about an hour, some of Cecilia's skin had started blistering and bleeding.

Blackie's fur was falling out in patches too, and he kept giving her that wounded look.

When she glanced over at him, he pawed at the bare spots on his coat with so much betrayal in his eyes, like he was saying, "Look at my fur!"

Cecilia couldn't help but laugh and pulled out some ointment to treat his skin. "You're fine. You'll be good as new in a few days."

Then it hit her. Would the spring water from the Space work better than the medicine? She poured some on her arm where the skin had blistered, and sure enough, the wounds healed right before her eyes.

That was a game changer.

After eating something and running through some drills with Blackie, Cecilia got to work on her project for the day. She pulled out two big pots and started making batches of hot coffee.

By evening, she'd filled the shelves in the Space with hundreds of containers of the stuff. When the freeze hit, this would literally save lives.

She planned to spend the next few days making more warming drinks and broths to help survive the cold.

But around seven that evening, she noticed the rain was getting worse.

The drops were huge now, hammering down from the sky like bullets.

The awnings over nearby shops had already corroded away, and the water that had only reached the steps yesterday was now flooding the first floor of the delivery center across the street.

Cecilia felt a chill run down her spine. Tonight was the night. She had to move.

That evening, Blackie had to use his litter box indoors, and he was clearly not happy about it.

Cecilia explained that they couldn't go outside right now because too many neighbors had their eyes glued to the windows watching everything.

Blackie still looked sulky, but he cooperated. Once he was done, Cecilia brought him to bed with her and set an alarm for 12:30 AM.

Tonight, she had big plans. Blackie didn't really get why Cecilia was going to bed so early, but he wasn't the type to ask questions. Whatever she did, he just went along with it, so he curled up and went to sleep too.

At twelve thirty, her alarm went off right on schedule. She got up, pulled on the black outfit she'd laid out earlier, grabbed Blackie, and headed downstairs.

The lobby was pitch black. The building's security guards had stopped showing up for work days ago, and water had already flooded into the first floor.

The property management had tried blocking it with sandbags, but that was only a temporary fix. By tomorrow, the whole floor would probably be underwater.

Braving the wind and rain, Cecilia swam across the street with Blackie.

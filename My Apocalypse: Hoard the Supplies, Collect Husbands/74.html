Chapter 74 Bitter Twist of Fate

Once everything was secured, the sound of voices finally drifted in from outside.

Unfazed, Cecilia led the way, pulling their loaded boat behind her.

They reached the main entrance just as the other group was entering.

A bitter twist of fate—it was Bryan and his crew.

The sight of Cecilia made Bryan flinch back instinctively.

The gash on his shoulder, courtesy of her machete, had been deep enough to trigger a raging fever.

It had only broken that very morning.

Their gang had managed to steal some fever reducers from an old man on the twelfth floor—the desperate pleas of the old folks meant nothing to them.

To gain entry, they'd preyed on the couple's sympathy. Once inside, they hadn't just taken the medicine; they'd ransacked the entire apartment.

Word had it that the old man hanged himself at dawn.

Bryan's crew couldn't have cared less.

Lately, they'd been breaking into every door they could pry open, so supplies weren't their immediate concern.

That morning, they'd even spotted rescue teams, sparking a faint hope that this nightmare might soon be over.

But then someone in the group had voiced a nagging worry. "Once the waters recede and things go back to normal, we won't be able to just take whatever we want, will we?"

The question had lingered, planting a seed of urgency.

So they'd headed to a nearby shopping plaza that evening, intent on one last haul—focusing on jewelry and gold, dreaming of the fortune they'd cash in once order was restored.

The supermarket was just a stop along the way.

Bryan never expected to run into Cecilia here.

Fear was his first reaction, but it quickly curdled into resentment. The memory of how she'd held him at blade-point still stung his pride.

His eyes swept over her again, and he noticed she looked even more striking than before.

Cecilia was dressed in a form-fitting black wetsuit, with her hair tied back, damp strands clinging to her pale neck and cheeks.

A dark, possessive heat stirred in his gut.

"Fancy meeting you here, neighbor," Bryan said, his eyes narrowing.

A realization struck him—a woman like Cecilia, in a normal world, would be utterly out of his reach. This chaos was his only chance.

If rescue really was coming and society was about to snap back into place, then this was his last opportunity.

He took a deliberate step back. From behind him, a pair of hulking twins cracked their knuckles and moved forward, each standing well over 6.5 feet tall.

"So you're the ones who cut our buddy Bryan. We just met up with him today. Didn't think he'd take a hit like that. We're gonna make you pay," one of them growled.

They'd been living in the next building over and had only linked up with Bryan earlier that day while scavenging.

Seeing his growing crew and the loot he'd already amassed, they'd been eager to join.

Now was the perfect time to prove their worth.

A cold smirk spread across Bryan's face. These two had trained for years. "Let's see how tough you are now, Cecilia," he murmured.

Cecilia slid the compact blade from the side pocket of her wetsuit.

At the threat to his owner, Blackie moved forward, planting himself solidly at her side.

The sight of the imposing dog gave the twin brothers pause.

They exchanged a glance before reaching behind their backs to pull out a pair of baseball bats.

From behind Cecilia, Arthur stepped forward, placing himself between her and the advancing men.

"I'll handle this," he said, the words light, his expression unchanged.

Truthfully, Arthur had been ready to step in moments earlier, but Cecilia had moved first.

Watching from behind, he found himself intrigued by the slender young woman. Her grip on the knife was clean and decisive, her stance unflinching even against several large assailants.

Her form wasn't polished—more like a wild, self-taught style—but it was clear she had real fighting experience. There was no hesitation in her, no flicker of fear.

It was as though a taut wire ran through her, warning that a second's delay could mean death.

That kind of sharp, ruthless readiness? It felt earned. Forged through countless trials, moments where life hung by a thread.

It also made Arthur feel somewhat redundant in their little trio.

From the moment the threat appeared, Cecilia hadn't even glanced his way—like a lone wolf who trusted no one but herself.

A faint sigh brushed his thoughts. Did he really seem that unreliable?

Well, if she wouldn't ask, he'd offer.

"Heh. Was gonna deal with the girl first, then you and the other ugly one," one of the twins snorted, eyeing Arthur's slightly shorter, leaner frame.

He gave his bat a testing swing. "But if you're volunteering..." With that, he charged.

Chapter 43 The Gray Parrot

Those fabrics would come in handy sooner or later.

On the eighth floor, there was a small pet sanctuary.

The moment Cecilia stepped inside, she was met with a chorus of animal cries.

Many of the animals had already starved to death.

Among the survivors were three rabbits, two alpacas, a sheep, a miniature horse, a calf, five or six Rhode Island Red chickens, two guinea fowl, four guinea pigs, and a gray parrot.

The gray parrot was incredibly clever. The moment it saw Cecilia, it spoke in a perfectly clear human voice. "Long time no see, you old dog."

The sinister tone made Cecilia nearly draw her knife on instinct.

It was only when she realized it was just a parrot that she broke out in a cold sweat.

She couldn't imagine what the bird had been through to pick up such bizarre phrases.

She gathered all the animals and stored them in her Space.

With her farm being so vast, these new additions were exactly what she needed.

Besides, rabbits breed like crazy. They were perfect for a steady meat supply.

Guinea pigs were edible too, considered a delicacy in some parts of the world.

However, Cecilia didn't have the heart to eat them since she'd kept them as pets as a kid.

Still, she decided to keep them anyway. Who knew when they might come in handy?

Her Space could easily support a whole farm.

The cow would provide milk.

The sheep could supply both meat and milk.

Cecilia noticed it was a female, the same breed as the mountain goat she had picked up earlier. It was perfect for pairing.

The miniature horse didn't serve much purpose for now, but in the future, it could be a riding animal.

The alpacas were mainly for their wool. Their fleece was extremely warm. It was perfect for harsh winter conditions.

The Rhode Island Reds and guinea fowl were real treasures, providing both eggs and meat.

Thinking of all this made Cecilia grin from ear to ear.

As for the gray parrot, she'd keep it around for company.

After all, gray parrots were among the smartest birds in the world.

Of course, Cecilia also took all the feed and rations stored in the sanctuary.

Just the hay and pellets alone would keep these animals fed for at least six months.

With the eighth floor cleared, she moved on to sweep through the ninth.

From this floor on, the building was filled with small private bistros, restaurants, and yoga studios.

Cecilia left no stone unturned. She stripped the floors clean.

On the tenth floor, Cecilia came across a gold jewelry shop. The place was stuffed with hundreds of pounds of gold ornaments and dozens of pounds of gold bars. She swept every last piece into her Space.

By the late apocalypse, cash would be worthless.

But gold and silver would be the new currency.

Came that time, every ounce of this treasure would be priceless.

The eleventh floor was a large gym.

Cecilia grabbed every piece of equipment and even took the two full-length mirrors, stashing them all in her Space.

By the twelfth floor, Cecilia was too exhausted to climb another step, but her eyes lit up with excitement.

Tonight's haul dwarfed anything she had collected before.

Her money had been limited, but these 12 floors, with dozens of companies' inventories, felt like a bottomless treasure trove and endless surprises waiting to be claimed.

Then, Cecilia heard a rapid set of footsteps.

It was Blackie running.

Cecilia was immediately on alert.

As expected, she saw him bounding up the stairs between the eleventh and twelfth floors, his face set and serious.

Their eyes met, and she instantly understood his meaning. Someone was coming.

As expected, she soon heard another set of quick, steady footsteps heading straight up.

This person had a purpose. They hadn't lingered on any other floor. They were racing upward.

It was obvious what they were after. It was either Cecilia herself or someone who had emptied the entire building.

As the footsteps drew closer, Cecilia didn't hesitate. She grabbed Blackie and slipped straight into her Space.

From inside, she couldn't see what was happening outside, but she could still hear everything.

About ten seconds after entering, the footsteps sounded right next to her ears.

Whoever it was, they were fast.

And from the rhythm alone, she could tell this person moved with steady, controlled steps. He was clearly trained.

"Where did he go?"

The voice was low and calm, probably belonging to someone in their twenties.

Blackie crouched beside Cecilia. Even inside the Space, it stayed highly alert, every muscle tensed, ready to pounce at a moment's notice.

Cecilia stroked Blackie's back, signaling it to relax.

Then she leaned closer and whispered into its ear, "Remember that voice. If you hear it again, give me a heads-up."

Blackie blinked. Whether it was understood or not was anyone's guess.

The intruder lingered at the stairwell for a while, as if trying to pick up on any sound.

Unfortunately for him, he was bound to come up empty-handed.

After hearing nothing, he finally headed down the corridor to the right.

Cecilia held her breath, listening closely.

The man paced back and forth across the twelfth floor, kicking open every door, clearly searching for whoever had been there.

Cecilia let out a quiet breath, her back damp with cold sweat. She was glad she'd trusted her instincts and hidden in her Space. If she had run into him head-on, it would have been nothing but trouble.

After a long while, the sounds of searching for a person faded, replaced instead by the unmistakable noises of someone looting supplies.

Chapter 71 Faint

Cecilia felt her mouth twitch.

The note's message seemed both perfectly logical and utterly absurd at the same time.

But she wasn't one to take things lying down.

If the Space thought it could fob her off with trinkets, she had a reply ready. All the cardboard boxes from the afternoon's unpacking were gathered and fed into the Recycler in one go.

Knowing the daily limit was one item, she had taken care to nest them—larger boxes holding smaller ones, stuffed with paper bags, the whole bundle tightly bound with cord.

After tossing the lot in, Cecilia dusted off her hands and eyed the machine with quiet satisfaction.
Try outsmarting me now.

With that done, she settled in for a well-earned dinner.

Jet had been left to the tablet all afternoon, binge-watching shows. The entertainment, however, had taken an unexpected turn.

"Your servant... wishes to dine," the parrot croaked in a crude imitation of a consort.

Cecilia, who had just taken a sip of warmed milk, nearly choked.

What on earth had it been watching?

Shaking her head in amused disbelief, she refilled Jet's food bowl and promptly switched off the series she'd been streaming for days.

Tomorrow, she decided, they'd try a variety show instead—something less likely to corrupt a parrot's vocabulary.

Not long after the meal, a knock sounded at her door.

Hailey stood outside, her expression difficult to read.

"Everything alright?" Cecilia asked, remaining in the doorway.

They weren't yet familiar enough for her to invite the other woman in.

"The acid rain outside... It's stopped."

Cecilia gave a slight nod.

She'd expected as much.

"I also heard rescue teams came by this morning, but they left. They said supplies would be distributed later."

Another nod.

Cecilia studied Hailey's face, noting the distinct lack of excitement at the mention of "rescue" or "supplies." It struck her as odd.

"You don't seem too thrilled. Everyone else in the building is over the moon—they think help has finally arrived."

Hailey let out a cold scoff, the scars on her face twisting into something almost grim.

"Cici—mind if I call you that? You saved my life. I'd like to get to know you better, but... right now, I can barely look after myself. I'm relying on you and Mr. Barnes for safety, and I have nothing to offer in return. That's why I've kept my distance."

Cecilia waited silently for her to continue.

"I don't think this is over. Before the networks went down, I saw reports from overseas. This isn't some local disaster—it's global. If rescue were really imminent, they'd be acting now, not telling us to wait," Hailey said, her gaze steady.

Cecilia found herself respecting the woman's clear-headedness.

They exchanged a few more words before parting ways.

Later that evening, Cecilia decided to ask Hailey for details about Bryan—how many were in his group, which floors they occupied—anything to better prepare.

But knocking on Hailey's door yielded no response.

Just as she began to wonder if the woman had stepped out, Arthur's door opened across the hall.

Without a word, he strode over and kicked Hailey's door open.

"She came home this afternoon and never left. I've been keeping an ear out—in case anyone came looking for trouble," he explained, his voice low.

Arthur's vigilance was, as ever, impressively sharp.

Once inside, Cecilia found Hailey collapsed on the floor.

Since moving to the 66th floor, Hailey had been surviving without any proper food or clean water. She had pushed through the day using crude distillation methods to obtain a few sips of water, too proud to ask Cecilia or Arthur for help.

When the acid rain finally stopped that evening, she had planned to venture out in search of supplies. Instead, hunger and exhaustion overcame her, leaving her unconscious in her own apartment.

Cecilia woke her up.

Listening to Hailey's explanation—and her frustrated admission of her own helplessness—Cecilia let out a soft sigh.

From Hailey's pale face and genuine distress, it was clear this wasn't an act.

She really had fainted from hunger, and she truly regretted not lasting until nightfall.

Arthur returned from his unit with a bottle of water and a pack of crackers. "You can pay me back once you find supplies," he said simply, then left without another word.

Cecilia watched as Hailey slowly regained her strength, the water and food gradually bringing color back to her face. Minutes passed in silence before Hailey finally lifted her gaze, her expression shifting into one of grim determination. "Cici, I want to borrow Blackie."

From behind Cecilia, the dog perked up at the sound of its name, tilting its head curiously.

Cecilia's eyes narrowed slightly. "Why should I lend it to you?"

"I have nothing left—no food, no water. What happened today... I didn't expect it. I thought I could tough it out alone, that I could handle whatever came my way. But going out there by myself now... It's too risky.

"With Blackie, I'd stand a better chance. In return, I'll give you and Mr. Barnes half of whatever I find, just like we agreed. And from my own share, I'll give another half to Blackie."

Her eyes held Cecilia's without flinching.

From where she stood, Cecilia could see the freshly healed scar on Hailey's face.

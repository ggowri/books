Chapter 19 A Stroke of Luck

Hearing that, Cecilia brushed a bead of sweat from her brow.

A cold snort slipped out, though—anyone foolish enough to scheme against her, a reborn soul, had earned their misfortune.

She bent down to pat Blackie's head gently; he was undoubtedly a hero today.

Sleep evaded Cecilia long after the police left.

She first messaged the security door installer, asking them to come the next day to reinforce the door for extra sturdiness.

To her surprise, the installer was still up and immediately fired off a series of solutions.

Cecilia found the plans more than satisfactory.

With that sorted, she lifted Blackie into her arms, running her fingers softly through his fur. "Thanks to you today," she murmured.

Blackie feigned indifference, turning his big head to the side—but Cecilia caught the faint wag of his tail the second her hand touched his head, swinging so fast it blurred into a tiny, whirring windmill.

Clearly loving the attention, yet stubbornly pretending not to care.

What a two-faced dog.

The night hung thick and dark, and with the lock damaged, Cecilia couldn't settle enough to sleep.

She headed to the kitchen instead, deciding to put the time to use practicing her cooking.

First on the menu was a spicy sausage and egg skillet.

She cracked 15 eggs into a bowl, tossed in sliced spicy sausage and a dash of paprika, then cooked up a heaping pot.

Grabbing the meal prep containers she'd stashed earlier, Cecilia portioned out small servings into each one.

That way, when the apocalypse hit and cooking grew impossible, she could pull out these ready-made meals and savor them—no more choking down tasteless compressed biscuits when time or resources ran thin.

Next, she moved on to the second dish: honey-glazed pork ribs.

She chopped the ribs into chunks, blanched them in boiling water, then stir-fried them with honey, garlic, thyme, and a splash of apple cider vinegar. Finally, she added water and let the pot simmer until the meat turned tender and the sauce thickened into a glossy coat.

The rich aroma of glazed ribs hadn't even fully filled the kitchen before she felt a large head press against her back—Blackie, his eyes glowing with eagerness, fixed intently on the pot.

Cecilia had no choice but to hand over two pieces.

Then she sautéed a batch of garlic spinach, making extra to split into five portions.

By the time she finished the three dishes, she'd put together five complete boxed meals, each topped with a serving of freshly cooked quinoa.

She sealed the containers tightly and set them aside.

However, she didn't stop there. Cecilia fired up another pan and started on a creamy chicken and mushroom stir-fry.

She kept at it well into the night, and by early morning, she'd made over thirty boxed meals—simple home-cooked fare, but hearty, meaty, and all to her taste.

In the pressure cooker, a pot of lemon-herb braised chicken simmered; she planned to let it cook a little longer to soften the meat, then steal a short nap while she waited.

When Cecilia woke up, she'd have several more servings of lemon-herb chicken bowls ready.

The thought brought a small smile to her face.

Blackie still looked bright-eyed and energetic, having snuck countless treats from her throughout the night.

Cecilia walked over and patted his big head. "I'm going to sleep for a bit. Wake me if anything happens," she said.

Then, she flipped on the air conditioner and set it to 79°F—a deliberate choice, to keep Blackie from cranking it down to 64°F and freezing her half to death again.

Three hours passed before Cecilia woke up.

Blackie was curled up right beside her, a trickle of drool dribbling from his mouth—nothing like his usual aloof self.

She reached out to pinch his ear, but he jolted awake instantly, his gaze sharp and alert as he locked eyes with her.

"Boring," Cecilia muttered, rolling her eyes and stretching before getting up to do her belated morning workout.

When she returned from her walk, three missed calls lit up her phone—all from the renovation company.

They'd presumably called to apologize, begging her not to go public with the incident.

After all, a company that hired a murderer would be ruined the second the news got out.

Cecilia took a deep breath before answering, "You'd better thoroughly vet all your workers' backgrounds. This can never happen again. If it weren't for my dog, I could be dead right now!"

The renovation company representatives flooded her with apologies, their voices trembling with urgency.

Cecilia refused to let them off easily, but she had no patience for drawn-out arguments. "Want to fix this? Pay up," she said bluntly.

A visible sigh of relief came through the line at the mention of money.

That afternoon, 250 thousand dollars hit Cecilia's account—the agreed-upon compensation.

She'd avoided legal proceedings; they were too time-consuming.

The renovation company, too, wanted to steer clear of legal trouble, fearing irreparable damage to their reputation, so they transferred the money without hesitation.

Adding in the 200-thousand-dollar reward for capturing the fugitive, Cecilia had emerged from the ordeal unscathed and 450 thousand dollars richer.

It was almost a stroke of luck amid the chaos.

The incident with the worker had opened Cecilia's eyes to potential security gaps in her home—gaps she couldn't afford to ignore.

She went out and bought two more locks, even more advanced than the previous ones.

In addition, Cecilia picked up a small, palm-sized padlock to secure the door from the inside; it couldn't be pried open without cutting through it entirely.

And while she'd once thought the bulletproof glass was enough, she now decided to reinforce the windows with security screens and burglar bars.

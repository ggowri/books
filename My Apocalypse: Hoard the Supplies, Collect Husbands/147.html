Chapter 147 General Caldwell

"I'm listening."

Logan nodded, though something about Cecilia's expression felt... off.

"I've got addresses for a few hidden warehouses. Could be a huge help for your rescue operations down the line. But... what are you willing to trade for them?"

Cecilia was talking about some of the more distant warehouse locations she'd grabbed from the Boss's office.

Those spots? Too far for her to reach right now.

Once Heat Stage hit, more buildings would collapse.

The frozen ground from Ice Stage would thaw completely. Floods would swallow everything again.

Those warehouses might not survive.

Better to sell them to the military.

At least she'd get something out of it.

Of course, the closer warehouses? Cecilia planned to raid those herself when she got the chance.

So really, she only had three she could sell—all over sixty miles out.

Logan's eyes lit up, but he quickly tamped down his excitement.

"Ms. Summers, first I'll send someone to confirm the warehouses. If the supplies are really there in those quantities, I'll need Command's approval before we discuss terms."

"Sounds good, Captain. I'll be waiting."

And that was that.

Before leaving, Logan thanked Cecilia—said that folk remedy she'd given them had somehow saved their General.

Cecilia shrugged. "Probably just a coincidence."

Logan figured it might be.

After all, he'd tried that colorless, tasteless "remedy" on one of his comrades.

The guy's wound had healed, sure. But not that fast.

So he'd never thought much of it.

Truth was, Cecilia had diluted the Magic Spring water to almost nothing.

Whatever the General had, even that watered-down stuff helped him.

Pure dumb luck.

Just as they wrapped up and were about to leave, someone shouted from below.

"General Caldwell's here!"

Richard Caldwell had bad legs, so they all headed downstairs.

Richard was already downstairs in one of the cleared-out rooms. A soldier had lit a fire for him.

Cecilia saw him sitting in a chair, back ramrod straight.

First impression: integrity.

Second impression: Grandpa.

Ryan had made it to regimental officer before health issues forced him into early retirement.

The man radiated authority.

But his health never recovered. He died young.

Cecilia remembered how Ryan used to sit in a chair with a cane, staring into the distance. Just like Richard.

When Richard saw Cecilia, his first words caught her completely off guard.

"You're Cecilia? Ryan's granddaughter?"

Cecilia froze.

Then she saw a smile break across Richard's face.

"I served under Ryan. He was my squad leader."

After chatting a bit, Cecilia learned Richard was three years younger than Ryan. Ryan had been his mentor when he first enlisted.

Ryan retired early due to injury. Richard stayed in the service and climbed all the way to General.

"You really do look like him." Richard smiled, clapping Cecilia on the shoulder.

After some small talk, Richard waved his hand.

Two veterans stepped forward carrying a box.

It wasn't big, but inside was half a box of oranges and a whole pig leg.

"You saved my life with that medicine. I looked everywhere at home—this is all I could scrape together as thanks." Richard smiled.

Cecilia shook her head.

In the apocalypse, this stuff was precious. Especially oranges—vitamin C meant you wouldn't get scurvy.

Cecilia remembered in her past life, plenty of people survived the disasters and found food, only to die of scurvy from lack of fruits and vegetables.

Richard's box of oranges? That was thoughtful.

"Thank you." Cecilia smiled.

During their conversation, Cecilia learned what had happened to Richard.

He'd been attacked by a mutated animal and suffered a severe wound.

At first, Richard didn't trust the water Cecilia gave him. He tested it on an injured animal first. When it actually worked on the wound, only then did he use it himself.

And sure enough—the medicine healed him.

"Actually, I came here today to ask—do you have more of that medicine? You might not know this, but our troops are fighting mutated creatures right now. A lot of our young soldiers are badly injured. If we could use that medicine in large quantities, we could save a lot of lives. I remember your grandmother was a famous doctor, right?"

Richard looked at Cecilia, waiting for her answer.

Cecilia's grandmother had indeed been a renowned doctor.

But after Ryan died, grief consumed her. She passed away not long after him.

Cecilia looked at Richard and nodded slowly.

After a moment's thought, she answered, "That medicine wasn't some folk remedy I found outside. My grandmother left it before she died. I don't know the formula. When the disaster hit, I got injured too—I've used it all up."

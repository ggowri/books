Chapter 73 Coming Back for Another Load

Thrilled as she was, Cecilia knew she couldn't linger to inspect the Space's new level now.

From behind the counter and its drawers, she pulled out some supplies the shop owner had left: a bag of wheat, a bag of quinoa, and a bag of oats, ignoring the rotten tomatoes and vegetables nearby.

Arthur and Hailey had already moved the rigid inflatable boats to the floor.

After a brief discussion, Arthur deflated their original raft. They decided each would pilot a RIB back—quicker and more reliable.

The store held other gear, too: ice skates, sports gloves, golf clubs, and windbreakers.

Hailey snatched a golf club. "For protection," she declared.

As she noticed Cecilia pick up a pair of ice skates, Hailey's eyes lit up. "Smart, Cici! If you're good on these, they're faster and nimbler than running—perfect for a quick escape."

She grabbed a pair in her size, and Arthur followed suit.

Cecilia smiled inwardly. These would be priceless when the Ice Stage hit.
Good eye.

Once they'd gathered what they needed, the group headed out.

When the others weren't looking, Cecilia swept the leftover items—unwanted golf clubs, windbreakers, and skates—into her small purple Space.

With her primary Space still upgrading, she'd worn the backup for exactly this reason.

The mall was vast. Its supermarket had clearly been ransacked earlier, shelves for snacks and drinks stripped bare.

But tucked away in a corner, a small, forced-open door revealed an untouched storage room, still packed with goods.

Hailey gasped, rushing forward to grab a bottle of water, gulping it down desperately.

Cecilia noted her thirst—yet in all their time together, Hailey had never once begged for food or water.

Respectable.

Arthur was already loading duffel bags with sturdy supplies.

Cecilia worked just as quickly, filling her shopping tote while discreetly funneling more into her Space.

In half an hour, they'd packed over 20 bags.

Cecilia's Space held even more, though no one noticed how barren her chosen aisles had become.

"I'm coming back for another load," Hailey announced, too polite to ask the others to join.

Cecilia and Arthur likely had decent supplies already.

Before she could dwell on it, Arthur spoke up, "I'll make another trip too."

A flicker of surprise crossed Cecilia's face. She hadn't expected Arthur to volunteer.

Her eyes followed his intense gaze, fixed on a shelf packed with vacuum-sealed bags of spicy jerky, pepperoni sticks, and Cajun-style dried meats.

So that was it. Arthur had focused on practical items this time—flour, wheat, pasta, things to stave off hunger.

Those flavorful, spicy treats were a luxury for later, making a return trip perfectly reasonable.

"Guess I'm coming along for another round then," Cecilia said.

It seemed she was in for the long haul.

Her attention shifted to Blackie, standing watch nearby with an air of boredom. Pulling a stick of beef jerky from her pocket, Cecilia unwrapped it and offered it to the dog.

Blackie's tail wagged furiously as it eagerly accepted the treat, munching away with evident pleasure.

But halfway through the jerky, Blackie suddenly froze.

The half-eaten treat was dropped, and its body tensed, lowering into a guarded crouch aimed toward the distant supermarket entrance.

"Someone's coming," Cecilia whispered, her voice dropping to a hushed tone.

Blackie was smart; instead of barking, it remained silent, offering only this silent posture as a warning.

No sounds of footsteps reached them yet—whoever it was remained at a distance.

A thought struck Cecilia. She looked at the alert animal. "How many?" she murmured, more out of instinct than expectation.

To her surprise, Blackie seemed to consider the question. Its nose twitched several times, sampling the air with deliberate sniffs. After a moment, it lifted its right front paw and tapped the dusty floor four times with soft precision.

Four taps. Cecilia felt a wave of relief.

Hailey wasn't much in a fight at the moment, but Cecilia herself could hold her own against two.

Arthur's capabilities were unknown, but she had a feeling he could handle three easily enough.

And Blackie? The dog was in a league of its own, easily worth four or five opponents.

A silent glance passed between the group. While Hailey's nerves showed, Cecilia and Arthur moved with calm efficiency. They began loading their gathered supplies onto the rigid inflatable boat, then scrounged for empty cardboard boxes to place underneath. The plan was to use the boxes as makeshift sled runners, allowing them to pull the laden boat away quietly.

They couldn't risk others finding this hidden storage room. Once everything was secured, working together, they maneuvered a heavy shelf unit in front of the warehouse entrance.

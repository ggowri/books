Chapter 136 Too Late

The shelter had always tried to maintain order.

Plenty of people like Phoebe traded their bodies for supplies.

As long as you didn't make it too obvious, the government looked the other way.

But when shit hit the fan? They came down hard.

Phoebe, Jason, and Amanda all got chewed out.

But even officials couldn't settle family drama. Just a verbal warning.

They told Phoebe to get a real job, stop taking shortcuts. They'd be watching her.

With medicine scarce, Phoebe's injuries got a quick bandage job and that was it.

The cold kept infection away, but her inner thighs throbbed constantly. She could barely walk straight, let alone join supply runs.

She couldn't stay in this shelter anymore.

Those old creeps who'd swarmed her before—plus some new young guys—started circling again.

They didn't give a damn that she was hurt.

She had to hide from officials while screwing them in the shadows.

Some bastards didn't even pay up.

Actually, Jason snuck her food a few times.

But then Amanda caught him and beat Phoebe's ass again. Said she'd do it every single time she saw her.

Phoebe was terrified. She started walking with her head down everywhere.

Later she found out—Amanda worked construction hauling bricks. Built like an ox, strong as hell. Someone you absolutely did not mess with.

Scared of getting pregnant, Phoebe kept trading supplies for birth control pills.

But eventually, she couldn't afford it—that stuff was rare as gold.

The government did offer shelter residents a chance to work for supplies.

But her injuries never healed, and with her reputation trashed, guys harassed her constantly on the job. She couldn't get anything done.

So when she heard they were building a new shelter—right in her old apartment building—she lost her mind with excitement.

Word was, people still living in the apartments got to keep their units!

And the 66th floor had cut a deal with the officials—the top floors wouldn't be seized!

Her room was on the 65th floor!

Did that mean she'd get the entire floor to herself when she went back?

So when Logan was heading over to the apartment to set things up, she made her move.

She knew Logan was a gentle, caring guy.

And lucky for her, she overheard him talking about the 66th floor possibly having guns. He even mentioned Hailey by name.

"There are three people on the 66th floor. Cecilia, Arthur, and Hailey. Survivors from this building say they've got weapons. We're in the apocalypse now—our manpower's stretched thin. We can't interrogate them like terrorists. If we push too hard and they snap, innocent people could get hurt. So yeah... it's a mess." Logan sighed.

Phoebe's heart raced. This was her shot.

"Captain Jackman, Hailey and I used to live on the same floor. I know Cecilia too. Maybe I can help you..."

Just like that, Phoebe shamelessly claimed she and Hailey were close friends. Said maybe she could snoop around the 66th floor and find out if they had guns.

With that one lie, she hitched a ride back to the apartment with Logan.

Finally—finally—she'd escaped that disgusting, vile shelter.

Logan wasn't stupid.

He managed these villas. He knew exactly what kind of person Phoebe was.

But he also knew she'd actually come from the 65th floor of that apartment building.

So he agreed to her proposal. Then gave her a hard look.

"Ms. Walls, let's cut the crap. Guns aren't a joke. This affects everyone's safety in the new shelter. So if you can't pull this off, or if you try to play me with those people..." He paused. "Our unit has execution authority now."

Phoebe shuddered.

She'd seen plenty of people shot by these soldiers lately.

Troublemakers. People fighting over supplies. Thieves.

Logan was gentle with regular folks.

But anyone who didn't follow orders? First offense—warning and lecture. Second offense—bullet to the head.

No mercy.

His soldiers were all hardasses.

That iron-fist approach was the only reason things hadn't fallen apart yet.

Phoebe only figured that out later.

She'd even wondered—what if, when she first got bullied, she hadn't chosen obedience and trading her body for scraps?

What if she'd gone to the guards, demanded fairness?

Would things be different now?

But it was too late for that.

So here she stood, at the stairwell door to the sixty-sixth floor, watching them haul load after load of supplies inside.

She thought about the shelter—how she had to let those old bastards grope her just for an extra bite of food...

And the only reason she'd been lurking here on the sixty-sixth floor instead of going back down? Because even standing at the stairwell, she could feel the heat pouring out from inside.

They had to have serious heating equipment in there. The stairwell was a good ten degrees warmer than outside.

She didn't want to leave.

She'd been standing at the stairwell this whole time, mind racing.

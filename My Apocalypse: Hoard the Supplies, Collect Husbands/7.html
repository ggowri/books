Chapter 7 Stock

Cecilia knelt down, gently patting Blackie's head and pointing to the long scar on his back. "His injuries were too severe; he's no longer fit for strenuous physical activity, so he had to retire."

Noticing many in the crowd looking moved, she stood up and raised her voice. "If a decorated hero like this can't integrate into ordinary life or enjoy a peaceful retirement with a family after serving, where is our conscience?

"Is the fate of these military dogs to serve the people, only to be cast aside? They're living beings—they've saved more lives than most of us will in a lifetime!

"When I adopted Blackie, the officer who handed him over kept telling me to take good care of him. Blackie was their comrade-in-arms, enduring harsh training and standing on the front lines of danger just like those soldiers!

"He deserves respect and a good life, not slander from petty people!"

The man was still livestreaming, hoping to stir up more trouble. But seeing the crowd turn completely against him and hearing Cecilia's impassioned words, he frantically fumbled to end the stream.

A round of applause erupted after she finished speaking. The loudest claps came from the young woman and the police officer.

In the end, the man slunk away, being cursed by many as he left.

The young woman and the officer gave Cecilia and Blackie a thumbs-up.

Blackie sat quietly beside Cecilia, panting with his tongue out, showing no sign of pride or conceit, unperturbed. It only made him more endearing.

Once the crowd dispersed, Cecilia knelt down and gave Blackie a big hug. "Blackie, were you protecting me just now? You're amazing!"

Blackie pushed her away with a paw, his large eyes filled with a hint of disdain.

After all the commotion, though, Blackie refused to get back in the car no matter what.

Reluctantly, Cecilia had to take him upstairs.

By then, the agent had finished discussing with his boss and called Cecilia over.

Cecilia figured she might as well bring Blackie upstairs to check out the penthouse—see if he approved. After all, he'd be one of the future residents, too.

Since Cecilia was selling her house far below market value, the agency's boss decided to buy it himself and resell it later.

A contract was signed that afternoon.

Located in a prime area and spanning 1,615 square feet, her house was always a top seller in the city. At market price, it would have sold for around 4.6 million dollars.

By cutting the price by 200 thousand dollars, Cecilia would net 4.4 million dollars.

Right after signing the contract, the boss paid her a 50-thousand-dollar deposit, saying the title transfer would be completed the next day.

With the deposit in hand and time still on her side, Cecilia decided to take Blackie out for a treat.

First, they went to the nearest pet store.

Once inside, she told Blackie to pick out any treats or toys he liked.

Taking the hint from Cecilia's slight wave, Blackie wasted no time in "raiding" the shelves.

He grabbed a pile of freeze-dried snacks, canned food, and dried chicken strips.

For every item he liked, Cecilia asked the store how much they had and bought all of it.

He even chose his own dog food, sniffing left and right at the bulk bins before selecting a few favorites.

Again, Cecilia bought every bag the store had.

Most pet stores didn't carry large inventories, though, so after visiting two or three shops, they only had a trunk full of dog supplies.

It was then that Cecilia discovered Blackie was a picky eater. He favored chicken and beef flavors, turning up his nose at cod, salmon, and other fish-based products.

He only reluctantly settled on two cans of salmon.

Looking at the trunk full of supplies, Cecilia tapped Blackie's head. "Picky eating isn't good, Blackie. Can we pick out some snacks of different flavors at the next store?"

Blackie acted as if he hadn't heard her, turning his big head to the side.

Clearly, he was stubborn when it came to food.

So, at the next store, Cecilia chose several fish-based cans and snacks for him.

She wanted to add some variety to his diet.

What if he got tired of eating only chicken, beef, and venison in the future?

People's tastes changed, and dogs' might too.

Blackie looked unhappy, his ears drooping and his eyes rolling slightly, as if saying, "I dislike you."

Cecilia sighed helplessly, but she knew a balanced diet was important.

"Picky eaters aren't good kids. We'll just buy a little, okay?"

After some persuasion, Blackie finally perked up a bit.

When it came to toys, Cecilia noticed Blackie had a soft spot for plush ones.

He liked all styles—even a colorful, ugly little dog toy that Cecilia thought was almost unbearable to look at. He carried it off the shelf as if it were a treasure.

Some of the plush toys were no bigger than her palm, barely filling Blackie's mouth, but she bought them all because he liked them.

Cecilia also purchased more than twenty dog beds from several pet stores, wanting to ensure Blackie would sleep comfortably.

Thanks to the large quantities she bought, each store threw in some extra chew sticks as gifts.

It was then that she remembered dogs needed to brush their teeth too!

Plus, after the apocalypse, Blackie wouldn't be able to go to pet stores for baths, and water and electricity would be cut off.

She bought enough pet shampoo and toothpaste to last Blackie a lifetime, along with five or six large hair dryers for drying his fur.

After the apocalypse, Blackie would mutate and grow even larger. During the extreme cold, wet fur could easily make him sick if not dried properly.

Cecilia also picked up various other miscellaneous items, and after visiting more than a dozen pet stores, she finally felt satisfied.

Every time the trunk was full, she stored the supplies in the Space.

After making seven or eight trips like this, Blackie had more than enough food and supplies to last through the apocalypse.

Just then, Cecilia thought about Blackie's bathroom needs. And her own.

With no water or electricity after the apocalypse, flushing toilets would be useless. She needed an alternative way to take care of business.

It was then that Cecilia spotted cat litter and pet pee pads in the pet store.

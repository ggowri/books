Chapter 116 Acting Dumb

Hearing Jet's fake-sweet tone, Cecilia instantly got it—she'd been letting him watch those trashy romance dramas lately.

The scheming side character in those shows talked exactly like this.

Now Jet had picked up every trick!

Annoyed, Cecilia grabbed a big tub from the Space, filled it with Magic Spring water and acid rain, and without a word, dumped both Blackie and Jet in.

Once Jet hit the water, he finally shut up for a second.

Too busy splashing around. Finally, some peace and quiet.

Cecilia grabbed Blackie's bath brush and started scrubbing him down.

Blackie half-closed his eyes, humming contentedly whenever she got under his chin.

He looked pretty damn happy.

Cecilia wasn't sure if it was from the run outside or what.

But seeing both animals so cheerful made her feel better too.

Blackie was filthy.

After one tub of water, Cecilia had to dump it and fill another.

Jet was paddling around like a duck, splashing water everywhere.

Cecilia tossed in a rubber ducky and let her play.

Then she lathered Blackie up with soap until he smelled fresh and was squeaky clean.

After that, Cecilia filled another tub for herself and climbed in.

Slowly, she drifted off to sleep.

She didn't know how long she'd been out—she woke to the sound of Blackie shaking water everywhere.

She checked the time. 2 PM. Her stomach was screaming.

She got up quickly, dried off Jet and Blackie.

She felt way more refreshed now.

She asked Blackie again if he'd gotten the supplies.

Blackie just panted, tongue hanging out, head tilted—like he had no idea what she was talking about.

Cecilia's worry spiked.

Does he look fine but actually have internal injuries or something?

Otherwise why's he acting so dumb?

She checked him over again. No wounds anywhere. Finally, she relaxed.

He was just a dog, after all. Probably traumatized from everything. She'd ask him properly later.

Right now, she needed to deal with those carcasses—the big white cat, the weasel, and that half-eaten black cat the weasel had been munching on.

Time to bury them in the Black Soil.

But then—what the hell?

No matter how hard she tried to summon them, the three bodies wouldn't appear.

She frantically searched the Space, looking for where they'd gone.

Nothing. Absolutely nothing.

The three carcasses had just... vanished!

Last time she'd brought in a mutant bird carcass, she'd entered the Space with it and buried it directly in Plot One.

This time...

She'd just tossed all three bodies straight into the Space.

So the Space had just... eaten them?

"Damn Space! Could've at least told me before scarfing them down! Made me waste all this time searching!"

Cecilia flipped off the empty air.

Of course, the Space didn't respond.

She'd had plans for that weasel pelt too!

Mutant animal fur—obviously insanely warm.

Temperatures were going to keep dropping. Sure, she had her thermal suit and mink coat, but mutant fur would've been way better!

Now? Gone. All of it.

What could she even say?

When you're stuck with a Space system like this, you just gotta deal with it!

But wait—she still had one thing: the mutant white cat's tail!

She'd kept it strapped to her body, so the Space hadn't snatched it.

She walked over to the garbage processor, eyes gleaming with an idea, and tossed the tail in.

The processor swallowed it instantly.

Cecilia rubbed her chin, curious what it would spit out tomorrow.

After that, she checked what today's processor haul was.

Actually, the processor had been halfway decent today.

It had produced... snow boots! A matching pair!

Cecilia almost cried with joy.

This damn machine had given her one glove and one sock before.

And still hadn't produced the matching pairs!

Oh, it had given her another sock a few days back.

Except the first sock was red. The second one? Green.

Those two socks sitting together felt like the processor was straight-up mocking her.

As for Plot One and Plot Two, Cecilia started planting on a massive scale.

Except Plot One was a total troll.

Cecilia would plant a whole field of seeds, and it'd randomly spit out whatever it felt like.

On good days? An entire field of bullets.

The day she harvested those, Cecilia couldn't stop grinning—a whole damn crate!

But on bad days? She'd break her back planting tons of seeds, vegetables, all kinds of crap.

And what did she get? One steaming cup of vegetable juice.

Cecilia wanted to cry.

Then there was Plot Two.

She transplanted that tree from Plot One over there, and what happened?

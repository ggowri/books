Chapter 113 A Hole

Three mutant cats.

Cecilia scanned the floor. Another production line. Beef and chicken canned goods.

She smirked. Well, well. Blackie's favorites.

She tied the cat tail to her belt and strolled right in.

The mutant cats noticed her immediately.

Just like she'd thought.

The second they saw the corpse dangling from her waist, the cats shrieked and bolted off the floor.

Cecilia exhaled. Finally.

Time to loot.

Unlike the pristine ninth floor, this level was trashed—mutant cats had torn through half the inventory.

She sorted carefully, taking only the intact cans into the Space.

Eighth floor done. Then seventh. Sixth. Fifth.

Same story every time—cats howled and scattered the moment she showed up.

One big black cat puffed up and hissed at her, like it was ready to fight.

It lasted all of three seconds before it bolted.

Over those three floors, Cecilia scored some other canned meats.

But the real jackpot? Fifth floor—pet food cans.

No wonder all the cats hung out here.

Most of the cans were ripped open and devoured, but Cecilia still salvaged a few hundred.

Before leaving, Cecilia eyed the entire pet food canning production line on this floor. Her eyebrow quirked. Then she swept the whole thing into the Space.

Who knows? Maybe it'd make some canned food for Blackie someday.

And once that idea took root, she headed upstairs and grabbed two regular canning lines too. Into the Space.

Done and dusted.

Now came the hard part.

Cecilia stared at the passage leading down to the fourth floor.

According to the files she'd read, the warehouse was down there.

Upper floors? Production lines. Lower floors? Storage.

Four entire floors, plus two basement levels below—all stocked with meat!

These six floors were basically one giant freezer, a cold storage facility the factory had built, wrapped tight in stainless steel with its own generator power.

According to the floor plan in the contract, the lower levels only had one main door connecting them, with an internal staircase inside.

Normally, the production line workers used the external stairs in the back. They only entered through the fourth-floor stairwell when they needed to access the cold storage.

When Cecilia saw that description in the contract, she'd been sure the place would be well-sealed—safe from the acid rain. That's why she'd risked coming here.

Only problem? This area sat slightly lower than her building. So the acid rain hadn't just flooded four floors—it'd risen halfway between the fourth and fifth.

And at the stairwell landing, a window had been left open. Acid rain had poured in.

Now a solid foot and a half of ice blocked the way.

No way Cecilia could break through that. Not with her current strength.

That's when Cecilia suddenly spotted it.

Right next to the ice layer was a hole about a foot and a half across.

Her nerves kicked in instantly.

Some kind of mutant creature had dug this.

But after the initial panic came pure excitement!

The animal had tunneled through after the Ice Stage, and the hole led downward—meaning there was livable space below for mutant animals.

Which meant the area below was well-sealed. The acid rain hadn't gotten in!

And... this hole? Blackie could totally fit through it now that he could change size!

"Blackie!"

Cecilia called him over and pointed at the hole.

"Can you get in there?"

Blackie glanced at the opening, then looked back at her with droopy eyes, whites showing slightly, like—seriously? You doubting me?

Cecilia didn't hesitate. She immediately pulled out that purple pendant she'd gotten at the gemstone market.

She dripped another drop of blood on it, then mentally communicated with the Space to unbind it.

Soon, she felt the connection sever. Unbinding successful.

The moment she did, her Space started vibrating like crazy against her chest, practically screaming—eat it, want it, smells so good, give it to me!

Ignoring the Space's tantrum, Cecilia took out a needle and pricked Blackie's paw pad, letting his blood drip into the Space.

Blackie watched, confused.

But once his blood fully merged with the Space, his eyes went wide.

Cecilia knew—he'd connected with it.

She pulled a can from her own Space and demonstrated several times how to put it inside the Space.

Blackie watched quietly, big head tilted, looking puzzled.

After multiple demos and lots of verbal coaching, Blackie finally touched the can with his paw—and it vanished!

Just to be safe, Cecilia had him practice with a few more items.

Blackie nailed them all.

"Blackie, the mission to collect supplies from the floors below is all yours!"

Cecilia gave his head a gentle pat.

Blackie's brow furrowed hard. He looked at her with those droopy lower whites showing, face screaming do I have to?

Cecilia knew this was a tall order for a freshly evolved dog, but she didn't have any other options.

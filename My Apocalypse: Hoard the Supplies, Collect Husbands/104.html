Chapter 104 Charcoal

Then, late at night when everyone was asleep, she collected every piece of furniture and firewood she could find nearby and crammed it all into her place—filling the 48th floor's rooms and hallways to the brim.

Other people's problems? Not her concern.

She wasn't the building manager anymore. Why should she care?

But then, while Mabel was sleeping warm and cozy at home, shouting from the stairwell jolted her awake.

Her husband Charles—a real estate agent—grabbed a cleaver in one hand and an axe in the other, and charged out.

"What the hell's going on? Why are you making noise at my door!"

Brandon was hacking at the door with a massive machete.

"Open up, you son of a bitch! Did you two strip the whole damn building of firewood? Trying to freeze us all to death?"

Charles's eyes darted. Then his face split into a grin.

"Oh! Brandon, buddy! Easy now—what are you saying? We've been friends, haven't we? Neighbors don't do that to each other. That was all the 66th floor—those bastards!"

Charles and Mabel had rehearsed this excuse.

Pin it on the 66th floor. Because their stash wasn't just firewood—they had way more supplies hidden away. If the others found out, they were dead.

"Bullshit! They went down looking for firewood and came back empty-handed!" Brandon roared, ignoring Charles completely. He kept kicking the door.

But he'd barely eaten in days. After a few weak kicks, he was gasping for air.

The others crowded around, hurling insults.

One skinny kid—tall but stick-thin—glared at the door with bloodshot eyes. He was shaking. Rage? Cold? Hard to tell.

"You stole our firewood! Open up, or we're breaking in!"

Charles realized the jig was up. No talking his way out now. He grinned at the door.

"Go ahead. Try. Hope you make it in before you starve."

He turned and walked back inside, leaving them to their tantrum.

He felt pretty damn smug about copying the 66th floor's strategy—sealing off the stairwell with heavy iron gates.

Those half-starved skeletons could scream all they wanted. They weren't getting through.

Inside, Mabel clutched her chest. "You okay?"

Charles chuckled and stroked her cheek. "I'm fine. Let them yell. What are they gonna do?"

Mabel giggled and nestled into his arms. "You're so smart, honey."

Outside, the mob eventually gave up. They spat at the door a few times.

"Don't you dare come out! I'll kill you!" Brandon landed one last pathetic kick, then stormed off.

He didn't notice the eyes watching him from down the hall.

Cold. Hateful.

Ashley's daughter-in-law.

******

Meanwhile, Cecilia and the others tried to cross from Tower A to Tower B through the connecting walkway.

But when they got there—the walkway was gone.

Well, not completely. The 40th-floor corridor was semi-outdoor, with a small roof overhead and open sides for ventilation.

After months of acid rain and days of brutal cold, the middle section had collapsed.

A gap of about ten feet separated the two sides.

They stood at the edge of Tower A, staring across.

No wonder nobody from Tower A had made it to Tower B.

"I can make it," Arthur said after a moment.

Cecilia glanced at him. Not surprised.

"I've got rope."

She pulled a thick hemp rope from her pack—two fingers wide.

She'd suspected this might happen. After everything she'd seen in her past life, acid rain destroying buildings was nothing new.

Guess her hunch paid off.

Arthur took the rope—then backed up.

Cecilia was about to ask what he was doing when he sprinted forward, covering a dozen meters in seconds. Then he launched himself across the gap like a damn leopard.

Clean. Effortless.

"Wait here. I'll find something to bridge the gap."

Arthur disappeared. A few minutes later, he came back, dragging a massive tabletop. He dropped it across the gap, connecting both sides.

Just like that, they crossed into Tower B.

As expected—fewer residents, way more supplies.

Hailey and Arthur weren't hurting for food anymore. And they had their mink coats.

What they needed was firewood—especially hardwood that burned slow.

So first priority: find wood. As much as they could carry.

But on this floor, in one of the offices, they found something even better.

Charcoal.

Looked like some company had planned an outdoor camping retreat. Then the apocalypse hit and they never left.

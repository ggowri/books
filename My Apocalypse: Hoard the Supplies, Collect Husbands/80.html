Chapter 80 Regret

Otherwise, with Alexander looking so sickly and peaky right now, Cecilia would never choose him.

When the door opened, Cecilia shot Blackie a look, and he quickly moved to block their apartment entrance. He stood guard to make sure no one would try to sneak in and steal anything while they were gone.

Arthur gave Cecilia a slight nod and positioned himself at the doorway, preventing more people from crowding in. Too many people would just create chaos on their floor.

Hailey stayed with Blackie, and the two of them guarded the entrance together.

The three of them looked so defensive that several people started complaining, but when they saw Blackie—who was almost as big as a small pony—they shut their mouths real quick.

Still, with this many people around, someone always had to stir up trouble.

One person spoke up, saying they were worried Cecilia and her group might secretly keep some of the supplies for themselves.

Cecilia shot them an icy glare. "You people asked us to pick volunteers to carry the supplies upstairs. You chose who went up. And now you're saying you're worried about people stealing? Please. You're just looking for a reason to complain."

That shut everyone up.

Cecilia followed the group of volunteers upstairs. When they opened the small door to go in, everyone formed a single-file line and entered one by one.

Alexander ended up standing right behind Cecilia.

She caught a whiff of something rotten and nearly gagged.

She remembered that Rebecca had soiled herself before, and they must have used up half their drinking water just to get cleaned up.

Over the past few days, any water they had went straight to drinking—there was no way they had enough left to stay clean.

Alexander reeked. He smelled worse than almost everyone else in the crowd.

Cecilia could feel the stench rolling off him as Alexander leaned in close and whispered in her ear, "Cici, for old time's sake, could you let me and Rebecca move up here?"

She fought back the urge to gag. The smell was disgusting, but his intentions were even worse. And he actually thought she'd say yes?

"So you want me to find you a safe room on the sixty-sixth floor and give you and Rebecca the most comfortable bed on the entire level?" Cecilia asked with a slight smile, watching him closely.

Alexander froze, and his eyes lit up with hope. He rushed to explain, "No, no, anywhere is fine! The floor below ours is completely flooded with acid rain, and our walls are starting to rot and fall apart. We can't live there anymore!"

"Can't live there?" Cecilia wanted to laugh. If they couldn't live there, they could pick any other floor. Why did it have to be the sixty-sixth?

"And let me guess—you also want me to share my water and food with you?" Her smile grew softer, almost gentle.

At the mention of water and food, Alexander licked his cracked lips. "Cici, you wouldn't just let us die, would you? Rebecca and I are completely out of water and supplies. Yesterday she almost drank the acid rain outside. We built a makeshift distillation setup, but we only get a few drops of drinking water each day. We're dying here."

Cecilia stared at this pathetic, reeking man and wondered how she'd ever been blind enough to love him. She held her breath and stepped closer.

She could see his face flush red. Of course it did. She'd always been beautiful, but after drinking Magic Spring water, she'd become stunning. Getting this close to any man would make him blush.

Right now, she had no idea how much Alexander regretted choosing Rebecca.

He wanted to slap himself. He'd even considered whether Cecilia would forgive him if he got on his knees and begged her to take him back.

Now, looking at Cecilia—beautiful, composed, untouchable—and thinking back to Rebecca crying in the corner with her pants down, expecting him to clean up after her, Alexander's regret burned through him like acid.

"Cici..." His voice came out soft, almost pleading, and he angled his face just slightly to the side. He remembered how she used to love his profile.

"Keep dreaming. Go back to your house." Cecilia's words cut through the air, cold and final. She turned and headed for the top floor without looking back.

Alexander stood frozen, stunned.

What no one expected was the chaos waiting on the top floor. The two men were fighting, throwing punches, while the woman they'd sent up to help tried to pull them apart. One of them shoved her away, and she hit the ground hard.

And Ashley? She sat on the floor in silence, cramming food into her mouth as fast as she could swallow.

Cecilia's eyes narrowed as she recognized the two men. They were from the 23rd floor—business partners who'd supposedly been "brothers." She remembered passing their floor weeks ago during one of those midnight climbs after clearing out the building across the street.

She'd overheard them talking about sticking together through the crisis, facing whatever came as a team.

Now they were beating each other bloody over scraps.

But Ashley was worse. The supplies scattered across the floor weren't hers alone—they belonged to everyone. Yet she'd already torn through the pile like a starving animal, empty water bottles and torn wrappers littering the ground around her.

Cecilia did the math quickly. There wasn't much up here to begin with. Maybe two bottles of water per person, a few packs of compressed crackers at most.

Ashley had already downed three bottles and was still shoving crackers into her mouth, her cheeks bulging.

Cecilia didn't say a word. She simply turned and walked out.

Ignoring Alexander completely, she moved to the stairwell door and pulled it open. "Everyone can come up and see for yourselves," she called down. "Just don't trample anyone on your way."

She stepped back and caught Arthur's eye. Together, they moved to a safe distance.

The people who'd been waiting anxiously below craned their necks, desperate to see what was up there.

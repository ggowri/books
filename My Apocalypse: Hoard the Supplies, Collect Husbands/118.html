Chapter 118 Murder

Cecilia finally grabbed Blackie by the ears and told him to control himself—he couldn't eat this much!

But instead of slowing down, Blackie acted starved every single day.

Cecilia did the math. His appetite had gone from bowls to entire pots.

Jet saw Blackie eating so much and turned it into a competition, stuffing himself nonstop. Within days, he'd gotten noticeably bigger too.

Not from mutating—just fat.

Cecilia wanted to cry. She didn't stockpile food for this!

It took five full days before Blackie's appetite finally returned to normal.

Cecilia's conclusion? That trip outside had drained him massively. He needed days to replenish all that energy.

So Blackie was powerful, sure—but she couldn't let him use his abilities freely.

Otherwise, she really couldn't afford to feed him!

******

A few peaceful days passed.

That night, Cecilia woke up freezing again.

She jumped up and loaded every stove with Space-produced firewood.

Looking out the floor-to-ceiling windows, everything outside was a white blur!

Dawn was breaking. The sun barely rising, reflecting bright white light off the snowflakes.

The flakes plummeted down fast, whipped by wind into white curtains.

A blizzard had arrived.

Then Cecilia heard someone pounding on the stairwell door.

She got dressed, grabbed Blackie, and rushed out.

Hailey and Arthur appeared too, both bundled up tight.

At the stairwell, Cecilia heard Mabel sobbing loudly outside.

"Please, please give us some firewood! Those animals broke into our place! They took everything we saved! My husband—my husband's freezing to death! Please!"

Cecilia looked at Mabel standing there, wearing layers upon layers, even blankets tied around her body with rope.

This was the average person in the apocalypse. Without mink coats or real cold-weather gear, they had to wrap themselves in whatever they could find.

"What's that got to do with us?"

Cecilia ignored Mabel and shut the door.

Outside, Mabel wailed and pounded on it.

The door stayed closed.

No one answered. No one helped.

Arthur said nothing, just headed back to his place looking half-asleep—like he'd been napping.

Hailey walked over to Cecilia and said, "Mabel used to be one of us. She was the building manager back then. Everyone liked her—she smiled at everyone, got things done. But after the apocalypse hit..."

She trailed off.

Cecilia got it. Hailey had been here longer—seen more, knew more. No wonder she looked so damn tired.

But to Cecilia? Mabel was just another stranger. Another person to watch out for in this hellscape.

After everyone went back inside, Mabel kept wailing outside.

Started with begging. Ended with cursing.

She cursed Cecilia's group for being heartless, cold-blooded, leaving people to die. Finally claimed if her husband died, it'd be their fault!

Inside, Jet—with his sharp hearing—was fuming, muttering insults back at Mabel under his breath.

His wings flapped wildly around the room, and his beak hammered the wooden perch Cecilia had set up for him.

Bang. Bang. Bang.

Loud enough to show just how pissed off he was.

"Dare curse her? I'll peck you to death!"

"Don't leave! I'll knock you flat when I get out there!"

"I can't take this anymore! I'm gonna explode!"

Finally, Cecilia grabbed his head.

"Okay, that's enough. Stop bouncing around."

Jet hopped around so much he even tried pecking Blackie's fur out of spite.

One look from Blackie sent him scurrying.

Who knew Mabel would get more fired up the longer she ranted!

She didn't know the others on the sixty-sixth floor, but she knew Cecilia and Hailey's names.

So she went after their entire bloodlines with the foulest mouth imaginable.

Cecilia's brow furrowed. She let out a cold snort, grabbed her knife, and walked straight out.

No words. She opened the door, raised the blade, and swung down at Mabel.

"AHHH! Murder! She's killing me!"

Mabel's face went white as a sheet.

The blade sliced clean through half of Mabel's coat—but didn't touch skin.

She was wearing too many damn layers.

Hailey burst out right behind her, face pale, wrapped in a fur coat, gripping her golf club.

Her trembling lips said it all—she was furious.

Cecilia knew some of Hailey's story. Her parents had been deeply in love, treated her like gold. Before the disaster hit, she'd called them. Then nothing.

She'd been worried sick ever since.

The moment she got the rigid inflatable boat, she went straight to their place. The apartment was trashed—blood, signs of a struggle. But her parents were gone.

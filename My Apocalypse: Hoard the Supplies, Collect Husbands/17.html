Chapter 17 In Place

That night, sleep came deeper and more restful than Cecilia had dared hope.

Cutting ties with Alexander—her greatest pre-apocalyptic burden—and Rebecca before the world fell apart felt like mending a jagged regret from her past life.

But this was merely the prolog.

When the apocalypse arrived, there would be no shortage of chances to settle old scores.

Early the next morning, she woke to the harsh blast of air conditioning—on again, without a doubt, thanks to Blackie.

She crossed to where he lay, gaze soft with helplessness.

The dog showed no hint of remorse, just a lazy flicker of an eyelid as he glanced up at her.

"Blackie, I don't mind the cool air, but can we dial it back a little? It's freezing," she said, a sneeze catching in her throat.

The comment earned her a disdainful look, as if the dog were scoffing, "You're far too fragile."

She let it slide.

Too much work awaited to bicker with a stubborn dog.

First on the list was inspecting the anti-corrosion renovations, followed by arranging for the heating stoves to be installed.

Tomorrow, she'd move out for good—the real estate agent was coming to reclaim the old place's keys.

After their usual morning workout and a quick stop for breakfast, Cecilia took Blackie to the new penthouse for the inspection.

Before they reached the door, voices drifted over from the corridor corner—construction workers, gossiping about her project.

"Just a young chick—she'll never catch on we swapped her materials," one grunted.

"Total waste. Blowing cash on fancy anti-corrosion walls? Such a waste of good money," another agreed.

Cecilia pulled out her phone and hit record, capturing every word.

Instead of heading inside, she turned on her heel and called the renovation company directly.

"Fix my walls with the exact materials I paid for in two days. If not, I'll post this recording online, and you can deal with the fallout," she demanded, voice sharp.

The company owner scrambled to distance himself, insisting the workers had acted alone and vowing to make amends.

Cecilia rolled her eyes—she'd heard enough excuses. Instead of arguing, she used the incident to negotiate compensation, adding a tidy sum to her stockpiling fund.

She also pressured the company to source a batch of advanced anti-corrosion materials—harder to obtain, more effective, and far pricier than her original choice.

This batch, she decided, would go straight to the penthouse rooftop, an extra layer of security against the apocalypse's harsh elements.

The company promised to send trusted workers for the rooftop job.

With that settled, Cecilia took Blackie to shop for heating stoves.

Blackie was a model of obedience in public, sticking close to Cecilia's side—matching her steps, pausing when she did.

They visited several building materials markets without a hitch. While there, she picked up practical odds and ends: folding chairs, a folding table, and camping gear.

Cecilia wouldn't need them immediately, but they might prove invaluable when the worst of the extreme weather passed and she had to leave the penthouse.

When she returned to the construction site that afternoon, the walls were being repainted as promised—a wave of relief washing over her.

The workers' attitudes had shifted, too; their earlier contempt was gone, replaced by wariness, and they worked with newfound diligence.

That evening, she and Blackie headed to the suburban slaughterhouses.

Just as she remembered, the area housed facilities for cattle, sheep, pigs, and poultry.

To her delight, Cecilia also spotted an egg farm nearby—stocked not just with chicken eggs, but quail, goose, and duck eggs too.

Cecilia informed the farm owner that she was launching a supermarket chain and placed a substantial order: 300 pounds of chicken eggs, quail eggs, and duck eggs per day, along with a selection of preserved and seasoned egg products. She committed to a one-month supply agreement.

Eggs were versatile—scrambled, poached, in soups, custards, puddings—all delicious, and all essential for survival.

She paid the deposit on the spot, giving them a vague delivery address near her new penthouse.

The area, part of the city's largest commercial district, was bustling with businesses—frequent deliveries would raise no eyebrows.

Each day, Cecilia would push a small cart to pick up the goods, then find a quiet moment to stow them in the Space.

Next, she visited the slaughterhouses, using the same "supermarket chain" cover to place daily orders: 500 pounds each of pork, beef, lamb, and chicken, also for a month.

With money no longer a constraint, Cecilia could stockpile without hesitation.

Before heading home, she stopped by a coal yard to buy a large batch of coal and charcoal.

These were hard commodities, resistant to haggling, but after persistent negotiation, she secured a 10% discount.

Next door to the coal yard was a salt factory, so she ordered several thousand pounds of salt.

The factory owner happened to know a sugar supplier from the south, and through him, she added several thousand pounds of various sugars to her stockpile.

Most of the essentials for food, daily use, and warmth were now in place, but building a comfortable post-apocalyptic life would take time.

The generator and solar panels she'd ordered online had already shipped, on their way to her.

Chapter 138 All Armed

Cecilia had heard everything from inside.

She just hadn't stepped out—wanted to see how Hailey would handle it.

After all, of the three of them, Hailey was the weakest.

"Still, shame Phoebe got rescued." Cecilia sighed.

******

Meanwhile, after being saved, Phoebe grabbed the sleeve of the young soldier who'd helped her.

"Please, stay with me? I'm so scared."

The kid was just a conscript—second year of service, nineteen years old.

Phoebe was years older than him. The act was pathetic.

The soldier frowned and shook her off.

"Sorry, we've got work to do. A medic will treat your wounds. Get some rest."

But Phoebe wouldn't let go. She gripped him even tighter.

"Your Captain wanted me to find out if the 66th floor has guns. I found something important. Please take me to him!"

Thanks to her thick clothes, the stab wound hadn't hit anything vital. Besides the pain, the bleeding had stopped.

One thought consumed her. Take down the 66th floor, or I'm dead.

Hearing it involved firearms, the soldier nodded and brought her to Logan.

When she saw Logan, Phoebe got straight to the point.

"Yes. Everyone on the 66th floor has a gun. I just got Hailey pissed off enough to let it slip."

Logan's brow furrowed. He pictured Hailey stabbing Phoebe moments ago.

In his eyes, the 66th floor was no different from a gang of thugs now.

"Captain Jackman, if we let the 66th floor keep acting like this, the shelter's in danger!" Phoebe's eyes brimmed with tears.

******

After hauling up the first load, Cecilia and Arthur gave Hailey some instructions before heading back down for round two.

But before they even reached the ground floor, Cecilia heard Blackie barking wildly.

"Woof! Woof woof! Grr... WOOF!"

His barks were frantic—a warning.

Cecilia bolted downstairs.

The floor had been too high earlier—she hadn't heard anything.

Had something happened to Blackie in just those few minutes?

When she burst outside, she saw seven or eight men surrounding him.

Each one held knives and clubs, eyes gleaming with vicious greed as they stared at Blackie.

"This dog's so fat—bet he'd taste great in a stew! I haven't had meat in forever!"

"Boss, there's gotta be stuff in that RV. Let's kill the dog and take everything!"

"Today's our lucky day! Fresh meat!"

Blackie stood guard at the RV door, following Cecilia's orders to the letter. He hadn't moved an inch.

He'd even cleverly shoved the rigid inflatable boat against the RV's window to keep the men from smashing the glass.

But there were too many of them. All armed.

Blackie was exhausted from defending the RV and the boat—he couldn't protect himself.

Already injured, he now had fresh wounds all over his body.

He howled loudly, his body moving lightning-fast to shield the RV behind him.

He wasn't gonna let anyone touch the RV or the boat!

But there were too many of them. Sticks rained down on Blackie. The leader's knife nearly stabbed his eye!

Even then, Blackie didn't shift into his true form to scare them off. He followed every one of Cecilia's commands.

"Get away from my Blackie!"

Cecilia's eyes blazed red.

She sprinted toward the RV, grabbed her massive knife, and charged into the mob.

No words. She swung straight at the guy with the blade aimed at Blackie.

The man screamed and collapsed, blood spraying everywhere.

But it wasn't enough!

Cecilia had seen at least five gashes on Blackie—all from that bastard's knife. One spot had been stabbed deep, blood pooling in the white snow.

Her heart shattered.

As she raised the knife for a second strike, the other men snapped out of it. They swung their clubs at her.

Cecilia kicked one guy down, then slashed at another.

In seconds, three were on the ground.

But there were still more.

Like a pack of rabid dogs, eyes locked on Blackie, the RV, and Cecilia—her hat knocked off, hair spilling loose from the fight.

"Well, today's our lucky day. Let's score big, boys."

One guy she'd slashed got lucky—missed anything vital. He was already back on his feet, didn't even care.

Anyone who could still move stumbled upright.

A dozen men closed in, circling like wolves.

Cecilia held Blackie close with one arm, knife in the other hand. Her rage was boiling over.

The men knew both Cecilia and the black dog were dangerous. Nobody made the first move.

They just circled, waiting for an opening.

Then the leader shot a look at his crew.

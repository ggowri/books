Chapter 5 Penthouse

After studying the Space, Cecilia returned to scribbling in her notebook, filling pages for quite some time.

Physical fitness topped the list—non-negotiable.

Even with Blackie's protection, she couldn't afford to grow dependent on him.

In her last life, she'd been skilled with a knife, her apocalypse-honed vigilance and hard-earned combat moves sharp and effective.

But her current body was weak, lacking muscle mass; building strength was essential.

The rest of Cecilia's list centered on stockpiling: food, medicine for harsh weather, clothing, daily necessities, and supplies for Blackie.

She also needed vehicles to handle snow, floods, and earthquakes: an RV, a sled that Blackie could pull, an off-road truck, and a small boat.

Tents, camping lanterns, shovels, solar panels, generators, and water purification equipment were just as crucial.

Home renovations came next.

Cecilia looked around her current house—spacious at 1,615 square feet, left to her by her parents, and perfectly located in the city center.

But the floor was too low.

Acid rain, floods, and blizzards would submerge buildings up to the 20th floor. For safety, she needed a unit on the 20th floor or higher, preferably the top floor.

A penthouse would offer quick access to the rooftop, an extra layer of security if disaster struck.

The thought triggered a memory from Cecilia's last life.

A month after the apocalypse, acid rain had been pouring nonstop, submerging most streets and forcing travel by boat.

Society had collapsed, and most families were running low on food.

Everyone scrambled to scavenge supplies, terrified of what lay ahead.

Cecilia had joined a small group of survivors to search for resources, stumbling upon a 12-story mid-rise building.

The first two floors, a courier hub, were flooded. But the upper floors housed live-streaming e-commerce companies, stocked with everything imaginable.

Unfortunately, the prolonged rain had corroded the building's structure.

Goods in the courier hub were soaked in acid rain, inedible.

The old building's poor waterproofing had led to leaks, damaging much of the upper-floor supplies.

Though the group left with full loads, they grieved the wasted resources.

With that memory fresh, Cecilia grabbed her phone, opened the map, and searched for the building by memory.

After a few failed attempts, she recognized it in the satellite view—Nova Tower.

"That's the one!"

She jumped for joy, dropping her pen.

Beside her, Blackie, mid-chew on a fluffy sheep toy, froze, his head snapping up. His mouth hung open in mid-bite, looking faintly ridiculous.

"Blackie, let's go out!"

Cecilia rushed over, throwing her arms around his neck.

Startled by the sudden affection, Blackie sat up, staring at her as if she'd lost her mind.

Undeterred, she grabbed his leash, grabbed her car keys, and headed straight for Nova Tower.

She planned to rent a high-rise apartment near Nova Tower.

Once the apocalypse hit and floodwaters submerged the first floor, she and Blackie would sneak into the tower and claim all remaining supplies—better than letting them go to waste.

Driving around the area, she quickly found a promising building. A 66-story mixed-use apartment complex with two towers, Tower A and Tower B, and a sprawling penthouse on the top floor.

The large space was a must for Blackie; trapped indoors during the apocalypse, he'd need room to move.

Cecilia called the real estate agent listed on the rental app, asking about penthouse availability, preferably a large unit.

The agent's voice lit up with excitement. "You're in luck! We just had a 2,153-square-foot penthouse free up. Are you looking for a long-term or short-term lease?"

Blackie watched Cecilia, or more precisely, the phone in her hand, his ears drooping as if dismissing the agent's enthusiasm as nonsense.

"I'm downstairs. Let's meet in person," Cecilia said.

Thrilled, the agent gave her the unit number.

Cecilia glanced at Blackie in the car, grabbed two of his toys, a bowl of water, and cracked the window slightly. "Blackie, I need to check out the apartment. I'll be right back. Stay here, okay?"

He tilted his head, then picked up a rabbit-shaped toy and lay down on the back seat, chewing quietly.

Cecilia sighed; their bond still needed work.

He showed no sign of separation anxiety. She'd secretly hoped for a clingier companion.

Upstairs, the agent waited, wasting no time in showing her around. "Look at this view. Panoramic city skyline, perfect!"

He gestured to the floor-to-ceiling windows in the living room.

Cecilia looked out. Directly below was Nova Tower.

As the tallest building in the area, the penthouse offered unobstructed visibility of the surrounding neighborhood—a crucial advantage for monitoring her surroundings.

"I want a five-year lease. I'll handle renovations myself, pay quarterly, and put down two months' rent as a deposit. Can we sign the contract this afternoon?" she asked.

"Five years?" The agent looked stunned.

These apartments were pricey, and long-term leases were rare.

"Yes, and I'll renovate. If that's acceptable, we can sign today," Cecilia said.

The agent nodded eagerly, calling the landlord immediately.

Considering it was the peak tourist season in town, signing a five-year lease at this price was a real steal.

The landlord demanded that Celia not damage load-bearing walls or the main structure, and that she restore the unit to its original condition at the end of the lease.

Cecilia agreed without hesitation, signing a high-liability compensation clause and paying the first three months' rent on the spot.

In three months, the apocalypse would arrive. The landlord would never come to collect rent again.

With her renovations, this penthouse would keep her and Blackie safe through the first six months of the apocalypse's natural disasters. She was certain of it.

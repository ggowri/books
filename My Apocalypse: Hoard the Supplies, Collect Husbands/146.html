Chapter 146 The Deal

Cecilia smiled. "What's the problem? That grain can actually save lives. The other stuff might be harder to find, but we're not asking for that much."

Logan's face turned dark.

The list had over a thousand pounds of meat on it. Not that much?

Then something clicked. "What about mutant pig meat?"

Bingo. Cecilia had seen this coming.

Hell, she'd been banking on those mutant pigs the moment she decided to squeeze Logan for interest.

In her past life, meat had been scarce at first.

But later? The military suddenly started handing out canned pork.

That pork was better than regular meat—tender, juicy, loaded with fat.

Rumor was, there was a pig farm on the outskirts. After the acid rain, the pigs mutated.

The big ones grew elephant-sized. The small ones were the size of ponies.

And here's the kicker—they didn't eat pig feed anymore.

After mutating, they only ate grass.

Pigs were omnivores. Sure, they ate grass, but they also needed corn, rice bran, that kind of thing. Grass was just a side dish.

But now? These pigs ate nothing but grass and got fat as hell.

Literally eating grass and turning it into meat.

Probably because after the apocalypse hit, they couldn't find much food nearby. But there was tons of livestock feed floating in the water from the farm. So they adapted.

In her past life, the military discovered them right when the Ice Stage hit and started breeding them immediately. Later they'd scaled up to mass production.

This life? Not there yet.

"Mutant pig meat—is it even safe?" Cecilia put on her best worried face.

She knew damn well it was safe. Hell, it was delicious.

"Don't worry. The military's run tons of tests. The meat's fine. We're already looking into mass production—just need the right equipment."

But Cecilia kept up the act. "Yeah, but it's mutated. What if it's fine now but causes problems later? We're handing over perfectly good, un-mutated, well-stored grain here!"

Logan stared at her, trying again to convince her.

Finally, Cecilia sighed like she was doing him the world's biggest favor and tapped the list.

"You said these pigs are over a thousand pounds each now. Huge. And you've got a whole herd. We're taking on risk here, so every meat item on this list gets doubled. Plus, add five hundred pounds of pork to Hailey's list too."

Logan's jaw clenched so hard his teeth hurt.

How is she so damn difficult?

Before the apocalypse, he'd thought Cecilia was a sweet girl.

And when he'd shown up with guns earlier? He hadn't actually wanted conflict with her.

He just wanted to scare the kid a little.

She was barely in her twenties, after all.

But this back-and-forth haggling? It was killing him.

"Captain, we can have more deals down the line. After all, I know where that equipment you need is."

Logan thought about the pork supply back at base. He gritted his teeth and agreed.

Then Cecilia remembered her farm and perked up.

"Oh, almost forgot—I want two live piglets. Newborns. One male, one female."

Logan shook his head immediately. "Live pigs? Can't do it."

"I'll give up two hundred pounds of pork for them." Cecilia grinned. "From what I know about mutant animals, they breed fast, right? And piglets are what, a few dozen pounds? You're still coming out ahead." Cecilia grinned.

Logan knew mutated creatures bred fast.

Hearing Cecilia offer two hundred pounds of pork, he agreed.

What he didn't know? Cecilia hadn't "almost forgotten"—she'd saved the live pig for last on purpose.

A live pig, even a small one, would be heavily monitored now. A renewable resource. Not something Logan could just hand over on his own.

She needed him to feel like he was getting the better deal—so he'd actually bust his ass to get her that pig.

"Of course, if you actually get that machine tool, the payment for that's separate. Gotta keep things square, even between friends. I know it's for the government, but... well, you know. It's the apocalypse."

Cecilia laid it out bluntly, not giving Logan an inch.

Logan didn't seem bothered. "Fair enough. We won't screw you over."

Cecilia smiled, eyes crinkling.

Then her mind clicked to something else.

"Actually, besides the machine tool and the grain, there's another deal I want to pitch to the government. Interested?"

Chapter 185 Jet Guards the Home


After striking Colonel Barnes, the creature vanished at once.

One of the soldiers rushed over to check his injury and found that the entire skin of his forehead had been torn open. Flesh hung loose as blood poured down without stopping, soaking the front of his uniform crimson.

"Forget about me. Get inside and find the Space. You will find it!" Colonel Barnes shouted, pressing a handkerchief to his forehead, his face twisted with fury.

At once, one of the soldiers sprang up and made for Cecilia's flat.

But the moment he was about to step over the threshold, a bird's cry rang out from somewhere unseen.

A dark blur flashed past.

"Ah!"

The soldier screamed.

This time, the whole of his forehead had been ripped up as well. Blood and mangled flesh covered his face. Even one eyelid had been slashed open, the skin peeling back as his eyeball rolled grotesquely in its socket.

Then the thing disappeared again.

For a moment, nobody dared move.

Cecilia's threshold seemed almost enchanted.

Anyone who tried to cross it was attacked at once.

Among them, the seasoned veteran stood with a pistol in hand, scanning the surroundings sharply, as though searching for something.

"Get in there! Move! Find that Space for me!"

Colonel Barnes, swaying with pain, shouted like a madman from where he stood.

The few uninjured soldiers glanced at one another, fear plain on every face.

But military orders were absolute. None of them dared disobey.

"Protect your heads and get inside!"

Colonel Barnes barked the order again.

The soldiers immediately began wrapping whatever they could around their heads. Some even covered their entire faces, leaving only a narrow slit for their eyes.

Three soldiers exchanged a look and prepared to charge.

This time they had agreed on a plan. They would rush in together, fast, and no matter what was inside, they would fire wildly first and kill the thing before worrying about anything else.

But the veteran said nothing. He remained at the back, staring fixedly at the area above the doorframe.

When Colonel Barnes glanced at him, the veteran leaned in and murmured quietly, "On the shoe rack beside the door."

Colonel Barnes nodded. He understood at once and quietly drew his gun.

"Get in there and search!"

At his final roar, the trembling soldiers sucked in a breath, raised their guns, and stepped forward.

Sure enough, the dark shadow appeared again.

It was even faster than before, darting straight for the eyes of the soldier in front.

Bang.

Colonel Barnes fired.

The bullet struck the black shadow cleanly.

A shrill, miserable cry rang out, and the creature dropped to the floor.

"He hit it! The colonel got it!" the veteran shouted from the back.

The soldier on the threshold had gone pale as paper. He stood frozen, staring at the thing on the ground.

It was a mutated parrot, even larger than a hawk, with grey feathers all over. It looked to have once been an African grey.

It was Jet.

The bird Cecilia had left at home to guard the flat.

One of Jet's wings was injured now. Blood dripped from it, pooling red on the floor.

Even so, it struggled back to its feet, fixing the intruders with sharp, hawk like eyes.

Jet knew it was weak.

It knew it was timid.

Just now, when people had tried to break in, it had only dared perch on the shoe rack and launch ambushes one by one. It had not dared confront them head on.

It had often thought that perhaps this was why the human never took it out with them, always taking Blackie instead.

The one time they had taken it along, it had been overjoyed.

It had obeyed every order and not uttered a single word outside.

But when faced with the mutated cats, it had been terrified out of its wits.

It could help scout for the human and for Blackie, yes, but it had never dared to fight those cats directly.

More often than not, it had hidden behind Blackie.

And after that, the human had never taken it outside again.

Blackie had never mocked it for being cowardly. He would even pat its back gently with a paw and tell it that it was still little.

But Jet knew the truth.

Blackie was only one year old.

And Jet... Jet was already three.

This time, the human had told it to guard the home.

So it would guard it.

No matter what.

Jet dragged its injured wing and slowly rose from the floor.

It could no longer fly, but as long as it stood here, it would not let these two legged creatures cross the threshold.

This was the trust the human and Blackie had placed in it.

It puffed out its chest and shouted at the intruders at the top of its voice, "ON!! Go to hell!"

Jet was certain that, at this very moment, that was exactly what the human would want to yell too.

Colonel Barnes was already striding over from the back, raising his pistol in fury.

"You little brute. You dare swear at me?"

At that critical instant, he heard chaos erupt behind him.

There were cries of alarm from the rear, followed by bursts of gunfire.

He spun round at once.

What he saw was an enormous black head charging straight at him, two icy blue eyes blazing in the gloom.

It was a dog's head, black as night, so dark in the dim corridor that its features were almost impossible to make out.

But the furious light burning in those pale blue eyes sent a violent shudder through his whole body.

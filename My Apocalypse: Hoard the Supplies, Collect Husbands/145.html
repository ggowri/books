Chapter 145 All Meat

"Well, that explains where you got the firepower." Logan sighed.

"Logan, the person you took in—that was Mabel, right?" Cecilia spoke up.

She laid out everything Mabel had done in this building, the whole chain of events, then looked at him.

"Who's more likely to be your enemy—someone like that, or us?"

After some back-and-forth, they cleared the air.

Logan looked ashamed.

"I was fooled. I'll deal with those two."

Cecilia just nodded.

Actually, she could handle Mabel and Phoebe herself.

Especially Phoebe... who lived so close.

"Captain, there's no need for us to be enemies. We might be able to help each other. After all, I've got a business deal I'd like to make with the military." Arthur spoke up suddenly.

Logan's head snapped up. "What kind of deal?"

Arthur glanced at Cecilia.

She caught on immediately. A shrewd smile spread across her face.

"Do you need food?"

Logan's eyes lit up instantly. "I've got over ten thousand people in the shelters under my command. Of course we need food."

"How much do you have? What kind?"

"Rice, flour, noodles, compressed biscuits, wheat berries—everything. Problem is, those wheat berries are too coarse to grind. We've been looking for a working food processing plant."

Cecilia's eyes gleamed even brighter.

"What if I told you we could provide corn and sorghum—way more filling. And we can get you food processing equipment. What can you offer us?"

Logan shot up from his seat.

He remembered that guy Dyson who'd come by earlier, claiming Cecilia and her crew had cleared out an entire warehouse.

He'd thought about trading with them.

But he never imagined Cecilia had food processing equipment!

They'd been searching for that stuff forever.

And Command had already ordered them to speed up the hunt for food processing machinery.

"What do you want? Name it. If we've got it, we'll trade."

Cecilia smiled. "Give us a sec to talk it over."

She shot Hailey and Arthur a look.

They followed her out.

Selling to the military was way easier than dealing with anyone else.

First, the grain was sitting in that abandoned building—could be discovered any day.

Second, they'd never eat it all, and they needed to trade it for other supplies. No regular person could handle this much volume. Only the military had that kind of capacity.

Third, Cecilia had dirt on the military—perfect leverage for better terms.

Even then, the military was still getting a steal. What they paid would benefit production and disaster relief long-term.

Cecilia played it greedy on purpose, asking for the moon. She wanted the military to think she wasn't someone they could push around.

She wanted them to believe she'd just gotten lucky, that this was a one-time deal, so they wouldn't come sniffing around for more.

Especially after seeing how Logan handled things, Cecilia knew she had to be extra careful dealing with the military.

Trust no one. Guard yourself. Better safe than sorry.

After some discussion, they each decided to list out what they needed.

The grain they'd hauled from the warehouse and buried underground worked out to about 5,000 pounds of corn and 2,000 pounds of sorghum per person.

"Don't forget to charge interest. Can't let Logan get off easy—not after he pointed a gun at us." Cecilia shot them a meaningful look.

So when they drew up their lists, Hailey was the most reasonable.

She traded at a ratio of three pounds of corn and sorghum for one pound of rice or flour. Then added some hydroponic equipment, coal, and random daily necessities—including sanitary pads.

Quick note: the grain they'd collected from the warehouse was all unprocessed. The corn still had the cobs factored into the weight.

But everyone knew—coarse grains like that kept you full longer, really stuck to your ribs.

So whether the three-to-one ratio favored Logan or Cecilia was debatable.

The real interest? That was all the extra stuff tacked on.

Arthur didn't hold back.

Same ratio—three pounds of corn and sorghum for one pound of coarse or refined grain. Then he added several hundred pounds of meat.

Meat was way more valuable than grain in the apocalypse.

Some people hadn't tasted meat in months—not since they'd finished whatever was in their pantries before the apocalypse.

Cecilia kept it simple: all meat.

She had Blackie to feed, after all.

"Alright, that'll do."

When Cecilia slid the list across to Logan, his face turned pale.

"This... there's no way I can agree to this."

Chapter 119 I Killed Her

From the traces inside—including the drag marks on the windowsill—she couldn't help but suspect... her parents were already dead. Someone had thrown their bodies out the window.

So hearing Mabel curse her parents now? She couldn't take it.

"I'll go."

As Cecilia moved to chase after Mabel, Hailey stopped her, face set with determination.

Cecilia nodded.

Hailey had been standing in the back, protected, for so long. It was time she took this step.

Mabel bolted down the stairs in a panic. Hailey went after her.

Cecilia turned and went back inside.

At this moment, Mabel regretted everything.

She and her husband had been so in love. After the apocalypse hit, they'd supported each other. Life got hard, but they still had hope.

They'd been smart—sealed off their floor early, stockpiled heating supplies.

Plus they'd collected plenty of food and water. They had enough to survive.

But who could've seen it coming?

That day, those people banded together and smashed through the security door they'd installed. Then they just waltzed right in like they owned the place!

And leading them? That clean-cut young guy.

She remembered him—Evan Blake. Always so polite, so scrawny. Just a kid, really. He used to call her "sis."

Now he looked at her like she was his worst enemy.

He'd even hurt her husband Charles!

"You evil bitch! You and your husband both deserve to die!" Evan snarled, throwing Charles to the ground. Then he and the others ignored her protests and hauled away all their firewood and furniture. Didn't leave them a damn thing.

That night, they burned their last piece of firewood.

Then Charles spiked a fever.

Mabel used their precious gas to boil water. She nursed him. Fed him fever meds. Stayed by his side.

Nothing worked. The fever wouldn't break.

And he kept muttering, "Cold... so cold..."

Mabel was desperate. And furious.

She begged Evan. Begged everyone in the building.

No one cared. Some spat at her. Slammed doors in her face.

Out of options, she went to the 66th floor.

She knew they had firewood. Food. Medicine.

She'd do anything. Anything. Be their slave if that's what it took—just save Charles.

But the second that door opened and she saw those cold, dead eyes staring back at her?

She knew. They wouldn't help.

She begged anyway. Again and again.

They refused.

And something inside her snapped.

She screamed at them. Cursed them.

She hated them.

Why did they get to live so well while everyone else froze and starved?

She'd smelled meat cooking up there. Multiple times.

Just standing near their stairwell, she could feel warmth.

They definitely had heat—tons of it!

She could even see light coming from inside. They still had electricity!

So she cursed them with every vile word she knew.

Now?

Now she regretted it.

Hailey was behind her.

That once-polite, cold but kind girl—the one with that weird sense of justice—was now a goddamn executioner, weapon in hand, hunting her down.

The second Mabel slowed, Hailey would strike.

So Mabel ran. Ran like hell.

******

Hailey's heart was hammering.

When Mabel tripped and tumbled down the stairs, Hailey froze. Just for a second.

Then she tightened her grip on the golf club, screamed to pump herself up, and charged after her.

She knew the truth: if Mabel survived today, she'd come back for revenge.

Hailey had seen it in her eyes. That hate.

That kind of hate? In the apocalypse, it turned into killing intent.

She charged forward, swinging the golf club hard at Mabel.

Mabel screamed, trying to get up and fight back.

But Mabel had barely eaten or stayed warm these past days. She'd been up all night caring for Charles, totally drained.

Now on the ground, at a disadvantage, she couldn't get up fast enough.

Hailey hit her again. And again.

Mabel's head rang. Vision blurred.

But Hailey wasn't done.

She pulled out a fruit knife and stabbed.

"AHHHHH!"

Everything came pouring out—days of terror, the agony of her ruined face, Mabel's endless curses about her parents, all those taunts.

She stabbed. Over and over.

Until she had nothing left.

Gasping for air, Hailey stared down at Mabel's body.

With trembling fingers, she checked for breath.

Nothing.

Relief flooded through her.

She looked at her hands. Covered in blood.

I killed her.

A few months ago, she'd been a yoga instructor. Daily breathing exercises. Peaceful life.

Chapter 112 Close Call

That's when she realized her heart was hammering in her chest, her breathing ragged.

Close call. Way too close.

Cecilia had retreated back to this floor because something suddenly clicked—everything here was still neat and tidy, untouched by those big cats.

Which meant there had to be something on this floor that scared them off. Something that kept them out.

She needed to find it.

Yeah, she still hadn't given up on scavenging supplies from downstairs.

First reason? After collecting all those cans on this floor, Cecilia knew this building had to be loaded with meat!

Meat!

Sure, she wasn't hurting for it now, but Blackie could eat. The dog's appetite was already huge and only getting bigger.

Plus, this entire floor was stocked with canned fish—the kind that lasted forever.

Later on, these would be pure gold. The most valuable, most sought-after currency for trading supplies.

Second reason? She had plenty of catnip left. And not just that—cat food, toys, treats!

All scavenged from that pet sanctuary before.

Even if she couldn't find what scared those big cats, she could use this stuff to lure them away again.

Worst case? She had the Space to duck into.

With that settled, Cecilia started moving around the room.

What could possibly keep those big cats from coming in here?

Now that the door was shut and the cats were gone, Blackie had calmed down. This floor felt safe to him.

Which meant... whatever scared those cats wasn't some dangerous creature.

Right then, Cecilia realized Blackie had stopped moving.

Not just Blackie—even Jet, who'd been circling overhead, hadn't followed.

Cecilia turned back. Three feet away, Blackie's face was twisted in disgust. Jet looked equally disturbed.

She listened carefully. Watched. Nothing.

Then she smelled it.

Not the usual rotting fish stench. Something sharper. More pungent.

The place was relatively clean, but it was too dark—all those machines creating shadows everywhere. Hard to see into every corner.

She pulled a mask from the Space and added a high-powered outdoor flashlight to her headlamp.

The beam cut through the darkness, illuminating more area.

Finally, she saw it—a massive carcass wedged under one of the machines.

Another cat.

A huge, fat white cat.

Nearly six feet long. Long-haired. Body stiff, lying beneath the machinery, legs stretched out, eyes squeezed shut, face contorted—like it had died in agony.

Playing it safe, Cecilia grabbed a wooden stick from the Space and poked the white cat from a distance.

No reaction. Its flesh was frozen solid.

Now she understood why the other cats wouldn't come up here.

Animals feared the dead of their own kind.

And this white cat was bigger than all the others.

Meaning it was stronger. More dominant.

Maybe even their leader—had given orders before coming up here.

But then it came up... and never came back down.

She glanced back at Blackie and Jet. Both were hanging back about three feet, clearly hating the smell coming off the white cat's carcass.

Cecilia had no choice. She crouched down and dragged the body out.

Once it was in the light, she saw it—the white cat's entire body had grown spike-like protrusions. Parts of its skin looked burned.

She studied it for a while, then reached her conclusion: it had died mid-evolution. Couldn't handle the transformation.

No wonder the other cats feared this white cat. No wonder Blackie and Jet hated it. Higher evolution level meant it gave off some kind of intimidating scent.

Cecilia pulled a machete from the Space and hacked off the white cat's tail.

She grabbed it and took a couple steps toward Blackie and Jet.

Jet immediately flapped away, circling overhead. His beady eyes darted around like he had something to say but was holding back—obeying Cecilia's "no talking" order.

Blackie wanted to back up too but held his ground for now.

Cecilia got it—the tail worked just as well as the white cat.

She stashed what was left of the white cat in the Space. Maybe she could bury it in Plot One later.

Gripping the tail, Cecilia carefully opened the stairwell door and started down.

A few flights later, she spotted a big "9" painted on the wall. So this building had nine floors.

When she reached the eighth-floor entrance, three pairs of glowing green eyes locked onto her from inside.

Chapter 106 The Real Boss

Cecilia and Arthur exchanged glances. Both smirked.

Who would've thought that even in the apocalypse, they'd run into such a character?

When Dyson's crew cut the bridge between Towers A and B earlier, Cecilia and Arthur could've stopped them. They knew exactly what those guys were up to.

But they didn't move.

First, they had plenty of supplies for now.

Second, Dyson's group wasn't as simple as they looked. All that smooth talk and dodging? Classic misdirection. These guys were hiding their real strength.

Besides Dyson up front, Cecilia felt another pair of eyes watching from that building.

Dyson probably wasn't the real boss.

And whoever came up with the plan to cut the bridge between Towers A and B—and actually pulled it off—wasn't some random nobody.

Just bouncing around forty stories up with nothing but a rope for safety? That took guts.

"Hey, you guys got skills! Let's stay outta each other's way, yeah?" Dyson shouted across.

Cecilia glanced at the five or six people on his side and waved back. Deal.

"And hey, gorgeous—if you're interested in any of us, we're very available!"

They ignored the B Tower crew and hauled the last of their stuff inside.

On the way up, Cecilia turned to Hailey and Arthur. "Overtime workers? I'm not buying it."

Arthur nodded slightly. "Probably showed up after the acid rain hit."

"How'd you figure?"

"During the fight, I ripped a few of their shirts. Saw heavy chemical burns all over their skin. You only get that from long exposure to acid rain."

Cecilia glanced at Arthur's neck—the sliver of skin visible through his collar. Same kind of scars. She nodded.

"Look back!"

Hailey's voice cut through suddenly.

They were on the 62nd floor now, right by the stairwell window.

Through the glass, Cecilia spotted someone across in Tower B—a woman, staring toward Tower A.

Too far to make out details.

But when their eyes met, Cecilia felt it. Pressure.

That woman was dangerous.

Cecilia was almost certain—she was the real boss of Tower B. Not Dyson.

Hell, "Dyson" probably wasn't even his real name.

Still, for now, they weren't stepping on each other's toes. No need to dig deeper just yet.

They hurried back to the 66th floor and divided up the haul from the hallway.

Cecilia took charcoal. Skipped the wood.

Arthur grabbed some charcoal and a few pieces of furniture.

They'd already swept the floor clean earlier and grabbed plenty of lumber. The rest went to Hailey.

Hailey stared at them, clearly grateful. After a long pause, she finally managed. "Wanna... come over for dinner?"

But they were exhausted. They said maybe another time and headed home.

Back inside, Blackie shuffled over, ears drooping, looking miserable.

Cecilia frowned. What, mad about guard duty? Since when?

She turned to Jet, who'd flown over and was nuzzling her face, acting cute.

"Jet, what's up with Blackie? Why the long face?"

Jet tilted his head, beady eyes flicking toward Blackie. "Blackie hurts."

Cecilia shot to her feet and rushed over, wrapping her arms around Blackie's neck.

"Where? Where does it hurt?"

Her mind raced. Did someone try to mess with the 66th floor? Did they hurt him?

"Been hurting for days. Blackie said it was nothing. Told me not to tell you."

Jet said it casually, but Cecilia's heart dropped.

Days? And she had no idea?

Cecilia knew animals like cats and dogs could endure pain far better than humans. Sometimes dozens of times more.

When she was little, she'd had a fat tabby. Round. Soft. Adorable.

It barely moved. You could pet it however you liked—it wouldn't react.

Everyone said it was lazy. Then it started eating less and less. Turned out it was sick.

At the vet, they found massive stones in its stomach.

Now, kneeling beside Blackie, Cecilia checked him inch by inch.

No wounds. No blood. No visible illness.

Still, unease crept up her spine.

She coaxed Blackie into drinking a whole bowl of Magic Spring water.

That night, she was jolted awake by faint whimpers.

Jet was already there—flying frantic circles over Blackie, so panicked he could barely form words.

"Blackie hurts."

"Blackie's dying."

Cecilia had Jet ask Blackie where it hurt.

But even though both had evolved, their minds weren't quite... human. Communication was messy.

After going back and forth, she finally got an answer.

Blackie hurt everywhere. And it felt like fire burning from the inside out.

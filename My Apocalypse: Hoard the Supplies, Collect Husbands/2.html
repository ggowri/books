Chapter 2 Space Upgrade

Near the market stood a large agricultural wholesale market, the biggest in the district.

Cecilia went straight for it, grabbing nearly everything in sight: winter jackets, tracksuits, loungewear...

Running her fingers over the sturdy, comfortable fabrics, she picked out dozens of each style, asking the vendors to deliver them to her home.

To explain the bulk purchase, she claimed to be buying for resale. With an average build, she opted for medium sizes across the board.

Dried goods came next—dried mushrooms, kelp, and sun-dried beans, all sold in bulk at wholesale prices.

Cecilia also ordered cases of seasonings—Worcestershire sauce, black pepper sauce, vinegar, sesame paste, ketchup—and paid on the spot.

The biggest surprise came when she spotted a vendor selling seasonal wild greens, foraged from the mountains and hard to come by. Perfect for raviolis or stuffed bread, these greens were usually only sold by local farmers near the outskirts.

Without hesitation, she bought every last bunch the farmer had.

Fresh seasonal vegetables joined the haul too, purchased after a round of haggling, with delivery scheduled to her building at a convenient time.

Once done at the agricultural market, a glance across the street revealed some bookstores. Figuring she was already there, Cecilia headed over.

With power and water cuts looming in the apocalypse, she raided several bookstores, grabbing anything that might prove useful: comics, novels, cookbooks, tool manuals, maps, and even niche titles—repair guides, animal husbandry handbooks, gardening references.

If it seemed remotely valuable, it went into her pile.

*****

By the time Cecilia loaded everything into her car, the vehicle was packed to the brim, not an inch of space left for one more book.

Checking the time, she saw nearly five hours had passed, so she drove straight back to the jewelry shop.

Mark had finished the custom piece, the chain secured exactly as she'd requested.

After settling the bill, Cecilia returned to her car and quietly stowed the books and weapons into the Space. Eager to continue scavenging, she set off again.

Unexpectedly, as she passed a street stall, the pendant at her chest trembled slightly.

Years of apocalyptic vigilance kicked in instantly.

Cecilia froze, stepping back three feet, and the pendant trembled again.

Before her was a roadside stall peddling "antiques"—a quick scan revealed they were all fakes, meant to scam unsuspecting buyers.

Curious, she squatted down to look closer.

"Looking for something, young lady?" The stall keeper, who seemed to have been waiting ages for a customer, perked up at her arrival.

Her eyes swept over the merchandise. They were mostly aged-looking ornaments and agate bracelets.

Then, she spotted a raw stone in the corner, its properties unclear.

When she reached out to touch it, the pendant at her chest pulsed rapidly.

Cecilia knew she had found what she was looking for.

"How much for this?" she asked.

The vendor's eyes darted. Clearly, he saw her youth as an opportunity to overcharge.

Before he could speak, she picked up the stone and said, "It's a gold ore raw stone, but the gold content looks low from the outside. The shape's ordinary, too small to make an impressive ornament—only slightly better than a regular decorative rock.

"A thousand dollars, for my collection. Deal?"

Two minutes later, she tucked the stone into her pocket and left.

Finding a secluded spot, she stowed it in the Space.

Entering the Space with her mind, Cecilia watched as the stone cracked open, releasing several blue glimmers that vanished in the blink of an eye.

Suddenly, the Space erupted in change—thick white mist rolled in, and violent winds howled.

Panic fluttered in her chest; Cecilia worried the blue particles might be harmful.

But when the mist cleared and the winds died down, a new patch of black soil, roughly 1,076 square feet, appeared.

Just looking at it, she could feel its richness.

The Space leveled up?

A grin spread across her face as she clenched her fists. Cecilia wandered the market for another two rounds, but no other item made the pendant tremble.

Whatever triggered the upgrade remained a mystery; for now, she decided to set it aside.

Securing supplies came first. She could study the Space later once she was better prepared.

Passing a seed wholesale shop on her way out, Cecilia went in and bought a variety of seeds: vegetables, flowers, fruits... anything that might grow in the new soil.

By nightfall, exhaustion weighed heavily on her legs, cramping slightly from a full day of walking.

This body, she thought, was far less resilient than the one she'd had in her last life.

The skills and stamina she'd built over ten years of apocalyptic survival felt too much for it now.

But with months left until the end, she was confident training would get her back to peak condition.

No time to slack off, though. Cecilia grabbed a notebook and jotted down her to-do list, circling "Improve physical fitness" with a thick marker.

She tested the Space again and made a nice discovery—it had a preservation function. It wasn't just refrigeration, but perfect temperature retention: a cup of hot water at 185 degrees Fahrenheit stayed exactly that temperature when she took it out. It was a huge help.

Meanwhile, a crucial thought hit Cecilia.

She needed a dog. Not just any dog, but something special.

The early days of the apocalypse would bring mild acid rain, enough to keep people indoors.

Meteorologists would blame it on global pollution and unexplained atmospheric changes, downplaying the threat due to its weak acidity.

But the rain would intensify, flooding two-story buildings in some areas.

Only then would panic set in, as people could no longer shop or live normally.

That was just the start.

After the acid rain came blizzards, temperatures plummeting to minus 140 degrees Fahrenheit. With power and water long gone, survival would be brutal. The snow would fall for three months straight, burying ten-story buildings.

It was then that animal mutations would begin—creatures growing larger, stronger, and more intelligent, becoming invaluable survival tools for many.

Cecilia's goal now was to buy a rejected military dog.

From her memories, the local army would be auctioning off a batch of unqualified military dogs in the next few days.

During military and police K9 training, some candidates were eliminated for hyperactivity, timidity, or poor agility.

Though unfit for service, these dogs were still far stronger than most pets.

In her last life, Cecilia had hesitated and never followed through.

This time, she was determined to get a strong, obedient one.

Pulling out her phone, she searched for information. Sure enough, she found notices about the auction.

Excitement surged as Cecilia contacted the poster, who replied promptly, arranging for her to visit the base early the next morning.

Too excited to sleep, she decided to study her upgraded Space.

Grabbing a packet of flower seeds, Cecilia used her mind to scatter them across the black soil before finally resting.

Normal seeds took days to germinate; she was eager to see if the soil accelerated growth or had other properties.

She'd always thought this dirt was unusual.

*****

The next morning, Cecilia arrived at the military base.

A tall, straight-backed officer with a friendly smile approached her.

"Ms. Summers, good morning." He greeted.

Using her past-life memories, she'd tracked down the auction notice and contacted the base's point person the previous day.

To her surprise, the officer had mentioned two retired military dogs unsuitable for auction, asking if she'd be interested.

Cecilia agreed immediately. She remembered some rejected dogs selling for over a million dollars at the auction, far beyond her budget.

The officer, First Lieutenant Logan Jackman, was in charge of K9 training and the one who'd messaged her online.

As they walked, he asked about her knowledge of military dogs and dog ownership.

Her parents had kept a dog when Cecilia was young, and she'd played with military dogs at the base before, so she answered smoothly.

At the K9 training ground, the sound of barking filled the air as she spotted twelve dogs being photographed for the auction listing.

Eight German Shepherds, three Labradors, and one Doberman Pinscher.

Her attention was instantly drawn to the Doberman.

With a sleek black coat and muscular build, it sat at chest height on her—imposing even without mutation. Just its presence would deter any ill-intentioned strangers.

And as luck would have it, this Doberman was one of the two retired dogs not up for auction.

"Why was this Doberman retired?" she asked Logan, struggling to hide her excitement.

Logan led her two steps closer.

Nine feet away, the Doberman turned its head, large skull slightly lowered. Its eyes, slanting upward, glinted with menace.

A single glare from it was enough to inspire fear.

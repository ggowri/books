Chapter 88 Those Who Remained

Arthur accepted with clear interest, and the two squared off once more.

The fight was fierce from the start, a whirlwind of motion where neither could gain a clear advantage.

Cecilia was in deadly earnest this time, and Arthur gave no quarter.

After several intense exchanges, Cecilia feinted. Using the wall for leverage, she launched herself upward, landing squarely on Arthur's shoulders.

Arthur's brow furrowed as he tried to dislodge her, but her grip was firm.

With no other option, he drove her forcefully into the nearest wall.

Cecilia, however, had anticipated this. As he lunged, she grabbed the metal frame of the security door, using it to swing herself down and away.

She spun in mid-air, aiming a powerful kick at his side.

Her aim was off. The kick landed much lower than intended, striking Arthur squarely in the groin.

He twisted sharply, avoiding a direct hit, but the glancing blow was still enough.

Arthur's face instantly paled, his features tightening in pain.

Every man understood the vulnerability of that particular area.

The sparring session ended there.

Arthur left without a word, merely waving a hand in a dismissive gesture as he walked away, his gait slightly stiff.

Cecilia could tell he was in pain.

She rubbed her own forehead in exasperation. What a wretched coincidence. She truly hadn't meant to hit him there.

Early the next morning, the building erupted in cheers again, an echo coming from the neighboring towers.

Government officials had arrived with news: shelters were ready. Those wishing to evacuate should pack; transportation would arrive the following day.

Cecilia stood by the floor-to-ceiling window, stirring her coffee as she watched the government boats shrink into the distance.

A cold, calculating light flickered in her eyes.

Alexander and Rebecca were absolutely not to reach those shelters.

Once the Ice Stage arrived, she still wanted to watch them suffer.

That night, while Rebecca and Alexander were lost in each other, someone struck them from behind, rendering them unconscious.

They had been in high spirits, dreaming of the shelters, of reconnecting with the Barnes family and securing a comfortable future.

Rebecca, her mind wandering lately with thoughts of the Barnes family's power, had been especially attentive to Alexander.

However, they never woke in time for the government boats the next day.

Even worse, the Ice Stage arrived that very night.

The government vessels only evacuated a portion of the residents.

Others who wanted to go were left behind, told that more boats would come in a few days.

Even so, Cecilia's building, which had never been densely populated, lost more than half its occupants.

Only about a dozen people remained.

This included the three on the 66th floor, Rebecca and Alexander, Andy, the wife-beater from the 58th floor and his spouse, a couple, a tall, skinny young man from the 43rd floor, a young couple in their twenties, and Ashley's daughter-in-law.

Essentially, households with elderly or children were prioritized, leaving the already sparse building nearly empty.

The most interesting case was Phoebe.

After Bryan's gang was decimated, Phoebe had used her youthful looks to attach herself to a short, unattractive man in his fifties.

This old man was a hoarder with plenty of food, and as a former locksmith, he knew how to pick locks.

He'd broken into several supposedly secure apartments on the upper floors, amassing a small stockpile.

In the few days with him, Phoebe had even put on a little weight.

When the government arrived, the old man got so excited that he suffered a hypertensive crisis and was taken away for medical care.

Phoebe, registered as his "family member," left with him.

As she departed, she cast a triumphant, sneering look back at the building, especially towards the 66th floor.

She remembered the disdain in Hailey's eyes and the accusations of having no self-respect or dignity.

But what good had Hailey's resistance done? Her face was ruined, and she was staying behind to suffer.

Thinking of the fools on the 66th floor, Phoebe felt a surge of contempt. They thought they were so tough, ignoring the government's offer entirely.

With this sense of prideful superiority, Phoebe "luckily" boarded the government rescue vessel.

She had no idea what awaited her: rooms crammed with dozens of people, filthy and chaotic conditions, and even more violence and conflict than before.

The government shelters had been set up too hastily, just a few rows of villas on a hilltop, utterly inadequate for the numbers.

Some of the people there had experiences Phoebe couldn't imagine; many had blood on their hands.

A slightly plump, pretty young woman like her would only face humiliation there.

The old man with his chronic hypertension would be no protection.

In truth, from the moment she decided to run with Bryan's crowd, there was no turning back for her.

As for Ashley's son and grandson, they never boarded the government boats.

They were dead.

After learning the 58th-floor abuser had killed his mother, Kent took a knife and stormed upstairs.

Even Ashley's grandson tagged along, eager for the confrontation.

Kent's wife tried to stop them but was shoved aside.

Kent confronted the abuser, ready to kill him.

The man initially fell to his knees, begging for mercy.

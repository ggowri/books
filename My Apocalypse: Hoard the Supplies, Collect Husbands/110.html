Chapter 110 Cans Everywhere

The snow had stopped. A huge, brilliant moon hung in the sky, casting white light across everything.

The snow-covered ground turned silver-white under the moonlight, reflecting tiny pinpricks of light everywhere.

Cecilia pulled out her prepared snow goggles and put them on—no way she was getting snow blind out here.

Under cover of the dead of night, she trudged through the snow in her heavy boots, walking dozens of feet until she found a hidden spot.

Then she pulled out the sidecar and Blackie.

Blackie hadn't been out in forever—and this was his first time ever on snow!

He lifted one paw. Carefully set it back down.

Lifted another paw. Set it down again...

Then froze.

His big dog face scrunched up in confusion, eyebrows furrowed, jowls drooping extra long.

Cecilia almost laughed at how dumbfounded he looked.

While he was distracted, she fastened the pre-tied rope harness onto him.

Blackie stared at the ropes, clearly confused about what she was doing.

After securing Blackie, Cecilia gave the ropes a hard tug.

The hemp rope from the Space was definitely way better than regular rope—and had some flex to it!

She was satisfied.

"Blackie, run!" Cecilia shouted.

Blackie hesitated for a split second, staring at the outdoors—different as it looked, but god, he'd missed it. Then he bolted, running like his life depended on it!

Cecilia felt the jolt, like a rocket launch. She shot forward in an instant.

"Blackie, left!"

She tugged the left rope.

"Blackie, right!"

She tugged the right rope.

After a few tries, they fell into rhythm.

And with less than a mile to cover, they got there fast.

Cecilia hopped off the sidecar and untied the ropes from Blackie.

She scanned her surroundings. In front of her stood an eight or nine-story building.

Problem was... the thing was almost completely buried.

And where she needed to go? The storage room at the very bottom.

After searching around, Cecilia finally found a window she could squeeze through.

She kicked in the glass and crawled inside.

What she didn't expect? This building buried under snow actually had "residents."

More than one.

Once inside, Cecilia flicked on her headlamp.

The floor was a factory—a canned food production line, to be exact.

Except the place had been shut down ages ago.

The ingredients had rotted, everything reeked.

Cecilia took two steps forward, then her foot hit something.

Clang clang clang...

Metal rolling across the floor.

She jumped back, alert.

Blackie backed up too, baring his teeth at whatever was ahead.

When nothing happened, Cecilia realized what she'd kicked—just a can.

She crouched down and carefully picked up the can. Intact. Unopened. Sardines.

She swept her headlamp around.

Holy shit—there were cans of sardines everywhere on this floor!

All unopened. Just scattered across the ground.

Not just sardines—salmon, tuna, mackerel... You name it.

Cecilia collected them as she walked.

But when she reached the stairwell, she froze.

Blackie was getting antsy. His eyes locked onto something below, staring hard at the darkness down there.

Cecilia quickly killed her headlamp and strained her eyes at the stairwell. Nothing.

She listened. Silence.

But she trusted Blackie's ears and nose.

Something was definitely down there.

And honestly? This whole place felt wrong.

After the acid rain stopped, the government had done a rescue sweep. People had been scavenging nearby for supplies ever since.

These cans were shelf-stable, meat-based—exactly what survivors would grab. Yet they looked untouched.

Cecilia did a full lap of the floor.

The raw materials on the production lines had rotted naturally. No signs of looting.

The cans on the ground were few—most were still neatly stacked on shelves.

The whole floor was too damn organized for the apocalypse.

A few things looked knocked over, sure, but otherwise? Pristine.

No way this building—or at least this floor—hadn't been found.

The acid rain had flooded at least four floors. This building had eight, maybe more buried under the snow. Cecilia couldn't tell for sure.

But the locals had to know there was a food processing plant here.

So why hadn't anyone come?

What made them turn back?

Suddenly, an idea hit her.

She pulled Jet out of the Space.

Jet circled overhead, clearly uneasy.

Yeah, he looked big and tough now, always roughhousing with Blackie at home. But outside? Total coward.

Still, bringing Jet was a calculated move.

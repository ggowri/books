Chapter 129 One Step Too Late

Later, Jet conned Cecilia out of another round of pet treats before finally settling down. He flapped off to nap somewhere.

Cecilia stepped outside. Arthur and Hailey were already waiting.

She glanced at Arthur.

He wore a windbreaker too, but with a short fur coat layered underneath—made him look kind of puffy.

Behind him, he dragged a rigid inflatable boat.

The boat had been modified—it even had a canopy on top.

One look at that clean welding work told Cecilia he'd done it himself.

She glanced back at her own unmodified boat and raised an eyebrow.

Thorough guy.

But he wasn't done. Arthur ducked back inside and returned with panels, roping them onto Cecilia's boat.

The panels stood upright, massively increasing the cargo space.

Two boats meant they could haul back way more supplies.

Cecilia nodded, impressed.

The two of them plus Blackie headed downstairs.

Before leaving, Cecilia grabbed Hailey. "No matter what happens, no matter who shows up—don't open that door. Don't even answer. Act like all three of us are still here on the sixty-sixth floor."

"I got this," Hailey said.

Downstairs, at a hidden spot, they lashed the two boats together, then looped rope around Blackie and secured it tight.

"Blackie, go!"

Cecilia snapped the rope. Blackie shot forward.

And just like that, they vanished into the blizzard.

The whole way there, Arthur called out every turn.

The trip was about twenty miles. Blackie ran for over an hour straight before they arrived.

When they finally got there, one thing hit Cecilia hard—Arthur's memory was insane.

In a city half-buried under snow, she wasn't sure she could clearly recognize or remember every street.

But Arthur nailed every turn without hesitation. Didn't miss a single intersection.

When they stopped, Blackie stood tall in the darkness, showing zero signs of fatigue.

Cecilia patted his head and pulled out two treats from her pocket.

Arthur climbed down and gently stroked Blackie's head too.

"Your dog. He's really something."

In that moment, Cecilia felt like a proud parent whose kid just got praised. She grinned and patted Blackie's back.

"Damn right he is."

And Blackie's greatness went way beyond what Arthur could see.

Ahead of them loomed a snow-covered hill.

The grain silos stood a few hundred feet away, towering structures now completely buried in snow—like little white towers.

Clearly, there'd been an avalanche here recently. Most of the snow had piled up at the base of the hill. Otherwise, finding the silos would've been way harder.

Without wasting time, they had Blackie keep pulling the rigid inflatable boat while Cecilia and Arthur climbed the slope.

Soon, they reached the silos.

But then Blackie suddenly arched his back, growling a warning toward the silos.

Arthur and Cecilia both stepped back.

"Hey guys. Funny how we keep running into each other, huh?"

A sighing voice came from behind the silo.

Then a man stepped out.

He was bundled up tight, but those ridiculous scrunched eyebrows and that obnoxious tone gave him away instantly.

Cecilia knew right away—it was Dyson.

Dyson from Building B.

Two more people emerged behind Dyson.

Bundled up thick, each hauling massive bags.

What really shocked Cecilia though?

They had a car.

A tricked-out SUV—top of the line—looking slick as hell rolling up.

In this frozen wasteland, most cars wouldn't even start. The ones that could were buried under snow. Finding a working vehicle was damn near impossible.

Cecilia glanced at her setup: a dog pulling sleds, two modified inflatable boats for "transportation."

Suddenly felt pretty pathetic.

Then she shot Arthur a look. My car got totaled because of this idiot!

Sure, Cecilia had vehicles in the Space, but she couldn't exactly pull one out right now.

Arthur caught her glare, but he pretended he didn't, turning his head away.

Cecilia rolled her eyes hard.

But now wasn't the time. She turned to Dyson.

"Yeah, small world. Didn't expect to run into you here."

She watched his crew load bag after bag of grain into their vehicle. Her chest tightened.

One step too late.

Dyson chuckled. "Don't look so sad. There's plenty here. First come, first served, right?"

He waved his guys forward, grabbing even more supplies from the warehouse.

"Babe, we'll be back the next couple days. Let's be friends—call me Dyson next time!

"Oh, and watch yourself!"

After loading up, Dyson and his crew drove off.

"We need to move. Fast." Cecilia's voice went cold.

They'd planned multiple trips too—about two days to clear this warehouse completely.

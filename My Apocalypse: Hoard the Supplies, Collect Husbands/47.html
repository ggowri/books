Chapter 47 Flood

After she finished picking up the eggs, a strong force shoved her forward again, straight in front of the dairy cow.

This time, a bucket appeared in her hands.

So she was supposed to milk the cow? But she had no idea how.

What a cursed system. Couldn't it be a little smarter?

Still, figuring the system level was probably too low, Cecilia cursed under her breath and accepted her fate.

She copied what she had seen on TV, fumbling around for ages. She nearly scared the poor cow half to death before finally squeezing out half a bucket of milk.

Once all the "byproducts" were collected, Cecilia left the farm with a dark look on her face, eggs in one hand and milk in the other. She decided to turn the hardearned fresh ingredients into a proper meal.

Inside the villa, she took out a gas canister, a stove, and some cookware from storage and made a simple egg pancake.

She whisked the eggs with a bit of flour, brushed oil across the pan, and fried it carefully until both sides turned golden. A pinch of salt finished it off.

Since Blackie would be eating too, she made a smaller pancake without any salt.

She also grabbed a small saucepan and warmed the milk.

Before long, the rich smell of eggs and milk filled the Space, bringing a muchneeded sense of everyday life back with it.

With the food ready, Cecilia carried everything into the small room she had arranged exactly like her old bedroom. Looking at photos of her parents, she smiled, petted Blackie, and ate her meal.

The only thing ruining the peace was the gray parrot. "Where is this place? Old dog, where did you go? Stinks!"

The bird actually called the warm milk smelly.

Cecilia decided to ignore it for now and deal with it later.

Unfortunately, the parrot kept circling above her head, even trying to grab her hair.

After one bite of her egg pancake, Cecilia finally snapped. She yanked the parrot down and warned, "Behave, or I'll throw you back into the farm."

Blackie stood up beside her, giving the gray parrot a warning glare.

For once, the parrot's intelligence showed. It shrank its neck down and stayed quiet.

After filling her stomach, Cecilia checked the time. It was already noon.

She busied herself in the Space for a while, planting a small amount in both farmland plots.

She kept it minimal, worried that the Space's energy might not be enough. She needed to continue upgrading it, learning the ropes before attempting largescale planting or raising animals.

She also dug a tiny fish pond, filled it with some Magic Spring water, and added a few of the ornamental fish she had collected earlier, curious to see what would happen.

By the time she finished, it was six in the evening, and darkness was starting to fall.

Finally feeling fatigue creeping in, Cecilia dragged Blackie—and the gray parrot, sulking after its earlier scolding—onto the small bed in the room and fell asleep.

When she woke up, the clock read two in the morning. It was perfect timing.

She could finally go home.

She listened outside the Space—everything was quiet—then carried Blackie and stepped out.

As for the gray parrot? It was too noisy and still untrained, so she tossed it back into the farm for the time being. Training could wait until later.

The whole neighborhood was dark; the world had returned to silence and shadow.

When Cecilia reached the ground floor, she froze. The first floor was completely submerged.

The water had already reached the second floor.

Her stomach sank. At this level, the first floor of her own building must be underwater as well.

Trouble. Big trouble. Something serious was probably happening in the neighborhood.

Cecilia hurriedly swam with Blackie across to the street, then climbed through the back gate of her complex.

Before entering her building, she found a hidden spot and tucked Blackie into the Space, just in case its presence was too conspicuous.

You never knew; someone might still be awake, peeking outside.

The nearby neighbors probably couldn't make out who she was from a distance, but with Blackie being such a huge dog, its presence was hard to miss.

To be safe, she took a slow loop around the other floors, waited a bit, then used her diving gear to approach her unit from the lower floors.

Just as Cecilia expected, the rain had already swallowed the first floor.

Even the second floor looked like it might go under next.

And the acid rain kept coming—harder than before.

She climbed in through the stairwell window on the second floor.

The elevators were definitely out of commission by now, and the generators were probably fried too.

Even if they worked, she wouldn't have dared to use them.

The first floor being underwater had already sent most people a clear message—the situation was serious.

Cecilia ducked into the Space to change clothes and slip on a pair of soft, lightweight shoes. Then she took a deep breath and started moving floor by floor.

The halls were eerily quiet, broken only by the sound of her own breathing.

Yet she still tiptoed, careful not to draw attention.

After all, in the apocalypse, those gentle, moralhearted neighbors could quickly turn into toothbaring predators.

After climbing seven or eight floors in one go, Cecilia felt her stamina held up well—a reward for the training she'd stuck with these past few days.

Her building, however, had over 60 floors; the road ahead was still long.

Taking advantage of the moment, she also kept an eye on the neighbors below.

After the first floor was flooded, she didn't spot anyone in the hallways of the upper floors.

It seemed the flooded households had found a way to evacuate.

But it was unlikely given the pounding acid rain and lack of transport tools.

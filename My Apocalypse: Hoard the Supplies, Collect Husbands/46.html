Chapter 46 Care Ranch

Once she had everything sorted out, Cecilia let out a sigh of relief. She decided to start large-scale planting and make full use of the two farms when the situation allowed.

But the note "overuse will cause dormancy" made her grind her teeth.

The wording was maddeningly vague. There were no concrete details, and she'd have to figure it out on her own.

This Space system was downright sneaky. It was even more cunning than Blackie.

After finishing with the farms, Cecilia switched to the last option: [Care Ranch.]

[Care Ranch]

[Level: 1]

[Function: All animals in the Care Ranch live in harmony. They are automatically fed by the Space's energy and managed entirely on autopilot. Of course, the Space owner can also feed them manually.]

[Limit: If the Space energy is low, it will draw from the Magic Spring, so remember to feed them.]

[Note: After all, these are living creatures. Pay some attention.]

Reading the description made Cecilia so frustrated she wanted to punch the floating screen.

All this talk about "full automation"—what a joke!

But looking at the number of animals she currently had, Cecilia realized she could easily handle them herself. That gave her a little peace of mind.

When she had collected supplies earlier, she had also grabbed enough food and essentials for these little creatures. They wouldn't go hungry anytime soon.

Still, the warning served as a wake-up call. The Space was handy and packed with functions, but it was also tricky.

The real key was to keep upgrading the Space and replenishing its energy. That was the hard truth.

Cecilia checked the ranch and noticed an invisible barrier separating the animals completely.

It prevented any winged ones from flying out.

She was referred to the gray parrot.

Cecilia stood in front of the farm, watching the feisty gray parrot slam against the invisible barrier, trying again and again to break free.

Once, twice, three times...

The parrot flapped its wings furiously, its dark eyes fixed on Cecilia, as if it couldn't understand why it couldn't charge forward and peck at the two-legged creature.

Cecilia returned its gaze, equally puzzled.

She had always heard that gray parrots were the smartest in the world. So why did this one seem so clueless?

"You old dog, where've you been?"

The barrier could stop physical objects, but not sound.

The parrot's words were startling.

Cecilia jumped back, shivering. "Oh wow, who taught this little thing to talk like that?"

What Cecilia didn't know was that this parrot was named Jet, a social media star.

Previously, it used to livestream twice a week, and viewers had taught it all sorts of bizarre words.

Jet had a fiery temperament and was extremely clingy.

Its owner had gone out of town before the world ended, leaving it in the care of the indoor zoo staff. But then, the apocalypse happened.

It had never gone so long without seeing its owner, and now it was trapped in this strange environment, surrounded by other clueless creatures. No wonder it was on edge.

Cecilia stared at the parrot, then at the other animals in the farm, so out of place with it. She figured maybe she could keep this parrot close.

At the very least, it had a deep, gruff voice when it swore—almost manly.

It could be handy for intimidation.

With that in mind, Cecilia released the parrot.

To her surprise, it showed no fear, hopping straight onto Blackie's head. It fluffed its wings, raised its head, and glared at Cecilia with authority, letting out another booming, "You old dog, where've you been?"

Blackie's face drooped again.

Cecilia went through the farm, placing feed for all the animals.

She realized the farm was surprisingly well-designed.

There were tiny barriers separating each group, keeping the animals from fighting or trampling each other.

The first rabbit she had put in from the Space output had already gotten into a tussle with the other bunnies.

When they saw Cecilia, the little rabbits shrank their necks in fear.

Cecilia didn't mind. She set out food and water for them, figuring she wouldn't need to check back for three or four days.

Next, she went to the areas with the alpacas, the dairy cow, and the miniature horse, making sure their feed was in place.

The mountain goat was particularly clingy, rubbing up against her as soon as it saw her.

Cecilia patted its little head and rewarded it with a carrot.

Then she tended to the guinea fowl, the hens, and the guinea pigs, making sure everything was in order.

She felt a little guilty about bringing in the guinea pigs.

In the north, people mostly kept them as pets, and she wasn't much of a cook.

But in the south, they were a delicacy, far more prized than rabbit meat.

She figured she could at least care for them for now.

Just as Cecilia finished and was thinking she wouldn't have to touch the farm for a few days, a screen suddenly popped up.

Bold letters blinked on the LCD: [Collect farm byproducts now?]

Cecilia raised an eyebrow and hit "Yes."

Suddenly, a force pushed her toward the guinea fowl's pen.

Somehow, she ended up holding a basket.

She glanced inside the nest and saw a few eggs already waiting. Cecilia's lips twitched as she picked them up.

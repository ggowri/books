Chapter 50 Joint Defense

She first peered through the peephole and saw a tall man standing outside.

He had short, slightly messy hair and wore a black top. His shoulders were broad, and his skin smooth. All in all, he was easy on the eyes.

The moment Cecilia took in his height and build, her right eyelid twitched. Surely it couldn't be that much of a coincidence.

He was close to sixtwo, lean at the waist and broad across the shoulders, every inch of him radiating strength, like a panther ready to spring.

Wasn't this the same man she had seen hauling supplies in the opposite building, the one who had nearly boxed her in on the twelfth floor?

Unless there happened to be a second man in this building with a body sculpted to perfection.

Her hand, which had been gripping the doorknob, slowly dropped.

Perhaps hearing her footsteps at the door, the man outside spoke. "Hello. My name is Arthur Barnes. I wanted to talk to you about setting up a joint defense on the sixtysixth floor to deal with outside threats."

Cecilia raised an eyebrow. Straight to the point, wasn't he?

"Outside threats? And what kind of crisis does Mr. Barnes think we're facing?"

The two spoke through the closed door.

Faced with her unfriendly tone, Arthur showed no irritation at all. Calm and composed, he answered, "The end of the world."

His words were blunt and to the point.

After a brief silence, Cecilia spoke again. "Mr. Barnes, what exactly do you mean by 'joint defense'?"

The man outside seemed endlessly patient and didn't mind in the least that she hadn't opened the door. "There are only two households on the sixtysixth floor now, both on the east side. We can remove the doors from the vacant units and use them to block all access points to this floor. I wanted to know if you'd agree to that."

Cecilia felt a spark of interest at that.

Sealing off the hallway with doors was the best defense.

Out here, the apocalypse didn't scare her as much as what people were capable of.

She was just a young girl, with a big, plump dog at her side. To anyone else, that made them prime targets, especially for thugs like Bryan, who had come knocking before.

Later, when food became even scarcer, Blackie would only be more tempting.

She had thought about sealing off the entire floor herself.

But she didn't have enough doors, and doing it alone would only broadcast one thing—"Cecilia has supplies," which might paint a bigger target on her back.

Now, though, it was different. A neighbor had moved in.

Joining forces like this gave a legitimate reason to block the floor.

Still, could she really trust this stranger?

Cecilia stayed quiet for a long moment, studying the man outside.

Then she saw there was a large, rotting patch on his neck. Some areas were red and swollen; others had already started to peel and decay. Yet he seemed completely unconcerned, with not even a trace of ointment or treatment.

It was the aftermath of acid rain.

In this apocalyptic world, everyone knew acid rain was dangerous, and few would dare go out.

A wound this bad didn't appear overnight. It took prolonged exposure outside, like what she and Blackie had endured—sometimes even wading directly into the acid rain.

That confirmed it for her. This was the same man who had been hauling supplies across the way last night.

She frowned. Why had he moved in right next door? Was it just a coincidence, or had he seen Blackie yesterday and already figured out who she was?

Cecilia didn't answer, but the man outside was patient, waiting silently.

After a moment, she made up her mind. Whatever his intentions, she wasn't about to let fear rule her.

"Hold on, I'll change my clothes. I'll come out and talk," she said, then retreated inside.

She slipped into jeans, a hoodie, and sneakers—everything meant for quick movement. From the Space, she grabbed a small knife and tucked it into the back of her waistband, then slipped a palm-sized stun baton into her sleeve.

Through the peephole, she cast one last glance at the man outside. If he tried anything funny, one zap and a quick stab would settle it. No loose ends.

Outside, Arthur, on his first visit to a neighbor, had no idea he'd picked a floor with someone as fierce as Cecilia.

He had lived on the sixth floor before, but the relentless rain had started to weigh on him.

At 28, he'd grown up in the country but spent the last few years abroad, only returning recently on business. He hadn't expected the acid rain and flooding to trap him here.

Having completed some extremely dangerous missions in the past, he had a sixth sense for trouble.

While others shouted about waiting for rescue or blindly trusting that authorities wouldn't abandon them, he started stockpiling supplies and moving between floors.

Cecilia opened the door and got a closer look at the man.

He was tall and broad-shouldered, with a resolute appearance; even up close she could make out the contours of his muscles.

He looks like a trained fighter, extremely dangerous. She concluded silently.

She didn't know that Arthur was doing the same—assessing her.

Her simple clothes didn't hide her beauty. Her long, silky hair, flawless skin, rosy cheeks, and perfectly shaped brows and eyes made her stand out.

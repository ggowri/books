Chapter 23 Water

Cecilia wasted no time calling the 4S shop. To her relief, the staff informed her the cars had just arrived—ready for pickup.

She and Blackie hurried over to collect them, then drove straight to a gas station to fill the tanks.

She pumped the fuel herself, seizing the chance to secretly store extra in the Space.

This had been her routine for over a month—visiting several gas stations daily and topping up multiple tanks to stockpile fuel.

Her Space now held enough gasoline to last years, and over the past month, Cecilia had also amassed mountains of firewood and coal for stoves—more than enough to see her through the upcoming extreme cold.

The next day, Cecilia rushed to collect the water storage tanks and shelves she'd ordered.

The shop owner apologized profusely for the delay, fretting that she'd had to brave the rain.

As she left, he handed her around twenty small corrosion-resistant buckets, mentioning they might come in handy for her "factory."

Cecilia's eyes lit up at the sight of the buckets, a plan taking shape in her mind.

She accepted the free ones and bought fifty more.

Could these hold acid rain?

She knew a secret about the apocalypse—one few others were privy to.

In the early days of the end times, most people cowered indoors to avoid the acid rain.

But once the natural disasters subsided, many began to mutate, developing superpowers.

Without exception, every single one of those with abilities had been exposed to the toxic downpour.

Those who'd never been caught in it showed no signs of mutation.

Cecilia had never uncovered the reason behind this before her death in her past life.

But if she could store some acid rain, might she one day help people who would never have mutated on their own gain superpowers?

Buckets in hand, Cecilia headed straight for a nearby reservoir.

Dusk had fallen, and the area was deserted.

She dived into the water, focusing on filling the buckets in the Space as she swam.

Sure enough, a vortex formed around her, water swirling into the pendant at her chest.

Half an hour passed, and the vortex still hadn't faded—the Space wasn't full yet.

Exhausted from swimming, Cecilia waded to shallower water to keep going.

Before she knew it, she'd drifted off to sleep in the current.

When she opened her eyes again, it was the middle of the night.

Blinking away sleep, Cecilia stared in disbelief—the reservoir's water level had dropped by half!

Dazed, she scrambled back to shore, grateful the remote spot had no security cameras.

She changed into dry clothes and drove home, only realizing once in the car that she'd spent over five hours in the water.

Cecilia clicked her tongue in frustration.
Why does collecting water with the Space take so long?

Waiting at a red light, she glanced into the Space to check the buckets. What she saw made her freeze.

The buckets were empty—completely dry, not a drop of water inside.

The light turned green, forcing her to focus on driving.

She'd have to investigate once home.

Back at the house, Blackie lay by the door, his big face drooping as he glared at her disapprovingly.

Cecilia hurried to take him outside to relieve himself.

While he bounded around the lawn, she slipped into the Space, panic rising.

Water was too precious to lose—where had it all gone?

Once she entered, her jaw dropped. A river had appeared in the Space, winding from beside the small cottage through the fields and vanishing into the thick black mist in the distance, endless as far as the eye could see.

"What the... hell," she whispered, stunned.

Blackie trotted back to her side, sitting down and tilting his head as if asking, "What are you thinking about?"

No longer in the mood for a walk, Cecilia herded him back inside.

She dropped his leash and rushed into the Space again to examine the river.

It was more like a stream—about three feet wide and one and a half feet deep—its crystal-clear water shimmering with faint blue light, exactly like the glowing specks from the stones that had upgraded the Space before.

Cecilia cupped her hands and dipped them in the water. Icy cold, the blue flecks danced in her palms.

An irresistible urge came over her, and she brought the water to her lips.

Sweet and refreshing, it was far better than any she'd tasted before.

She drank again, and again.

Cecilia lost count of how much she drank, only feeling as if the water held some sort of enchantment, luring her to gulp it down without pause.

By the time she came to her senses, her stomach was distended, round as a balloon.

She was about to laugh at her own greed when a sharp pain erupted in her abdomen, spreading rapidly to every part of her body.

Instead of panicking, excitement surged through her.

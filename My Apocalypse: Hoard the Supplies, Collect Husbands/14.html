Chapter 14 Show Me the Proof

She hadn't lied about the restaurant—it really was excellent, its head chef poached specifically for his skills, a legacy passed down through generations of his family.

The moment Miranda stepped inside, a faint stir of emotion crossed her face.

A small smile appeared on Cecilia's face.

Ryan Summers—her grandfather—had been an exceptional cook in his youth.

Rumor had it that Miranda had fallen hopelessly in love with Ryan back then, captivated entirely by his culinary talents.

The restaurant's decor was nostalgic.

From Miranda's expression, Cecilia knew she'd been transported back to the days when Ryan was part of her life.

Dinner with Miranda was surprisingly pleasant.

Over the meal, Cecilia shared several amusing anecdotes about Ryan in his later years, drawing hearty laughter from Miranda.

Sarah, however, sat pouting nearby, her dislike for Cecilia evident.

When Cecilia excused herself to the restroom, Sarah followed close behind. "What are you planning, Cecilia? I know you need something from my grandma. And I noticed you talking about your grandpa to win her over!" she snapped.

Sarah wasn't stupid, but Cecilia had no desire to engage. She simply smiled, a response that made Sarah stamp her foot in frustration.

Shortly after, Sarah stepped out to take a call. Left alone with Miranda—who was eating a tender, flaky crab cake—Cecilia felt her sharp gaze on her.

"Cici, let's get to the point. I'm old and need to head back soon. What do you want?" Miranda said.

Cecilia met her eyes, sighing softly, "Madam Barnes... have you been happy, truly, in this life?"

Miranda froze, then replied after a long pause, "I have a house full of children and grandchildren. What reason could I have to be unhappy?"

"But what if... you'd ended up with my grandpa? Or if... Sir Barnes hadn't..." Cecilia let the words hang, trailing off before finishing.

Everyone knew Hugh Barnes, Miranda's husband, had cheated on her with a woman twenty years his junior.

Back then, Miranda had acted swiftly, sending the mistress abroad. But whispers persisted that the other woman had been pregnant, a lingering threat that had never fully vanished.

In recent years, rumors had circulated that the child, now in their twenties, might return to claim a share of the Barnes family fortune.

With Hugh's health declining rapidly, the matter weighed heavily on Miranda's mind.

"Your grandpa and the Barnes family go way back. And you and Alexander were betrothed at birth—that's why I've indulged this conversation. What purpose do these questions serve? What are you really here for?" Miranda said, her face darkening.

Cecilia remained unfazed.

The angrier Miranda grew, the more it revealed how deeply these matters touched her.

"I don't want to marry Alexander, Madam Barnes. And I don't think he wants to marry me, either," she stated plainly.

Miranda's scowl deepened. "Nonsense! Alexander has been looking forward to this engagement. We've already sent out the invitations—calling it off now would ruin the Barnes family's reputation!"

Cecilia gritted her teeth, leaning slightly forward. "I have proof. And I'll show it to you—right now."

Before Miranda started to refuse, Cecilia cut her off gently. "You know I lost my parents young, Madam Barnes. My grandpa raised me alone. His last wish before he died was for me to live a happy life. He even told me that if I ever needed help, I could come to you."

Her voice softened. "I can't bear to live a life like yours. It would break his heart."

Miranda stared at the half-eaten crab cake in her hand, silent for a long moment.

Finally, she took a bite, swallowing slowly before speaking, "Show me this proof, then."

Half an hour later, Cecilia, Sarah, and Miranda arrived at Julia's hotel.

The second they walked in, Sarah fumbled for her phone. She'd tried repeatedly to text Alexander on the way over, only to be stopped by Cecilia and Miranda each time.

Now sensing trouble, she turned to run, phone in hand.

But Cecilia, her strength improved by weeks of training, was too quick. She grabbed Sarah's arm, pinning her in place before snatching the phone and slamming it to the floor, where it shattered into pieces.

"What do you think you're doing!" Sarah shrieked.

Cecilia nodded at the broken phone bits. "I'll buy you a new one tomorrow. But right now, you're going to stay quiet. Otherwise... I'll make sure the entire city hears about your brother's other indiscretions, and the Barnes family will be the laughingstock of the town. We can resolve this quietly—if you let us—and save your family what little dignity they have left."

"Sarah, since you're already here, behave." Miranda's face was icy.

Sarah fell silent, glaring daggers at Cecilia but daring not to protest further.

Julia, who'd been waiting as arranged, sighed and handed Cecilia a hotel key card.

"Don't tell anyone I gave you this. If word gets out, I'd have to bear the brunt," she muttered.

Giving out guest key cards without authorization was a serious breach of protocol—Julia was taking a real risk.

This was the favor Cecilia had asked of her.

Cecilia nodded in thanks, saying nothing more as she led Miranda and Sarah toward the elevators.

By the time they reached the hotel room door, the sound of laughter—loud and unrestrained—could be heard from inside.

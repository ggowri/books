Chapter 17

"How long will it take to process one hundred chickens?"

Lila was in a hurry.

"If we start now and work into the evening, we should just about finish tonight. If you haven't got anything urgent on, you might as well wait here."

The man checked the time. They could get it done today. This young woman clearly was not from the village, and making another trip back and forth would be a nuisance. She might as well stay.

"All right. I'll wait here, then."

Lila nodded.

The man began unloading the chickens from the van. By then, his wife had finished eating as well. She called over the older couple and a child of about thirteen or fourteen.

The whole family of five set to work.

The man killed the chickens one by one. His wife took them and drained the blood. The older couple boiled water, then took the bled birds and scalded them before plucking the feathers.

At once, the smell of hot water and wet feathers filled the courtyard.

It was not exactly pleasant, but Lila found it familiar.

When she was little and visited her grandparents, Agnes would kill a cockerel for her, and the yard would always smell just like this.

The man worked quickly. One clean movement per bird, and before long, all one hundred chickens had been killed.

He put down the knife and helped his wife drain the blood.

The large basin filled higher and higher with bright red blood. Lila found it rather unsettling to look at.

She did not know how to cook with chicken blood anyway. After hesitating for a moment, she simply said, "I don't need the blood. You can keep it."

"Oh, don't say that. Chicken blood is lovely in a stew. You shouldn't waste it."

The woman spoke quickly, as if she felt sorry for Lila on behalf of the food itself.

Lila smiled. "I'm not used to eating it, and I don't really know how to cook it. You should keep it and make something nice."

"Well, thank you. We won't stand on ceremony, then."

The man thanked her at once. He loved chicken blood, especially the chicken stew with suet dumplings his wife made.

His wife nudged him with her elbow and mouthed something.

The man quickly added, "Then we'll charge you a bit less. It's usually eight dollars per chicken, but since you're giving us all this blood, we'll make it six dollars each."

"That's very kind of you. Thank you."

Lila accepted without too much fuss. Saving a little still counted.

While they processed the chickens, Lila sat in a corner of the courtyard and looked around.

The place had green hills and clear water. The surroundings were rather lovely.

This village was just outside Lower Vale, a little closer to town.

If one followed the mountain road further in, it would lead to Lower Vale.

Once the man and his wife had finished draining all the chickens, they began the next step after the older couple had plucked the feathers, gutting and cleaning them.

They picked out the gizzards, hearts and livers and set them aside. Then they rinsed each opened chicken thoroughly in clean water twice, and it was done.

One chicken, two chickens, three chickens.

Several hours passed, and the sky had gone dark by the time all the birds were ready.

The family helped Lila load the chickens into the van.

Lila thanked them, paid the money, and quickly drove away.

She had ended up working into the dark again.

When she reached the garage, she moved all the chicken into the Vault in one go, then drove out to eat.

Over the past few days, whenever she got busy, she had simply ordered takeaway. She had not sat down to eat a proper meal in ages.

Tonight, she did not want takeaway. She wanted to eat before going home.

Lila went to the lively little restaurant she liked.

Since it was not the weekend, there were not many customers left at this hour.

Lila knew the menu well and ordered her usual favourites: chilli pork stir-fry, crispy sweet-and-sour pork, and slow-braised pork belly.

When the dishes arrived, they tasted as good as ever.

After working all day, Lila was starving. She ate in large mouthfuls and drank plenty with it.

Halfway through the meal, she raised a hand.

"Could you make a few dishes for me to take away?"

The server was used to requests like that and came over with a menu and pen.

"Five portions of chilli pork stir-fry, five portions of crispy sweet-and-sour pork, five portions of slow-braised pork belly, five portions of poached fish in chilli broth, and five portions of red-braised pork belly."

"You want that many?"

The server looked as though she could not believe what she had heard.

"Yes. They're late-night meals for my colleagues at the office."

Lila replied calmly. She had grown used to this excuse.

"Please wait a little, then."

With such a large order, the server hurried straight to the kitchen.

Those dishes would take a while, so Lila ate slowly and did not rush.

By the time she was completely full, the takeaway dishes were ready too. After she paid, the server helped her carry everything to the car.

Even the leftovers from her own table were packed up and put in the car.

She had eaten from those dishes herself, so there was nothing to mind. After the Collapse, food like this would be a luxury.

Lila drove to a spot near Oakwood Estate, casually reached towards the passenger seat, and moved all the packed food into the Vault.

Once she got home, she quickly showered, washed off the exhaustion of the day, and lay down in bed.

Lila picked up her phone and saw more than a dozen messages from Annabel.

They were all holiday photos, along with little comments about how wonderful it would have been if Lila were there too.

Even though Lila did not want to deal with her at all, she still replied.

Have fun. It's not easy to go on a trip like that, so make sure you get your money's worth.

Annabel replied almost instantly, thanking Lila for being so good to them.

Lila sent back an emoji and left the chat.

She opened the notes app on her phone and began calculating which essentials she had not prepared yet.

Petrol, vegetables, fruit, and self-defence gear.

Those three categories had to be dealt with.

It was only 1 June. There were still thirty days before the rainstorms arrived.

There was time. There had to be time.

Recently, to save every possible minute, she had been rushing about stockpiling without a pause. Now that the schedule looked a little less suffocating, Lila finally relaxed slightly.

She was almost impressed with herself. In ten days, she had managed to do so much.

With that sense of relief settling over her, she soon fell into a deep sleep.

On 2 June, Lila's plan was to go into the countryside and buy a batch of vegetables.

While eating breakfast, she realised the jerrycans she had ordered online had all arrived.

She went to the parcel depot first and collected them.

After loading the jerrycans into the back of the van, she drove to Lockridge Storage Unit.

Inside the unit, she moved two hundred and eighty of them into the Vault, leaving twenty in the van.

Once everything was arranged, she drove towards the countryside.

Along the way, whenever she passed a petrol station, she stopped to fill the jerrycans.

To fill jerrycans at a petrol station, she needed a reason.

Lila said a friend's car had run out of fuel on the road, and she was buying two cans of petrol to take to him.

It was a good excuse. After the staff registered the details, they sold her the petrol without any fuss.

However, she could only buy two cans at each station. Any more would look suspicious and draw attention.

Every time she bought two jerrycans of petrol, Lila immediately moved them into the Vault. Petrol was different from other supplies. It was safer to store it away as soon as possible.

After stopping for fuel all along the way, passing five petrol stations and spending three hours on the road, Lila finally arrived at Sunwell, a town rich in agricultural produce.

Sunwell had put great effort into developing farming and cash crops.

It was the perfect place to stockpile.

Chapter 9

After deducting the money for reinforcing the house, buying winter clothes and bedding, and giving money to those greedy people, Lila still had $1.83 million left.

She opened Amazon and saw that many of the items had already been dispatched.

The delivery address was still the same collection point she had used for the imitation title deeds. It was far from home, so there was no chance of Edward or Maggie hearing about it.

Lila stretched and got out of bed briskly.

Today, she needed to sort out a van and a storage unit.

Her own car was only a saloon. It would not be able to carry much once she began stockpiling. A van would be far more reliable.

When the time came, she could fill an entire van with supplies, drive it into the storage unit, and quietly move everything into the Vault.

The thought alone was satisfying.

After breakfast, Lila searched online for van rental companies. She found that renting a Luton van for two months would cost at least $6,000.

She changed her approach and took the Tube to a second hand vehicle dealer instead.

A used Luton van in decent condition, with a payload of about one tonne, also cost only $6,000 to buy outright.

Without another word, Lila signed the contract, completed the paperwork, and bought the van.

As for the storage unit, the cheaper the better.

Lila opened a short video app and searched for storage units to rent. A pile of listings appeared at once.

A remote storage unit on the outskirts of Ashton cost only $100 a month.

Lila called the number directly, then drove the van over.

No wonder it was cheap.

There was absolutely nothing around the place. It was wonderfully remote.

The unit was a little basic, but it was large.

Lila was very satisfied. The more secluded, the better.

The owner could not even be bothered to draw up a contract. He took $200, tossed her the key, and said it did not matter if she used it for a few extra months.

Unfortunately, Lila would not be able to take advantage of that bargain for long.

In less than two months, the end of the world would arrive.

Before the supplies she had ordered online arrived, Lila still had one very important thing to do.

She needed storage racking.

All kinds of racking for organising supplies.

Based on her experience from her previous life, small items placed into the Vault would end up scattered all over the floor if there was no proper shelving to support and organise them.

The racking did not need to be excellent quality. It only had to stand upright and help her sort things by category.

She opened Gumtree and searched for used storage racking in Ashton.

A row of listings appeared.

"Supermarket closing down, racking for urgent sale."

"Batch of racking for low price. Any reasonable offer accepted."

"Warehouse clearing storage racking. No bargaining. Collection only."

Lila browsed through them. The warehouse clearance racking was the cheapest.

She contacted the seller.

He had fifty units in total. Bought separately, they were $70 each. If she took the whole lot, it would be only $2,500.

Lila followed the address the seller sent her and drove over immediately.

Fifty units of racking would be enough.

Only food, tools and similar items needed to go on shelves.

Large items like grain could simply be stacked neatly on the floor.

When she arrived, the owner took Lila into the warehouse to inspect the racking.

Fifty units really were quite a lot. They were also large.

They were all white and did not look old.

"If I didn't need the space cleared quickly, I'd never sell them this cheap," the owner said, clearly pained. "Each one should be at least a hundred."

Lila touched the metal panels of the racking. They were indeed thick and sturdy.

$50 each was excellent value.

She paid on the spot.

The owner called over several warehouse workers to help carry the racking onto the van.

Even with the van fully loaded, they only managed to fit half of it.

Lila waved a hand.

"I'll take this lot back first, then come back for the rest."

Driving the van, Lila kept glancing around.

There were cameras almost everywhere in the city, and she was not good at finding blind spots.

To be safe, she drove all the way back to the storage unit she had rented.

She drove the van inside, shut the roller door, and only then reached out and moved all the racking into the Vault.

Closing her eyes, she used her consciousness to arrange all the racking along the far left side of the Vault.

Between each row, she left a passage wide enough for one person to walk through.

That way, it would be easier to find things later.

Once the first batch of racking was in place, Lila did not rest. She drove back immediately to collect the second batch.

By the time all the racking was neatly arranged inside the Vault, Lila was tired again.

She really was a low energy human being.

Even Lila herself wondered when her stamina had become so poor.

Then again, she had done quite a lot today. It was already past five in the afternoon.

She simply drove the Luton van home and parked it in a space outside Oakwood Estate.

Once inside, she ordered takeaway, ate, washed up, and lay down on the bed.

Lila was checking the delivery information on her phone when Anna sent another video.

In the video, Anna and Daniel were learning to dive together.

Maggie and Edward sat on the diving boat, watching them with tense expressions.

Lila gave a cold smile and replied, "Amazing. You're so good, Anna."

25 May.

Lila got up, had breakfast, and drove to Lower Vale.

When she arrived, the construction team was already there, sitting on the slope outside the courtyard gate.

Two lorries were parked by the entrance.

They were loaded with all kinds of machinery, and there were even tents.

It seemed they planned to eat and sleep on site so they could finish the project in one go.

Lila exchanged a few polite words with Markus and paid the deposit.

The workers then began.

But Lila did not leave.

After all, it was the first day of construction. She felt more reassured watching from the side.

The workers removed the windows and door frames, then smashed open the heated hearth benches and stoves with sledgehammers.

It really was no small project.

At noon, the workers stopped to rest and took out the food they had brought with them.

Lila was hungry too, so she drove away.

Before she left, Markus repeatedly assured her that their team had an excellent reputation and that they would carry out every project to the highest possible standard.

Lila suddenly wanted clam noodle soup again.

She drove to Baker's Lane Market behind the university.

Knowing the place well, she went straight into Gran Lily's shop.

"Gran Lily, one clam noodle soup with extra chilli oil and vinegar, please. And one pork loin bap."

Lila sat at a table and ordered with practiced ease.

Gran Lily came slowly out from the kitchen, holding a clean little cloth.

"Coming, love. I'll make it for you now."

The clam noodle soup and pork loin bap were soon placed on the table.

It was the same familiar smell.

The moment Lila caught the scent, she could not help swallowing.

"Gran Lily, could you make twenty portions of the clam noodle soup and twenty pork loin baps? I'll take them away in a bit."

Gran Lily was shocked.

"What? Why are you buying so many, love? You can't buy that much at once. By the time you take it back, the noodles will be all swollen."

"No, I'm buying them for my colleagues. I'm treating them to lunch today."

Lila found an excuse.

Gran Lily walked over to Lila's table with great seriousness.

"Tell me the truth. Are they bullying you? Are they making you buy lunch for twenty people all by yourself?"

Lila felt touched by Gran Lily's concern, but she also nearly laughed.

"No, Gran Lily, they're not bullying me. I'm treating them because I want to. A colleague treated me to a big meal a while ago."

Only then did Gran Lily relax.

A smile appeared on her face.

"All right, then. As long as no one's giving you trouble. I'll make them for you. I'm not sure whether I've got enough noodles, but there should be enough..."

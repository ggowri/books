Chapter 88

"I'd rather fight for a chance to live. Even if I die on the road, that's better than sitting here waiting for death."

Callum had already made up his mind.

He was going to the refuge.

Leo pressed his lips together.

"Mrs. Harris, how many days of food do we have left?"

Mrs. Harris looked around at the group.

"If we're careful, maybe five days."

The answer silenced the room.

There seemed to be a decent amount of food piled against the wall, but communal ration meals fed sixteen people every day.

The supplies disappeared faster than anyone liked.

Nobody spoke.

Everyone wanted safety.

Nobody wanted hunger.

"Take thirty minutes to think about it," Mrs. Harris said.

"We'll vote afterward."

With that, she returned to cleaning the cooking pots.

The Foraging Teams had only found significant food supplies within Oakwood Estate itself.

Outside the estate, most useful locations had already been stripped by other survivors.

Finding enough food to sustain everyone was becoming nearly impossible.

Half an hour later, Mrs. Harris gathered everyone again.

"Have you all made your decision?"

The room was filled with grim faces.

She nodded and continued.

"All right. If you want to go to the refuge, raise your hand."

Sophie raised hers immediately.

Leo followed.

Then Callum.

Chloe.

Nathan.

Lauren.

The younger survivors naturally wanted a chance at a better future.

Lauren had a daughter to think about.

Giving up wasn't an option.

Elsewhere in the room, four or five more hands slowly went up.

Then Mrs. Harris raised her own.

She wanted to survive too.

The moment the others saw her vote, panic spread among those who had hesitated.

Even Daniel, hiding quietly in a corner, slowly lifted his hand.

Out of twenty six people, twenty four voted to leave.

Only two remained.

One was gravely ill.

The other was an elderly man.

The old man leaned against the cold wall and closed his eyes.

"I'm seventy six."

"I don't have many days left anyway."

"My children are gone."

"My wife is gone."

"I'll stay here."

The words were simple.

But the sadness behind them filled the room.

"If you want to come, I'll get you there."

Mrs. Harris hated seeing things like this.

She wanted everyone to survive.

That had always been her goal.

The vote existed only because she couldn't bear the responsibility of forcing people into danger.

The old man smiled faintly.

"No."

"I'd only slow you down."

"Red..."

His tired eyes met hers.

"You make sure you survive."

Something about the way he said it made it sound like a blessing.

Mrs. Harris felt her throat tighten.

"Come with us," she urged softly.

The old man chuckled.

"If I go with you, I'll probably die faster."

"Leave me here."

In the end, she couldn't argue.

At his age, the journey might truly kill him.

As for the gravely ill survivor, the situation was even more obvious.

The man was barely hanging on.

Nobody was willing to carry someone already at death's door across frozen wilderness.

The decision was painful.

But unavoidable.

Mrs. Harris took a deep breath.

"Then let's divide the supplies."

"Everyone gets an equal share."

"Those staying and those leaving."

The first step had begun.

Every item from the communal food stores was laid out on tables.

Each category was divided into twenty one equal portions.

When the distribution was finished, everyone received only a small bundle.

The supplies looked much smaller once spread across so many people.

Before dismissing the meeting, Mrs. Harris gave one final round of instructions.

"Spend the rest of today preparing."

"We leave first thing tomorrow morning."

"If your clothing isn't warm enough, search the empty rooms."

"Take coats from abandoned supplies."

"Sew blankets into extra layers if you have to."

"If you're carrying a lot of gear, find a large board or remove a door."

"Tie a rope to it and make yourself a sled."

"It'll save energy during the journey."

That night, the shelter buzzed with activity.

Wood scraped.

Tools clattered.

People packed supplies and sorted clothing.

For the first time in days, hope filled the air.

The road ahead would be difficult.

But they were heading toward a new refuge.

Toward possibility.

Toward survival.

Sophie decided she needed a sled.

The situation at the refuge remained unclear.

The more prepared she was, the better.

She planned to use two Utility Sacks.

Food would be packed tightly and distributed between them.

That way, if one bag was stolen or lost, she wouldn't lose everything.

Above the food, she packed essentials.

A Vacuum Flask.

Spare gloves.

Face coverings.

Extra winter clothing.

Even then, she worried her coat wasn't thick enough.

She searched several abandoned basement rooms and eventually found a massive padded coat.

Old.

Heavy.

But sturdy.

It fit comfortably over her existing layers and provided significantly more insulation.

Leo and Callum chose a different strategy.

They would pull a single sled together.

That way, they could alternate and rest when needed.

Their supplies were packed separately to avoid confusion.

Because food occupied most of the space, they limited the rest of their gear to necessities.

Extra fur coats.

Mink hats.

Reindeer hide boots.

Basic survival supplies.

To build the sled, they followed Mrs. Harris's advice and removed a door.

Conveniently, the hinge side already contained sturdy openings.

Leo happened to have brought climbing rope into the shelter weeks earlier.

Threading the rope through the existing holes, he quickly secured a strong towing harness.

"That's impressive."

Callum couldn't help grinning.

Leo simply continued working.

Most of the sled construction had been his idea anyway.

Once the supplies were loaded, the two men lashed everything down carefully.

Then they added several more layers of rope.

By the time they finished, the cargo looked practically indestructible.

Even if the sled rolled over several times, nothing would come loose.

Callum was genuinely impressed.

"How about we stay in the same room tonight?"

"If something happens, it's safer."

Leo didn't answer immediately.

He simply nodded.

Then he noticed four Vacuum Flasks sitting nearby.

Without a word, he secured them along the edge of the sled.

Two each.

Filled with hot water, they might make a huge difference during the journey.

Lauren didn't have many supplies.

Her food reserves were modest compared to the others.

But she still needed a sled.

Her daughter couldn't possibly walk through snow that deep.

A sled would carry both supplies and child.

In many ways, it would make the trip easier.

After a tremendous amount of effort, she managed to remove a door panel.

She attached a rope.

Then she laid two thick blankets across the surface.

The supplies were secured toward the rear.

At the front, she placed a large cardboard box.

Additional blankets were packed around the inside walls.

More blankets were draped across the top.

Once her daughter climbed inside, Lauren planned to tie everything securely in place.

The wind wouldn't be able to reach her.

And if all went well, her little girl would stay warm throughout the entire journey.

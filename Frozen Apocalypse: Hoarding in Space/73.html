Chapter 72

Cedric's hand lingered momentarily on her arm, but through the thick coat, there was nothing to feel. His gaze drifted downward briefly, then he quickly averted his eyes, realising his lapse.

"Are you alright?" he asked.

"I'm fine, thank you," Annabel said, meeting his gaze briefly before lowering her eyes. Her expression was calm, almost mischievous, but her lips, lightly tinted, gave a sense of delicate poise.

Behind him, one of the officers stepped forward. Mrs Harris leaned over, saying, "Ah, she's young and inexperienced. I'll step in and guide her."

"No need, no need," Cedric waved off the concern and glanced once more at Annabel. "She lives here?"

"Yes," Mrs Harris replied, a touch flustered.

Cedric nodded and walked down the corridor. Mrs Harris exhaled, relieved, and followed.

Annabel straightened, feeling a surge of self-assurance. She returned to the basement, sitting on her bed in quiet contemplation.

"What's wrong, love?" Maggie asked, noticing her unsettled look.

Edward glanced over briefly, seeing no physical harm. Annabel ignored Maggie, smoothing her hair as she reclined carefully, protecting her style.

After a while, she could no longer remain still. "I'll get some hot water," she said, picking up the bottle and leaving. With Cedric present, hot water was available all day.

She approached the security guard, a polite smile in place. "Where is the inspector resting?"

The guard hesitated, noticing her neat appearance, and simply said, "Mrs Harris requested discretion."

Annabel slipped two sweets into his pocket, giving a playful squeeze. "Just curious," she said sweetly.

The guard cleared his throat. "Next door to Mrs Harris. Stay nearby, but don't enter."

"Understood, thanks for your effort," Annabel said, her voice light, and made her way to Cedric's resting quarters.

She adjusted her hair and collar before knocking.

"Who's there?" came the voice.

"It's me. I wanted to bring you some hot water in thanks for earlier," she said, glancing around to make sure no one watched.

After a pause, the door cracked open. "Come in," Cedric said.

The room was warm, lit by a small portable heater. Cedric had removed his mink coat, wearing only a dark grey wool jumper as he sipped tea.

Annabel entered, standing with the bottle in front of him silently. Cedric's gaze swept over her, then returned to meet her eyes.

"Have a seat," he said, pointing to the chair across from him.

She approached, placing the bottle on the table. With no one else around, Cedric let his gaze settle naturally. Annabel instinctively covered herself slightly before sitting.

Cedric took a sip of tea. "How old are you?"

"Twenty-two."

He nodded and set the cup down. His hands rested on the table, a gold ring catching the light.

"Tough times, I imagine," he said casually.

Annabel lowered her eyes. "I'm used to it."

"Used to it?" Cedric gave a short laugh.

"Why are you delivering the water?" He was perceptive.

Annabel smiled faintly. "You're someone who helps us. I admire that."

Cedric considered her words, then remarked, "A young woman like you shouldn't be in such conditions."

She looked up, meeting his eyes with quiet determination. Cedric stood and moved beside her. His presence was commanding, and the subtle scent of smoke and grooming filled the air.

He placed a hand on her shoulder lightly. "Lean but strong," he observed.

Annabel stiffened, then relaxed, taking a steadying breath. "Sir," she murmured.

"Call me Cedric," he said, adjusting his posture.

She nodded, standing straighter. Cedric observed her quietly, then lifted her chin. "Well done."

Annabel simply clenched her fists, meeting his eyes with composure.

"Don't be nervous. There are benefits in following my instructions," he said calmly.

Later, he reached for a cigarette, lighting it as Annabel arranged her clothing.

"Supplies will be redistributed in a couple of days," Cedric said, exhaling. "I'll make the arrangements on your behalf."

Annabel's eyes lit up. "Then I'll handle things here. Don't forget me," she said lightly.

"What do you want?" Cedric asked, knowing full well the intentions of those around him.

"I'd like to have more chances to manage our estate, to keep in touch with you more often," she replied tactfully.

Cedric smirked. "Alright, wait for my word." He extinguished the cigarette and began dressing.

As he donned his jumper, Annabel noticed a dark patch of sweat under his arm.

"I should go. Other estates await," he said, finishing his preparations.

Annabel sat back, the bedding slipping slightly, and watched him depart. From his bag, he tossed her a few supplies: four compressed biscuits and two tins of food. She looked at them but made no move.

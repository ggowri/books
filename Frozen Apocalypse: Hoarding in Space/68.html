Chapter 67

Lila took a steaming, fragrant bowl of clam vermicelli soup out of the Vault, along with a crispy roujiamo. She dug in with enthusiasm.
It was her first time eating clam vermicelli since the Long Freeze began. The numbing spiciness was just as satisfying as ever. The roujiamo was as crispy as if she had bought it fresh from the shop.

She felt a little pang of regret. She knew that every bit of takeaway food she had stockpiled was finite. Eat one portion, lose one portion.
But she had accomplished something major today. She had made that perfect plant stand. She deserved a good meal to reward herself.

Having talked herself into it, she ate with a clear conscience and enjoyed the food even more.
When she was full, her strength returned. She moved the stools away from the wall and shifted the potted plants somewhere out of the way.
She slid the tiered plant stand into place. It fit perfectly, filling the gap between the bookshelf and the writing desk.

She arranged the pots on the stand. It looked much better now, giving the room the feel of a garden library.
The only pity was that the seedlings had only just sprouted. It would be a long time before the flowers bloomed.
But the days were long, and Lila was in no hurry.

She thought the plant stand looked a bit sparse, so she took four more empty pots.
She went to the cave to dig some soil and planted two pots of roses and two pots of chrysanthemums.
The flower seeds she had bought weren't just for decoration. They were all edible and practical.

Roses, jasmine, roses, and chrysanthemums could all be steeped for tea. Not only did they taste good, they had various health benefits.
Rose calmed the mood. Jasmine helped with bloating and stomach discomfort. Rose also invigorated the blood. Chrysanthemum reduced internal heat.
Besides tea, they could be used for desserts, pastries, even skincare products and sachets.

Lila's heart was already blooming with joy just thinking about all the uses for these flowers.
She could only hope they would grow quickly and bloom soon.

She washed the soil off her hands, took out the opened bag of dog kibble, scooped out a handful, and put the bag back into the Vault.
This would prevent bacteria from growing in the kibble and keep it from getting damp.

Lila crouched down on the floor. "Here, boys!"
The two puppies knew they were being called. They wiggled their chubby little bottoms and trotted over.

Lila held out her hand. "Shake."
Snowy had mastered this. He quickly placed his paw in Lila's palm.
"Good boy!"

Lila fed Snowy a piece of kibble.
The kibble was small, but it tasted good. Snowy was delighted.

Patches grew impatient. Why did his brother get a treat and he didn't?
His little tail wagged so fast it blurred. He made whining noises.
Lila held out her hand to Patches. "Shake."

Patches looked confused. He leaned forward and gummed Lila's fingers with his toothless mouth.
Lila sighed, pulled her hand back, and held it out again. "Shake."
Still nothing.

Snowy had had enough. He stepped forward, placed his paw in Lila's palm, and looked back at Patches.
Then he pulled his paw back and placed it in Lila's palm again, looking at Patches again.
His little expression seemed to say, "Got it now, you idiot? It's this simple."

Patches tentatively lifted his paw, just a little, then put it down.
Snowy had to demonstrate again. This time, Patches finally got it. He successfully put his paw in Lila's palm.

"Oh my God! You learned!"

Lila hadn't held out much hope. She had thought the little dummy would never learn. Who would have guessed he had such a good brother?
She divided the kibble in her hand between Snowy and Patches, two pieces each.
One piece as a reward, one piece as tuition.

"Shake." Lila held out her hand again. Both dogs placed their paws in her palm.
They had learned it. They had really learned it.
Happy, Lila divided the remaining kibble in her hand into two portions and gave it all to the two little ones.

At that moment, both puppies realised that obeying their human meant tasty rewards.
Lila patted their heads and stood up. She had one more thing to do today.
The thing she had decided on yesterday: practice ejecting objects from the Vault for ten minutes each day.

She found a decent sized block of wood from the Vault and placed it on the kitchen floor.
She took a steel fork, stored it in the Vault, and practised repeatedly.
Store it. Eject it.

She was practising not just force, but accuracy.
She demanded that the fork land exactly where she wanted it. That way, if she ever faced an enemy, she could strike with one fatal blow.

Ten minutes later, beads of sweat dotted Lila's forehead.
Using the Vault really drained her mental energy.
And she still hadn't found a way to recover it quickly.
The best she could do was take an apple out of the Vault, wash it, and bite into it.
Eating more seemed to relieve the fatigue a little.

She looked at the fork marks on the wooden block. Each one was deeper than the last. She was very satisfied with today's training.

Back in the east parlour, Lila changed into thinner lounge wear. She was exhausted after all that work.
She lay down on the warm hearth bench, pulled an ice cream bar out of the Vault, and slowly ate it while watching a show.

In the middle of the Long Freeze, enjoying an ice cream bar in a warm house. That was something special.
Lila felt a wave of gratitude.

Meanwhile, in the basement under the city, people were going through a very different kind of time.

The supplies finally arrived two days late, which meant they had effectively missed one delivery cycle.
Mrs Harris opened the boxes and sighed. She had hoped that skipping one delivery would mean a larger shipment this time.
But in fact, the amount was even smaller than before.
Apparently, transportation costs were too high, and much of the supplies were lost or damaged on the road. So less and less was reaching them.

Still, something was better than nothing.
Mrs Harris locked the supplies in the estate management cabinet. From now on, she would have to ration carefully. If the deliveries were delayed again, they would starve.

The mood in the basement had been grim lately.
Ever since the mother and child had died, people were terrified of getting sick themselves.
And just at that moment, a third of the residents came down with colds.
Coughing echoed through the basement constantly. Some of the sick were so bad they couldn't even get out of bed.

Mrs Harris knew the cause. The basement was too damp, too crowded, and there was no proper ventilation. Viruses were spreading like wildfire.
She decided to do a thorough disinfection. She took the few remaining bottles of alcohol from the estate management supplies, poured them into a spray bottle, and disinfected every corner of the basement. She hoped the sickness would improve after this.

Sophie, Leo, and Callum had noticed the problem too.
Whenever they went out to queue for food, they heard more and more people coughing.
So now, before leaving their basement, all three of them put on masks to block the virus as much as possible.
When they returned to their own basement, they disinfected the space with whatever little alcohol or liquor they had left.

All of them had some medicine stockpiled. But a single bad cold could use up most of it. None of them could afford to gamble.

"Good news. New supplies have arrived tonight. We'll have dinner as usual."

Mrs Harris announced the news to the people on Basement Level One.

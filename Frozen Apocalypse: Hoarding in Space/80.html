Chapter 79

With this daring idea in mind, Lila hatched an even more reckless plan: she would go outside and collect lightning itself.

She scrambled from the basement, heading for the front gate. The scene outside froze her in place.

The sky looked like shattered black glass, countless cracks shooting blinding white light. Thunder rolled overhead, vibrating her chest.

It was terrifying.

Even with her spatial abilities, Lila trembled uncontrollably. After decades as an ordinary person, her instinctive fear of nature's fury was deeply ingrained.

She pushed her fear aside, donning her extreme-cold suit, gloves, and hat.

Gripping the door handle, she took a deep, steadying breath.

"It's fine, it's fine. The space can contain it...I won't get hurt," she muttered three times. On the last whisper, her heart sank. With a surge of courage, she pushed the door open and stepped boldly into the yard.

Lightning danced like silver snakes in the darkness, striking repeatedly. Lila raised her hands, closed her eyes, focusing on a single thought: collect it, all of it.

The first bolt made her heart skip. The second, third… After five strikes, she was still standing, unharmed. Tension released. It was really possible.

She held her arms aloft, waiting ten more minutes. Only three bolts struck near the house and were absorbed into her space. Too few. Not nearly enough for what she needed.

Looking up the slope, lightning was denser and fiercer. Lila squared her shoulders, pushed open the yard gate, and climbed step by step. She pulled a steel pipe from her space and raised it high. Its metallic sheen glimmered in the dark.

Countless bolts were drawn to the pipe, converging on her. A silver-white net of lightning erupted above her, dense and blinding.

Lila tilted her head back, staring into the blinding light, screaming, "Ahhh!" She braced herself, terrified she would die. Yet she did not lower her hands.

The next moment, a torrent of lightning poured down, all absorbed into her space. She stood rigidly as wave after wave of bolts disappeared into her vault.

After a long while, the sky cleared, the thunder moved distant, and Lila dared to open her eyes. The black heavens had calmed. She returned to the yard, still holding one hand aloft, the other placing the steel pipe back in her space.

Her arms ached from holding the position. Gloves off, her hands were red and numb. She rubbed them, brought her two dogs up, and removed her heavy gear, sitting on the sofa to warm herself.

Outside, the distant forest fires reminded her of the chaos she had just survived.

She entered her space and surveyed the lightning. It was spectacular. Not isolated bolts now, but thousands of them, thick and thin, tangled like old vines. Despite the apparent disorder, Lila had precise control...each of the 1,093 bolts could be lethal.

"I never imagined I could wield this," she whispered, a mix of awe and triumph.

The terrifying lightning had become her ultimate survival tool. It could kill, start fires, destroy vehicles, wipe out groups of people. The potential was enormous.

Exhaustion finally washed over her. She knew the drill...overuse of her space could make her pass out. Adjusting her position, she covered herself with a small blanket and allowed herself to drift into sleep within the warmth of her room.

Outside, the city's residents huddled on open ground, shivering, eyes closed, waiting for the storm to end. The firelight made the cold more bearable, but the pervasive fear of death was overwhelming.

Lightning struck repeatedly. Buildings ignited, anyone struck instantly perished. Screams erupted from crouched, terrified figures.

Gradually, the bolts thinned, and thunder faded. The sky calmed, the electric web disappeared. Survivors cautiously raised their heads, crying freely for what they had endured. Some clutched scorched bodies, dazed.

Mrs Harris staggered to count the living: one, two, three… only forty-six remained.

The basement, once crowded with 178 residents, now held just a fraction. Some had died from influenza, some never escaped the basement, and others had been struck in the open snow.

"It's alright! Everyone! We're safe!" Mrs Harris called, forcing her grief aside to reassure them. Her hoarse voice faded across the snow-covered expanse.

The survivors slowly moved closer to the burning buildings for warmth. Fires burned through the night, leaving only smoke by morning. Some sat motionless in the snow, overcome by exhaustion.

The security guards cautiously cleared the remnants of the collapsed basement doorframe. Smoke still lingered, walls blackened, but the basement itself hadn't ignited. Supplies remained intact.

"They're still here! They're still here!" the guards called from inside.

Chapter 66

When morning came, Mrs Harris, two security guards, and the mother who had lost her child each put on their expedition down parkas and went outside to bury the little boy.
As they laid the small body into the snow, the mother collapsed to her knees and wept. She couldn't even get back up.

It was too cold outside. The two guards tried to pull the mother to her feet, but Mrs Harris stopped them.
"Let her stay with her child a little longer."

Mrs Harris's voice was muffled behind her thick mask.
The three of them stood silently, watching the mother claw at the snow bank and cry.

Five minutes later, the four of them slowly walked back toward the basement.
Suddenly, the woman, her fingers stiff with cold, took off her expedition down parka and handed it to Mrs Harris.
"Put it on, put it on, for God's sake!"

Mrs Harris panicked. Her hands trembling, she tried to wrap the coat back around the woman.
The woman wouldn't listen to anyone. She ran like a madwoman back to the snow bank where her child was buried.
As she ran, the skin exposed to the air began to freeze solid.

Mrs Harris and the two guards chased after her.
The woman dropped to her knees and, with completely frozen fingers, dug her child out of the snow, handful by handful.
Mrs Harris was frantic. She reached the woman and, together with the two guards, tried to drag her away.

But after only two tugs, they tore a strip of frozen flesh from the woman's arm, along with bright red frozen blood.
The three of them let go immediately. They didn't dare touch her again.

With the last of her strength, the woman pulled her child tightly to her chest. Then she toppled over stiffly into the snow and went still.

Mrs Harris couldn't hold back her tears any longer. They streamed down her face, soaking the outer layer of her mask.
"You... you foolish woman!"

In less than five minutes, the woman had frozen solid in the snow.
Mrs Harris didn't dare cry anymore. If her tears wet all the layers of her mask, her own face would freeze too.
Together with the two guards, they buried the woman and her child together in that frozen landscape.

They couldn't stay outside long. After the burial, they hurried back to the basement.
The three of them returned in silence.

"Where's the mother? Why didn't she come back with you?"

A middle aged woman who had offered comfort the night before asked the question.
She didn't have any paracetamol, but she had secretly tucked a biscuit into her pocket, planning to give it to the woman later.

"She... she's not coming back."

Mrs Harris's voice was hoarse. She walked straight to her own basement without looking back.
She couldn't rest for long. After half an hour of pulling herself together, Mrs Harris emerged again, full of energy.

When breakfast time came, the supply lorry from upstairs still hadn't arrived.
The morning meal went the same as the previous afternoon. However much rice you gave, that's how much congee you got.

While Sophie was waiting in line for her congee, she heard that the child from yesterday and its mother were both dead. She felt terrible, but there was nothing she could do.
She just lit a candle while eating her congee in the basement.

Lila was up early. The first thing she did was light all the stoves in the house. The second was go into the cave to feed the chickens.
She went down to the cellar and switched on the daylight grow lamps.
From now on, she would turn the lights on every morning and turn them off at night after feeding the chickens.

Chickens need some light, but they also need darkness.
Like people, they need light to move around during the day and darkness to sleep at night.
So Lila set a fixed time for turning the lights on and off.

When she came back up from the cellar, she cooked instant noodles for breakfast, adding a sausage, a fried egg, and a handful of greens.
The golden noodles floated in a broth tinged with chilli oil, with bright green vegetables and a soft poached egg.
It looked as good as it smelled. Lila couldn't resist taking a photo with her phone.

She put down the phone and ate. The noodles were delicious, the portion not too large. Just right.
The smell of the instant noodles was so strong – all those artificial flavourings – that the two puppies on the floor started whimpering and stamping their paws.

"You can't have this," Lila said.

She used hot water from the fireplace hob to make a bowl of goat's milk formula, then added a handful of puppy kibble to soak.
When the milk had cooled to warm, Lila put the fragrant bowl of soaked kibble on the floor.

For two puppies who had never eaten dog food before, it was a feast.
They plunged their heads into the bowl and rooted around like little piglets. All she could hear was the sound of frantic lapping and chewing.
Soon the bowl was cleaner than if it had been washed.

With the daily chores done, Lila finally had some free time.
She looked at the stools she had been using under her flower pots and decided they weren't good enough. She wanted to make a proper plant stand.

She had in mind a tiered wooden plant stand.
When she had stockpiled scrap wood earlier, there had been plenty of long wooden strips.
She planned to use those.

One of the second hand books she had bought contained instructions.
She searched the bookshelf for a while and finally found it: The Complete Manual of Handmade Furniture.

Lila poured herself a cup of tea, sat at the writing desk, and carefully read through the section on making a tiered plant stand.
She read it twice, very carefully, and decided that her eyes understood the process.

She wrote down on a piece of paper the number and size of wooden strips she would need, then took the materials out of the Vault.
All the useful wooden strips and planks, a saw, a hammer, nails, and sandpaper.
The sandpaper had come as a freebie when she bought too many tools.

Lila laid everything out on the table in the main hall and got to work.
She started sawing the wood according to the measurements she had written down.

She had never done any sawing before. Whenever she used the wrong angle or force, the saw blade would bend and get stuck.
She had to stop every few strokes. "Oh, this is so annoying!"

She knew making a tiered plant stand would be difficult, but she hadn't expected to get stuck on the very first step.
Even though she complained, she was patient.

After half an hour of patient sawing, she got better at it. The blade stopped getting stuck in the middle of a cut.
It took her a full hour and a half, but she finally cut all the wood pieces to the sizes she needed.

Next came sanding. She used the sandpaper to smooth down every rough, splintery spot on the wood.
This was a detailed job, but mindless. She wasn't in a hurry. She sanded while watching a TV show, and before she knew it, all the pieces were smooth.

After that, she followed her drawing and began assembling the pieces, hammering in nails here and there.
Assembly took three hours. But in the end, Lila had built a complete tiered plant stand.

She straightened her aching back and let out a long breath. Finally finished.
Her head was completely foggy from exhaustion.
But the result was very good. The tiered plant stand was beautiful, sturdy, and stable.

Lila set the stand aside, swept up all the sawdust from the floor, and put it into a sack.
She could use it for fuel or for the chickens' deep litter bedding.

She had only eaten instant noodles for breakfast. Now she was starving.

Chapter 80

The numb, silent people suddenly sprang into motion.

At first, they stood slowly, then quickened their pace toward the basement entrance. Someone started running, and in an instant, the entire crowd surged toward the doorway.

They pressed forward, silent and chaotic, shoving their way into the basement.

"Slow down! Be careful!"

Mrs Harris frowned, calling out desperately, but nobody listened anymore.

Those who entered first began snatching every visible supply. Once the obvious stock was gone, they smashed doors and kicked them in. Weak doors gave way, revealing a small cache of food, which was immediately looted.

"This is mine! Everything here is mine!"

"Stop! Don't take my stuff!"

Shouts of protest were swallowed by the relentless crowd.

Strong doors held, so the mob turned on each other, seizing possessions from those carrying more. Anyone resisting received punches and kicks. Many were beaten until their faces were bruised and bloody.

Lila, her leg broken, could only be supported by Maggie, slowly making her way into the basement.

"Go! Everyone's grabbing! You too!"

Ignoring her injury, Lila shoved Maggie into the crowd, and Maggie staggered, joining the frantic scramble for supplies.

Edward adjusted quickly, protecting the items he had taken. Anyone who dared eye his stash got kicked or punched without hesitation.

The once-silent rush devolved into a full-blown brawl.

"What are you doing? Stop this! We're all in this together!"

Mrs Harris, witnessing the chaos, wept. Even as she tried to intervene, no one listened. Someone shoved her hard, and she fell, hitting her head on a corner and losing consciousness.

The melee lasted a long time. Many lay beaten on the floor, while the strongest and most aggressive men grabbed the majority of the supplies. Even Edward, ruthless as he was, couldn't hold onto everything and came away with new injuries on his face.

"Pah! You old fool, still trying to take more!"

Those with loot spat at him, carrying the supplies into Lila's basement. Edward wiped the spit from his face, glaring at the doorway.

Lila dared not breathe, fearful of drawing attention. Maggie helped her toward the underground parking area. They found a tattered blanket and laid Lila down, covering her broken leg which had been frozen for so long.

Leo and Callum had stayed out of it, crouched in a corner. Sophie had done the same, hiding within a partially burnt building, wrapping herself tightly and tucking all her food into her coat.

Eventually, the chaos subsided. Mrs Harris regained consciousness, slowly helped to her feet by the unfortunate who had gained nothing.

She scanned the survivors' faces. Many bore marks of the struggle.

"Why can't you all work together? We're down to just a few of us! Why can't we cooperate and survive? We've made it this far...it's not too much to ask to be kind?"

Her voice cracked, emotion raw. Only the weak seemed to feel her plea. Those who had taken supplies glared at her, clutching their gains with menacing intent. Any attempt to approach would be met with retaliation.

"Stop," Mrs Harris sighed. She propped the fallen basement door to cover the entrance, helping to keep it warmer inside.

Strong survivors claimed the accessible basement rooms, while those left with nothing huddled in the underground car park, helpless and hopeless.

Once the looting ended, the few who had avoided the frenzy quietly returned to the basement. One tried to enter their own room, only to be pushed aside and locked out by others. Helpless, they wandered to an empty spot in the parking area, curling up under a blanket.

Leo and Callum returned as well. Still strong, knives in hand, they kept watch over the others and secured their rooms.

Sophie endured the residual chill near a charcoal fire, keeping herself warm. She burned a few more pieces of wood with a lighter to maintain heat for another two hours, resisting sleep despite exhaustion.

Eventually, darkness fell, and the basement grew quiet. All around her, the faint sounds of snoring and dreaming filled the space. Sophie cautiously slipped into her room, alert to every sound.

A man approached her door, eyes gleaming. She ducked, slammed the door, and pressed herself against it, clutching her dagger, waiting for the danger to pass. He lingered for several minutes before finally moving on.

Sophie finally exhaled, heart racing, and eased into her bed. Ice-cold from the outside, she could not warm quickly, even under her covers.

Mrs Harris, still awake, had reinforced barriers in the parking area to protect the weaker residents. From this night onward, her responsibility narrowed: she would no longer safeguard the entire basement, only those most vulnerable.

She looked at her old basement room. The door hung crooked, the lock broken. She laughed bitterly at herself, wondering how she could possibly protect these weak ones.

It had been a terrifying night. Sophie, half-asleep, suffered through countless nightmares before finally giving up on sleep entirely. She cut her hair shorter, rough and uncatchable, the jagged strands framing her delicate face.

Satisfied, she knew one thing: hiding alone would no longer be enough. She had to survive by her own wits and strength.

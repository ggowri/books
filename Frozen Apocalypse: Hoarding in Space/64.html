Chapter 63

For the next while, Maggie and Anna were in absolute hell.
Maggie couldn't eat. The slightest opening of her mouth sent agony shooting through her face. She could only manage a few sips of rice water each day.
The wound was too close to her eye. With limited medical care, it became inflamed and swollen. Her right eyelid was puffy, making it hard to see.

Anna wasn't faring much better. In theory, she was only confined to the room. But Edward found her so annoying that he never took the handcuffs off. She stayed chained up just as she was.
She became filthy and reeked to high heaven, looking like a beggar.

Lila, on the other hand, was living better and better.
Her greatest pleasure each day was playing with the two puppies.

Snowy and Patches had grown a bit bigger. They had turned into proper little dogs, wagging their tiny tails as they followed Lila around.
After breakfast, Lila grabbed both puppies and climbed onto the heated hearth bench.
Her favourite game lately was to sit cross-legged on the bench and push the two little ones away with her hands. They would shake their heads, lift their tiny paws, and bounce right back to her side.

Both Lila and the puppies could play this game forever.
No matter how many times Lila pushed them away, they always came bounding back happily.

Then Lila had a sudden idea. She switched games.

"Shake hands."

She held out her hand in front of Snowy's paw.
Snowy tilted his head and looked at her, not understanding.
With her other hand, Lila placed Snowy's paw into her palm.
She repeated this three or four times. When she gave the command again, Snowy clumsily put his paw in her hand.

As for Patches, he was simply jealous that his brother got to interact so closely. He kept wiggling his rear end and rubbing against Lila's thigh.

Lila was amazed at how clever Snowy was. She pushed the bothersome Patches aside, grabbed Snowy by the shoulders, and lifted him up.
"Oh, my clever little Snowy! You're so young and you already know how to shake hands!" Lila cooed in an unusually high, sweet voice.

Snowy had never seen his mum like this. He froze for a second, then pressed his ears flat back against his head. His whole face looked perfectly obedient.

Patches went frantic. He couldn't understand why Mum was holding his brother and not him. He tugged at Lila's trouser leg with his tiny puppy teeth, making little whining sounds.
Lila gently patted Patches on the head. "Stop it!"

Patches, stunned by the pat, immediately calmed down.
Lila put Snowy down and held out her hand to Patches. "Shake."
Patches didn't get it. He wagged his head and licked her hand instead.
Lila took Patches' paw and placed it in her palm. Patches loved the attention. He made little growly sounds and pretended to bite Lila's fingers.
He did this several times, gumming her fingers with his toothless mouth.

Snowy had had enough. He shoved his brother aside with his rear end and placed his own little pink paw in Lila's hand.

"Well, you two don't seem like real brothers."

Lila fiddled with the two little creatures in front of her, genuinely puzzled. How could brothers be so different in intelligence?
But it didn't matter. Being a bit dim didn't make them any less happy.

Snowy and Patches were still young, after all. They tired out quickly. The two puppies cuddled up to each other and fell asleep on the warm hearth bench.

Lila suddenly remembered she hadn't fed the chickens yet.
She picked up the bucket of chicken feed and headed to the west room.

The west room was heated today. Warm air hit her face as soon as she walked in. Along with the warmth came the smell of chickens.
Even with the deep litter bedding, a whiff of chicken droppings was unavoidable when the temperature rose.
Lila felt a bit bad. It seemed a shame to use such a nice bedroom for chickens.
Besides, sharing a house with chickens made her worry about germs.

Then she thought of a good place: Rock Hollow.
The far end of the cave was ventilated, with a comfortable temperature and plenty of space.
It would be a great choice for both the chickens and herself.
The only thing missing was light. But Lila had bought daylight grow lamps from the second hand market. They would work perfectly.

She set to work right away. Lila took out two sacks, gently placed the chicks inside, and carried them all into the cave.
She went back to the west room, retrieved the fencing, nesting boxes, water dispenser, and feed trough, storing them in the Vault before taking them to the cave.

When Lila returned to the cave, the chicks were chirping noisily and scrambling around inside the sacks.
Lila rubbed her forehead. She had put them in too early.
They would have to tolerate it for a bit longer.

She surveyed the area carefully.
At the junction between the cellar and the cave, the air was mostly still. If she kept the chickens there, the smell wouldn't dissipate and would become overpowering.
She finally chose a spot near the far end of the cave, about twenty metres from the other exit.
Here, a breeze could blow through from the exit, keeping the air fresh. And the natural rock walls would maintain a stable temperature, not too cold.
It was the perfect location for the flock.

Lila clipped two torches onto the cave wall. She took the fencing out of the Vault and set up an enclosure three times larger than the previous one.
She placed the nesting boxes at the very back, the warmest spot.
Looking at the spacious chicken run, Lila felt very pleased. The chicks would love it.
She put down the feed trough and water dispenser, then tipped the chicks out of the sacks.

The chicks tumbled out, all dizzy and disoriented. The cave was dim, so most of them just crouched on the ground, silent and motionless.
They looked so pitiful that Lila's heart softened. "Wait a bit longer. Once the lights are on, it'll be fine."

Perhaps because they had grown used to Lila, the chicks chirped a couple of times at the sound of her voice, seeming more at ease.
Lila picked up a torch and searched along the upper part of the cave wall for a while, but couldn't find the wiring she was looking for.
Then she realised. She was grown up now, taller. The wiring should be lower down.
She moved the torch beam downward and finally spotted the electrical wire near shoulder height.

Her grandfather had run this wire years ago to light the cave.
Lila bent the wire slightly. It was still in good condition. Probably because it had been kept out of sunlight all this time.

She took the daylight grow lamps out of the Vault and wedged them into the rock crevices on the left and right walls. Four lamps in total.
She connected them to the wiring and flipped the switch. Nothing happened.
Her heart sank. Had the renovation of the old house cut this wire?
She hurried back to the cellar and felt along the wall until she found the plug.
It wasn't plugged in.

She pushed the plug into the socket and peered nervously back into the cave.
Light!
Yes. The daylight lamps were all glowing.
The entire chicken run was brightly lit with a warm yellow light very similar to natural daylight.

No need to struggle with rewiring. Lila's heart pounded with excitement.

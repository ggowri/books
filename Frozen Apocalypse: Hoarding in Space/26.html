Chapter 25

That was because the portable solar power station was something he had spent a great deal of time and effort inventing himself.

He liked tinkering with inventions whenever he had nothing else to do, and he firmly believed the quality was no worse than products made by major brands.

Unfortunately, no one appreciated it. He no longer had the funds to continue development either.

All he could do now was sell some of the finished products he had made before, raise a bit of money, and use it to develop something new.

"Oh, I see. Does it work well?" Lila asked.

She cared about the quality. What would be the point of buying a useless bit of kit and taking it home?

"Don't worry about that. It works brilliantly. Leave it in the sun for a day, and it can power a household for three days. If I weren't short of money lately, I wouldn't be willing to sell it at all."

As he spoke, he began demonstrating the portable solar power station for Lila.

He showed her how it charged, how the power display worked, and how long the remaining battery could last.

Lila was tempted.

"How much?"

"$5,000."

That was the stallholder's ideal price. Heaven knew how reluctant he was to part with his precious invention.

"$3,000."

Lila could not be bothered to haggle back and forth, so she cut straight to the point.

"Now hang on, young lady. This is worth $5,000. How can you offer only $3,000?"

The stallholder was unhappy. To him, it felt like an insult to his treasure.

Lila was just about to say he could sell it or not, but her attention was drawn to a large lamp beside it.

"What kind of lamp is that?"

It looked a little like something that simulated sunlight. She needed photosynthesis for growing vegetables, and she had not bought daylight grow lamps yet.

"This is a lamp that simulates natural sunlight. The light is gentle and very close to real sunshine…"

As he explained, the stallholder plugged the daylight grow lamp into the portable solar power station.

The lamp lit up at once. The warm yellow light was bright and full, and it looked as if the little vegetables would probably like it very much.

Before the stallholder could finish his introduction, Lila interrupted him.

"How much?"

"Well, I have two sets of these. $1,000 per set, so $2,000 for both."

"I'll take the daylight grow lamps and the portable solar power station together. $5,000 for the lot. Will that do? If not, I'll leave it."

Lila named her price decisively. The stallholder had no chance of overpowering her once she started bargaining.

The thought of getting $5,000 all at once made him hesitate. With that money, he could buy new materials and build an even better portable solar power station.

In the end, he gritted his teeth and agreed.

On 21 June, at three in the afternoon, Edward, Maggie and the others hurried back home.

Only when Edward successfully entered the passcode and the door unlocked did he finally let out a breath.

For some reason, while they had been in Seabourne, he had kept worrying that they might not be able to get back into the flat.

Now that he was standing on the carpet at home, that sense of unease finally disappeared.

"Dad, I told you that you were overthinking it," Annabel said, utterly speechless.

If her father had not rushed them back as if their lives depended on it, she would have stayed in Seabourne for much longer.

"It feels better to be home early," Edward said.

He carefully opened his suitcase and took out the piece of jade inside.

This jade had cost him $50,000. The quality was excellent. All that remained was to wait for Lila to reimburse him.

Maggie said nothing. She simply piled all the things she and Annabel had bought onto the sofa and tried them on one by one.

She was thrilled.

Every single item looked good. She just had not yet worked out how to bring it up and make Lila pay for all of them.

"Don't any of you think something is off with Lila?" Edward asked after admiring his jade for a while. Once again, the topic returned to Lila.

"What could be off? We watched that girl grow up. She's as slow as they come. What schemes could she possibly have? Once her fixed deposit matures, we'll find a way to deal with her."

Maggie's eyes were full of malice.

They were not only after the flat. They also had their eyes on Lila's money.

When Lila's parents died, Victor Clarke, her father's lawyer friend, had put all of Lila's money into fixed deposits. The timing was exact. She would only be able to withdraw it in the year she graduated from university.

Maggie's family had hated Victor for years.

Fortunately, the fixed deposits were finally about to mature.

At that moment, Lila was lying on the large hotel bed, watching the surveillance footage on her phone.

Hearing Maggie covet her savings, Lila gave a cold snort.

That family was still waiting in anticipation.

She had already withdrawn the money, and by now, she had spent most of it.

Seeing that it was almost time for dinner, Lila felt hungry again.

After confirming that Maggie's family would not be going out, Lila chose another buffet she liked.

This time, it was a relatively upmarket barbecue buffet. The selection was broad, complete, and included plenty of premium ingredients.

Naturally, she had to order the most expensive items first.

M9 Australian Wagyu. Lila ordered twenty plates straight away.

She wore Bluetooth earphones, and the surveillance footage from home played on her phone screen.

As she listened to Maggie's family loudly plotting against her, her hands never stopped moving.

The Australian Wagyu was grilled until fragrant and tender. She ate while stockpiling more into the Vault.

The grilled meat she stored in the Vault was already dipped in sauce and seasoning. When she wanted to eat it in future, she would not even need to prepare a dipping sauce.

She grilled forty plates of Wagyu, then ten plates of ox tongue, followed by twenty plates of various seafood.

The servers at this restaurant were just as stunned as the staff at the previous buffet.

They had never seen a young woman who could eat like this.

They had clearly watched her put each bite of meat into her mouth, yet she never seemed to get full.

After a hearty meal, the storage racking in the Vault had ten more plates of grilled meat and ten plates of ready-made dishes.

On the way back to the hotel, Lila passed the night market and stopped to stockpile a few portions of barbecue, several bowls of fermented tofu, beef noodles and other snacks.

At this stage, if she saw something, she stored it.

For the next two days, Lila did nothing else. She focused entirely on stockpiling petrol.

In those two days, she filled the remaining two hundred jerrycans.

The following day was also the handover date for the Lower Vale Mansion.

Lila held the renovation checklist she had written at the start and checked the rooms item by item.

From beginning to end, everything met her standards.

Even the heated hearth bench had been fired dry by the workers. It was now hard, solid and sturdy.

No wonder this crew usually handled projects for the wealthy. Their craftsmanship really was excellent.

Lila happily paid the final balance she had set aside earlier.

The site manager handed her a brand-new key.

The locks on the outer gate and inside the house had all been replaced with the newest, highest-quality ones. The manager had included them free of charge.

After saying goodbye to the manager, Lila could not help walking around the newly renovated mansion once more by herself.

From the outside, apart from the higher perimeter wall and the covered courtyard, it did not look much different from any other farmhouse.

But once inside the courtyard, the difference was unmistakable.

The large iron gate at the entrance was extremely thick. The courtyard roof was both sturdy and attractive. The walls of the house were thick and well insulated.

Inside, the heated hearth bench had been beautifully repaired. Its sides were inlaid with irregular stone bricks, giving it a distinctive charm.

Of course, the most striking feature inside was the inglenook fireplace.

It looked exactly like the fireplaces she had seen in textbooks when she was a child.

The fireplace was large enough to warm the entire east parlour.

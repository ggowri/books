Chapter 92

"It's sticky sweet corn!" the young soldier blurted out excitedly. "That's exactly what it smells like when it's been simmering long enough!"

Nobody answered.

A whole cluster of soldiers simply stood there staring at the distant plume of smoke, breathing in the sweet fragrance drifting through the frozen air.

One of the cooks stepped outside in an extreme-cold protective suit to dump some rubbish and immediately found himself surrounded.

"Hey, Frank," one of the veteran soldiers called out with a grin. "Any chance we've got corn in storage? Maybe we can have some tonight?"

The cook rolled his eyes.

"With the state of our cooking pot? We'd probably make it taste like dishwater."

Laughter broke out.

The men bent back over their work.

Still, the wind kept carrying that scent.

Sometimes strong.

Sometimes faint.

One moment it felt as though the aroma was right beneath their noses. The next, it seemed like a distant memory floating in from another life.

One of the younger soldiers had only enlisted shortly before the Long Freeze.

Unlike the others, he wasn't laughing anymore.

He stood motionless, staring at the farmhouse halfway up the mountain.

His eyes had gone soft.

He was thinking about home.

Thinking about autumn evenings when his mother would boil corn by the kitchen range.

The firelight would dance across her face, flickering gold and orange.

He wondered where she was now.

Whether she was even alive.

Frank noticed.

He patted the young soldier's shoulder.

Said nothing.

Then quietly headed back to the field kitchen.

Up on the mountainside, Lila decided the corn was finally done.

She lifted the lid.

A wave of rich sweetness rushed out instantly.

The aroma was so intense it almost made her dizzy.

Picking up an ear, she pressed a fingernail into one of the kernels near the tip.

The kernel burst open immediately, releasing soft, sticky golden flesh.

Perfect.

She set that ear aside to cool on a plate and transferred the rest into a large basin.

Thirty ears of corn had gone into this batch.

She kept one for herself and stored the remaining twenty-nine inside the Vault.

The fire in the kitchen range was still burning.

Without wasting time, she loaded another thirty ears into the iron pot.

More sweet sticky corn.

The lid went back on.

That batch could take care of itself for a while.

Carrying her plate, Lila headed back to the east parlour with Snowy and Patches trotting after her.

Once the corn had cooled slightly, she peeled back the outer husk.

Steam curled upward.

The dogs were already dancing impatiently on the floor.

Lila laughed.

"Alright, alright."

She blew on the corn until it wasn't quite so hot.

Then she snapped it in half.

The larger portion was hers.

The smaller half she broke again into two pieces and dropped one onto each dog's plate.

The pair lunged forward immediately.

Corn wasn't exactly easy to eat.

After a couple of bites, the cob rolled away.

Snowy quickly figured out a solution.

He dropped onto his belly, pinned the corn between his paws, and started chewing again.

Problem solved.

Patches wasn't nearly as clever.

He spent the next minute chasing his corn around the plate.

Lila nearly laughed herself silly watching him.

Then she took a bite of her own.

Sweet.

Tender.

Fragrant.

Absolutely perfect.

The batch was a complete success.

For the rest of the day, she barely did anything else.

Three batches of sweet sticky corn.

Two batches of fruit corn.

And after following an online recipe, one batch of buttered milk corn.

Every time a pot finished cooking, she carefully ladled the leftover liquid into storage containers and tucked them away in the Vault.

The sweet corn broth would make excellent hot drinks later.

By the time she reached the buttered milk corn, the soldiers down in the valley were reaching their limit.

"My God, how can corn smell this good?"

"I'm losing my mind."

"I'm eating an extra bowl of dinner tonight."

Of course, that was just wishful thinking.

Food rations were strictly controlled during the Long Freeze.

Extra servings weren't exactly an option.

Meanwhile, Frank quietly rummaged through his belongings and pulled out two precious tins of sweet corn.

He stared at them for a long moment.

Then carried them over to the field kitchen.

Once the rest of dinner was prepared, he set a large pot on the stove and filled it halfway with water.

When it began to boil, he opened both tins and dumped everything in.

Corn.

Liquid.

All of it.

The canned corn was already cooked and perfectly clean, so there was no need to do anything else.

Next came a thin slurry made from cornmeal and cold water.

He poured it in slowly while stirring continuously.

This was the secret.

Too thin and it wouldn't feel like soup.

Too thick and it would become paste.

Frank knew exactly where the balance was.

He cracked two eggs into a bowl.

Beat them.

Then slowly drizzled them into the pot.

Golden ribbons of egg immediately floated to the surface.

Satisfied, he removed the pot from the heat.

A handful of precious sugar went in.

He stirred.

Thought about it.

Then added another half handful.

That should do it.

"Dinner's ready!"

His booming voice echoed through the camp as he smacked a stainless-steel basin twice for attention.

Outside, darkness had begun to settle.

Workers dropped their tools and hurried toward the dining hall.

"Wait a second."

"Does anybody else smell corn in here?"

"You kidding? We've been smelling it all day. Somehow it's even stronger indoors."

The soldiers lined up with empty trays in hand.

Frank grinned.

"One bowl of corn chowder each. No seconds."

The thought of their reactions was already making him smile.

"What?! We get corn too?"

"No way."

"You've got to be kidding me."

"This is exactly what soldiers deserve after busting their backs all day."

The younger troops buzzed excitedly as they stood on tiptoe trying to peer into the pot.

Soon everyone had a steaming bowl.

It wasn't the same as biting into fresh corn on the cob, but the sweet, fragrant soup hit the spot.

For a few precious minutes, nobody thought about hunger.

Captain Walker arrived for dinner as well.

Frank immediately filled a generous bowl for him.

"Corn chowder?" Walker asked, surprised.

"This wasn't part of the allocated supplies."

Frank scratched the back of his head.

"Heh. My personal stash."

"The youngsters were going on and on about corn all day, so I figured I'd help them scratch the itch."

Walker laughed.

"That's just like you."

He took a sip.

Sweet.

Warm.

Comforting.

It had been a very long time since he'd tasted anything like that.

After dark, temperatures outside became unbearable.

The soldiers slept inside the completed section of the shelter.

Walker returned to his quarters.

A few minutes later, he picked up a tin of beef and headed toward Frank's room.

Frank opened the door.

Without a word, Walker shoved the can into his hands.

"Captain, I can't take this..."

Frank immediately recognized it as beef.

One of the most valuable food items left.

He tried to hand it back.

Walker raised a finger to his lips.

Then firmly pushed the can back into Frank's hands.

"That's an order."

Before Frank could protest again, Walker turned and walked away.

Standing alone in the doorway, Frank stared at the can for a long moment.

A smile crept across his face.

Then he carefully tucked it back into his pack.

...

The refugee convoy had been walking for another full day.

At last, Mrs. Harris led the exhausted group into the outskirts of Ashton.

She came to a stop.

"We're done for today."

She looked ahead into the growing darkness.

"If we keep going, we won't find anywhere suitable to spend the night."

The suburbs were filled with scattered farmhouses and single-storey homes.

After a brief search, they selected the largest farmhouse nearby.

No lights.

No signs of life.

The place appeared abandoned.

One by one, weary figures dragged their sleds through the gate and into the yard.

Then into the house.

The firewood outside had long since been used up.

Inside lay two frozen bodies.

Mrs. Harris let out a quiet sigh.

"I'm sorry for the intrusion."

Together with two others, she carefully carried the dead outside before the group settled in for the night.

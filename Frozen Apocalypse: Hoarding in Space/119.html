Chapter 118

Sophie, Leo, and even the driver exchanged surprised glances.

Ethan rarely smiled.

Outside of meetings or official business, he almost never chatted with anyone.

Yet here he was, carrying on an easy conversation with this mysterious young woman.

Before long, silence settled over the cab.

The heater kept the interior pleasantly warm.

The gentle swaying of the lorry soon made Lila drowsy.

Wrapped inside her thick, insulated clothing, she leaned against the window and quietly drifted off to sleep.

The others weren't much different.

Once they reached the city, everyone would be spending hours searching buildings.

Any chance to rest was worth taking.

About fifty minutes later, the lorry rolled to a stop.

The movement inside woke everyone up, including Lila.

Ethan climbed out first.

Once the entire team had gathered, he picked up a loudspeaker.

"Listen up."

"Your search area is limited to this district."

"You may work alone or with partners."

"You have two hours."

"Everyone must return to the lorries when time is up."

"Captain, only two hours?"

Callum sounded disappointed.

Leo had worked hard to secure him a place on this mission.

Two hours hardly felt like enough.

"We'll regroup here."

"Warm up, eat lunch, and recover."

"We'll head out for another two hour search this afternoon."

That had been Ethan's plan all along.

The crowd quickly dispersed in every direction.

Sophie had intended to search alone from the beginning.

But when she noticed Lila heading off by herself, she almost called out to ask if she'd like company.

The words never came.

Lila was already walking away.

They had only just met.

Of course she wouldn't trust a stranger.

Insisting on tagging along would only seem rude.

With that thought, Sophie lightly squeezed the knife hidden inside her sleeve before heading toward another building on her own.

Lila watched everyone disappear into the nearest apartment blocks.

Instead of following them, she deliberately chose a building farther away.

The fewer people nearby, the better.

If she had an entire building to herself, she could freely move everything valuable into the Vault.

Ethan had chosen a relatively isolated residential complex.

The buildings were tall, each roof protected by lightning rods.

They had escaped the devastating lightning storm almost entirely.

As she stepped through the entrance, Lila found herself silently approving of Ethan's planning.

He really was thorough.

He must have scouted this area before bringing everyone here.

The buildings had survived intact.

That meant the supplies inside would be much better preserved.

And because the neighbourhood sat well away from the city centre, it clearly hadn't been thoroughly searched yet.

Lila decided to work floor by floor.

The ground level contained nothing but the lift lobby.

She ignored it and climbed the stairs to the first residential floor.

The apartment door was locked.

She knocked firmly.

"Anyone home?"

"If nobody answers, I'm breaking the lock."

She called three separate times.

Silence.

Putting on a little performance, she reached into her backpack and pulled out a heavy sledgehammer.

One swing.

The lock held.

Two swings.

Still intact.

Closing her eyes, she put every bit of strength she had into a third blow.

With a loud crack, the old metal lock finally gave way.

Gripping the hammer tightly, she stepped cautiously inside.

The apartment was perfectly still.

Everything remained almost exactly as it had before the Collapse.

If not for the freezing temperatures, it could easily have passed for an ordinary home.

The furnishings, however, were quite basic.

Most likely a rental property.

Lila carefully checked every room.

No people.

No bodies.

Apparently the owner had been away when the Long Freeze began.

Or perhaps the tenants had left early to return to their families.

The old fashioned metal lock had been extremely sturdy.

No one else had managed to force their way inside before.

She had struck gold.

Excited, Lila headed straight into the kitchen.

The refrigerator came first.

Everything inside the fresh food compartment had frozen solid.

Five eggs.

Several cans of beer.

Some vegetables.

A few pieces of fruit.

The produce had frozen beyond saving.

The eggs and canned beer, however, were still perfectly usable.

Into the Vault they went.

The freezer compartment was the real jackpot.

Power had failed only after the Long Freeze arrived.

Everything inside had remained frozen ever since.

Nothing had spoiled.

She crouched down and began sorting through it.

Three large packs of pork and ribs.

Five bags of frozen wontons.

Two bags of frozen shrimp.

Three packs of shrimp dumplings.

Every unopened package disappeared into the Vault.

They could all be eaten later without any problem.

One half used package of frozen meat remained.

Lila placed that separately on the kitchen counter.

She could turn that over to the refuge.

If they refused it, she'd simply feed it to Snowy and Patches.

The refrigerator was empty now.

Next came the cupboards.

Five packets of river snail rice noodles.

Eight packs of instant noodles.

Several bundles of dried noodles.

Everything remained unopened.

She stored all of it in the Vault.

She wasn't short of food.

Still, more was always better.

Any seasonings that hadn't burst from freezing temperatures were packed together with the half used meat inside a cloth bag.

Those would all be handed over to the refuge.

The kitchen appliances consisted of nothing more than a rice cooker and an electric frying pan.

Both were old, greasy, and well worn.

She had no interest in keeping either.

Into the donation bag they went.

Leaving the kitchen behind, she searched the rest of the apartment.

The bedroom furniture was badly worn.

The mattress wasn't comfortable enough.

Not worth taking.

The wardrobe, coffee table, and dining table weren't her style either.

Anything made of solid wood went into the Vault for future firewood.

Plastic and glass furniture she ignored completely.

She quickly searched every drawer.

Nothing particularly valuable turned up.

Not wanting to waste time, she left and climbed another flight of stairs.

Considering how upscale this residential complex appeared, she couldn't understand why the previous apartment had been furnished so cheaply.

Still wondering about it, she reached the next floor.

The apartment door there remained closed.

A faint smell of smoke lingered outside.

She carefully removed the peephole and looked inside.

The once white walls had turned black with soot.

A huge pile of ashes lay in the middle of the living room.

The surrounding furniture had long since burned away.

A body lay beside the ashes.

Nothing useful remained.

No food.

No furniture.

She immediately moved on.

The apartment above had already been forced open.

Its front door stood wide open.

Inside, every useful item had vanished.

Lila sighed.

The next floor was no better.

Nor the one after that.

Apartment after apartment had already been searched.

Her first scavenging trip was turning into one disappointment after another.

Finally, she reached the ninth floor.

This door was still firmly locked.

No one had broken into it before.

As always, she knocked first.

Receiving no reply, she swung the sledgehammer twice.

The lock shattered.

The moment the door opened, Lila stopped in surprise.

The apartment was stunning.

Quiet luxury filled every room.

Large windows flooded the space with natural light despite the grey skies outside.

Every piece of furniture looked handcrafted from premium materials.

Even at a glance, everything here was worth a fortune.

She still followed her routine.

Every room first.

Only after confirming no one was inside did she begin searching for supplies.

Lila immediately took back every complaint she'd made about her luck.

This wasn't bad luck at all.

This was hitting the jackpot.

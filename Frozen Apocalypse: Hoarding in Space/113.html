Chapter 112

"Dad! I'm your daughter! You seriously don't believe me?"

Anna's shrill scream echoed through the room.

Edward frowned, turned his back on her, and refused to answer.

A loudspeaker crackled throughout the refuge.

"Firewood collection teams, assemble. Firewood collection teams, assemble."

It was the first organised trip into the mountains since everyone had arrived at the refuge.

Edward stood and began pulling on his winter clothes.

"Get up. You're coming with me."

"My leg's still injured! I can't go!"

Anna was furious.

If her mother had been here, there was no way she'd have let her climb a mountain.

Edward didn't waste another word.

He grabbed the back of Anna's coat, yanked her straight off the heated bed, and dumped her onto the floor.

"Get dressed and move."

"If you don't, I'll drag you outside exactly as you are."

Which meant wearing nothing but the thin padded jacket underneath.

The sudden burst of violence startled Anna.

With her mother gone, she no longer dared push Edward too far.

Pouting miserably, she slowly began layering on one padded coat after another.

"Hurry up."

"If you don't want to freeze tonight, move."

Watching everyone gather outside, Edward grew impatient.

He kicked the back of Anna's leg twice.

She gasped in pain and finally sped up.

Father and daughter left together.

Edward kept a tight grip on Anna's wrist while she stumbled after him, clearly miserable.

By the time they reached the dining hall, everyone else had already assembled.

A quick count put the group at well over four hundred people.

Fortunately, the refuge was surrounded by mountains covered in forest.

Otherwise there wouldn't have been nearly enough wood for so many people.

Since it was the first collection trip, nearly everyone who could work had shown up.

Callum was there.

Sophie was there.

Lauren was there.

Sophie's Legal Officer position paid well, but she still wanted to train her body while building up her own supply of firewood.

Jobs weren't guaranteed forever.

She trusted preparation more than luck.

Callum had his own reasons.

He still hadn't figured out another profitable business inside the refuge, so earning Survival Credits through physical labour was the best option for now.

Lauren's situation wasn't much different.

She had a daughter to raise.

She needed every credit she could earn.

Fortunately, Emma had been left in the dining hall under Mrs. Harris's supervision, so Lauren didn't have to worry about her safety.

An icy wind swept across the snowfields as the teams set off.

Everyone wore homemade insulated gloves and carried axes or woodcutting blades.

Under the supervisors' direction, they spread out toward different mountains surrounding the refuge.

By pure bad luck, Anna and Sophie ended up in the same group again.

Thankfully, Anna was too busy feeling sorry for herself to even notice Sophie.

Since this was the first collection day, the nearby slopes were covered with dead trees killed by the endless freeze.

After about ten minutes of walking, everyone reached the work area.

The stronger workers chopped down standing trees with axes.

Those with less strength simply gathered fallen branches scattered across the snow.

Powerful winds had battered the mountains ever since the Long Freeze began.

Frozen limbs snapped easily, leaving plenty of firewood lying everywhere.

For now, collecting wood was surprisingly easy.

Sophie had no interest in wasting energy chopping trees.

She focused entirely on gathering fallen branches.

Each piece was broken into lengths of roughly one metre.

Any side branches sticking out were snapped off as well.

The straighter the bundle, the more wood she could carry home.

She knew this easy supply wouldn't last.

Sooner or later, the nearby forests would be stripped clean and collecting firewood would become much harder.

She intended to stockpile as much as possible while it was still effortless.

Later, once the easy wood was gone, she simply wouldn't bother coming back.

She worked quickly.

Within minutes she'd assembled a bundle nearly as thick as a barrel.

Using the method she'd practised at home the night before, she tied everything tightly together with rope before adding two shoulder loops.

She swung the bundle onto her back in one smooth motion.

Her hands were already aching from the cold.

She ignored it.

A little pain was nothing compared to gathering firewood this easily.

Without stopping, she bent down and started assembling a second bundle.

Meanwhile, Edward practically dragged Anna up the mountain.

The cold hit her like a hammer.

Even standing still was unbearable.

Her padded gloves offered almost no protection against the brutal temperatures.

She shoved both hands into her pockets.

That was the only way she could tolerate the cold.

"Hands out."

"Get to work."

Edward had barely started collecting branches before seeing Anna standing there doing nothing.

His temper exploded.

He grabbed her right arm and yanked it out of her pocket.

Already freezing and unsteady on the icy ground, Anna lost her balance immediately and crashed into the snow.

She had never suffered like this before.

When they'd travelled from the Underground Car Park to the refuge, her mother had practically supported her every step of the way.

Now they expected her to gather firewood?

The very idea felt outrageous.

Anna finally snapped.

She burst into loud, dramatic sobs.

"Dad!"

"How could you do this to me?"

Her crying rang through nearly half the forest.

Back at the farmhouse where she was recovering, Maggie suddenly sat bolt upright.

"What is it, sweetheart?"

The old woman jumped at the sudden movement.

"I think..."

"I think I just heard my daughter crying."

Worry flooded Maggie's face.

The old woman said nothing.

She gently helped Maggie lie back down and tucked the quilt more securely around her.

"For goodness' sake, Edward."

"Tell your girl to keep it down."

"Gathering wood is miserable enough without listening to that."

"I'm going to lose my mind before I freeze."

"Next time just leave her at home."

The workers closest to Edward began complaining one after another.

Everyone was already freezing.

Nobody wanted their ears assaulted on top of everything else.

Edward's face burned with embarrassment.

"Shut your mouth."

"Pick up the wood."

"If you don't collect any today, don't expect to sleep on a heated bed tonight."

He was so disgusted that, for a moment, he wanted to shove his mud and snow covered boot straight into Anna's mouth.

Still sniffling, Anna didn't dare argue anymore.

She slowly climbed back to her feet.

Her worn grey padded coat was covered in snow.

With tears still running down her face, she bent over and began gathering sticks one by one.

Most people could finish a full bundle in ten minutes.

Anna managed only a small armful.

No one could remain outside for too long.

To avoid frostbite, the supervisors limited the work session to twenty minutes.

"Time's up."

"Head back."

The team leader shouted as he hoisted three bundles of firewood onto his back and started down the mountain.

Everyone followed.

Edward carried two heavy bundles himself.

Then he glanced at the pathetic handful of branches Anna was holding.

His face twisted with frustration.

"You're completely useless."

He didn't even wait for her.

Turning around, he marched downhill alone.

At least they were finally going back.

Terrified of being left behind, Anna hugged her tiny bundle of firewood and hurried after the others.

Sophie walked near the rear of the group.

She had worked efficiently.

In just twenty minutes, she'd collected four full bundles.

That was enough fuel for four separate fires.

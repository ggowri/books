Chapter 11

Lila had not planned to buy emergency ration biscuits.

With all the proper food she had stocked up on, there was no chance she would ever choose those dry, joyless things unless she absolutely had to.

Then again, after thinking it over, she decided the Collapse deserved at least a little respect. So she bought one box, purely for form's sake.

Drinks.

Three hundred pounds of assorted loose-leaf tea. Three hundred tubs of milk tea powder in various flavours. One hundred tubs of honey citron tea concentrate. One hundred tubs of passion fruit and grapefruit tea concentrate. One hundred boxes of milk. One hundred tubs of instant coffee.

Loose-leaf tea would be a true barter currency after the Collapse.

All the food supplies, tea and drinks together came to almost one hundred and sixty thousand dollars.

Lila stared at the bill in disbelief, checked it again, and realised she had somehow spent an enormous amount while trying to be careful.

The most expensive items were the cured meats, sausages and tea.

It hurt, but it was worth it.

Whether it became food for the rest of her life or barter currency after the Collapse, it was worth every penny.

That brought her first round of online food shopping to an end. By the time she finished, Lila was so exhausted she could almost have fainted. It had been a thoroughly satisfying spree.

She had not bought the especially heavy items, such as liquid drinks and bulky foods, online.

The postage would have been ridiculous, and the savings would barely matter. Another day, she could make a special trip to a wholesale supermarket and buy the lot in one go.

When she woke up, it was already eleven in the morning.

Lila picked up her phone and saw more messages from Annabel.

If Annabel had not promised to reimburse everything, Lila might almost have mistaken this constant attention for genuine affection.

"Lila, we're going fishing out at sea today. It's five hundred per net. How many nets do you think I should cast?"

There it was again. Another little test to see how much Lila was willing to pay.

"Cast as many as you like. The important thing is to have fun and eat your fill."

Lila had gone on a wild spending spree the day before and was in a good mood, so even her reply to Annabel sounded cheerful.

On the other end, Annabel was delighted when she saw the message.

There was not a trace of impatience in Lila's tone. That meant she could carry on spending freely. Lila, the convenient fool, would reimburse her anyway.

Annabel had grown up with Lila and knew her character well. Once Lila promised something, she always followed through.

By the time Lila got out of bed, it was already noon.

She first drove to the Lower Vale Mansion and looked around. Seeing the workers busy on site, she felt reassured and left.

Today was for stockpiling grain, salt and sugar.

She drove to Ashton's grain-and-oil wholesalers.

Aside from grain and cooking oil, the market also sold drinks by the case and all kinds of seasonings in bulk.

Lila checked the prices. Rice and flour here really were cheaper than in the supermarket.

Five hundred sacks of rice, totalling twelve and a half tonnes. Two hundred sacks of plain flour, totalling five tonnes. After the discount, the total came to seventy-five thousand dollars.

She also ordered two thousand bags of salt, two thousand pounds in total, and one thousand bags of white sugar, one thousand pounds in total.

Because the quantity was so large, the market offered delivery.

Lila asked them to load everything first and then follow her Luton van later.

While they were doing that, she went to buy oil, vinegar and other basics.

She bought three hundred five-litre bottles of corn oil and soya oil, plus three hundred bottles of vinegar.

The drinks and alcohol here were good value too, so she bought those as well.

Thirty cases of baijiu, fifty cases of assorted sweet liqueurs, one hundred cases of cola and lemonade, fifty cases of almond milk drink, one hundred cases of assorted fruit juice, and fifty cases of canned alcopops in various flavours.

Lila felt the milk she had ordered online was not enough, so she added another one hundred boxes of milk.

Whatever else happened, milk was nutritious. There was no harm in stocking up.

The grain wholesalers also sold all kinds of grains and pulses.

Lila thought it over and decided to buy some.

What if one day she suddenly fancied sweet glutinous rice balls? What if she wanted sticky rice parcels? What if she wanted a warm mixed-grain porridge during the depths of winter?

In the end, she bought five hundred pounds of glutinous rice, two hundred pounds of glutinous rice flour, one hundred pounds of red beans, two hundred pounds of mung beans, one hundred pounds of pearl barley, five hundred pounds of soya beans and five hundred pounds of millet.

Altogether, that came to fifty-three thousand eight hundred dollars.

The later purchases had cost plenty too, so the sellers still agreed to help with delivery.

Lila put the awkward drinks and alcohol into her own Luton van. Everything else would be transported by the wholesalers' lorries.

She drove her van at the front, with three large lorries following behind her.

When they arrived at Lockridge Storage Unit, Lila directed the drivers to move all the grain and supplies into the unit.

Only after everything had been carried inside did she settle the remaining balance.

She watched the lorries gradually drive away until they disappeared from sight. Then she hurried into the storage unit. It was already more than half full, and there was no way her own van could fit inside now.

Lila quickly pulled down the roller shutter behind her. With one sweeping motion, she took all the grain into the Vault.

The moment it disappeared, dizziness slammed into her. Her vision darkened in waves.

No. Her vanload of drinks and alcohol was still outside.

Forcing herself through the discomfort, she shook her head and waited until she was a little clearer. Then she opened the shutter, drove her van into the unit, and used the last of her strength to drag the roller shutter all the way down.

Her vision went black.

She lost consciousness.

When she woke again, more than an hour had passed.

She had fainted while slumped against the roller shutter, and now her entire right thigh was numb.

Lila thumped her leg a few times and used the wall to pull herself to her feet.

"Good grief. So that can happen."

She was shaken by the side effect.

No wonder she had felt so drained last time. If she took too many things into the Vault all at once, her body simply could not cope.

Once the tingling in her right leg faded, Lila climbed into the driver's seat of the van.

She rested her head on the steering wheel, sank into her awareness, and began organising the storage space.

All the grain she had bought was packed in sacks.

Using her will, Lila stacked all of it neatly against the wall.

Once that was done, she got out, climbed into the back of the van, and began transferring the drinks and alcohol into the Vault bit by bit.

She was extremely careful, terrified that one careless move would make her faint again.

This time, it took nearly half an hour before all the drinks and alcohol were safely inside the Vault.

She returned to the driver's seat, closed her eyes, and sorted everything again.

The drinks and alcohol were even easier to arrange. She only needed to stack the cases against the wall, and they looked neater than the bagged grain.

By the time she finished, Lila was utterly drained.

She had put far too much into the Vault today. Her body really could not bear it.

The sky was almost dark. There was still some time before the Collapse. There was no need to rush everything in one day.

Lila drove home. She did not even eat dinner. She lay down on the bed and fell asleep at once.

On 28 May, Lila woke early. She picked up her phone and saw that it was only half past six in the morning.

She could not remember what time she had fallen asleep the night before. All she knew was that her stomach felt painfully empty.

She ordered takeaway on her phone. While waiting for it to arrive, she checked the delivery updates to see which purchases had not yet been dispatched.

This time, she had not used the same delivery address as before. Instead, she had given an address in a town near the storage unit.

The closer the deliveries were to Lockridge, the easier it would be for her to move everything.

After food, the most important thing to stockpile was medicine.

After the Collapse, medical supplies would be worth more than gold.

The food she had bought online was almost here, so now was the perfect time to order medicine.

Lila opened several shopping apps and spread the orders across different sellers.

First came the basics: alcohol, saline solution and iodine, all in large quantities.

Then came prescription medicines, anti-inflammatory and antibacterial drugs, and all kinds of antibiotics.

After the Collapse, those would be life-saving.

Chapter 18

Sunwell was best known for its vegetable polytunnels. Its sweet potatoes, maize and watermelons were famous too.

Because the soil in the town was unusual, the fruit and sweet potatoes grown there were sweeter and more fragrant than those from other places.

Unfortunately, it was only early June. The sweet potatoes, maize and watermelons were not ripe yet. They would not be ready until the end of the month.

Lila did not mind. She could simply make another trip then.

Right now, the urgent task was to stockpile vegetables.

Lila parked by the roadside. When a passer-by came along, she politely stopped them.

"Hello. Which polytunnels in town have the best vegetables?"

"South Hill Polytunnels and Poplar Hollow Polytunnels. Good vegetables and cheap too," the passer-by answered without hesitation.

"Thank you. Thanks very much."

After thanking them, Lila drove towards South Hill Polytunnels.

South Hill was closer, which made it more convenient.

Along the slope were rows of long, wide polytunnels.

At the end of each one was a small building. Most of the people who managed the polytunnels lived there.

There was no one walking about outside, so Lila went to the small building beside the first polytunnel.

Someone was inside. An old man sat there listening to the radio while fanning himself.

"Hello. I'd like to buy some vegetables."

Lila greeted him.

"What?"

The radio was too loud, and he had not heard her clearly.

Lila repeated herself.

This time, the old man understood. "Oh, right. I'll take you to the owner."

He was only the gatekeeper. The owner was someone else.

After walking past three polytunnels, Lila was already slightly out of breath before she finally saw a farmhouse courtyard hidden among the rows.

"Mrs. Green, someone's here to buy vegetables!"

The old man called out. When someone inside answered, he walked away again, still fanning himself.

A middle-aged woman came out of the farmhouse courtyard. She was wearing an apron and sleeve covers, as if she had been cooking.

"You're the one buying vegetables? What do you want? I'll take you over."

As she spoke, Mrs. Green walked towards Lila.

"What vegetables do you have here? I want pretty much everything."

Lila could not explain it all in a sentence.

"Cucumbers, tomatoes, aubergines, peppers, celery, pak choi, little cabbages, crown daisy, all sorts."

Even Mrs. Green could not list them all at once. She grew a lot of vegetables in her polytunnels.

"How do you price them?" Lila asked.

"Depends how you buy. If you're buying small amounts, leafy greens are two and a half dollars per pound. Other vegetables are two dollars per pound. Wholesale is cheaper."

Mrs. Green could not quite tell whether the amount Lila wanted counted as retail or wholesale.

"Wholesale, then. I'll take half of every vegetable you have here."

Lila could have bought everything, but she did not want to go too far. She had to leave people some way to make a living.

"Everything?"

Mrs. Green was so shocked that her voice rose sharply.

"Yes. Our company is holding a large event, so we need to prepare a lot of vegetables. Could you work out a price for me?"

Lila made up an explanation on the spot. At this point, anything would do.

"Well, if it's a big event, then you really would need plenty..."

Mrs. Green was already struggling to separate reality from the sudden surprise. Her words came out a little jumbled.

"If you truly want that much, I'll give it all to you at one and a half dollars per pound."

Business had not been good this year, and there were not many buyers coming in for large quantities.

If she could sell half her stock in one go at one and a half dollars per pound with less waste, it would be a good deal for her.

"Fine. I drove here in a van. Please help me load it. I'll pay by the vanload. Is that all right?"

Lila did not plan to ask them to deliver. She had plenty of time now and could move the vegetables one load at a time herself.

"No problem. I'll call a few people over to help you load."

Mrs. Green thought this young woman was wonderfully straightforward. She had never done such an easy, satisfying deal in all her life.

Lila was arranged in the courtyard with a cup of tea while Mrs. Green's youngest son ran off to call people.

Before long, he brought back two men and one woman.

From the way they spoke, one man was Mrs. Green's husband, while the other couple were her older brother and his wife.

"This young woman wants that much produce?"

Mrs. Green's husband could not help asking his wife again in surprise.

Mrs. Green nodded while counting sacks with her head down.

"You look young, but you must be doing quite well for yourself."

Mrs. Green's sister-in-law came over to chat with Lila.

In her mind, procurement for a company was a job with plenty of opportunities. For such a young woman to have a role like that, she must have some ability.

"It's just an ordinary job. Exhausting, really."

Lila forced out a bitter smile, playing the part convincingly.

"Are you seeing anyone? My friend's son graduated this year and works at a state-owned company in the city. He's still single..."

The older woman became more and more enthusiastic, talking without pause as she tried to introduce a potential match.

"Sorry, I'm already married. My child is in Year Three at primary school."

Lila cut her off and lied smoothly.

"What? You don't look it..."

The older woman was even more surprised. This girl looked barely in her early twenties.

"All right, all right. Don't bother her. Come on, let's go and harvest the vegetables."

Mrs. Green handed everyone a pair of gloves and led Lila towards the first polytunnel.

The first two polytunnels were full of aubergines and tomatoes.

Perfect.

These were the most common vegetables. They were good stewed, good stir-fried, good in almost anything.

"You're sure you want half of all of it?"

Mrs. Green confirmed again, afraid Lila might change her mind after the vegetables had been picked.

"I'm sure. Go ahead and pick them. I'm more worried there won't be enough."

Lila nodded firmly.

Mrs. Green's brother and his wife stayed behind to pick tomatoes and aubergines, while Mrs. Green and her husband took Lila to the next polytunnel.

The third, fourth and fifth polytunnels were full of leafy greens.

The sixth and seventh had cucumbers, cabbages, green beans and more. Almost every common vegetable could be found there.

Lila followed Mrs. Green through every polytunnel in one go.

Once it was confirmed that Lila wanted half of all the vegetables, Mrs. Green and her husband joined the work as well.

Lila took a small stool and sat in the middle of a polytunnel, watching everyone work and listening to them chat.

"How long are we going to be picking for? Honestly, couldn't you be a bit more generous? Stop working the two of us half to death every day and spend a bit of money hiring someone."

Mrs. Green's sister-in-law complained, but her hands moved faster than anyone's. Sweat rolled down her forehead in streams.

"I'm only selling these vegetables for one and a half dollars per pound. If I hire someone, what profit will I have left? Work hard, and I'll stew some meat for you tonight."

Mrs. Green did not mind her talking like that. She only laughed and urged her to keep going.

The moment the older woman heard there would be meat that evening, she worked with renewed energy.

Her husband looked at her eager expression and could not help complaining to Mrs. Green's husband.

"Look at your wife. She's working mine like a pack mule."

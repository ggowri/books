Chapter 12

The things Lila bought the most of were ibuprofen, amoxicillin, azithromycin, cephalosporins and levofloxacin.

Once a bacterial infection set in, without antibiotics or anti-inflammatory medicine, the human body would struggle to survive.

After that came medicine for every illness she could think of. Cold remedies, frostbite cream, erythromycin ointment, painkillers, fever reducers, anti-diarrhoea tablets, laxatives, antihistamines, medicine for sprains and bruises, and fast-acting heart rescue tablets.

The heart tablets were necessary. If something went wrong and her heart suddenly felt off, they might just be enough to snatch her little life back from the edge.

Lila also ordered a few health supplements, mainly vitamins.

For the medical kit, she bought a blood pressure monitor, gauze, bandages, plasters, haemostatic cotton, sterile gloves, small medical scissors and a few little surgical blades.

Lila had studied the humanities. She knew next to nothing about medicine.

But the moment she imagined one day having to save herself by picking up a scalpel and cutting open her own skin, she thought it sounded rather impressive.

All the medicine together cost eighty thousand dollars.

She really had bought a lot. Kept inside the Vault, none of it would expire.

It could save her life, and it could also be traded as something precious. Completely worth it.

Lila checked her balance. She still had one million four hundred and fifty thousand dollars left.

One point eight three million, minus ten thousand for the van and other costs, one hundred and eighty thousand for online food, one hundred and ten thousand for offline food, and eighty thousand for medicine.

There was still plenty of money.

Looking over her Collapse preparation list, the things she absolutely still had to stockpile were fuel such as smokeless coal, diesel, household essentials, meat, vegetables, fruit and self-defence gear.

Those were non-negotiable. The other bits and pieces could wait.

In that case, she would start with coal.

When she was little, before her family had moved into a flat, they had lived in a single-storey house and used a small coal stove. Every winter, every household had to buy coal.

Lila still remembered the address.

It was on the west side of Ashton, at a coal yard that was not especially large. They specialised in wholesale and retail coal.

In winter, they would send someone round with a loudspeaker mounted on a vehicle, driving through the streets.

When people heard it, they would come out, stop the vehicle, and place an order. A few days later, the coal would be delivered straight to their door.

Some people went to the coal yard in person to choose the coal themselves.

How much coal did one family usually order back then?

Lila could not quite remember. Perhaps two or three tonnes.

Thinking it over, Lila drove her car to Westgate Coal Yard.

The yard gates were wide open, and by coincidence, a large lorry was just driving out.

The lorry was noisy. Every rumble sent little bits of coal dust spilling from the gaps in the back.

She had no idea where anyone could need this much coal in summer.

"Don't stand there. Move aside!"

The gatekeeper's voice rang out and gave Lila a fright.

She had already been standing by the roadside, but she quickly moved a little farther over.

Once the lorry had passed, Lila walked up to the security hut.

"I'd like to buy some coal," she called through the window.

The gatekeeper glanced at her. A fair-skinned young woman, soft-looking and clearly not very old. She probably was not buying coal for a factory.

"How much do you want?"

In his mind, he had already decided she would only need two or three tonnes.

Their biggest customers in summer only ordered forty or fifty tonnes.

"Five hundred tonnes," Lila said.

Coal was one of the most important guarantees for surviving the Long Freeze.

Now that the house would also be fitted with a power system connected to heat, she had to stockpile plenty.

Even under the worst-case scenario, with extreme cold all year round, ten tonnes a year meant five hundred tonnes would last fifty years.

And if the world was still frozen after fifty years, she did not need to keep living anyway.

The gatekeeper lowered the cigarette in his hand and stared at her with wide eyes.

"How much did you say?"

"Five hundred tonnes," Lila repeated patiently.

"Well then. I'll contact Gregory Shaw for you."

After making sure he had not misheard, the gatekeeper picked up his old basic mobile.

A short while later, a plump middle-aged man came hurrying out of the yard.

He had run so quickly that sweat had broken out at his temples.

Under the blazing sun, his forehead and cheeks shone with oil.

"Who is it? Who wants to order five hundred tonnes of coal?"

This was a major order. Gregory had to handle it properly.

"That would be me. What grades of coal do you have?"

Lila wanted better-quality coal, preferably the sort that did not smoke much. That way, the house would not fill with fumes during the Long Freeze.

"Come inside with me. I'll talk you through it."

Gregory wiped the sweat from his temple, still a little breathless.

"All right."

Lila nodded and followed him in.

They entered a simple brick office. Gregory handed Lila a price list covered in stiff plastic.

"Have a look. It lists all the types and prices."

As he spoke, he switched on the fan.

The air blew with a steady whirr, and Lila finally felt a trace of coolness.

The price list recorded the coal prices from top to bottom. Lila frowned as she read.

Eight hundred to one thousand one hundred and fifty dollars. The prices varied, and so did the quality.

Seeing her frown, Gregory thought she found it too expensive.

"These are the usual prices. Since you're buying so much, I can give you a discount," he said quickly.

Lila nodded. "Which ones are smokeless?"

"These two at the top. Neither of them gives off smoke, and they burn well."

Gregory pointed at the two most expensive options.

"I'll take the best one. What's the lowest you can do for five hundred tonnes?"

Lila did not hesitate. She still had plenty of money, and coal was something she needed to buy properly. During the Long Freeze, she would be relying on it for heat.

"Since you want so much, I can do one thousand one hundred per tonne," Gregory said after some thought.

"One thousand per tonne. If that works, I'll pay the deposit now."

Lila gave an extremely low counteroffer.

Gregory's eyes widened at once, and he waved his hands repeatedly.

"No, no, that won't do. You can't bargain like that. I'd lose money at one thousand per tonne. This is top-grade stock at one thousand one hundred and fifty. You can't compare it with the cheaper stuff below."

"That really is the lowest I can go."

Lila's budget was limited now. She had to save wherever she could, but the quality still had to be good.

Good coal produced better heat. Only then would the house stay warmer.

Gregory took out his phone and did some calculations. After thinking for a long while, he gritted his teeth and agreed.

"Fine. One thousand it is."

The quantity was huge. Even with a thin profit margin, he would still earn something.

He truly could not bear to let a five-hundred-tonne order slip away.

"I want the best grade, exactly this one. If you mix in anything else, I'll be able to tell."

Lila deliberately put on a stern expression.

"Oh, come on now. You can trust me. One hundred per cent genuine."

Gregory gave his assurance.

"I won't delay payment. Once you finish the delivery, I'll settle the full amount in one go. But you'll need to deliver it to the place I give you, pack the coal into sacks, and stack it inside the storage unit when you arrive."

Lila stated her requirements cleanly.

Gregory hesitated for a moment, then nodded readily.

"Done. No problem. I'll cover the cost of the sacks and the labour."

A customer who paid promptly mattered more than anything.

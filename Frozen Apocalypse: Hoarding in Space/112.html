Chapter 111

The people living in the farmhouse were an elderly couple in their sixties.

They had already settled in for the night.

The old woman sat beside a candle, mending clothes, when a faint cry drifted in from outside.

"Did you hear that?"

She nudged her husband, who was snoring soundly.

"Huh? Hear what?"

He sat up groggily without even opening his eyes.

"Please... help me... I'm so cold... please..."

Maggie's voice was growing weaker by the second, barely audible through the walls.

"Good grief. Someone's outside."

The old man was instantly awake.

He peered toward the courtyard, but the solid iron gate blocked his view completely.

"What do we do? Should we help?"

He looked to his wife.

She hesitated.

"Do you think they'll freeze to death before morning?"

She honestly did not want to open the door.

"What do you think? Forget morning. At this rate, they'll be dead any minute now."

The old man scratched his head, torn.

"We've only got a few sacks of rice left."

Their kitchen rice bin was already empty.

Only a handful of bags remained in the cellar, and nobody knew how long the Long Freeze would last.

The couple sat there in silence.

Then there was a dull thud outside.

The cries stopped.

It sounded as though the woman had collapsed.

"Oh, for heaven's sake. Let's bring her inside first."

Ironically, the person who had been resisting the idea was now the first to make up her mind.

She hurriedly layered on several thick winter coats, pulled a fur hat over her head, and headed for the door.

The old man sighed.

Then he quickly bundled himself up and followed.

The moment they opened the gate, the frozen woman toppled stiffly through the doorway.

"Oh dear!"

The old woman jumped in surprise.

"Give me a hand!"

Together they carried Maggie back inside.

The house was cold by normal standards, but compared with outside, it felt almost warm.

There was no stove, but the heated bed was still radiating plenty of warmth.

They had saved their firewood all day and burned extra before going to sleep, so it was still hot.

Working together, they lifted Maggie onto the heated bed.

She still clung to the last thread of consciousness.

"Please... save me..."

"Don't fall asleep, sweetheart."

"Whatever you do, don't close your eyes."

To the old woman, Maggie was about the same age her own daughter would have been.

The couple had lost their daughter years ago.

Seeing another woman frozen nearly to death made their hearts ache.

Following the old remedies everyone in the village knew, they did everything they could to keep her alive.

The old man climbed down from the bed platform and cooked a pot of thick rice porridge in the cast iron pot.

The old woman spooned it into Maggie's mouth one careful sip at a time.

Even with her eyes closed, Maggie swallowed instinctively.

The warm porridge tasted wonderful.

She had spent months giving every decent meal to Anna while going hungry herself.

She could not even remember the last time she'd eaten something this hot.

After a long night of effort, the couple finally pulled her back from the brink.

The old woman removed Maggie's outer coat, helped her lie comfortably against the head of the heated bed, and tucked her beneath a thick quilt.

"There you go."

"Get some sleep now."

"You'll feel much better tomorrow."

Only after seeing that Maggie would survive did she finally relax.

She and her husband squeezed together at the opposite end of the heated bed beneath a small blanket.

Before long, they were asleep as well.

The next morning, pale daylight filtered through the window.

Maggie slowly opened her eyes.

Her eyelids hurt.

She instinctively reached up to rub them, only to discover both hands wrapped in bandages.

"You're awake, sweetheart."

"How are you feeling?"

The old woman had cooked another pot of porridge, despite barely having enough rice herself.

The moment she heard movement inside, she hurried back from the kitchen with the wooden spoon still in her hand.

"I'm awake..."

"Where am I?"

Maggie's memories were hazy.

She had been frozen so badly the night before that everything afterward felt like a blur.

"You collapsed outside our gate last night."

"We carried you inside."

"Did you come from that refuge down below?"

The old woman took a ceramic bowl from the cupboard while checking on the porridge.

"Yes."

"I... got turned around in the dark last night."

"I'll head back later."

Maggie carefully avoided mentioning her punishment.

The last thing she wanted was to be thrown out again.

As long as she stayed here until evening, she'd be allowed back into the refuge.

"This weather's deadly."

"How on earth did you get lost?"

"You poor thing."

"I've made some porridge. Have a little."

Every time the old woman looked at Maggie, she saw the daughter she'd buried years ago.

Her heart softened even more.

Maggie thanked her without hesitation and settled deeper beneath the quilt.

She needed her strength back before making the journey.

Her arms and legs burned with stabbing pain.

Large, clear blisters covered the skin.

The frostbite from last night was obvious.

The old woman placed a small table on the heated bed.

Soon three bowls of porridge and a small plate of pickles were set out.

"Breakfast's ready."

She called to her husband outside before turning back to Maggie.

"Eat up, sweetheart."

"The more you eat, the faster you'll heal."

"Thank you."

Maggie lowered her head and picked up the bowl.

The porridge was piping hot.

She barely noticed.

She gulped it down as though she had not eaten in weeks.

The old woman stared in surprise.

Then she understood.

Anyone who'd been starving that long would eat exactly the same way.

Within moments Maggie emptied the bowl.

Her eyes drifted silently toward the old woman's half finished serving.

With a helpless smile, the old woman pushed her own bowl across the table.

Maggie accepted it.

"Thank you."

The words were barely understandable before she finished that bowl too.

Only then did she quietly ask,

"Last night... did anyone from the refuge come looking for me?"

"I don't think so."

"We were taking care of you most of the night."

"I didn't hear anyone outside."

The old woman thought back carefully.

No.

She was fairly certain no one had come.

Maggie sighed softly.

My daughter...

There's no way Anna wouldn't come looking for me.

Unless...

Unless she'd gotten dragged into this somehow.

The very daughter she kept worrying about was still curled up on the heated bed inside the refuge.

"It's freezing."

"It's so cold."

"Dad, hurry up and heat the bed."

Anna was shivering uncontrollably.

Even though she usually ignored Edward, she finally had no choice but to speak.

"With what firewood?"

"We burned the last of it last night."

"This morning you're going into the mountains with everyone else to collect more."

Edward was cold too.

The last traces of warmth had already faded from the heated bed.

He had no desire to go outside himself.

There was no point raising a daughter if she couldn't pull her own weight.

"What?"

"You expect me to go?"

"Dad, don't you know Captain Walker already picked me for the Legal Officer position?"

"I have to stay home and wait for the announcement."

Anna looked genuinely offended.

Someone like her wasn't supposed to haul firewood alongside ordinary refugees.

What if the icy wind ruined her beautiful face?

Who was supposed to take care of this family then?

"You'll go whether you like it or not."

"What announcement?"

"The Legal Officer job's already been filled."

"Stop living in a fantasy."

Edward was convinced there was something seriously wrong with his daughter's thinking.

She spent every day building castles in the air.

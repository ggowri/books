Chapter 44

Could it be that the only person in this family who had ever cared about her was the outsider she had been scheming against all along?

Maggie shook her head hard.

What was she thinking?

Lila was not related to her by blood. What real affection could there be?

She looked up at Anna sitting on the sofa. Perhaps Anna was still young and did not know how to care for people yet. Deep down, though, she still depended on Maggie as her mother.

The cabbage was frozen solid, and there was not even a proper kitchen knife left at home.

Maggie picked up her phone and asked in the residents' group chat if anyone had a spare kitchen knife.

No one replied.

During the Long Freeze, with police stretched thin, residents would only become more guarded. Almost no one would dare open their door to lend things out.

"Mum! Why are you asking to borrow things in the group again? It's so embarrassing."

Anna truly could not stand Maggie. One minute she was borrowing quilts, the next she was borrowing a knife. She was making them look completely pathetic.

"There's nothing at home. How am I supposed to cook?" Maggie said anxiously.

"It's all Lila's fault. If that awful woman hadn't stolen everything from the flat, we wouldn't be struggling like this."

The moment Anna mentioned it, she was furious all over again.

Just then, a very unusual message appeared in the residents' group chat.

"Hello, neighbours.

The weather is so cold that no one can go out to stock up, and the government supplies aren't enough, are they?

I've contacted a food wholesaler and secured priority access to food supplies. Anyone who wants to buy can add their order in the group. I'll deliver tomorrow. Orders over ten thousand will be delivered to your door. Orders under ten thousand must be collected downstairs.

Eggs, one hundred each.

Spring onions, one hundred each.

cabbage, three hundred each.

Potatoes, two hundred each.

Dried noodles, five hundred a pack.

Instant noodles, four hundred a pack.

Bread, three hundred a loaf, variety random.

Corn, two hundred a cob.

Sausages, one hundred each.

Full box of instant noodles, thirty packs, ten thousand.

Leafy greens, five hundred a bundle, variety random.

Salt, five hundred a bag.

All food is frozen stock. If you want anything, add your order in the group."

The person who sent the group-buy message was none other than Callum, the same man who had sold reindeer-hide boots to Lila and Leo.

His usual work was buying and selling bits and pieces, making a profit from the price difference.

From the moment the temperature had started dropping, he had known his chance had come.

Especially today. The government supplies were meagre, and all major supermarkets and shops had closed. It was the perfect opportunity to make serious money.

What was the easiest money to earn?

Survival money, of course.

An old classmate of his was in the vegetable and food wholesale business and still had several warehouses of stock.

The two of them hit it off at once and launched an outrageously expensive group buy.

When the residents heard there was a group buy in the chat, they rushed to place orders.

But the moment they saw the price list, their enthusiasm collapsed.

Some of the more difficult residents even felt Callum was taking advantage of the disaster.

"Have you lost your mind? One hundred for an egg and four hundred for a pack of instant noodles? You're mad."

"Isn't this profiting from people's desperation?"

"Forget it. Not even a dog would buy this. Making money from a disaster? Have you no conscience?"

The angry and foolish were busy scolding him. The clever ones had already contacted Callum privately.

People like Leo and Sophie.

Sophie was thinking along much the same lines as Leo. She also believed there was something deeply wrong with this temperature plunge. For all they knew, this really was the Long Freeze.

If there was any chance to stockpile supplies, she had to stock up as much as possible.

She was prepared to convert every bit of savings she had into cold-weather gear and food.

After all, money could be earned again. A life could not.

Unfortunately, she did not have a great deal of money, so she could only do her best to buy as much food as she could.

"Fifty eggs, five boxes of instant noodles, fifty loaves of bread, fifty packs of dried noodles, fifty sausages, ten cabbages, two bags of salt and ten spring onions."

There was no meat available, so eggs would have to do for protein. Fortunately, frozen eggs were still edible.

As soon as Sophie's message came through, Callum replied immediately.

"Of course, of course. Send me your address and I'll come by tomorrow."

He was about to make another huge profit. Callum was delighted.

As for what people were saying in the group chat, he did not care at all. What good did harmless scolding do? Making money was what mattered.

As soon as he had sorted Sophie's order, Leo messaged him too.

Leo had more savings, and his order was twice Sophie's.

Leo made a point of telling him that his supplies had to be traded at night. He did not want the neighbours finding out.

Callum understood perfectly. At such a sensitive time, letting others know there was food stored at home would only invite trouble.

Callum would be responsible for delivering the goods, mainly because he had enough cold-weather gear and did not suffer as badly outdoors as everyone else.

Of course, he was not taking that risk for nothing. He could pocket half the price difference.

So when he contacted his old classmate to place the wholesale order, Callum ordered the same quantity as Leo.

That portion was for himself.

He was not stupid. Business was business, but supplies still had to be stockpiled.

After sending over the three large orders first, Callum began organising the orders in the group chat.

People complained, but the ones who needed to buy did not delay.

They all knew exactly how precious supplies had become.

The order chain in the group chat grew longer and longer. Some people ordered a lot, some only a little.

"Look at the group chat! There's a group buy! Let's buy more!"

Anna was the first to see the notice and hurriedly called Edward over.

Edward looked at the price list. The more he read, the tighter his frown became.

"This is far too expensive..."

Edward was reluctant to buy.

Maggie was even more unwilling.

"One box of instant noodles costs ten thousand. That's more than your father earns in a month. Even in the Long Freeze, we can't throw money around like this."

Daniel said nothing.

He was staying at Anna's home, so the cost of food should naturally be covered by her family. There was no need for him to express an opinion.

Edward thought for a long while.

"How much is left in the bank account? Spend half of it on supplies. There are so many mouths to feed. If we don't stock up on something, how will there be enough? Just buy noodles and salt. Nothing else."

After saying that, Edward glanced at Daniel.

Seeing Daniel still sitting there without a word, he silently clenched his fist.

"There are forty thousand left in the account. There used to be more, but the trip to Seabourne and that meal at the upmarket French restaurant cost far too much..."

Maggie looked at the balance on her phone, her heart aching.

Edward's salary had never been high. Maggie was a housewife, and Anna usually spent a great deal. In truth, they had not saved much at all.

But because Lila had quite a bit of money, they had always assumed that Lila's savings would sooner or later become theirs. They had never felt there was anything wrong with not saving.

Now, Lila had suddenly broken free of their control, and the extreme cold had arrived. They were truly trapped.

"Daniel," Edward said through gritted teeth at last, "you and Anna aren't married yet, so strictly speaking, you're not part of our family. If we're buying supplies, you'll need to pay for your own share. Otherwise, we have no obligation to keep you here."

The moment Anna heard that, she became anxious.

"Dad! What are you saying? Daniel is staying with us. How can you ask him to pay for himself?"

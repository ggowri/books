Chapter 54

Lila woke once again in her warm bed.

The duvet was soft and cosy, and she had taken a hot shower the night before, so even her skin felt smooth and clean.

She stretched lazily, then fumbled sleepily for the phone beside her pillow.

The signal was poor today. Only two bars.

She opened the online forums and gave the phone a firm shake. The loading circle spun for ages before a few posts finally appeared.

"Water and power cut off! The Long Freeze has truly arrived!"

"Everyone, get to the Basement to shelter from the cold!"

"Help, my phone's only got ten per cent battery left!"

Lila rubbed her eyes, still half asleep.

So the water and power had gone.

She opened the surveillance app, but it showed that the signal had been cut off.

It seemed Maggie's family had lost both electricity and internet too. She wondered whether they had moved into the Basement by now.

"Never mind. Not being able to see them is better for my peace of mind."

Lila gave up on watching that family any further.

She got out of bed and shivered. She had not noticed it under the duvet, but the moment she stepped out, the room felt cool.

The indoor temperature was seventeen degrees.

It had suddenly become much colder.

She looked at the thermometer outside the window.

Minus seventy-two.

That was right. In her previous life, the coldest temperature she had experienced had also been minus seventy-two. This day was the true beginning of the extreme cold.

Lila had no choice but to pick up her old-fashioned padded jacket and pull it on. The first thing she did was light the inglenook fireplace.

She had realised there was no need to set aside space in the real world to store firewood or coal.

She could simply take it straight out of the Vault and burn it. It saved space, and it was clean and convenient.

Once the fire in the east parlour was burning, she went to the kitchen as she had done over the past few days, lighting the kitchen range to heat the hearth bench.

The moment she left the east parlour, it was even colder outside. The temperature was probably around ten degrees.

She wrapped the padded jacket tighter around herself, hurried into the kitchen, put on cotton gloves and began feeding wood into the range.

By the time the kitchen range was properly hot, the tip of Lila's nose had gone chilly.

She needed something warm for breakfast.

Lila placed a frying pan on the small kitchen hob and added a generous layer of oil.

She cracked two eggs into a small bowl and beat them together. When the oil was hot, she poured all the golden egg mixture into the pan.

With a sharp sizzle, the egg puffed up quickly and turned into a golden omelette.

Lila stirred it lightly a few times with chopsticks, breaking the whole piece into smaller chunks.

Once it looked about right, she tipped the scrambled egg back into the small bowl she had used earlier.

There was still some oil left in the pan. Lila chopped up a tomato and threw it in to fry.

After a few stirs, she sprinkled in a thin layer of salt.

The salted tomato soon released its juices. Then she poured the cooked egg back in.

She added two small bowls of water to the pan, followed by the right amount of seasoning.

When the water came to the boil, Lila dropped in a small handful of noodles.

The noodles rolled through the red-gold broth, and the tempting scent of tomato and egg curled around her nose.

Once the noodles were cooked and had absorbed the rich red soup, Lila poured everything into a soup bowl and scattered a handful of bright green spring onion over the top.

It looked beautiful.

Red, yellow and green mingled together, a feast for the eyes as much as for the appetite.

She carefully carried the tomato and egg noodles to the dining table in the east parlour.

Using a spoon, she scooped up a mouthful of thick soup, blew on it gently a few times, then drank it.

Slightly sweet, lightly savoury, thick and full-flavoured.

Delicious.

The freshly cooked noodles were still a little too hot, so she had to eat slowly.

She found the drama she had not finished the day before and ate while watching it.

With the steaming noodles in her stomach, warmth spread through her whole body, driving away the chill from lighting the fire that morning.

After breakfast, Lila quickly washed the pots, bowls and utensils.

Once that was done, she fed the chicks and the dogs.

The chicks had grown a lot recently. Six of the weaker ones had died along the way, but the remaining nine were lively and healthy.

They were now about half the size of her palm. The box they lived in was starting to feel cramped, so it was time to think about settling them properly.

Lila pulled on thick quilted trousers and a padded jacket, picked up a pair of pliers and went to the west room.

From the Vault, she took out the materials she needed: large sheets of plastic, wire mesh for fencing, a wooden henhouse, nails and wooden poles.

The wooden poles were ones she had picked out from the scrap pile at the timber yard when collecting wood offcuts. She had thought they might be useful one day, so she had stored them separately in the Vault.

She nailed the whole sheet of plastic across the walls and floor of the area where the chickens would be kept.

That way, when she no longer kept chickens there in the future, she could simply pull the whole sheet of plastic away, and the floor and walls would not be dirtied.

With the preparation done, it was time for the actual work.

First, she placed the wooden henhouse on the floor in a corner. Then she fenced off the area around the henhouse and the rest of the west room floor with wire mesh.

She left only one narrow passage beside the heated hearth bench.

The wire mesh was not fixed directly to the floor, so Lila nailed wooden poles along the bottom where the mesh met the ground, then added several upright supports to form a frame. That would stop the fencing from shifting about.

Once the general frame was in place, Lila took the deep-litter bedding out of the Vault.

She spread the material across the entire floor area and inside the henhouse.

This would absorb odours as much as possible and reduce any smell in the room.

The henhouse was finished.

Lila placed a thermometer in the west room. It showed minus eight degrees.

Yes, this room was far from the kitchen range and far from the east parlour, so it was the coldest room.

But that did not matter. The west room had a heated hearth bench too. Since she had stored plenty of smokeless coal, she did not mind heating one more bench.

And so Lila began her first attempt at heating the hearth bench in the west room.

The hearth benches and flues in both the east and west rooms had been modified by the German retrofit crew. They did not smoke back into the room and were very easy to use.

After burning firewood for a while, she added a full firebox of coal and blocked the stove opening with a sheet of iron. Then she could leave the west range alone.

Coal burned for a long time, especially when one opening was blocked to reduce airflow. Left to smoulder like that, it could last for several hours.

If the west room was only being used for animals and plants, heating it once a day would be enough.

By the time Lila had finished all this work, not only was she no longer cold, she was sweating through her clothes.

She went first to the east parlour and drank a large mug of warm tea, then went to the bathroom for a shower.

She washed off the bits of deep-litter bedding clinging to her and the sticky sweat on her skin.

After changing into a fresh set of pyjamas, she returned to the east parlour.

She lay on the sofa, drinking hot tea while reading a book.

Today's book was a Western classic, written in a witty and amusing style. It had its own particular charm.

Before long, Lila was hungry.

She had worked too hard that morning and could not be bothered to cook lunch.

She took a portion of steamed rice rolls from the Vault and ate them slowly. Then she threw the takeaway box into the special rubbish area in the Vault, wiped her mouth and continued reading.

This time, whether it was the carbs making her sleepy or the book itself, she drifted off before long.

When she opened her eyes again, an hour had passed.

Lila went to the west room.

She touched the heated hearth bench. By now, it was already warm. The thermometer showed five degrees above zero.

As expected, heating the hearth bench was very effective.

Chapter 37

Daniel was not stupid.

There was nothing in this flat. The air conditioning could not warm the place, and there was no bed or bedding. He was not staying here to suffer.

Once Daniel left, Edward, Maggie and Annabel simply stared at one another.

"That's right. Why didn't we follow Daniel back to his place just now? His flat has everything!"

Maggie slapped her thigh, furious at herself for being so slow.

"Mum, forget it. His place is eight kilometres from here. By the time we walked there, we'd be frozen solid."

It was not that Annabel had never thought of going to Daniel's place. But if they could not drive, there was no point. Walking there would only get them killed faster.

By now, the temperature outside had already dropped to minus twenty-four.

Annabel and Maggie tore their dresses open and tied the fabric around their legs like makeshift trousers. At least that would stop the wind getting straight through.

The three of them wrapped their padded jackets tightly around themselves, gritted their teeth and left the flat.

Indoors, the walls could block out some of the cold, so it was only around minus ten. The moment they stepped out, they felt the full force of minus twenty-four.

"Mum, it's too cold. I'm not going. I'm not going."

Annabel shivered violently and turned to run back inside.

Edward and Maggie could not bear to let their precious daughter freeze outside. They opened their mouths, but neither of them stopped her.

The couple rushed into the lift and left without looking back.

When they reached the ground floor, they saw quite a few people coming back with bulging bags.

Instant noodles, snacks, padded winter clothes, electric blankets, spirit stoves.

Seeing that other people had managed to buy things, Maggie felt a little steadier.

It clearly was not as dreadful as the Oakwood residents' group chat had made it sound. Plenty could still be bought.

And if she could buy directly from these people, she would not have to go outside and freeze.

A woman in black-framed glasses came towards them, carrying a huge black rucksack on her back and holding a pile of instant noodles and fruit in her arms.

"Excuse me, love. Could we have a word? Sell us a few of your things, would you? We're getting on a bit, and we're not in the best health. It isn't easy for us to go out and buy supplies ourselves..."

Maggie forced a smile as she moved closer, the expression creasing her whole face.

The woman in glasses looked at her warily and hugged her food tighter. Without saying a word, she walked around Maggie and entered the lift.

"Well, what sort of person is that? That's no way to treat your elders. Not even a word before walking off."

Maggie raised her voice before the lift doors closed, making sure the woman in glasses could hear her.

Sophie, who had just come in, heard every word.

She had always been righteous and quick-tempered, so she spoke without hesitation.

"What sort of person? Going out once in this weather could cost someone half their life, and you want her to hand over what she risked herself to get? Age really doesn't stop some people from losing all shame."

With that, she squeezed into the lift just before the doors shut.

The girl in black-framed glasses looked at Sophie gratefully.

"Thank you."

"No problem. Things are different now. You need to be tougher from here on."

Sophie lifted her chin slightly and could not help giving her a warning.

Maggie and Edward were both fuming. Since they clearly could not get ready-made supplies from others, they had no choice but to go out themselves.

The moment they stepped outdoors, the biting cold sliced into their legs like knives.

Cold.

For the two of them, wearing only a single thin layer on their legs, it was even colder.

Without another word, the couple followed the path other residents had trampled through the snow and ran towards the estate entrance.

By the time they reached the gate, there was finally a cleared road to walk on.

The road at the entrance had been cleared by Oakwood Estate Management staff early that morning, after an exhausting effort.

But once the superstore opened, Oakwood Estate Management stopped working. The manager had said that, with the shops open, everyone could go and stock up.

They were ordinary people first, estate staff second.

Everyone had to seize this brief window and grab supplies. In this bitter cold, only enough food could keep them alive.

So only the main road by the estate gate had been cleared. The paths between the buildings were still buried under deep snow.

By the time Edward and Maggie reached the entrance, their legs were almost numb with cold.

Even so, for the sake of the days ahead, neither of them dared slow down. First, they went to the small convenience shop by the estate gate, but its door was shut tight.

Edward knocked hard several times. No one answered.

With no other choice, they hurried towards the nearest superstore.

Halfway there, they saw snowploughs working on the main road.

"They're so slow. They still haven't cleared it. If they'd finished earlier, we could have driven out," Maggie complained bitterly.

In her mind, it was all because these people worked too slowly. Otherwise, she and Edward would not have had to freeze.

"Save your breath. Keep your strength. Walk faster."

The moment Edward opened his mouth, even the flesh on his face hurt from the cold.

By the time they reached the superstore, it was still packed with people. There were more people than shelves.

Fortunately, the heating inside the shop was on. The two half-frozen figures pressed through the crowd, slowly shaking off the chill.

Instant noodles, bread and other quick staples had already been stripped clean. Edward and Maggie could only take what others did not want, the things that were not filling.

But it did not matter. As long as it was edible, they were not going to miss it.

The ground floor sold food. The first floor sold clothing and household goods.

Maggie stayed on the ground floor and grabbed plenty as she moved along, including packets of pickled vegetables, pickled kelp, a handful of lollipops, and even seven packets of dried noodles.

The dried noodles had been kicked under a shelf and spotted by Maggie's sharp eyes.

Edward hugged the wall, found gaps in the crowd and headed straight up to the first floor.

Anything properly warm had already been taken. Electric blankets, down duvets and padded jackets had long since sold out.

There were still plenty of thermal tops and leggings, socks, and hideously old-fashioned bedsheets on the shelves.

Most households already had these things, so people coming out to stock up had not bothered fighting over them.

But Edward's home had none.

Their stripped-bare flat had nothing at all.

Edward yanked more than a dozen sets of thermal tops and leggings from the shelves. He even found three sets of thermal base layers and threw them all into the trolley. Then he grabbed several sets of bedsheets and the only three thin quilts left.

Remembering that they had no pan at home, he casually took a frying pan and a kettle as well.

He wanted to find spirit stove fuel or a spirit stove too, but there was no sign of any.

Judging that he had found enough, he guarded the full trolley and went back down to the ground floor to find Maggie.

When Edward found her, she was crouched in a corner with her arms full of goods.

The moment she saw him coming, she hurriedly stuffed everything into the trolley. Edward guarded it while pushing forward inch by inch through the crowd.

With her hands freed, Maggie set her sights on what other people were carrying. She pulled and snatched, again and again, and somehow managed to grab four packets of instant noodles, three loaves of bread and four more packets of dried noodles.

"Have you got no manners? Those are things other people picked out!"

"Honestly, taking food from a seventy-year-old as well? Have you no conscience?"

The people queuing nearby could no longer stand Maggie's behaviour and began scolding her.

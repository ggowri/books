Chapter 123

"Thanks."

Lila nodded gratefully to Ethan.

After all, getting permission to join the scavenging runs into Ashton meant access to abandoned luxury furniture and countless valuable supplies.

She owed that opportunity to him.

"My pleasure."

Ethan smiled.

"Here's to a successful partnership."

With that, he headed toward the storage warehouse to unload the day's haul.

Lila had spent the entire day outside.

She was absolutely exhausted.

Even so, she had no intention of heading home just yet.

The refuge had only recently been established.

Right now, firewood was plentiful and dirt cheap.

That wouldn't last.

Once the surrounding forests had been picked clean, the price of timber would skyrocket.

She'd earned a healthy pile of Survival Credits today.

Might as well put them to work.

"You heading home?"

Sophie asked, sounding oddly reluctant to say goodbye.

Lila didn't live inside the refuge.

Running into her wasn't exactly easy.

"Not yet."

Lila shook her head.

"I'm going to trade for some firewood first. I'm almost out at home."

The first half was true.

The second was carefully chosen.

No reason to let people think she was living in luxury.

"I'll come with you."

Sophie smiled.

"I was planning to stock up on a few bundles myself."

The two women walked toward the warehouse together, chatting casually.

Neither noticed the pair of eyes following them from behind.

Anna stood in a patched gray winter coat, her face twisted with resentment.

Why?

Why did that woman get to come and go with Ethan?

Laughing with him.

Talking to him like they were close.

Shameless.

Jealousy burned so fiercely inside Anna that it almost hurt.

Soon, Sophie and Lila reached the firewood depot.

Lila could only carry so much.

Ten bundles was about her limit.

She traded twenty Survival Credits for ten bundles.

Sophie bought five.

After saying goodbye, Lila tied all ten bundles together with a length of rope she'd been carrying inside her backpack.

Looping the rope over one shoulder, she prepared to drag the load uphill.

It was far easier than carrying each bundle separately.

"Want me to help you take them home?"

Sophie frowned.

The load looked brutal.

"I'm good."

Lila smiled naturally.

"Get some rest yourself."

It sounded exactly like something she'd say to an ordinary friend.

"All right."

Sophie still looked worried.

"Just be careful."

Lila looked several years younger than she was.

Watching such a small girl drag that much firewood up a mountain felt almost painful.

The moment she left the refuge, Lila leaned her full weight into the rope.

Every step uphill felt like punishment.

Since the Collapse began, she'd spent most of her days living comfortably inside the mansion.

A full day of scavenging had already pushed her physical limits.

Now she had ten bundles of wood dragging behind her.

Her shoulders screamed.

Her legs burned.

She hauled the firewood up the slope one slow step at a time, breathing hard enough that every exhale came out as a cloud of white mist.

Back at the refuge entrance, Anna watched her disappear toward the mountains.

"So that's where the bitch lives."

A cold smile spread across her face.

She remained there until she saw Lila unlock the iron gate and disappear into the courtyard.

Only then did Anna limp back toward her own house.

Her injured leg still hadn't healed.

But once it did...

She'd make that woman pay.

First thing she'd do was rip that mask right off her face.

She wanted to see exactly what kind of seductive little fox had Ethan so wrapped around her finger.

Inside the courtyard, Lila finally stopped pretending.

She let out a long breath.

With a flicker of will, all ten bundles of firewood vanished into the Vault.

Dragging herself inside, she shut the front door behind her.

Warm air wrapped around her immediately.

She had loaded the inglenook fireplace with plenty of smokeless coal before leaving that morning.

The fire had settled into glowing embers, but it was still comfortably warm.

Snowy and Patches heard her come in and immediately came racing over.

They circled her legs, whining nonstop as if demanding to know where she'd disappeared to all day.

Careful not to step on either dog, Lila shuffled over to the wardrobe.

Off came the extreme cold suit.

The reindeer hide boots.

Moments later she had changed into loose lounge clothes and comfortable slippers.

"Oh... that's heaven."

She sighed happily.

Without another thought, she collapsed onto the sofa in the east parlour.

No matter what the outside world had to offer, nothing compared to her own little sanctuary.

Even through all those layers, the cold had seeped into her hands and feet.

The warmth from the fireplace slowly chased it away.

She hadn't even found the energy to eat.

Curled comfortably beside the fire, she closed her eyes.

Barely fifteen minutes later, she was fast asleep.

Meanwhile, Callum and Leo hurried straight home.

Both of them had hidden enough supplies inside their coats that another minute outside risked everything spilling onto the ground.

The moment they stepped inside, the house felt cold and quiet.

Callum placed his stash on the heated bed before crouching beside the kitchen range to light the fire.

"I've got it."

Leo pulled him aside.

"You start the fire every day. Let me do it this time."

Callum wandered over to the iron stove, watching Leo feed wood into the flames.

Suddenly he grinned.

"You know what?"

"We should make wraps."

Leo looked up.

"What?"

"That girl, Lila."

Callum laughed.

"Those wraps she had today have been living rent free in my head ever since."

"I found some white flour today."

"Let's fry up a couple of flatbreads and roll something inside."

"Sounds good."

Leo nodded.

"I managed to keep half a bottle of oil. Perfect excuse to use it."

Truth be told, he'd been craving them too.

Whether they could make anything half as good as Lila's was another matter.

They got to work immediately.

Callum heated a pot of water first.

Then he carefully measured out a precious bowl of flour.

He mixed the dough with warm water from the stove.

Warm water made softer, more elastic flatbreads.

Leo set the half frozen bottle of cooking oil beside the fire until it melted.

While the dough rested, Callum climbed onto a chair and rummaged through the ceiling storage.

Eventually he came down with a packet of pickled vegetables and a tin of luncheon meat.

The meat was too precious to finish in one meal.

He sliced the block in half.

One half became thin slices for dinner.

The other went back into the tin for tomorrow.

Soon two soft white flatbreads came off the pan.

Callum rolled one with pickled vegetables.

The other held slices of luncheon meat.

Honestly...

They looked pretty convincing.

"There."

He handed the larger one to Leo before eagerly taking a huge bite of his own.

Maybe it was simply because it'd been so long since he'd eaten fresh bread.

Maybe hunger made everything taste better.

Either way...

It was delicious.

Still...

Something was missing.

It didn't have that glossy finish.

It lacked that incredible homemade sauce whose smell alone could drive people crazy.

Even so...

It hit the spot.

Callum gave Leo a vigorous thumbs up.

"This is freaking awesome."

Leo laughed.

"Nice work."

"Next time we'll use my luncheon meat."

For the two of them, this qualified as a feast after a full day of scavenging.

Elsewhere, Ethan finally finished dealing with the day's paperwork and supply reports.

He was just about to head home.

"Captain."

Fish hurried over after putting away the last of the recovered supplies.

"Dinner's about to start. You might as well head to the canteen."

Ethan blinked.

He'd been so busy he'd forgotten he was hungry.

"Good point."

The canteen already had a long queue stretching across the room.

As commanding officer, Ethan bypassed the line and entered through the side entrance.

"Captain Walker."

The elderly veteran responsible for the kitchen immediately walked over.

"What can I get you tonight?"

Ethan looked across the serving counter.

There were multigrain rolls.

Soft bread rolls.

Mixed grain pilaf.

Plain rice.

For soup, there were only two small pots.

One held cabbage soup.

The other contained a simple broth with shredded potatoes and radish.

Almost nobody could afford vegetables these days.

The kitchen only cooked a small batch each meal.

Once it sold out, that was it.

The Strategic Reserve Depot No. 7 still contained substantial food reserves.

But with well over a thousand mouths to feed, no one dared waste a single bite.

Chapter 70

Anna was full of resentment, but regaining even temporary freedom was a good thing for her.

Soon it was time for duty. Dirty and dishevelled, Anna shuffled out of the basement.
For the first time in days, she saw somewhere that wasn't the basement.

The bright lights in the central area stung her eyes.
There wasn't much light in the basement. She had spent many days in darkness.

People around her shot Anna strange looks.
Everyone was in the middle of the Collapse, but no one looked as bad as Anna. Her hair was a bird's nest, her face was filthy, and she stank to high heaven.

Anna shrank from their stares, burning with shame. When had she, Anna, ever fallen so low?
It was all that bloody Sophie's fault. And Mrs Harris's too. Thinking of them, Anna quietly clenched her fists.

Mrs Harris came by to hand out expedition down parkas. When she saw the state Anna was in, she frowned deeply.
How could someone so dirty wear the communal clothes?

Mrs Harris still had half a bottle of hot water left in her thermos. She called out to Anna, "You. Come here."
Anna followed Mrs Harris into her basement.

Mrs Harris's basement looked like a cosy bedroom. It had everything you could need.
Compared to the basement where Anna had been locked up for days, the contrast was bitter.

"Here, wash your face."

Mrs Harris poured the remaining half bottle of hot water into a basin.
Anna said nothing. She bent down slowly and touched the warm water, which she hadn't felt in so long.
She washed her face and hands in silence. Then she looked up and saw herself in the mirror. Pale face, messy hair.
But even like this, her pretty features were still visible.

"Do you have a change of clothes? What you're wearing isn't really suitable for going out," Mrs Harris said tactfully.

Anna shook her head. She had no other clothes. What she was wearing was all she had.

Seeing Anna in this state, Mrs Harris felt a bit guilty. If she hadn't locked Anna up in her own basement so harshly, the girl wouldn't have ended up like this.
Mrs Harris rummaged through the wardrobe in her basement and found a small red jumper and a pair of grey cotton trousers. She handed them to Anna.

"Wear these for now. They've been washed."

Anna didn't refuse. She took the clothes, gripping them tightly in her hands.
She changed right there in Mrs Harris's basement, put on the expedition down parka, and went to work.

Anna stood not far from the waste bins. People came one by one to dump their chamber pots. Some of the liquid splashed onto the floor.
She fought back the urge to vomit. She didn't say a word.

Anna was going to pretend to be good. She would use Mrs Harris's pity to make the woman think she had reformed. Then she wouldn't have to be locked back up in that dark, sunless basement.

"Sweetheart, switch with me. I'll empty the bins. You do the cooking." Maggie had spotted Anna standing silently in the distance. Her heart ached for her daughter.

Anna looked up, gave Maggie a flat stare, and said, "No."

Maggie was stunned. It felt like her daughter had suddenly changed.
"Get moving!" Edward bellowed from the doorway of the basement. Maggie didn't have time to think. She quickened her pace and hurried after him.

When everyone's waste bins had been emptied, Anna and another worker dragged the large bins toward the entrance.
Mrs Harris was on the satellite phone.

"A leader is coming? Yes, yes. Wait, let me get a notepad."
"Forty seven years old, male, drinks alcohol..."

Mrs Harris jotted down a list of the leader's preferences. Anna listened carefully, taking in every word.

As soon as they stepped outside the basement door, the air cut into their skin like knives.
The two of them emptied the waste bins and hurried back to the basement.

Mrs Harris was still in a meeting with the estate managers.
"Tomorrow, when we hand out supplies, a leader is coming to inspect the basement conditions for better resource allocation. If we take good care of the leader, maybe we can get more supplies."

Mrs Harris was excited. There was so much they lacked in the basement. If they could perform well and get extra supplies, it would be a huge blessing.

Anna heard this and smiled faintly. She walked over to Mrs Harris. "The bins are empty. Is there anything else you need me to do, Mrs Harris?"

Mrs Harris looked at the well behaved girl and suddenly felt that the punishment had been too harsh.
"No, love. Go and rest."

Anna turned obediently and slowly walked back to her basement.
"Wait, love. You can move freely from now on. Just watch your temper a little."

She knew it!
Anna was genuinely happy. She turned back to Mrs Harris and said, "Thank you, Mrs Harris. I won't be like that again. I promise."

Back in the basement, Edward and Maggie hadn't returned yet.
Anna touched her messy bird's nest hair. She made a bold decision. She was going to wash her hair.

No hot water? Then she would use cold water.
She filled a basin with icy water and took a deep breath.
She plunged her hair into the cold water. The shock gave her a headache and made her dizzy.
Anna gritted her teeth, steadied herself, and kept splashing water over her hair.

When she finally finished washing, her entire scalp was numb with cold.
Anna straightened up and let out a long sigh. Finally clean.

In the small mirror in the basement, she finally saw herself again. Clean face, smooth hair.
Anna was satisfied. Yes. This was how she should look.

She dried her hair until it was damp, then smoothed down her red jumper.
The small jumper Mrs Harris had given her fit snugly, showing off her slim figure.

When meal time came, Anna took her lunchbox and went to the central area to queue for food.
People around her saw Anna. Their eyes flickered with surprise and something like admiration.

Those who didn't know her thought this young woman was surviving the Collapse rather well.
Those who did know her wondered silently how she had transformed so completely in such a short time.

Anna stood up straight, lifted her chin, and felt a secret thrill of pride. A swan is a swan. How could it ever compare to a flock of ordinary ducks?

Anna reached the front of the queue. Edward and Maggie were helping Mrs Harris serve the food.
When Maggie saw Anna, she was frightened. "Anna, go back!" she said urgently.
If Mrs Harris saw Anna out here, there would be trouble.

"Get back there!" Edward heard Maggie's voice and looked up. When he saw Anna, his face darkened with rage. He spat out the words.

Anna looked up at Mrs Harris with a pitiful expression.
"Oh, I told the girl she could come out," Mrs Harris said. "Let her move freely from now on. She's not a bad sort at heart."

Mrs Harris went back to serving the food.
Maggie was both shocked and relieved. Edward's face softened.

Chapter 55

Once the temperature in the west room had risen, Lila moved the nine chicks into the newly built henhouse.

They had only ever lived in a cramped cardboard box before. Now, placed in a much larger space, especially one with the floor covered in deep-litter bedding, they were wildly excited.

They ran about inside, not even caring when they accidentally bumped into the fencing.

The chicks scratched at the bedding with their tiny claws. Whenever they seemed to spot something, their pale yellow beaks dipped down for a peck or two.

Watching them move about so freely, Lila could not help smiling.

It was lovely.

This little world, far beyond anything they had ever known, would be their home from now on.

Since she had finished building the henhouse today, Lila decided not to give herself any more work.

After all, during the Long Freeze, there were only so many jobs she could actually do. Better to spread them out and take things slowly.

She checked the temperature in the west room again. In just this short while, it had already risen to eight degrees.

As long as she kept heating the hearth bench every day, the room temperature should remain above ten degrees.

Chickens were naturally able to withstand low temperatures. Around ten degrees above zero was enough for them to live in.

Lila returned to the east parlour to lounge about, drink tea and read in peace.

Meanwhile, the Basement in the city was anything but peaceful.

There were only five military-grade cold-weather lorries in the whole city, and all of them were now being used to deliver supplies to residents.

At three in the afternoon, a supply lorry arrived at the Basement entrance of Anna's estate.

Soldiers wearing extreme-cold protective suits unloaded three large sacks of food, a huge iron cooking pot, two sacks of firewood and two sacks of smokeless coal at the Basement entrance, then drove away.

The supplies would be managed and distributed by Oakwood Estate Management.

The moment residents in the underground car park saw supplies being brought in, they gathered early.

They stood obediently in place, not daring to move about, afraid that if they did anything wrong, they might not be given their share.

This time, the supplies were not being handed out raw for people to cook themselves.

Instead, Oakwood Estate Management would cook the food and distribute it as communal ration meals.

The three large sacks of food were meant to feed everyone in the Basement for three days.

The food was dragged straight into a large cabinet brought by the estate staff and locked away. The residents waiting nearby looked openly disappointed.

Ruby Harris, the estate manager, carried a megaphone as she moved through the lower ground level.

"From now on, all supplies will be distributed as cooked food. Meals will be given twice a day, at nine in the morning and five in the afternoon. Per-head rationing will take place at the Basement entrance. Please bring your own bowls and utensils."

The announcement played on a loop across the lower ground level to make sure everyone heard it.

Naturally, most people were pleased by the news.

Naked flames were banned in the Basement, so almost no one could cook food themselves. Being able to receive hot food for free was already something to be grateful for.

Anna was lying under her bedding, looking forward to the supply distribution. When she heard raw food had been changed to cooked meals, her expectations rose at once.

"There'll be meat, won't there? There has to be meat!"

Her eyes were full of greed.

Sophie polished the dagger she kept with her until it gleamed. From now on, she would have to go out every day to collect food. Protecting herself was going to matter more than ever.

The loudspeaker outside rang out again, calling everyone to a meeting.

Sophie slipped the dagger into her sleeve, then pulled on her padded coat and went outside.

Everyone gathered near the Basement entrance. Mrs. Harris held the megaphone and got straight to the point.

"Starting tomorrow, everyone will be eating communal ration meals. Cooking will take place on the ground floor. From now on, every resident will take turns going up to help. Don't worry. Anyone assigned to work on the ground floor will wear expedition down parkas provided by Estate Management. We'll follow the list I have here."

"The first group will go up this afternoon to build the stove. Anyone whose name I read out must stay. Everyone else may leave."

The moment Daniel heard his own name, disbelief spread across his face.

He had only just moved into the Basement. Why did he have to go back upstairs already?

"Can I not go?" Daniel forced himself to step forward. "My leg's frostbitten. I can't walk properly."

Mrs. Harris gave him a cold look.

"You don't have to go if you don't want to eat the communal meals."

"Then I'll go," Daniel said at once.

Not eating was out of the question.

That afternoon, a team of eight made up of estate staff and residents put on expedition down parkas and went up to the ground floor.

Using the materials available, they built a stove. The large iron pot fitted neatly on top.

They burned firewood and coal, and before long, the stove dried out. The temperature in the ground-floor lobby also rose quite a bit.

At nine the next morning, the communal ration meal was served on time.

Frozen potatoes and cabbage stew, with rice porridge.

Each person received one ladle of rice porridge and one ladle of stew. No more.

When it was Anna's turn, she looked at the serving, which did not even fill half a bowl, and instantly flared up.

"This is all you're giving me? Look at it. Would even a dog be full on this?"

"Next," Mrs. Harris said expressionlessly, not even bothering to look up.

"What is wrong with you? Estate Management is supposed to serve us. What sort of attitude is this?"

Anna smashed the bowl onto the floor. Thin porridge and stew splashed everywhere, letting off a little steam.

Mrs. Harris looked at the wasted food on the floor, heart aching. The residents queuing around them felt the same.

"What sort of time do you think this is?" Mrs. Harris's eyes reddened with anger. "Supplies are already this tight, and you still dare waste food like this? If you don't want to eat, you can leave it. Someone else could have had one more mouthful."

Only heaven knew how hard it was to stretch that tiny amount of food across everyone present for three whole days. Seeing so much wasted on the ground truly hurt.

"That's right. We'd be grateful for one extra bite. How could you just throw it away?"

"Don't give food to someone like her. Look at that face. From now on, don't let her collect any meals at all!"

"Move. Don't hold us up. We're waiting to eat."

The people waiting in line began attacking Anna with their words.

The Basement was not exactly warm, and with the power off and naked flames banned, who did not want a mouthful of hot food?

"Fine, I won't eat it! Enjoy starving over scraps!"

Anna shouted in fury, then turned and ran back to her family's basement room.

"She's still young. How can you treat her like that?"

Maggie felt desperately sorry for Anna when she saw everyone blaming her.

"You failed to teach your daughter properly, and now you blame us for being harsh? Says plenty about your family too."

During the extreme cold, everyone was irritable. No one had the patience to indulge anyone else. The crowd showed Maggie no mercy at all.

Maggie did not dare say anything more.

She turned to look at her husband standing ahead of her.

Edward did not even look back. He simply held his bowl and waited for his food.

Maggie's eyes reddened, but she did not dare utter another word.

The family silently collected their meals and returned to the basement room.

Anna was still angry inside. In one go, she ate four shortbread biscuits.

When Maggie entered and saw Anna eating, she assumed Anna must have been starving. Her heart ached for her, and she hurriedly handed over the food in her own bowl.

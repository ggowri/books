Chapter 76

Lila carefully put away the iron skewers, reserving them for emergencies. With her breakfast digested, she returned to the east parlour, lifting the kettle from the hob. The water had boiled.

She made herself a latte, mixing milk and coffee, and carefully placed it on the little table beside her giant dog bed. Since creating the bed, it had become her favourite piece of furniture, replacing the sofa entirely.

She searched through her bookshelf, finally finding a practical medical reference, and brought it over to read while nestled in her giant bed. She marked important points with her pen. Living alone meant illness was her greatest threat, so acquiring knowledge about medicine was essential.

Whenever she grew tired, she sipped the rich latte, enjoying the quiet.

The basement was tense. Three residents had recently died from severe colds, untreated and escalating. Fear of death gripped everyone.

"Could it be a virus? Are we all going to die?"

"It's just a cold, but with no treatment, all we can do is wait," someone replied.

Indeed, those who died had avoided medicine, allowing simple colds to worsen fatally.

"Colds can heal on their own, right?" Callum asked over the walkie-talkie to Leo.

"Yes, but only if you're warm and well-fed," Leo answered. Cedric nodded in agreement. After weeks in the dark basement, subsisting on thin rations, health was impossible to maintain.

"Everyone, take precautions, maintain hygiene, minimise illness risks," Mrs Harris instructed through the basement with her megaphone after attending to the three bodies.

Sophie had fallen ill despite her careful habits. Her lungs itched unbearably, leaving her coughing constantly. Fortunately, she had medicine. After hearing of the three deaths, she dared not save it but took her cold and anti-inflammatory medicine and slept heavily.

She woke too late for the evening meal. Dizziness struck, yet she forced herself into her coat, gripping the dagger hidden in her sleeve, carrying a hot water bottle. She intended to fetch some hot water.

Upon arriving, she discovered the supply had just run out. She sighed, weakly returning to her basement.

Callum chatted casually with Leo while carrying hot water bottles. Sophie passed by. Her face was pale, alarming Cedric, though he initially dismissed it; pale faces were common in the basement. Then he recognised her...the young lady who had sheltered him during a previous harsh delivery.

Seeing her empty bottle, he called out, "Do you want some hot water?"

Sophie paused, turning slowly. She knew both men, neither posed a threat.

"For you?" she asked in disbelief.

"Yes," Cedric said, bending to pour most of his own bottle into hers. Exhausted, she accepted it without hesitation, thanked him, and returned to her basement.

The water was still hot, offering comfort to her cold hands and feet. Unsure of its safety, she placed the bottle in the centre of her bedding and crawled in, wrapping herself tightly to share the warmth. She drifted off, coughing intermittently.

At night, piercing cries echoed through the basement. Another death...a husband from a family of three. His wife and child clutched his stiffened body, weeping inconsolably.

Mrs Harris rose in the middle of the night to handle the aftermath. Meanwhile, another elderly woman also passed away. She realised this might be more than a normal influenza. She quickly contacted the authorities via satellite phone.

"Leaders, residents are dying consecutively. This may not be a normal flu. Can you send antiviral medicine?"

A reply came: "Temperatures have dropped. Influenza outbreaks are affecting multiple estates. We will send medicine with the next supply run. Ensure residents take precautions."

Mrs Harris donned her mask, considering her limited options. With no medicine or specialised tools, she set up a quarantine area in the underground car park using plastic sheeting. Anyone showing symptoms was relocated there, while healthy residents remained separated.

She patrolled with her megaphone, instructing repeatedly: "If you have flu symptoms, move to the central quarantine area. If isolated in your basement, remain there."

Maggie's household woke up. Annabel, still sleepy, muttered, "Why are they shouting outside in the middle of the night?"

"They're isolating those with flu. Fortunately, we're all fine," Maggie said, relieved.

"More deaths, the better. Means more food for us," Annabel laughed, considering it a silver lining.

Maggie smiled, content, and soon fell asleep again.

The next day, Mrs Harris handled two more bodies. The basement atmosphere grew heavier, filled with those grieving lost family members. With no medicine yet, residents boiled water themselves to provide some relief.

Sophie had drunk enough hot water and continued her medications, easing her symptoms. Leo and Cedric coordinated shifts to fetch water safely, reducing infection risk.

Meanwhile, Lila sat on her hearth bench, eating spicy strips, and sneezed suddenly. Likely, her time in the cold kitchen yesterday had caught up with her. She quickly poured a cup of hot water and prepared a dose of cold medicine from her kit.

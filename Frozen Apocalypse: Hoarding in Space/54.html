Chapter 53

When the basement door opened, the room inside was empty, but the space was not small.

As soon as the four of them went in, they shut the door tightly behind them.

Ignoring the dust on the floor, they spread the quilts taken from Old Mr. Finch's flat on the ground, then laid their own duvets on top.

Without a word, the four of them climbed into their bedding and waited for their body heat to recover.

Footsteps continued outside in the Basement corridor. People were moving in one after another.

Those who owned a basement room moved into their own. Those who did not found a patch of space in the underground car park, laid down bedding and settled there for the time being.

It was still early. Dawn had not yet broken.

Leo was a light sleeper and was woken by the footsteps in the corridor.

When the sound moved farther away, he quietly opened his door a crack to check what was happening outside.

Only then did he realise the Basement was no longer empty. Large numbers of residents had moved down to the lower ground level.

In fact, he had the feeling that almost everyone had come down.

Leo picked up the walkie-talkie from the wall.

"Callum, Callum. Are you still asleep?"

The walkie-talkie was loud and full of crackling static. It soon woke Callum.

Callum mumbled sleepily, "What's wrong?"

"The neighbours have all come down to the lower ground level. Be careful. Don't let anyone raid your place."

They were all ordinary people, but when people were driven into a corner, they could do extreme things.

You did not have to mean others harm, but you still had to guard against it.

"They've all come down?"

Callum sat bolt upright. Only then did he hear the faint sound of footsteps outside his basement door.

He usually slept in thin pyjamas, and now that he was sitting up, he actually felt a little cold.

Callum reached out and touched the heating pipe beside him.

It was completely cold.

"The heating pipe in my room's gone cold. That was quick. The good days really didn't last long."

He spoke into the walkie-talkie as if leaving a long voice message on a phone.

"The district heating must be out, so everyone's come down here to keep warm. The lights in my room won't turn on either. Looks like the power's gone too."

"Thanks for warning me. I'm not that daft. Cheers, Leo."

Callum understood Leo's good intentions.

"With more people in the Basement, the temperature should rise a bit," Leo said, though even he knew it was a thin sort of comfort.

After a pause, he added, "And if I'm guessing right, since the government has organised everyone to shelter underground, there may be management and distribution later. Whatever happens, survival itself shouldn't be the problem. The quality of life will just drop."

Their conversation ended there. They put down their walkie-talkies and lay back under their bedding to catch a little more sleep.

After all, no matter how chaotic it became outside, they had moved in ahead of time and settled everything. There was nothing for them to worry about for the moment.

Two hours later, at seven in the morning, Oakwood Estate Management staff closed all the doors connecting the lower ground level to the ground floor.

Two hours had passed. Anyone who could come down should already have come down.

To stop people opening and closing the doors repeatedly and letting the Basement lose heat, the estate staff locked them. No one else was allowed to open those doors without permission.

The people who had fled to the lower ground level, especially those from the higher floors, had mostly suffered frostbite on the way down.

Some had frostbitten faces. Some had injured hands. Some had even lost ears to the cold. Families sat huddled together, applying medicine to one another.

Most households had basic first-aid kits these days. Those with frostbite cream used it. Those without made do by disinfecting the wounds with iodine or alcohol.

The underground car park was already full of people.

Each family kept their belongings together, with only a small gap separating one household from the next.

Those who had bought basement rooms when they purchased their flats were luckier. At least they had a separate room to live in.

Seeing that it was about time, the estate staff walked back and forth between the car park and the basement rooms, holding megaphones.

"Please keep an eye on your personal belongings."

"Inside the Basement, reduce unnecessary movement. Wrap yourselves up and stay close together for warmth."

"Going outside is strictly forbidden. Using naked flames for heating is strictly forbidden."

The three announcements played on a loop through the loudspeakers, reaching every person sheltering in the Basement.

Some of those who had been numbed by the cold slowly recovered underground. Their frostbitten hands, damaged ears and wounded skin began to throb and sting.

Groans became the most common background sound in the Basement.

Sophie woke after a night's sleep. Hearing the heavy noise outside, she came awake at once and grabbed the self-defence knife beside her.

It was only when she heard the announcement over the Basement loudspeaker that she understood.

Everyone had been brought down here to shelter.

Still, this was within Sophie's expectations. At this point, the only place that could effectively avoid the extreme cold was the Basement.

She checked the locked basement door once over and made sure no one could open it. Only then did she pull on a thick padded coat.

From her supply box, she took out a packet of bread, broke off half of it, wrapped the remaining half back in its packaging and put it into the box again.

There were too many people in the Basement today, and she had not yet worked out the situation. She could not cook or make anything with a noticeable smell, so it was better to eat something plain.

In her bedding was a mineral water bottle she had filled with hot water the night before, using it as a makeshift hot-water bottle.

Sophie opened the bottle and poured the warm water into a cup.

She chewed the dry bread in her mouth, taking a sip of warm water whenever it became too hard to swallow.

After half a loaf, she no longer felt quite so hungry.

Once she had finished breakfast, Sophie took off the thick outer coat, kept on her fluffy fleece pyjamas and slipped back under the bedding.

She needed to preserve her warmth and strength at all times.

Maggie's family lay under the bedding for several hours before their bodies finally recovered a little.

As the head of the family, Edward had the authority to direct everyone.

According to his arrangement, the supplies were stored at the innermost part of the basement room. Edward slept beside the supplies, then Maggie beside him, then Anna. Daniel was placed closest to the door.

Daniel was unhappy about it, but he did not dare show it.

Only a few hours earlier, he had seen Edward kill someone. He was terrified that if he behaved badly, Edward might strike him down too.

But Daniel did not want to leave Anna's family.

Whether they robbed people or killed them, they had at least shared food with him.

As long as he could do nothing and still receive food and shelter, he did not care who he kept company with.

Once the bedding was laid out, Edward began rummaging through their supplies.

In the end, he took out two shortbread biscuits and broke them into four pieces, giving each person half a biscuit.

With tap water from the basement tap, they barely managed to fill their stomachs.

Edward's basement room was different from the others. It actually had an access point leading down to the sewers.

The opening was rectangular and about the size of a manhole, large enough for one person to climb up or down.

Edward stared at the access point thoughtfully. If they did not have enough food in the future, perhaps they could find a way through the sewers.

"If I'd known it would be like this, I should have used all our savings to buy supplies," Maggie said with a sigh.

She had not eaten enough and was staring regretfully at the box of food.

"It's all your fault. Dad said we should buy everything back then."

Anna gave Maggie an irritated glare.

Maggie had long since grown used to Anna speaking to her like this, so she did not answer.

Chapter 4

At present, Lila had $2.53 million to spend on stockpiling.

She would have loved to be like the main characters in apocalypse novels, reborn with the winning lottery numbers memorised.

Unfortunately, Lila had never had the habit of buying lottery tickets, nor had she ever paid attention to that sort of thing. Making a fortune that way was completely off the table.

She smiled and shook her head, clearing away those daydreams.

The money she had now would have to do.

Back in the car, Lila entered Lower Vale into the satnav.

Her mother's family came from Lower Vale. When Agnes and George were still alive, they had left behind an old country house there.

After they passed away, the property had naturally been transferred into Lila's name.

But for all these years, Lila had been studying, and there were no close relatives left in Lower Vale. She had never gone back.

Following the satnav and the faint scraps of memory from years ago, Lila drove for two hours, winding through mountain roads until she finally reached the entrance of Lower Vale.

At the village entrance stood a wooden sign. The white paint spelling out Lower Vale had long since become patchy and faded.

The area was surrounded by woodland. The entrance to the village was narrow, just wide enough for one car to pass.

Lila let out a long breath.

It was remote. So remote it looked a little like the kind of abandoned mountain village one might see in an American horror film.

Still, remote or not, Lila did not find it frightening.

From the perspective of building a safehouse for the Collapse, the more remote it was, the safer it would be.

Lila drove slowly into the village.

The narrow lane had woodland on one side and fields on the other.

After roughly half a kilometre, houses began to appear one after another.

The village homes were scattered sparsely, each standing far from the next. Even the nearest ones were seven or eight hundred metres apart.

Between the houses were either fields or trees.

The woodland was lush and dense. Some fields were planted with crops, while others were overgrown with weeds.

As Lila drove through, she saw that many of the houses had already been abandoned, making the entire village feel even more thinly populated.

The old house Agnes had left to Lila stood at the very deepest part of the village.

She drove all the way through Lower Vale, passed the final stretch of dense woodland, and at last reached the end of the village.

At the end stood a high mountain.

And Lila's old house was halfway up its slope.

The incline was gentle. Following the old track left behind from years ago, Lila managed to park smoothly in front of the courtyard gate.

The gate was made of iron. It was covered in rust, with a battered old padlock hanging from it.

Lila took a small key from her pocket and pushed it hard into the lock. With a click, it opened.

She slowly pushed the gate open.

Inside, the courtyard was paved with red bricks. Hardy weeds had grown through the gaps.

Lila looked around.

It was just as she remembered.

The courtyard was spacious, at least a hundred square metres.

On the west side stood two small side rooms. One was packed with farm tools, while the other was empty.

The rest of the courtyard was bare.

The walls were built from stone and were very high, about two metres.

Those tall stone walls, as it happened, had a lot to do with Lila.

When she was little and stayed with Agnes and George, she had always liked climbing over the wall at night to catch fireflies on the hillside.

Afraid she would keep sneaking out after dark, her grandparents had raised the walls to two metres. After that, little Lila had never managed to climb out again.

She patted the solid wall.

She had not been back for so long, yet the stonework was still firm and sturdy.

Then she turned to face the house.

It was a single storey stone house. Although the doors and windows were now damaged with age, the walls remained exceptionally strong.

The house door was locked too. Lila searched her pocket again. She had the key for this one as well.

The iron door opened. The movement stirred up the dust on the floor. In the thin shaft of sunlight entering the room, Lila could see every drifting speck clearly.

She covered her mouth and nose and walked inside.

Apart from the dust, the house was almost exactly the same as it had been when she was a child.

The entrance led into the main room, which served as a small sitting room. In the centre stood a square mahogany table with four chairs.

There was a door on either side of the main room, leading to the east room and the west room.

The east room was the master bedroom, larger than the other. Nearly a third of it was taken up by a heated hearth bench.

The west room was the smaller second bedroom.

Both bedrooms were simply furnished. Their most striking feature was that each had a large, bright window.

Through the main room and further back was a spacious kitchen running from east to west.

There were two stoves in the kitchen, one on each side, connected respectively to the heated hearth benches in the two bedrooms.

At the far west end of the kitchen, a separate bathroom had been built.

That bathroom had been added specially because, as a child, Lila had not been used to the outdoor privy. Agnes and George had spent extra money to build it for her.

The fittings inside, however, were now very old.

"Looks like all of this will need replacing," Lila murmured, pressing the worn toilet button.

She turned on the tap, and clear water rushed out at once.

Lila washed her face. The water was cool and refreshing.

The house was connected to the well in the courtyard. The well water came from underground streams flowing down from the mountain, almost like spring water. It was clear and faintly sweet.

Lila came out of the bathroom and walked straight to the west side stove.

She pushed aside the broom and shovel piled in the corner, then lifted one of the floor tiles.

This was the entrance to the cellar.

To keep it hidden, the wooden cover of the cellar had been fitted with the same tile as the rest of the floor.

Without looking closely, anyone would simply think there was ordinary flooring beneath it.

Lila glanced around, picked up a box of matches beside the stove, and struck one twice.

To her surprise, it still lit.

She dropped the burning match down. It did not go out at once, which meant there was oxygen below.

Only then did Lila turn on the torch on her phone and climb down the cellar ladder.

The cellar was deep, but not very large below, only about ten square metres.

The temperature was cooler than above ground, around twenty degrees.

But that was normal underground. Warm in winter, cool in summer, and almost the same temperature all year round.

The cellar was dim, and it was hard to make anything out.

Lila did not rush forward. She felt along the cellar wall until her fingers suddenly caught on a metal plate.

With a click, the plate loosened.

Lila opened a small iron door.

Behind it was Rock Hollow, leading towards the rear of the house, to the north.

That was the direction into the body of the mountain itself.

Lila bent down and passed through the little iron door, entering the private world she had loved most as a child.

It was a natural rock hollow, two metres high and more than two hundred metres long.

When Lila was little, this had been her favourite place to play. It was not cold in winter, nor hot in summer. It stretched a long way, and when she ran, her footsteps echoed. It had been endless fun.

Before George built the house, he had discovered this natural rocky cave.

Back then, no one paid much attention to undeveloped mountain land. The older generation could choose places to build houses as they pleased.

For the sake of this cave, George had decided to build the new house here.

With the house built in front of the cave, the cave effectively became part of the property. It could be used directly as a shelter without the need to spend time, labour and money digging one out.

A naturally formed rock hollow was also far sturdier than any man made earth shelter.

"Good thing that crafty old man liked getting something for nothing. He really did leave me something priceless."

Lila muttered to herself, suddenly admiring her grandfather's cleverness.

At the far end of Rock Hollow was a stone wall formed from many large rocks piled together.

There were small gaps in the rocks, allowing fresh air and faint light to seep in.

This place must lead to the outside world. With a digger, it might even be possible to open it up.

But Lila would not do that.

If it were opened, it would be easier for others to discover. It was safer to leave the rocks exactly as they were.

Knowing the way well, Lila returned by the same route, shut the door to Rock Hollow firmly, and climbed back up the cellar ladder.

She dusted off her hands, deeply satisfied.

A familiar place was best after all.

If she could ride out the Collapse here, her sense of security would be through the roof.

In her previous life, she had not had the ability to return. She could only bring that ungrateful family into the Basement with her.

This time, she would settle into this sturdy old mansion in comfort and watch the end of the world unfold from safety.

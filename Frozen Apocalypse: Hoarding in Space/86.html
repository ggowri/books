Chapter 85

The success of the first Foraging Team did not go unnoticed.

After seeing people return with food and useful supplies, many others became eager to try their luck.

Following the Duty Roster, survivors began forming their own teams, with one group heading out each day to search for resources.

Mrs. Harris was pleased.

The arrangement guaranteed a steady supply of firewood for cooking and hot water.

In fact, they were bringing back more than they immediately needed.

A growing reserve of fuel was slowly taking shape.

"Keep all the firewood stored here in the shelter," Mrs. Harris reminded everyone repeatedly.

"When it's time to cook, we'll take out what we need."

"No point leaving it somewhere it can be stolen."

She treated every bundle of wood like treasure.

Because in the Long Freeze, it practically was.

Annabel watched all of this with growing jealousy.

"Look at them."

"They go out once and come back with bags full of food."

She turned toward her family.

"Why can't we do that too?"

Maggie was just as anxious.

She glanced nervously at Edward.

Edward remained completely indifferent.

Finally, Maggie spoke.

"I'll sign up."

Ever since the Long Freeze began, Edward's temper had become increasingly unpredictable.

And Maggie had grown more and more afraid of him.

She didn't dare suggest that he volunteer.

Instead, she offered herself.

Annabel's leg was still injured.

She needed better nutrition to recover.

And if they could find medicine, even better.

Maggie signed up immediately.

Unfortunately, there was already a waiting list.

Her turn wouldn't come for another five days.

The second Foraging Team returned with excellent results as well.

Just like the first group, they brought back four large sacks of firewood.

And every member returned carrying a sizable bag of food.

Hope spread quickly through the shelter.

As long as they had food and fuel, perhaps they could survive underground for a very long time.

Unfortunately, Oakwood Estate only contained three apartment buildings that hadn't burned during the Lightning Storm.

Within three days, all three had been thoroughly searched.

On the fourth day, the next team had no choice but to venture farther away.

That meant greater risk.

But potentially greater rewards.

They aimed high.

Their destination was a large supermarket about a kilometer away.

The same supermarket everyone had rushed to when the Long Freeze first began.

Early that morning, after breakfast, the four person team set out.

Mrs. Harris couldn't hide her concern.

The destination was farther than any previous scavenging trip.

"Be careful out there."

"If you start getting too cold, come straight back."

"Doesn't matter if you don't find anything."

"Getting home safely is more important."

She repeated the warning several times.

The team confidently assured her they'd be fine.

Soon they disappeared into the snow.

Each step through the deep drifts was slow and exhausting.

Wrapped in bulky winter gear, they trudged toward the supermarket.

Fortunately, the building had lightning rods on its roof.

There was little chance it had burned.

The one kilometer journey took nearly thirty minutes.

When they finally saw the structure standing intact in the distance, everyone's spirits lifted.

Their pace quickened.

Then they reached the entrance.

And reality hit.

The group had brought axes and heavy wrenches, expecting to break through locked shutters or chains.

Instead, the front entrance stood wide open.

The place had already been picked clean.

Still unwilling to give up, they searched it anyway.

The shelves were empty.

Every aisle had been stripped bare.

Someone else had gotten there first.

They checked the second floor.

Then the third.

Only a few miscellaneous household items remained.

Not particularly valuable, but better than nothing.

Since they had already made the trip, they filled their sacks with whatever they could use.

Toothbrushes.

Mugs.

Several unbroken ceramic bowls.

A few plates.

That was about it.

With nothing else worth taking and the cold steadily seeping through their clothing, they finally decided to head home.

On the way back, they spotted movement in the distance.

Another scavenging group.

Six figures dressed in heavy winter gear.

Each carried large sacks.

They were obviously searching for supplies too.

The strangers outnumbered them.

The Oakwood team immediately gave them a wide berth.

In weather like this, any conflict could easily turn deadly.

Both groups kept their distance and continued on their separate paths.

When the team returned to Basement Level One, their sacks looked disappointingly empty.

Mrs. Harris took one look and shook her head.

Not in disappointment.

In relief.

"You're back."

"That's all that matters."

One of the scavengers quickly told her about the encounter.

"We ran into another Foraging Team."

"They were looking for supplies too."

"If we want to build up reserves, we'd better move fast."

"Otherwise everyone else will get there first."

Mrs. Harris frowned thoughtfully.

It made perfect sense.

If she had realized scavenging was necessary, then other shelters would inevitably come to the same conclusion.

That evening, she gathered everyone together.

Her expression was serious.

"From now on, if you're heading out to search for supplies, be careful."

"Avoid conflict whenever possible."

"Nothing is more important than staying alive."

The remaining residents were mostly decent people.

Once the violent troublemakers had been removed, the shelter had become far more united.

Naturally, everyone listened.

At the Lower Vale Mansion, Lila enjoyed a hot breakfast before feeding the chickens and dogs.

Afterward, she headed into the west room to water her vegetables.

The crops had fully matured.

She could harvest them whenever she wanted.

Some were already beginning to grow past their prime.

Using a knife, she cut several heads of leafy greens and stored them inside the Vault.

The roots remained in the planters and would continue producing.

Once her chores were finished, Lila brewed a cup of hot loose leaf tea.

She sat by the window, sipping quietly while staring outside.

Ever since her trip to collect lightning, something had changed.

The outdoors no longer frightened her quite as much.

Yes, it was brutally cold.

But her equipment was exceptional.

As long as she didn't stay outside too long, she'd be fine.

And honestly, being trapped indoors day after day was beginning to feel suffocating.

The thought lingered.

Before long, she found herself wanting another walk.

Finishing her tea, Lila stood up.

She put on her expedition down parka and reindeer hide boots.

Then she headed outside.

The moment the door opened, a cloud of warm white vapor drifted outward into the freezing air.

After carefully closing the door behind her, she wandered around the courtyard.

The fresh air felt surprisingly refreshing.

Being outside, even briefly, made her feel lighter somehow.

She strolled a few laps around the property.

Then she bounced over to one of the viewing gaps built into the wall.

Peering through it, she scanned the landscape.

Just as before.

A vast white expanse of snow.

No footprints.

No signs of life.

She was about to leave the courtyard for a short walk beyond the walls when something caught her eye.

Wait.

Her gaze snapped back.

Far in the distance.

Something was moving.

Lila narrowed her eyes.

A vehicle?

Her heart skipped a beat.

That couldn't be right.

The area around the Lower Vale Mansion was incredibly remote.

Who would be driving around out here during the Long Freeze?

With a flicker of will, she pulled a pair of binoculars from the Vault.

She raised them and focused on the distant movement.

About eight hundred meters away.

The image sharpened.

Lila's eyes widened.

It really was a vehicle.

Actually, several vehicles.

Through the circular lenses, she could clearly see seven or eight large construction machines moving slowly along a temporary road carved through the snow.

Excavators.

Bulldozers.

Heavy lorries loaded with building materials.

What caught her attention most was how unusual they looked.

These weren't ordinary construction vehicles.

Their designs appeared reinforced.

Modified.

Built specifically for extreme cold weather operations.

Lila felt a knot tighten in her stomach.

Instinctively, she held her breath.

When she'd chosen the Lower Vale Mansion, one of its biggest advantages had been isolation.

No neighbors.

No traffic.

No attention.

Far from people.

Far from trouble.

She had believed that even after the Collapse, no one would come here.

But now, less than a kilometer away, construction was underway on a stretch of countryside that had been completely abandoned before.

And that realization sent a chill through her that had nothing to do with the weather.

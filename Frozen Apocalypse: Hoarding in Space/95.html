Chapter 94

After dinner, everyone immediately scrambled onto the heated bed, hoping to claim the best spots before someone else did.

The farmhouse quickly descended into chaos.

Mrs. Harris didn't have time to deal with it.

After finishing her own bowl of porridge, she carried in several more basins of snow and dumped them into the iron pot.

There were still a few grains of millet stuck to the bottom.

Another batch of hot water would turn into a thin rice broth.

That could keep people warm through the night.

"Alright, enough. Let's sort out sleeping spaces."

Some people wanted warmth.

Others preferred being able to stretch out.

Fortunately, dividing the spots wasn't too difficult.

Callum and Leo chose places where they could lie down.

In their experience, the entire heated bed would eventually become warm anyway.

The section closest to the firebox could actually become uncomfortably hot.

Sophie settled beside Lauren Hart and chose a place where she could sleep lying down as well.

Anna immediately started complaining.

She insisted on taking a spot at the warmest end.

In the end, Maggie sat beside her daughter against the wall near the hottest section of the bench.

Edward claimed a sleeping spot farther down where he could stretch out.

Daniel hesitated for a while before deciding to stay near Anna.

He squeezed into a sitting position beside her.

Soon the hearth bench was packed shoulder to shoulder with bodies.

Some sat.

Some lay down.

Only Mrs. Harris and one member of Oakwood Estate Management remained on the floor.

There simply wasn't any room left.

"We'll make our beds in the kitchen," Mrs. Harris said.

"Sleeping near the kitchen range should still be warm enough."

The estate worker nodded immediately.

Honestly, he wasn't interested in squeezing into that overcrowded bench anyway.

The kitchen floor sounded far more appealing.

"Anyone want some millet broth?" Mrs. Harris called.

Before bed, she planned to empty the pot completely.

The iron pot itself acted like an oversized radiator once heated.

It would help keep the kitchen warm all night.

"I do!"

"Me too!"

Nobody on the hearth bench wanted to move.

They were terrified of losing their spots.

Patiently, Mrs. Harris carried around a large basin of broth and poured some into everyone's cups.

Once the pot was completely empty, waves of heat rolled off the exposed metal.

The kitchen became wonderfully warm.

"Get some sleep."

Night had fallen.

Mrs. Harris was exhausted.

She and the estate worker spread blankets on the floor and lay down fully clothed.

Darkness swallowed the farmhouse.

Nobody could see much of anything.

Conversation faded.

One by one, snores began filling the room.

Daniel remained awake.

Slowly, he leaned toward Anna.

"Did you miss me?"

His voice was barely above a whisper.

At the same time, he gently took hold of her arm.

"Hmph."

Anna turned away.

She refused to look at him.

But she wasn't yelling.

She wasn't cursing him out either.

Daniel immediately realized he still had a chance.

"I missed you," he whispered.

"More than you know."

His warm breath brushed against her ear.

Anna's neck tingled.

She instinctively shrank away.

Daniel chuckled softly.

"You're still adorable."

Anna stayed silent.

"I was wrong," he continued.

"I'm sorry. It was all a misunderstanding."

He lowered his voice further.

"Just trust me one more time."

In the darkness, Anna quietly squeezed his hand.

Daniel's grip tightened immediately.

A smile spread across his face.

Being alone during the Collapse was terrifying.

No resources.

No protection.

No future.

He knew all about Anna's attempts to attach herself to influential people.

They were getting close to the refuge now.

For all he knew, she might successfully connect herself to some senior official once they arrived.

Daniel needed to repair this relationship before then.

If Anna rose in status, he wanted to be standing beside her.

That way he might enjoy some of the benefits too.

Hidden by darkness, his hand slowly slipped beneath her coat.

Seeing that Maggie was already asleep, Anna relaxed against his shoulder like melting ice.

...

The next morning, everyone enjoyed another hot breakfast before preparing to move out.

Before departure, they checked their supplies and treated their injuries.

The results were grim.

Many people had frostbite on their feet, calves, arms, faces, and ears.

Some legs were covered in patches of angry red and deep purple.

The skin looked stretched and shiny.

Touching it caused burning pain.

Those people carefully wrapped their lower legs in cloth and prayed the damage wouldn't worsen.

Others had developed blisters.

Some were as small as peas.

Others were nearly the size of eggs.

Yellow fluid filled the swollen sacs beneath painfully tight skin.

They heated needles over open flames and punctured the blisters.

After draining the fluid, they wrapped the injured areas tightly before putting their winter clothing back on.

At least that allowed them to keep walking.

A few cases were far worse.

Their skin had turned dark grey.

Pressing a finger into the flesh left a dent that remained for several seconds.

The frightening part was that they couldn't feel anything anymore.

Everyone understood what that meant.

Nobody said it aloud.

But fear spread through the group.

They all wondered whether another day or two of walking would leave their own legs looking the same.

Eventually, everyone packed up.

Wrapped themselves in layers.

And prepared to continue.

Mrs. Harris wasn't doing much better.

Her feet had turned blue and purple from the cold.

She had started wearing plastic bags inside her boots, hoping the extra layer might trap a little more warmth.

The route ahead left the shelter of the city behind.

Out in the open countryside, there was nothing to block the wind.

The gusts were even stronger than before.

People could barely keep their eyes open.

Most walked with squinted eyes and lowered heads.

Just keep moving.

One step at a time.

Halfway through the day's march, a dull thud sounded behind Mrs. Harris.

Someone had collapsed.

"What happened?"

Her voice carried over the wind.

The figure lay motionless.

Mrs. Harris immediately dropped her sled and hurried back.

When she reached the woman, she knelt and shook her shoulder firmly.

"What's wrong?"

The woman stared up at the grey sky.

Her voice was weak.

"I'm done."

"I can't walk anymore."

She slowly pushed a small bag of food toward Mrs. Harris.

"Take my food."

"I just want to stay here."

Then she closed her eyes.

Her face was pale and expressionless.

"No."

Mrs. Harris slapped her chest lightly.

"You can't sleep."

"Get up."

"Come on."

But the woman never responded.

She remained still.

Silent.

Gone.

Mrs. Harris didn't waste time.

She dismantled the woman's sled and removed the remaining supplies.

A bag of grain.

Two candles.

Then she called for help.

Together, several people lifted the body onto the sled and covered it with blankets.

Mrs. Harris looked down at her.

"Rest now."

"You won't be tired anymore."

"You won't be cold anymore, either."

That was all.

The supplies were tied onto Mrs. Harris's sled.

The group resumed walking.

Nobody spoke.

Heads lowered.

Feet moving.

That was all anyone had strength left for.

Around midday they reached a patch of woodland.

It became their rest stop.

A fire was lit among the trees.

Everyone ate a little, warmed themselves, and continued onward.

By afternoon, two more people collapsed.

Neither ever stood up again.

Three deaths in a single day.

The effect on morale was devastating.

Panic spread through the group.

Everyone feared they might be next.

"We're getting close!"

Mrs. Harris shouted toward the people behind her.

Her voice fought against the wind.

"When we arrive, there'll be hot bread."

"Warm buildings."

"Just hold on a little longer!"

People clung desperately to that image.

Hot food.

Warm shelter.

Safety.

The dream pushed them forward.

They walked harder.

Faster.

But luck wasn't on their side.

According to the map, there should have been buildings nearby.

Instead, darkness arrived first.

By nightfall, there wasn't a single house in sight.

Nothing.

Tonight, they would have to sleep out in the snow.

Chapter 116

The wrap looked a little oversized.

Lila sliced it cleanly down the middle, turning it into two manageable halves.

It looked much more appetising that way.

There was still plenty of dough left in the bowl, so she cooked four more cornmeal flatbreads.

The shredded potatoes and onions were only enough to fill three wraps.

The remaining two flatbreads stayed plain.

The three large wraps became six halves altogether.

Lila kept one half outside.

The other five disappeared into the Vault.

She tore the two plain cornmeal flatbreads into bite sized pieces and stored those in the Vault as well.

Those would be tomorrow's meals for Snowy and Patches while she was away.

Holding one of the warm wraps, Lila couldn't wait any longer.

She took a big bite.

It was still piping hot.

That was exactly how it tasted best.

The cornmeal flatbread had a rougher texture than one made with white flour.

It wasn't nearly as soft or chewy.

But frying it in oil brought out a rich toasted corn flavour that became more fragrant with every bite.

The real star was the sauce.

It reminded her of the savoury spread used on the street food flatbreads she used to buy before the Collapse.

Paired with the crisp potato strips and fresh onion, every bite was rich yet refreshing.

A sausage or a grilled meat patty would have made it perfect.

Unfortunately, bringing food like that into public would attract far too much attention.

Even so, she was more than satisfied.

Tomorrow's breakfast was going to be excellent.

Filled with excitement, Lila went to bed early.

She couldn't wait for morning.

More importantly, she wanted a full night's sleep.

Tomorrow was not a day she could afford to be exhausted.

Meanwhile, something equally exciting was happening inside the refuge dining hall.

A crowd had gathered around the small platform as Ryan read names from a list.

These were the people selected for tomorrow's supply recovery mission into the city.

Every one of them had been chosen from the volunteers.

The first names belonged to staff members.

Leo Turner, military physician.

Responsible for handling medical emergencies during the mission.

Sophie Hale.

Responsible for maintaining discipline within the team.

There were also a vehicle maintenance engineer and several other specialists.

Ryan stopped after reading twenty nine names.

He went no further.

The final place had been quietly reserved for Lila.

Of course, no one noticed.

Everyone cared only about hearing their own name.

Even if someone had realised there was one space missing, nobody would have bothered arguing over it.

Callum's name was on the list.

The moment he heard it, he grinned from ear to ear.

His chances of being selected hadn't been particularly high.

But Leo had personally recommended him to the soldiers.

Doctors were priceless during the Collapse.

Asking for one extra teammate in return was hardly an unreasonable favour.

The soldiers had agreed without hesitation.

Thirty people.

Nearly a thousand residents.

The odds were about the same as winning a scratch card.

Lauren wasn't chosen.

Mrs. Harris wasn't chosen either.

Standing among the crowd, Edward's family listened as every name was read.

None of theirs appeared.

Edward and Maggie had expected as much.

Neither had any special skills or useful connections.

Only Anna looked devastated.

She bit her lip until her eyes turned red.

It wasn't supposed to happen like this.

She should have had a chance.

Yesterday she'd seen Ethan heading toward the registration desk and hurried after him to sign up as well.

Instead, she'd been delayed by that soldier blocking her path.

Then she'd fallen over.

By the time she got back up, Ethan was already gone.

She stared at Ryan on the platform as he folded the list away.

Her fingers clenched into a fist.

Then slowly relaxed.

Early the following morning, Lila woke before her alarm ever had the chance to ring.

She poured herself a large mug of hot milk and ate a meat filled bun.

By the time breakfast was over, she felt full of energy.

She retrieved the cornmeal wraps from the Vault.

Four halves went neatly into an insulated lunch container before returning to the Vault.

When lunchtime came, she'd simply take them back out while they were still steaming hot.

If anyone questioned why the food was still warm, she'd just credit the insulated container.

The last thing to prepare was water.

She filled a vacuum flask with hot water and stirred in a little white sugar.

Then she paused.

One flask wasn't enough.

She filled a second one with the same sweetened hot water.

This flask stayed inside the Vault instead of going into her backpack.

That way she could travel with less weight.

If she emptied the first flask, she could quietly swap them later.

After filling the dogs' bowls with food, Lila dressed in her warmest winter gear.

She slung the backpack over her shoulders, picked up the bag containing the daylight grow lamps, and stepped outside.

The padlock securing the front gate was nearly the size of her hand.

No ordinary thief would ever force it open.

She checked every lock twice to make absolutely certain the house was secure.

Only then did she head downhill toward the refuge.

When she arrived, the city expedition team had not assembled yet.

After showing her identification to the guards, she headed straight for the Military Quarter.

The soldier on duty still stopped her at the entrance.

Lila lifted the bag in her hand.

"I'm delivering the daylight grow lamps to Captain Walker."

Ethan had already informed them about the delivery the previous evening.

The guard immediately waved her through.

Lila knocked on Ethan's door.

He was still eating breakfast.

"You're here early."

He wiped his mouth with a napkin, clearly surprised.

Lila laughed awkwardly.

She knew she'd arrived a little too early.

It wasn't really her fault.

She simply hadn't travelled anywhere in so long that she was too excited to stay home.

"Here they are."

"You can check them if you like."

She handed him the bag.

"Come in."

"Have a seat."

Ethan invited her inside before opening it.

His room had electricity.

He plugged one of the daylight grow lamps in immediately.

The lamp shone brilliantly.

Its warm golden light filled the room with a comfortable glow.

Ethan nodded with satisfaction.

"These are excellent."

He carefully unplugged the lamp and stored it inside his wardrobe.

"I should add some Survival Credits to your account."

"This is a valuable contribution for us."

He felt uncomfortable accepting something so useful without compensation.

"I thought we already agreed."

"You take me on every supply mission."

"If that's the arrangement, you don't need to give me extra credits."

Lila genuinely worried that accepting payment might somehow replace her spot on future expeditions.

"You'll come on every mission from now on."

"I just don't want to take advantage of you."

"It won't be much."

"Let's say fifty Survival Credits."

Roughly half a month's wages.

That seemed fair.

"Then thank you, Captain Walker."

Lila wasn't about to refuse free Survival Credits when someone insisted on giving them to her.

As she accepted, her thoughts immediately wandered.

What could she spend them on?

Food.

Supplies.

Firewood.

Firewood.

That was actually a great option.

She hadn't stored an enormous amount before the Collapse.

Her house sat beside the mountains, so she'd always assumed she could simply gather more whenever she needed it.

If the refuge was selling firewood, though, that made life much easier.

She would definitely need to recover as many supplies as possible tomorrow.

The more Survival Credits she earned, the more firewood she could buy.

"Want some breakfast?"

Ethan sat back down and picked up his bowl again.

He asked politely.

Lila waved both hands.

"No, thanks."

"I've already eaten."

"I'll just wait outside."

She felt awkward sitting in someone's room while they finished breakfast.

Just as she stood, Ethan stopped her.

"No."

"It's freezing outside."

"Stay here."

"I'll be done in a minute."

After all, she didn't live in the Military Quarter.

There was nowhere else for her to wait.

Lila nodded and quietly returned to the sofa.

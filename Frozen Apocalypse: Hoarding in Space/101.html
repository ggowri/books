Chapter 100

"Why don't you stay here and work at the clinic as a doctor?"

Mr. Brown lowered his voice and leaned closer to Leo.

"Once the refuge is fully operational, food, supplies, and housing will all be distributed through work credits. If you're a doctor, you won't have to spend your days doing backbreaking labor."

Based on the number of survivors already arriving, Mr. Brown estimated that the clinic would need at least five or six doctors to function properly.

Yet despite asking nearly everyone who came through the doors, he still hadn't found a single qualified physician.

Leo found the information useful.

During the journey to Lower Vale Refuge, he had already assumed that free meals and free shelter couldn't last forever.

Sooner or later, everyone would have to contribute something in exchange for resources.

Working as a doctor might not be a bad arrangement.

"Could I have some time to think about it?" Leo asked.

"Think about it later," Mr. Brown replied immediately.

"We'll put you down as clinic staff for now. If you change your mind, you can always quit."

He was terrified that the only person with any meaningful medical knowledge would slip through his fingers.

Leo didn't argue.

Mr. Brown had a point.

If it didn't work out, he could always resign later.

Once both Leo and Mrs. Harris were cleared to leave, they bundled back into their cold-weather gear and waited outside the clinic for registration and housing assignments.

When it came time to process Leo, a soldier checked his information and led him directly to the house assigned to Callum.

Callum had already explained that the two of them were traveling together.

Although a large number of survivors had arrived over the past few days, housing remained plentiful.

Families and travel companions were generally assigned together whenever possible and given their own room.

At Sophie's request, she was assigned a residence of her own.

Of course, that meant she would be responsible for her own fuel supply and future housing costs.

When Leo entered the house, Callum was already sprawled across his bedding, completely exhausted.

Hearing someone enter, he lifted an eyelid.

A sleepy grunt escaped his throat.

Then he immediately drifted back to sleep.

Only then did Leo notice that the room wasn't cold at all.

Callum had already lit the stove.

The heated bed was gradually warming up.

Two Vacuum Flasks sat on the edge of the bed, both filled with hot water.

Leo chuckled softly.

He removed his outer coat, spread out the bedding provided by the soldiers, and slipped beneath the blankets.

The bed was warm.

Exhaustion washed over him.

Within moments, he was asleep.

Earlier, Lauren had spoken with one of the soldiers.

If Mrs. Harris wanted to share a room with her, she could move in immediately.

Mrs. Harris first retrieved her sled from Sophie and then dragged it across the settlement toward Lauren's house.

When she arrived, Lauren was struggling to tend the stove.

Her legs had suffered severe frostbite.

Every time she crouched down, sharp pain shot through her body.

She was feeding wood into the firebox while a pot of water slowly heated above it.

The moment Lauren saw Mrs. Harris, her eyes filled with tears.

She hurried over and helped pull the sled inside.

After shutting the door tightly behind them, the two women embraced.

"Mrs. Harris, we made it."

"We finally made it."

They held each other for a long moment before finally letting go.

Then Mrs. Harris looked around.

"Where's Emma?"

"Oh!"

Lauren blinked.

"She's still in the crate."

Only then did she remember.

When she had first arrived, the room had still been cold.

Afraid Emma might get chilled, she had left her tucked safely inside until the house warmed up.

Lauren pulled away the blankets covering the crate.

A small dark-haired head emerged.

Blinking against the sudden light, Emma poked her head out.

"Mom?"

"Are we stopping to rest again?"

She thought they had simply reached another temporary camp.

Lauren smiled and gently lifted her daughter out.

"No."

"We've finally made it home."

Emma had spent so many hours sitting in the crate that her legs barely worked.

Lauren carried her over and sat her on the edge of the heated bed.

The little girl looked around curiously.

The house was unfamiliar.

But it was clean.

And warm.

Would this really be home?

"Mom, are we going to live here now?"

"That's right."

Lauren brushed a hand across her daughter's cheek.

"We'll live here from now on. Does that sound good?"

Emma nodded enthusiastically.

"I like it here."

"As long as you're not working so hard anymore."

"As long as I get to stay with you."

"I'll like any home."

To Emma, a house wasn't what mattered.

Her mother was.

Lauren's eyes softened.

"You've been so brave."

She helped Emma remove her bulky winter coat.

The warmest spot on the heated bed had already been prepared.

Lauren tucked her beneath the blankets.

Emma sat there quietly and obediently.

"Emma," Mrs. Harris said with a smile, gently patting her head, "would you like me to live here with you two?"

Back when they had all lived in the basement shelter, Mrs. Harris had often slipped candy to the little girl.

Emma adored her.

Her eyes lit up instantly.

"Really?"

"That would be wonderful!"

"I love you, Grandma Harris!"

The room filled with laughter.

By then, the water had finished heating.

Lauren filled both Vacuum Flasks and removed the pot from the stove.

The hot iron cookware continued radiating warmth throughout the room.

Before long, the entire house felt cozy.

Even wearing a winter coat felt pleasantly warm.

"Drink some hot water."

"We should get some sleep."

Lauren handed a flask to Mrs. Harris.

The two women removed their boots, climbed onto the heated bed, drank their hot water, and crawled beneath the blankets.

Neither waited for nightfall.

Sleep claimed them almost immediately.

The journey had been long.

Exhausting.

Painful.

Nothing mattered more right now than a peaceful night's sleep.

Everything else could wait until tomorrow.

For everyone involved, the arrangement worked well.

Mrs. Harris and Lauren could look after one another.

Heating costs and future rent would be easier to manage as well.

When Sophie finally reached her assigned house, she let go of the sled rope.

The sled crashed heavily onto the floor.

She was completely spent.

It took considerable effort just to light the stove and boil some water.

Even that simple task drained what little energy she had left.

As the room slowly warmed, Sophie leaned against the wall beside the stove and chewed on a piece of beef jerky with her eyes closed.

Gradually, warmth returned to her body.

Strength followed.

Unfortunately, so did sensation.

Everything hurt.

Her hands.

Her feet.

Her legs.

Looking down, she saw frostbite sores covering her hands.

Thin pink fluid seeped from cracked skin.

The sled sat nearby.

She reached inside and rummaged through her belongings.

Eventually she found two tubes of Frostbite Ointment.

She had carried them throughout the journey.

Yet she had never dared take them out in front of anyone.

Supplies like these were too valuable.

Someone might have stolen them.

Now, finally safe, she filled a stainless steel basin with warm water from the stove.

After carefully washing her hands, she applied a thin layer of ointment to the damaged skin.

Hopefully it would help.

Next came her feet.

She removed her boots.

Her toes were mottled blue and purple.

Sitting a little farther from the stove, she slowly warmed them by the heat.

Sophie was exhausted enough to collapse where she sat.

But she forced herself to stay awake.

The sooner she treated the frostbite, the better her chances of making a full recovery.

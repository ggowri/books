Chapter 61

Lila opened the first jewellery box. Inside, several necklaces lay nestled together.
Silver ones, gold ones, and some that had long since lost their plating.
She lifted the box to her nose and inhaled. A strong smell of rust. All fake.
She opened another box. This one held just a single necklace, gleaming with a warm, rich lustre. Clearly solid gold.
Lila smiled, satisfied. Finally, something worth keeping.

She opened a third box and froze.
She knew this gold necklace intimately.
When she was little and couldn't sleep at night, fussing and crying, her mother would hold her in her arms and rock her gently.
Little Lila would lie there, eyes wide open, watching this very necklace sway back and forth against her mother's smooth neck.
The camellia pendant would swing left and right, and before long, Lila would drift off to sleep.
And now, her mother's pendant was locked away in Maggie's cabinet.

Lila gripped the jewellery box tightly, then reached for the others. Her mother's gold rings, bracelets, and various necklaces – all here.
She picked out every last piece that belonged to her mother and placed them under her own desk.
Back where they belonged.

In an open drawer sat several sealed boxes of skincare products and face masks. Lila pulled them all out. She could use those herself.
On the desktop were some already opened creams and lotions, plus the fake jewellery she had dug out earlier. All went into the rubbish bag inside the Vault.

Now empty, the dressing table got two sprays of alcohol, then a wipe with a cloth. Lila repeated the process until it was spotless, then put it back into the Vault.
If her own table ever broke, this would do as a replacement.

Lila wasn't in a good mood. Not because she felt any affection for Maggie. But because she had spent all those years treating her enemy as family. She felt she had failed her parents.

By midday, she couldn't be bothered to cook from scratch.
So she decided to eat something ready-made. Something good.

From the Vault, she took out a watermelon, cut it in half, left a quarter outside, and put the rest back.
She peeled the quarter, cut the flesh into mahjong-tile-sized cubes, and spread them across the bottom of a plate.
Then she grabbed a handful of cherries, a handful of strawberries, and one mango.
She washed the cherries and strawberries, peeled and chopped the mango, and scattered them haphazardly over the watermelon. It looked delicious.

She set the beautiful fruit platter on the dining table, then retrieved a pot of thick yoghurt from the Vault and spooned some into a delicate little bowl.
She carried both, along with a fork, back to her room.

After washing the stickiness off her hands, Lila returned to the east parlour and shrugged off her padded coat.
She took a clear glass, poured herself a small sweet wine, and propped her tablet on the table with her favourite show already playing. Then she pulled out from the Vault a plate of beef she had grilled earlier at the self-service barbecue place. Time to eat.

The fat around the beef slices had turned slightly crisp. Biting in, they were still hot enough to sting her lips. Crunchy and utterly delicious.
She speared a strawberry with her fork, dipped it in yoghurt, and popped it into her mouth. The creamy tang cut right through the richness of the beef.
Lila ate slowly, drank slowly. A few glasses of wine down, and every last bit of unhappiness was swept clean.

Full and content, her cheeks flushed and her head pleasantly light, she wiped her hands and mouth with a wet wipe. Ignoring the empty plates still on the table, she walked straight to the big sofa in front of the fireplace.
She pulled a thick, soft blanket over herself, closed her eyes, and fell asleep.

Lila dreamed.
It was winter, and she was back at her grandparents' house with her parents.
The whole family sat around the fireplace, eating baked sweet potatoes and drinking her grandmother's homemade mulberry wine.
The wine was sweet. The sweet potatoes were hot and soft. Lila said something silly that made everyone roar with laughter.
In her sleep, Lila curved her lips into a smile. Her face was the picture of happiness.

Anna was unusually quiet that day. When it came time for the afternoon meal, she obediently picked up her tray and prepared to leave.
Every day, Daniel waited to go with her. But today, he was leaving early.

"Daniel, why aren't you waiting for me?" Anna spoke up after a full day of silence.

Daniel didn't turn back. He paused at the doorway. "I'm going ahead." He walked out before Anna could say another word.

Sophie was afraid of bumping into the family next door. She left a little early too.
At the doorway, she made her usual habit of checking her surroundings. No danger. Then she headed straight for the queue.
She had come out early today, so the line wasn't long.
But to her surprise, the man who had chatted her up yesterday was already there.

"Hello again. Fancy meeting you here." Daniel greeted Sophie warmly.
A persistent suitor wears down resistance. He was sure he could win her over.
"Oh, and don't get the wrong idea. That girl yesterday? She's my sister. She's a bit... you know." He tapped his temple. "Hope you can cut her some slack."
With that, he neatly distanced himself from Anna.

"Whatever. None of my business."
Sophie's face hardened. She switched to a different queue. She didn't understand how this man could be so shameless.

"Bitch! Go to hell!"

A scream shattered the quiet of the basement. Anna shot forward like a human cannonball, straight at Sophie.
Sophie flinched, then reacted fast. She twisted her body aside, dodging Anna's lunge.
As Anna flew past, Sophie saw the knife in her hand very clearly.
Her heart sank. She scrambled backwards.

Anna missed Sophie, spun around, and charged again, knife raised.

Maggie had come out right behind Anna.
Seeing her daughter so out of control, Maggie rushed forward, afraid the girl would get hurt. Not understanding what was happening, she stepped directly into Sophie's path.

"Anna, what's wrong now? Talk to Mum!"

Terrified, Maggie opened her arms to catch her charging daughter.
Anna looked up. The face in front of her had changed. Panic flashed across her own. She jerked the knife upward.

"AAAAHHHH!"

The scream made the onlookers' eyes go wide.
Maggie clutched her face and crumpled to the ground in agony.
Blood dripped onto the concrete, pooling in a dark stain. Anna stared at the red staining her knife. Her hand trembled. She dropped the blade.

It all happened in an instant. By the time anyone could react, it was too late to stop.

Mrs Harris pointed her long serving spoon at Maggie, huddled on the floor. "Go check on her," she told the security guard beside her.

Two guards hurried over. They saw the shocking amount of blood. One picked up the discarded knife first. The other pinned Anna down.

The third guard crouched beside Maggie. "Are you alright?"

Only when Maggie had calmed down a little did the guard dare to pull her hands away from her face.
Her face was covered in blood. A horrifying gash ran halfway across it – from the inner corner of her right eye all the way to her right ear. A wound nearly eight inches long, with blood still pouring out.

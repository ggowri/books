Chapter 73

Cedric put on his mink coat, paused at the doorway, then turned back. "Be ready. I'll send you word in a few days."

Annabel nodded eagerly, her eyes alight with anticipation.

The door closed, footsteps fading.

She dressed quickly, pleased at the thought that she might finally gain influence with such little effort.

Outside, Maggie's anxious voice called: "Has anyone seen my daughter? Where's she gone?"

Annabel frowned and pulled on her coat faster. She tucked the compressed biscuits and tins into her pockets, trying to keep the bulge discreet.

"Here I am, stop shouting," she called back, heading for the basement.

Maggie's worry melted into relief. "Oh, I was terrified! Where did you disappear to?"

"Just chatting with a friend for a bit," Annabel replied lightly, not wishing to walk alongside Maggie. Her scarred face made her self-conscious.

"You have a friend?" Maggie asked, sceptical.

Annabel rolled her eyes and ignored her, slipping into the basement.

She retrieved the food Cedric had given her, laying it on her bed.

"Where did that come from?" Edward asked, glancing over his shoulder.

"The inspector took a liking to me. It's a gift," Annabel said proudly.

Maggie entered and was surprised by the sight. "Where did this come from?"

"Don't ask." Annabel tore into a biscuit, biting heartily. Dry and bland as the usual rations were, these compressed biscuits, with their sesame and peanut aroma, tasted heavenly, especially after the morning's exertions.

"Save some for later," Maggie urged, afraid Annabel would eat it all.

"I'm about to rise to power. No need to worry about food anymore," Annabel said, beaming.

From now on, she wouldn't have to worry about the days ahead.

"Mum, what exactly have you done? Tell me!" Maggie fretted, fearing Annabel might be putting herself at risk.

"The inspector liked me. He said I'll manage this estate. In a few days, the notice will come, and I'll never have to face anyone's scorn again. Mrs Harris and Sophie will see. I'll be first in line to deal with them," Annabel said, her eyes sparkling with the thrill of anticipated revenge.

"They liked you? A man in such a position noticing you! This is wonderful. No more hardship," Maggie exclaimed, her scarred face twisting into a broad smile.

Edward was momentarily stunned. He had treated Annabel harshly lately; if she truly succeeded, his past actions might backfire. Yet a smile formed on his face. "My daughter has talent. In the future, your parents will still rely on you."

Annabel snorted, recalling his previous treatment, yet she enjoyed his praise. She nodded lightly. "Mm."

"Want to share with your mum?" Maggie gestured to the biscuits, eager to taste.

Annabel split one in half, handing each portion to Maggie and Edward.

She spent the morning lounging, every bone relaxing. Then she got up to water the flowers and vegetables.

Some seedlings had sprouted; those that had previously emerged were now two centimetres tall. Annabel inspected the leaves, still indistinct in shape.

The bedding of Edward and Maggie had been airing outside for days and seemed ready. She donned her heaviest outfit, carrying both quilts back inside.

Dressed thickly, she stepped outside, circling the yard. A hole at 1.6 metres on the wall, as instructed by her, allowed her to observe the surroundings.

She bent to peer through it. Outside, everything was white and still, as if the world had frozen. The smooth snow showed no trace of movement.

After about five minutes, her eyes stung from the cold, and she hurried back inside. Removing her heavy clothing, only her eyes remained icy.

"I should have used goggles to protect my eyes from the wind," she muttered, putting her gear away.

The quilts, frozen after several days outside, were cold but dry and fluffy. She sniffed them; no odour remained.

It was time to make the giant dog bed she had been planning. The method was the same as for the small dogs, only larger. Two quilts were laid flat, stitched securely together. The edges were folded inwards, leaving an opening. She filled the surrounding space with Edward and Maggie's old clothes, then stitched a clean bedsheet over the top.

The process was simple in concept but tedious in practice. By the time it was done, three hours had passed.

Annabel carefully stored her sewing supplies and placed the giant bed on the bare hearth bench in the east parlour. It was just big enough for a person to lie down. The outer fabric was decorative and elegant.

She eagerly lay down, resting her head on the high edge, enveloped by soft quilts. It was exquisite comfort.

She could finally understand why the little dogs loved it so much. She even considered dragging quilts into the bed for herself.

After an afternoon of sewing, she was tired. Annabel lay back, scrolling through survival novels on her phone.

Suddenly, she found herself intrigued by castaway survival stories, imagining diving for seafood, drinking coconut water when thirsty. She would never do this in real life, but she could indulge in the fantasy while lying in her cozy bed.

Perhaps she should make a small hearth table for the bed, so she could snack while reclining. With nothing pressing to do, she planned to start on it tomorrow.

With that thought, Annabel got off the heated bench, selected a stack of books from the shelf, and carried them to her giant bed. She casually flipped through one, searching for instructions to make the small table. Finding them was easy; a few pages of the contents sufficed.

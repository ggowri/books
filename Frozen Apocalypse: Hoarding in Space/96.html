Chapter 95

Mrs. Harris found a relatively flat clearing deep in the forest, large enough for more than twenty people to rest.

A couple dozen old doors were hauled over and propped against tree trunks, forming a rough circle around the camp.

The doors were scavenged from abandoned buildings. Some still had rusted handles hanging from them, but together they created a surprisingly effective windbreak.

Inside the enclosure, the wind immediately weakened.

In the center, everyone piled together the firewood they had collected and built a large fire.

Around the flames, two layers of blankets were spread over the snow. Sitting on them kept people from freezing against the ground, and the heat wouldn't melt the snow underneath.

"The fire stays burning all night," Mrs. Harris announced. "Everyone needs to bring back at least one more bundle of wood this size."

She gestured to a large stack nearby.

Fortunately, there was no shortage of dead wood in the forest.

The brutal cold and constant winds had knocked countless dry branches onto the snow, making them easy to gather.

Before long, people returned carrying armfuls of wood and stacked them around the outer edge of the shelter.

Even so, icy drafts still slipped through the gaps between the doors.

Mrs. Harris ordered everyone to drape spare blankets over every crack and opening.

Then she climbed onto a stool and spread several blankets across the top, leaving only a small opening about the size of a wagon wheel.

Smoke spiraled upward through the hole.

Just right.

Finally, they could rest.

The survivors gathered around the fire, warming frozen hands, numb feet, and aching legs.

As warmth returned to their faces, tension eased from their shoulders.

Most people took off their boots and socks and laid them near the flames to dry.

Some discovered their socks had frozen directly to their skin.

A few couldn't remove them at all.

They had to warm their feet first, slowly thawing the fabric before trying again.

One man finally managed to pull off a sock.

The entire outer layer of skin came away with it.

Raw flesh showed underneath.

Several people immediately looked away.

The sight was hard to stomach.

The man's hands shook as he placed the sock beside the fire.

As for his foot, he simply closed his eyes and held it toward the heat.

If the exposed skin could dry out and form a protective layer, that would have to be enough.

"Does it hurt?" someone nearby asked softly.

The man shook his head.

"I can't feel it anymore."

Silence followed.

Almost everyone suffered from frostbite.

The atmosphere grew heavy.

The fire warmed their faces until they felt hot, while their backs remained painfully cold. Every few minutes people had to turn around to warm the other side.

A kettle hung beside the flames, bubbling steadily.

Later, a large pot was set over the fire and filled with Vegetable Stew.

Nobody cared what was in it.

When the food was ready, everyone received a bowl.

The hot meal slid into empty stomachs, giving them enough strength to face another day.

Maggie was in terrible condition.

She was easily the most exhausted person in the group.

Not only was she fighting the brutal cold, she had spent every day dragging another person behind her.

Whenever the group stopped to rest, she felt completely drained.

Meanwhile, Annabel spent nearly the entire journey wrapped in two thick blankets and was doing far better than most.

"I need the bathroom. Carry me over there."

She nudged Maggie.

Maggie slowly shook her head.

"You'll have to walk, sweetheart. I honestly can't do it anymore."

She barely had enough strength left to move her fingers.

Annabel clicked her tongue.

"Fine. Daniel, come with me."

Daniel immediately nodded and helped her out of the enclosure.

Once they were outside, he released her arm.

Annabel limped behind a nearby tree, took care of business, then limped back.

"Annabel... you can walk now?"

Daniel couldn't hide his surprise.

The faster she recovered, the easier it would be for her to keep charming people into helping her.

"Shh."

Annabel pressed a finger to her lips.

"Then why don't you just walk by yourself?" Daniel asked.

She grinned.

"Why would I? If somebody's willing to pull me around, I'd be stupid not to let them."

She looked extremely pleased with her own cleverness.

"But doesn't that wear your mom out?" Daniel said.

Even he thought that was pushing things a little too far.

Annabel shrugged.

"Come on. It's basically a sled. A dog could pull it. How much effort do you think she's using?"

Then she lowered her voice.

"And don't tell her I said that."

She didn't want to walk.

She definitely didn't want to spend any extra time in the cold.

Daniel nodded.

As far as he was concerned, keeping Annabel happy was what mattered.

Night fell completely.

Everyone slept fully clothed.

To stay safe, they organized pairs for night watch, rotating every two hours.

The fire never went out.

The night passed peacefully.

Still, sleeping outdoors during the Long Freeze was miserable.

When dawn arrived, Mrs. Harris forced herself to her feet.

Every joint in her body felt like it had been hammered overnight.

She gritted her teeth and joined the day's cooking team.

Together they prepared two enormous pots of Porridge.

When breakfast was ready, many people were still asleep.

Mrs. Harris kicked the nearest blanket.

"Up! Come on! Eat and then we're moving!"

People gradually woke and sat up.

But seven or eight remained motionless.

Mrs. Harris called out again.

Then again.

Nothing.

"What's gotten into them? Usually one shout is enough. Give him a shove."

She pointed at one of the sleepers.

The person beside him leaned over.

"Wake up. Food's ready."

He pushed once.

No response.

Twice.

Still nothing.

A bad feeling settled into his stomach.

He rolled the man onto his back.

His face immediately went white.

"He's... he's dead."

The man's skin had turned pale gray.

His body was already stiff.

Mrs. Harris's expression changed instantly.

"Check the others. Now."

The remaining sleepers were examined one by one.

Every single one of them was dead.

The group had shrunk dramatically overnight.

Mrs. Harris counted heads.

Only thirteen people remained.

There was one small advantage.

The same two pots of porridge were now divided among thirteen survivors.

For the first time in days, everyone ate until they were completely full.

After breakfast, the dead were wrapped in their own blankets and left resting on the snow.

Then the survivors gathered their belongings and continued toward Lower Vale.

Lila woke early and immediately noticed something different at the refuge below.

A tall pole had appeared near the settlement.

At its top fluttered a bright red flag.

The pole was high.

The flag was enormous.

Even from a great distance it was impossible to miss.

Looks like the survivors are almost here.

Over the past several days, Lila had been cooking nonstop.

Batch after batch of hearty comfort food disappeared into the Vault.

Beef Stew.

Hot Chili Beef.

Grilled Fish.

Seasoned Grilled Beef.

Seasoned Lamb Skillet.

Spicy Mixed Stew.

Long Johns.

Donuts.

Crispy Fried Pork Bites.

Every fragrant dish was carefully packed away.

Before the survivors arrived, she planned to make one final round of favorites.

Loaded Spicy Hot Pot Bowl.

Noodle Soup.

Hot & Sour Noodle Soup.

Spicy Noodle Soup.

The Loaded Spicy Hot Pot Bowl followed her homemade recipe.

The noodle dishes were even easier. She had already stockpiled packaged versions before the Collapse. All she needed to do was cook them.

Since she was making such huge quantities, she didn't bother cutting corners.

She prepared a massive assortment of ingredients all at once.

That way, every bowl she ate in the future would be loaded with variety and feel like a proper feast.

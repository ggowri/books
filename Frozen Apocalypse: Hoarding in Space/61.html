Chapter 60

Early in the morning, Lila lazed under the duvet and refused to get up.

The room was nineteen degrees, which was perfectly bearable, so she stayed tucked in and read a novel on her phone.

The signal icon at the top of the screen flickered.

The signal had actually come back.

Surprised and delighted, she quickly refreshed WhatsApp and the other social platforms.

Most people must already have known the signal had returned. Anyone whose phone still had battery was posting online, and there were official government notices too.

By now, in most estates with basements, residents had moved underground.

Lila already knew that.

With nothing better to do, she opened the Oakwood residents' group chat, wanting to see whether there was any news about Maggie's family.

But hardly anyone was speaking in the group, and nothing useful came out.

Forget it.

Living alone deep in the mountains, even with the signal restored, she found she was no longer interested in much.

Lila put down her phone, dressed and got out of bed.

The daily routine was still the same. Light the inglenook fireplace and the two kitchen ranges.

Once the house had warmed up, she fed the chickens and watered the vegetables.

The chicks had grown a little more, though their soft fluff was still there. They were still adorable.

The fire in the inglenook fireplace burned steadily. Lila took three sweet potatoes from the Vault, poked around in the embers and buried them in the hot coals.

Then she grabbed a handful of millet for the clay pot, added a few red dates and plenty of water, and set it on the fireplace hob to cook.

Lila went to the kitchen and placed a non-stick pan on the range.

Into the pan went oil, soy sauce, vinegar, sugar and a little MSG, all boiled together over a fierce heat.

Once the whole kitchen smelled delicious, she took the pan off the stove.

She poured the sauce into a clean empty jar.

From the Vault, she took out spring onions, cucumber, green peppers, celery, coriander and more, then chopped everything into small pieces. It came to exactly one jarful.

She soaked all the vegetables in the cooked sauce and stirred them evenly with clean, dry chopsticks.

She spooned some into a bowl, added a spoonful of the sauce, then covered the rest with cling film, put on the lid and stored the jar on the lowest shelf of the rack.

Lila did not dare keep eating the pickles she had bought. She was afraid that if she ate too much, she would one day become sick of them and never want them again.

Homemade pickles were the most reliable. She could eat them over and over without getting tired of them.

This method of pickling was particularly good. Her grandmother had taught it to her years ago.

The small bowl she had taken out today had not yet absorbed much flavour, but dipped in the sauce, it was already edible.

Lila placed the little bowl of pickles on the dining table.

By then, the clay pot on the stove was beginning to bubble softly.

Lila used a cloth to lift off the lid, so the foam would not spill over later.

She stirred the pot with a ceramic spoon. The mellow scent of millet porridge mingled with the sweetness of the red dates. It smelled wonderful.

She wanted to add a little sweetness.

She went to the kitchen, found the jar of honey and spooned a little into the clay pot.

The kitchen was cool, so the honey had already thickened. Lila stirred the honey-coated spoon round and round in the pot.

She watched the honey on the spoon grow less and less until it vanished completely, melting into the millet porridge.

Another fragrance joined the air in the room, the sweet scent of honey.

When it looked about right, Lila used a cloth to lift the clay pot down.

She ladled a small bowl of porridge and set it aside to cool.

Then she used the fire poker to rake out the buried sweet potatoes. They were thoroughly roasted now. When she poked one with her finger, it was soft, giving off the rich smell of baked sweet potato.

Lila found a stainless steel plate, lined it with kitchen paper, picked up the ash-dusted sweet potatoes and carried them back to the east parlour.

A lovely breakfast began.

She gently peeled open the skin of a sweet potato. White steam drifted out with the thick fragrance of roasted sweetness, revealing tender golden flesh inside.

Lila was not in a hurry to eat it. She let it release its heat for a while.

The porridge beside it was no longer quite so hot. Lila carefully scooped up a spoonful and put it into her mouth.

Thick, soft and smooth, with the rich aroma of red dates and honey.

Delicious.

A bite of sweet potato, a spoonful of porridge, and a mouthful of crisp pickled vegetables. Another steaming hot meal.

After breakfast, there was still one bowl of porridge and one sweet potato left.

Lila peeled the sweet potato and put it into the puppies' food dish, then added half a bowl of porridge.

The temperature was just right now.

She placed the dish on the floor, picked up the two puppies and guided their little heads towards the plate. After that, there was no need to manage them.

The two puppy heads automatically began clearing the food from the dish.

There was still half a bowl of porridge left, so Lila picked out some sweet potato skins with bits of flesh stuck to them and added those to the bowl too.

She took it to the west room and poured everything into the chicks' feeder.

The chicks knew that whenever Lila arrived, there was food.

They chirped and clucked, scrambling towards the feeder.

One chick grabbed a piece of sweet potato skin in its beak and flapped its wings, darting into a corner as if it meant to keep the whole thing to itself.

The others, having failed to get any sweet potato skin, chased after the fleeing chick.

Watching the chicks fight over food, chasing and snatching from one another, had its own particular charm.

After eating her fill, Lila took Maggie's dressing table out of the Vault.

The leftmost drawer was unlocked, while the middle and right drawers were both locked.

She used a hammer and broke open the lock on the right in a few quick strikes.

She wanted to see what was inside.

The drawer contained some useless documents and a notebook.

Lila suddenly grew interested and opened the notebook. But what was inside was not a diary at all.

It was a ledger.

Bought Lila a new school bag, $21.

Lila ate half of Anna's cake, $1.50.

Lila used a packet of tissues, $20.

Lila ate from the fruit platter, $1.

...

Bought Lila a birthday present, $1.20.

...

Bought Lila a birthday present, $99.

Lila was stunned by how detailed the ledger was.

For a moment, she did not know whether she should blame Maggie for being so petty or thank her for paying such close attention to her.

After Lila's parents died, Victor had placed most of Robert and Helen's savings into fixed-term deposits. A small portion had been left with Lila for daily expenses.

Although Lila had been young at the time, she had known not to spend other people's money. She had paid for almost all of her own expenses.

She had even often taken the initiative to buy groceries for the whole family to eat.

When she grew older, whenever there were holidays or family meals out, Lila was usually the one who paid.

She had believed that giving went both ways.

But it turned out that even the cheapest things she had received for free had been written down in this ledger.

Even a twenty-pence packet of tissues.

As for the ninety-nine-pence birthday present...

Last year's birthday present had been a diamond necklace in a beautifully made box.

Lila rarely bought jewellery, so she did not know much about it.

After Maggie gave it to her, she had carefully put it away in her dressing table and had never once been willing to wear it.

So it had been a ninety-nine-pence fake.

It was maddening.

She had not been ruthless enough.

Furious, Lila smashed the lock on the middle drawer.

The drawer opened.

Inside were gold jewellery boxes, one after another.

Chapter 86

Lila tightened her grip on the binoculars.

Then she looked closer.

Much closer.

The excavators were digging foundations. Their long mechanical arms rose and fell, tearing chunks of frozen earth from the ground.

Workers moved around the site bundled in so many layers they barely looked human.

Some directed traffic.

Others unloaded materials.

Construction was underway.

Not houses.

A refuge.

Memories from her previous life flashed through her mind.

Government notices about temporary settlements during the Long Freeze.

Announcements promising priority relocation for urban residents.

Back then, she'd been trapped and isolated. Most of the information she'd heard came from Annabel or rumors passed among survivors.

She had never paid much attention.

Now she finally understood.

The rumors had been true.

And the refuge was being built right in front of her.

Lila lowered the binoculars.

Her hands felt cold.

The construction vehicles continued moving.

A few more days and the foundations would be complete.

Then walls would rise.

Roofs would follow.

Rows of buildings would emerge from the snow.

And eventually, people would move in.

A lot of people.

Lila couldn't quite describe what she was feeling.

Anger.

Frustration.

Anxiety.

She was angry because her peaceful refuge was being invaded.

She was frustrated because the comfortable life she'd spent so long preparing for was being disrupted.

Most of all, she was uneasy.

Terrified that her quiet existence might disappear forever.

She returned inside the mansion.

Slowly, she removed her outer layers and collapsed into the giant dog bed.

For a long time she simply stared at the ceiling.

None of this felt fair.

Eventually, she sat upright.

No.

This wasn't helping.

Reality wasn't going to change.

If she wanted to survive, she'd have to adapt.

Lila immediately got to work.

She inspected the courtyard walls.

Still solid.

Still secure.

Next, she tested the electrified perimeter fencing.

Everything functioned perfectly.

Then she headed to Rock Hollow.

She checked the ventilation systems.

The electrical connections.

The emergency supplies.

Worst case scenario, she could always retreat into the cave.

And honestly, after thinking about it carefully, the situation wasn't quite as terrible as it first appeared.

The construction vehicles seemed to belong to the government.

The survivors moving into the area would almost certainly be regulated by the authorities.

Things would become inconvenient.

But not necessarily dangerous.

That thought eased some of the tension in her chest.

By nightfall, darkness had swallowed the valley.

Dressed once again in heavy winter gear, Lila stepped outside.

She positioned the binoculars at one of the observation openings in the wall and scanned the area below.

The valley wasn't completely dark.

A light glowed from a house on the right side.

Another shone from a house on the left.

Lila stared.

That meant there were still two occupied homes within roughly two kilometers of the Lower Vale Mansion.

As for the construction site, the vehicles were gone.

Only piles of steel rebar, cement, and bricks remained.

The workers had left before sunset.

Construction would continue tomorrow.

Strangely, seeing those two distant lights made her feel better.

Apparently she wasn't the only person whose peace had been interrupted.

That thought brought an unexpected sense of comfort.

Even so, worries lingered in her mind as she crawled into bed.

Eventually, exhaustion won.

The next morning, Lila was eating a bowl of wontons when someone knocked on the front gate.

The wonton in her chopsticks slipped and splashed back into the broth.

Hot soup landed on the back of her hand.

She immediately set the bowl aside.

Crossing the main hall, she began putting on layers.

Expedition down parka.

Mink hat.

Winter survival gear.

Everything.

Only after she was fully dressed did she head outside.

The cold hit instantly.

The warmth from breakfast vanished within seconds.

Lila peeked through a crack in the gate.

Two men stood outside.

Both wore white snow camouflage over military coats.

Their collars were raised high.

Frost coated their hats.

Their faces were wrapped in thick scarves, leaving only their eyes exposed.

Ice crystals clung to their eyelashes.

Even through all those layers, they carried an unmistakably military presence.

The refuge foundation had only been completed yesterday.

And officials were already making visits.

"What is it?" Lila called.

"Good morning."

The taller man spoke first.

His voice was muffled behind the scarf.

"I'm Captain Walker, commander of Homeland Response Corps – Lower Vale Command."

He slowly removed a folder from inside his coat.

"A refuge has been established in the village. We're registering local residents."

Opening the folder, he displayed a few pages from a distance.

"It'll only take a few minutes."

Lila considered it.

Refusing would only create unnecessary attention.

She unlocked the gate.

At the same time, a strand of Vault Lightning waited silently in her palm, ready if needed.

The moment the gate opened, freezing wind rushed into the courtyard.

The two officers stood knee deep in snow.

Captain Walker stepped forward.

Snow crunched beneath his boots.

His gaze lingered briefly on the reinforced roof covering the courtyard.

A flicker of surprise crossed his eyes.

But he said nothing.

Instead, he opened the folder.

As he turned a page, his glove brushed the paper.

The edge immediately frayed from the cold.

"Name: Lila Spencer."

He glanced up.

"Twenty three years old. Correct?"

Lila blinked.

Apparently they already knew quite a bit about her.

Then again, the property was legally registered in her name.

Government records probably explained that.

"Permanent address?" Walker asked.

"Here."

Lila didn't elaborate.

Walker made a note.

"At the moment, only three permanent households remain in the village."

"Your situation is somewhat unique."

"Beginning next month, the refuge will distribute food and supplies to all residents, including registered locals."

He flipped to another page.

"You'll need proof of identity."

"Distribution hours are from eleven in the morning until two in the afternoon."

"Collection takes place in the Residential Quarter."

Lila frowned.

"Only three hours?"

Walker nodded.

"Those are the warmest hours of the day."

"Outside that window, temperatures can reach minus sixty or seventy degrees."

"People could freeze waiting in line."

He closed the folder and removed a small laminated card from his coat.

Text covered both sides.

Handing it over, he said, "Keep this safe."

"It contains the Temporary Settlement Regulations and supply collection instructions."

"Fill in your information."

"You'll need this card whenever you collect supplies."

Lila accepted it.

"Thank you."

"One more thing."

Walker adjusted his scarf higher.

Only his eyes remained visible.

"Starting next week, displaced survivors from surrounding towns will begin arriving."

"Some of them have gone without proper food for weeks."

"Many walked here across frozen waterways."

He paused.

"The village won't stay this quiet."

His eyes met hers.

"You're a young woman living alone on an isolated property."

"Stay alert."

"Keep your doors and windows secured."

"Don't leave bright lights visible at night."

"If you run into trouble, report to the refuge and speak with the soldiers on duty."

The warning was direct.

The meaning was even clearer.

The world had changed.

When survival was on the line, some people would do almost anything.

And the authorities couldn't be everywhere at once.

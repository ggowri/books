Chapter 106

Four vacancies for doctors.

Applicants had to pass a professional medical examination.

Salary: 120 Survival Credits per month.

Six openings for cafeteria chefs.

Applicants had to pass a practical cooking assessment.

Salary: 80 Survival Credits per month.

Eight positions for serving staff.

Salary: 70 Survival Credits per month.

Three Legal Officer positions.

Responsibilities included handling minor crimes and civil disputes.

Applicants had to hold a valid law licence and pass the Refuge legal examination.

Salary: 100 Survival Credits per month.

Two cleaners for the Administrative Quarter.

Salary: 60 Survival Credits per month.

Two Vehicle Maintenance Engineers.

Salary: 100 Survival Credits per month.

Two Boiler Maintenance Engineers.

Salary: 100 Survival Credits per month.

Crop Cultivation Biologists.

No limit on recruitment.

Salary: 120 Survival Credits per month.

Beside the recruitment notices sat another thick stack of papers.

They listed the daily rotating jobs, alternative ways to earn Survival Credits, and a complete price list for living expenses inside the refuge.

Rotating Jobs

Day Patrol: 2 Credits per day

Night Patrol: 3 Credits per night

Guard Duty: 2 Credits per day

Street Cleaning: 2 Credits per day

Firewood Collection: 2 Credits per bundle

Water Convoy Assistant: 1 Credit per trip

Day Boiler Operator: 2 Credits per day

Night Boiler Operator: 3 Credits per shift

Additional temporary jobs and their daily Credit rewards would be posted on the cafeteria noticeboard every morning.

Patrols, cleaning, and guard duty all worked on a rotation schedule, so it could take several days before someone's turn came around.

Cutting firewood in the mountains, however, had no limit and remained the primary way residents earned Survival Credits.

Other Ways to Earn Credits

Recycling clothing:

Spring or autumn clothing: 2 Credits

Winter clothing: 5 Credits

Join supply recovery missions into the city.

Recovered items would be assessed and priced on the spot.

Successfully growing vegetable pots earned either 1 Credit per plant or 2 Credits per planter.

Living Costs Inside the Refuge

Room rent:

10 Credits per room each month.

Roommates could split the cost.

Winter coat:

10 Credits each.

Supplies are limited. Thank you for your understanding.

Mirror use:

1 Credit per mirror.

Other daily necessities were available from the warehouse upon request.

Firewood:

2 Credits per bundle.

Cafeteria Prices

Soft Bread Roll: 1 Credit

Multigrain Roll: 0.5 Credit

White Rice: 1 Credit

Mixed Grain Pilaf: 0.5 Credit

Country Stew: 0.6 Credit

Pickled Vegetables: 0.2 Credit

Soup: 0.1 Credit

The cafeteria kept prices low.

Rice and white flour remained expensive, but nearly everything else was affordable.

Anyone willing to work hard and accept simple meals could still keep themselves well fed.

After collecting their weekly ration, residents each received two information sheets before quietly leaving.

Lila stayed where she was for more than twenty minutes.

Only after the cafeteria had nearly emptied did she walk to the front desk, casually pocket two copies of the paperwork, then join the queue for her mixed grains.

She had no intention of applying for any of the jobs.

She was simply curious about the local economy.

Knowing the value of wages and supplies might prove useful one day.

If the opportunity arose, she could always barter items from the Vault for whatever resources she happened to need.

Cedric had barely spoken during the meeting.

His role was to assist William Sterling with the day-to-day operation of the refuge.

To put it bluntly, no refuge director could personally handle every decision.

The countless routine matters that required someone to make a call all landed on Cedric's desk.

That had been how authority was divided in their previous positions as well.

Having control over those smaller decisions had given Cedric more than a few advantages over the years.

During the meeting, Annabel had spent the entire time staring at Captain Walker.

She never even noticed Cedric sitting quietly off to one side.

Afterward, she focused solely on collecting supplies, making it even less likely that she'd see him.

As the crowd gradually dispersed, Cedric returned to the residential quarter.

He had managed to upset his wife first thing that morning.

If he didn't get home quickly to smooth things over, he'd have an even rougher day ahead.

William remained seated.

He and Captain Walker still had interviews to conduct for the refuge's key positions.

Leo Turner studied the recruitment sheet with surprise.

"So that's what they're paying doctors."

One hundred and twenty Survival Credits a month.

Not bad at all.

Callum leaned over his shoulder and let out a dramatic sigh.

"Mate, that's a serious salary. If I ever can't afford my rent, promise you won't kick me out."

Leo laughed.

"With your business instincts? You'll probably be richer than the rest of us."

Then he pointed back at the list.

"See if there's anything on there you can do."

Callum groaned.

"Forget it. Selling things is the only skill I've got. All this technical stuff? Not a chance."

After a brief pause, he added more seriously,

"Still, I get why doctors earn the most. Getting sick is about the worst thing that can happen now, so medical people are worth their weight in gold."

The two of them chatted as they headed toward home, weaving through the thinning crowd.

Callum happened to glance down.

His eyes immediately locked onto a familiar pair of boots.

Reindeer-hide boots.

The very same kind he'd sold.

He looked up.

Standing before him was someone who practically screamed wealth.

In the middle of the Collapse, Lila looked richer than anyone else he'd seen.

He didn't need to inspect anything beyond her clothing.

Every piece of gear she wore practically announced that she was loaded.

His eyes widened.

"Hey! It's you!"

He recognised her instantly.

Lila raised a finger to her lips.

"Shh."

Callum understood immediately.

She didn't want attention.

"Oh, right. Fancy running into you."

He carefully avoided saying her name.

"It really is a coincidence. I never expected to find you here."

A rare smile appeared on Lila's face.

If Callum had made it here...

Then the rest of the residents from Oakwood Estate probably had too.

And if certain people were as impossible to get rid of as ever...

They were probably somewhere inside this refuge as well.

She kept those thoughts to herself.

She and Callum weren't close enough for that sort of conversation.

"Well... we'd better get going. Maybe we'll catch up another time."

Callum knew when to leave things alone.

Since Lila clearly wanted to keep a low profile, he wrapped up the conversation without another question.

He led Leo away.

"Who was that?"

Leo rarely took much interest in strangers.

But the young woman was dressed far too well.

Her skin was healthy, her face smooth and pale, looking nothing like someone who had survived months of brutal cold.

His curiosity got the better of him.

"...An old customer."

Callum kept her identity to himself.

"You noticed she's wearing the same reindeer-hide boots we are."

Leo nodded.

"That is a pretty strange coincidence."

Callum frowned thoughtfully.

"Still... how come we never saw her before?"

They had lived in the same estate.

She hadn't travelled with the rest of them.

So how exactly had she arrived here?

"Maybe she was out of town when the temperatures dropped."

Leo shrugged.

It seemed as good an explanation as any.

...

After collecting her mixed grains, Lila tucked them into her backpack before studying the Credit guide again.

One line immediately caught her attention.

Captain Walker regularly led recovery missions into the city.

If she could join one of those...

Wouldn't that basically be unlimited free shopping?

Food wasn't even the main attraction.

If she could quietly stash away a few high-end sofas, proper beds, or expensive furniture that she'd never normally buy...

She might finally get to enjoy things she'd never imagined owning.

She looked toward the front of the cafeteria.

A long queue had already formed.

People were lining up to register.

Captain Walker stood nearby, brow tightly furrowed as he watched the applicants.

William's two assistants were busy recording everyone's information.

Ryan Holloway rubbed his temples in frustration.

"If you don't have the qualifications, don't sign up."

"You're only making extra work for everyone."

He had just watched an elderly man who hadn't even finished primary school apply to become a Legal Officer.

There was simply no way that was happening.

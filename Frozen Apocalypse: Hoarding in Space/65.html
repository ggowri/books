Chapter 64

Back upstairs, Lila put on a mask and started cleaning the west room.
She had laid plastic sheeting on the floor earlier to keep it from getting dirty.
Now she pulled the sheeting up in one go, rolled the deep litter bedding inside, stuffed the whole bundle into a sack, and tossed it into the rubbish area of the Vault.

Since the chicks had only lived in the west room for a few days, the room was mostly clean once the plastic was removed.
She sprayed the floor and walls with alcohol, disinfected everything, then ran a mop over the floor twice. Thoroughly clean.

Lila admired her work. On the heated hearth bench in the west room, two pots of vegetables had already sprouted little green shoots.
But she couldn't remember what she had planted in them. She would have to wait until the shoots grew bigger to tell.

After all that cleaning and moving the chicken coop, Lila was filthy.
She took off her padded trousers and coat, opened the front door, and faced the biting cold. She shook the clothes vigorously twice to knock off the dust, then quickly shut the door.
She stuffed the padded clothes into the washing machine, grabbed her lounge wear, and headed to the bathroom.

Half an hour later, Lila emerged clean and neatly dressed.
After a shower, she felt fresh all over.

She had been fairly active and couldn't be bothered to cook from scratch again.
But she didn't want to waste the takeaway food she had stockpiled, either. She simply put the clay pot with the leftover congee from breakfast on the fireplace hob to warm up.
She took two large steamed buns from the Vault and put a small plate of pickled vegetables she had made earlier on the table.

The pickles had cured nicely now and tasted much better.
The congee on the hob was hot. Congee with two big meat buns. Very satisfying.

After eating her fill, Lila sat down at the writing desk.
She pulled a piece of cardstock from the drawer and made a chart:
Clean the chicken coop every half month. Water the vegetables and flowers every two days.
She marked the dates carefully so she wouldn't forget.

She was determined to ride out the Collapse in comfort, but she didn't want to become completely lazy. Having a routine gave her a little structure and made life more satisfying.
She taped the chart to the door as a reminder.

Lila felt like some milk tea.
She hadn't had a proper cup of milk tea since the Long Freeze began.
She browsed the Vault for a while and settled on a large mug.
A big mug of milk tea would be much more satisfying.

Excited, she took the mug out, eager to set it on the table.
But she must have been too worked up. When she put the mug down, it made a loud bang that startled her.
Goodness. She had put it down so hard. She almost broke the mug.

Nervously, Lila examined the mug from every angle. Only when she was sure there were no cracks did she relax.
Then she froze.

Put the mug down hard?
That meant she could eject anything from the Vault with great force, couldn't she?
What about a stick? A knife? A sword?

Lila wanted to test her theory. She hurried to the kitchen, picked a small twig out of the firebox, and stored it in the Vault.
Then, focusing on the chopping board, she ejected the twig with force.
It shot out, hit the chopping board, and made a sharp crack.

Lila walked over to the board. Unfortunately, there was no mark. The force wasn't strong enough yet.
She tried again. This time, the twig bounced off the board and seemed to hit harder than before.

"Maybe this is something I can train?"

Lila felt a thrill of excitement. If she could train her Vault ability, she would have a powerful weapon to protect herself.
She took an arrow from her compound bow set. The tip was made of super hard alloy steel and looked extremely tough.

Lila aimed at the chopping board and ejected the arrow with all her focus. Thud. The arrow stuck firmly into the board.
It worked. It definitely worked.
So there was some lethal potential. All she needed was concentration and practice to make the arrows fly with more power.

Lila went back to the east parlour and added one more line to her chart: Practise archery for 10 minutes every day.

Why only ten minutes?
Because anything longer would drain too much of her energy.
Just from those few test ejections today, she already felt a bit worn out.
It was the same fatigue she had felt when she was stockpiling massive amounts into the Vault.
Her ability had limits. If she pushed too far, she would get tired or even faint.

But a small limitation was nothing compared to a huge advantage. She would just train through it.
This discovery made Lila incredibly happy. So happy that, for once, she took out a small cake.

A small cake with a large mug of milk tea. Pure bliss.
She put the milk tea and the cake on the hearth bench, propped up her tablet, found a feel good movie, and ate while watching. Perfect happiness.

The sweet smell of the cake spread from the warm bench. The two little ones, who had been tumbling around on the floor, caught the scent. They snuffled and whined and waddled over to the edge of the bench.
They were too short to reach. All they could do was paw at Lila's slippers and yelp upwards.

Lila couldn't see the dogs, only hear them.
"Quiet!"

She couldn't be bothered to explain that they couldn't have any. A sharp command would have to do.
The two little ones were startled into silence.
But their quiet lasted only five seconds. Then the sniffling and whining started again.

Lila pretended not to hear and kept eating, kept watching.
Cute as they were, she wasn't going to spoil them rotten.

When the movie ended, Lila had eaten only half the cake. She had a big appetite, but she got full on sweet things quickly.
She put the leftover cake back in its box and stowed it in the Vault.
She let out a burp. She was a bit too full.
She really shouldn't reward herself like that next time.

She opened a bottle of cola and downed half of it in a few gulps to cut through the richness.

Today was distribution day, when supplies were handed out every three days. The estate staff had been waiting at the entrance for a long time, but no supplies arrived.
Mrs Harris paced anxiously back and forth. She really didn't want the people in the basement to go hungry.

Then the dedicated landline phone rang. It was the higher ups.
There was some talk on the other end. Mrs Harris murmured a few "Mmhmm"s and hung up.
The temperature had reached minus eighty degrees. Two of the cold resistant supply lorries had broken down on the road.
The supplies wouldn't arrive today. Probably not tomorrow either.

Mrs Harris gathered her thoughts, picked up the megaphone, and shouted, "Everyone gather in the central area. Meeting!"

Since they had moved to the basement, they had only had one meeting. That meant whatever was coming was important.
People put down what they were doing and slowly gathered.

Edward thought the meeting might be about handing out supplies. He brought Maggie along. Anna was still chained up in the basement.
The right side of Maggie's face was red and swollen, ugly. Pus still oozed faintly from the wound near her ear.
She draped a scarf over her face and stood in the crowd.

"I have something to tell you," Mrs Harris said with difficulty. "The supply lorry broke down on the way. Tonight's dinner and tomorrow's breakfast and dinner – you'll have to manage on your own."

Chapter 57

Lila set Patches down, and the two puppies immediately tumbled together, play-fighting in a little heap.

Their tiny eyes had a faint blue tint to them, bright and clear. They could already see everything around them.

Lila looked at the dog crate beside the sofa and felt they no longer needed to be protected inside it.

She threw the old pad from the crate straight into the rubbish sack, then stacked the crate in the miscellaneous corner of the Vault.

From the Vault, she took out Anna's duvet to make a dog bed.

Although Lila felt a little uncomfortable about it, judging from all the years she had known Anna, Anna's things were at least clean.

Maggie washed them often, and the duvet still carried the familiar scent of laundry detergent.

"Oh, forget it. Look at the times we're living in. No point being precious."

Once Lila got over it mentally, she placed the duvet on the table in the main hall.

The table there had become her little workbench. Any job she did not want dirtying the east parlour was done here.

She stripped off the duvet cover, sprayed it all over with alcohol, then threw it into the washing machine.

That made her feel a little better.

She sprayed the inner duvet thoroughly as well, inside and out, then folded it in half.

She stitched all four sides of the folded duvet shut, turning it into a very thick, large cushion.

The cushion was honestly too big. Lila imagined how large Snowy and Patches would be once they grew up, then decided it still needed to be made smaller.

She stuffed Anna's clothes into the four edges of the cushion, then folded the sides inwards and stitched them completely shut.

The duvet had now become a proper dog bed, with high raised sides and a hollow in the middle.

Lila pressed the dipped centre. That part was two layers of duvet thick. Even though it sank in slightly, it was still very soft and comfortable.

She squeezed the raised edges too. They were plump, supportive and soft.

By then, the duvet cover in the washing machine was finished.

Lila laid it in front of the inglenook fireplace to dry. Before long, it was completely dry.

Then she carried on with the work.

She placed the duvet cover over the dog bed and measured it out, cutting it to the right size with scissors.

Then she wrapped the cover fully around the dog bed and carefully stitched it together, inside and out.

Before long, the cover fitted neatly over the whole bed.

The cover was pale green, decorated with tiny red and yellow flowers, making the dog bed look fresh and pretty.

Because the cover had been washed with Lila's own laundry detergent, it now smelled just like her own sheets and duvet covers. She no longer felt uncomfortable about it at all.

Satisfied, Lila placed the dog bed on the floor and put Patches and Snowy inside.

The moment the two little ones touched the soft surface, they began rolling around in it, thrilled beyond measure.

They were still so small, yet they somehow knew at once that this bed was theirs.

After rolling about until they were tired, the two puppies curled up against one another and fell asleep.

They looked so comfortable.

For a moment, Lila even had the urge to make a giant dog bed for herself.

She took Edward and Maggie's duvets out of the Vault, stripped off the covers and threw those into the washing machine.

She sprayed both inner duvets with alcohol, then prepared to hang them over the railings in the courtyard to air.

It was a bold decision.

Once again, Lila put on her reindeer-hide boots, expedition down parka and all her protective gear. Then she carried the two duvets and opened the door.

The moment the door opened, the courtyard's cold air rushed straight at her, forming layer upon layer of white mist at the doorway. Lila quickly shut the door behind her.

Even through the mask, the air she breathed into her lungs felt as though it was full of ice splinters.

Lila swiftly draped the two duvets over the washing line, tugged them left and right to smooth out the creases, then swung her arms and patted them a few times before hurrying back indoors.

Only after the door was shut again did she let out a breath of relief.

Even though she had been quick, the skin around Lila's eyes had still been chilled numb.

"Terrifying."

She had experienced this temperature in her previous life too.

Lila slowly removed her gear, put everything neatly back in the cupboard, and changed into her usual clothes for staying at home.

There was no rush to make herself a giant dog bed. She would leave those two duvets outside in the minus seventy-degree cold for a while.

Let the cold disinfect them.

After all that fussing about, she was genuinely tired, and she was definitely hungry.

Lila went to the kitchen and took a neat slab of pork belly from the Vault. She rinsed it under clean water, then cut it into chunks about the size of mahjong tiles.

She crouched down and lit the kitchen range. Firelight flickered inside the firebox, throwing shifting patches of brightness and shadow across her face.

The frying pan was already scorching hot. She tipped in the pork belly, skin-side down, and it immediately began to hiss and smoke.

Using the spatula, she pressed down on one piece and watched the skin slowly blister and turn golden in the heat. Her grandmother had always said that was what made it fragrant when braised.

She grabbed a handful of ginger slices, star anise and dried chillies, then tossed them into the pan. The burst of aroma made Lila lean back.

Once the heat was right, she lifted the pork belly out, scrubbed the frying pan clean, added oil and began caramelising sugar.

When the sugar turned amber and bubbled finely, Lila moved quickly, tipping the fried pork belly back in and stir-frying it.

After only a few turns, the pork belly was evenly coated in the caramel, glossy and beautiful.

She lifted the frying pan off the heat and poured all the caramel-coated pork belly into a clay pot, then added boiling water from beside the side hob.

She put on the lid and carried the pot to the fireplace hob in the east parlour.

The braised pork belly could simmer for a long while, so she left the clay pot to stew slowly over the fire.

Then Lila returned to the kitchen.

She washed the oil from her hands and rinsed a bowl of rice in a large porcelain bowl before placing it on the steamer tray.

She cracked two eggs into another porcelain bowl, beat them evenly, added diced mushrooms and a little oil, then placed that bowl on the steamer as well.

After cleaning the kitchen, more than ten minutes had passed.

Lila lifted the lid and, using a cloth, took out the steamed egg custard.

The timing was perfect. The egg custard was golden, glossy and tender at a glance.

Afraid it would cool too quickly, Lila carried it straight into the east parlour.

The east parlour was filled with the scent of braised pork belly. After setting down the egg custard, she went to check the clay pot.

Inside, the sauce bubbled gently. It was the warm, comforting sound of meat simmering.

It still needed a little longer. Pork belly cut into mahjong-sized pieces had to be stewed until soft and rich.

Lila was in no hurry. She sat on the sofa, drank tea and watched her drama while she waited.

The owner was in no rush, but the two puppies on the floor were frantic.

They had never eaten human food before. Recently, since opening their eyes, it was as if their sense of smell had suddenly awakened. They had become interested in food beyond milk.

Now they were squinting slightly, heads tilted up, sniffing the aroma of braised pork belly in the air.

When the smell became too tempting, they could not help letting out tiny whines.

Snowy and Patches moved in exactly the same way. Seeing it, Lila could not resist picking up her phone to take a photo.

Adorable and ridiculous.

Another ten minutes or so passed before Lila lifted the lid.

The sauce had reduced until thick and glossy, clinging to every piece of meat. Each chunk trembled softly. She poked the skin with her chopsticks. It was tender, rich and cooked through.

There was no need to transfer it to another dish.

Lila used a cloth to lift the clay pot off the fire and set it on a heatproof mat on the table.

Time to eat.

Chapter 77

After two more days in the basement, supplies of flu medicine finally arrived. Not a miracle cure, just standard cold remedies sourced from elsewhere.

The day before the medicines arrived, twelve residents in the quarantine area had passed away. Mrs Harris distributed the medicine immediately, prioritising those already sick. Even those isolating alone in the basement corners received a dose.

Sophie, whose symptoms had been mild, recovered quickly after taking her medication. The next day, several others with lighter symptoms began to improve. Those who had been seriously ill from the start, however, did not survive.

A shadow of death hung over the entire basement. Residents stayed masked, huddled in their small spaces, afraid to move.

Leo and Cedric avoided leaving their room entirely, relying solely on their stockpiled food. Venturing out was too risky. Other nearby estate shelters were seeing similar deaths from the outbreak. Bodies were removed and buried in the snowy grounds surrounding the estates.

Thanks to effective quarantine, few new infections occurred beyond the initial cases.

Annabel's household had eaten all their reserves, subsisting only on the thin communal porridge. Over time, doubts about Cedric's promises began to creep in...they might never receive any message.

Edward finished his portion of porridge in a single gulp, slamming the bowl onto the table. The sound startled Annabel and Maggie, though no one spoke; clearing the household stock had been Annabel's decision.

With too many eyes in the basement, it was impossible to retrieve extra food without being noticed.

Lila suddenly felt a slight chill, a mild cold creeping in. Two doses of the cold remedy from her kit quickly restored her health.

The vegetables in the west room had grown substantially. To encourage better growth, Lila thinned out the densely packed plants. She filled a ceramic bowl with tender green leaves. Even though her Vault contained plenty of produce, home-grown vegetables felt particularly wholesome and rewarding.

She scrambled two eggs into a fragrant sauce and simmered a pot of rice. Realising something was missing, she retrieved a cucumber from the Vault, washing it clean. Today's meal would be vegetables with egg sauce.

Tender greens coated in rich, savoury egg sauce paired perfectly with the rice. Lila finished a large bowl of greens and half the rice pot.

Her days of eating and sleeping were pleasantly simple. She reminded herself to stay sharp, practising self-defence and improving her medical knowledge.

A week later, the influenza outbreak was finally under control. Only roughly half of the residents had survived.

Mrs Harris exhausted the last of the disinfectant and alcohol, sanitising the entire basement. Vacant areas were repopulated with families and the elderly. Fewer people remained in the underground parking area.

The catastrophe had come swiftly and passed just as quickly. But the challenge of surviving the extreme cold was far from over.

Whether due to depleted resources or reduced staff, supply deliveries shifted from every three days to every five. Daily rations were reduced further. Many households had nearly finished their stockpiles, leaving everyone pale and thin, surviving on thin porridge.

Sophie observed the other residents through the door crack, noticing everyone looked unwell. She covered her own healthy appearance with dirt on her face and messy hair, carrying her hot water bottle out.

Leo and Cedric had a similar idea, making themselves appear dishevelled.

At last, with the disease behind them, survivors could breathe easier.

In the early hours, everyone slept. The basement was pitch dark. Some snored, some murmured in dreams, children nestled with parents, all deeply asleep.

Suddenly, a flash of lightning illuminated the world outside, as though daylight had returned. The first thunderclap shook the ground. Not the usual summer rumble, but a sharp, explosive crack, like the sky itself had split.

The lightning startled most of the sleeping residents. Some murmured prayers, some fell silent, mouths open but no sound came.

Another bolt struck, closer this time. Some cried quietly, covering their mouths. No one understood the outside conditions...rain? But the temperature was below minus seventy. How could it rain?

The thunderstorm felt as sudden as the earlier temperature drop, intensifying the residents' fear.

Outside, the situation was worse than they imagined. Lightning struck, yet there was no rain. At minus eighty-two degrees, water had frozen into ice crystals. Lightning arcs glowed blue-violet, striking the frozen ground, momentarily lighting the world.

The first objects to ignite were trees. Frozen, brittle branches shattered explosively, sparks scattering. The trunks caught fire, flames licked the black sky.

Buildings caught fire too. Lightning struck rooftops, windows erupted with flames, glass shattered, falling to the snow in dark pits. Thick smoke poured from the buildings, sinking in the frigid air, rolling along the snow into the basement.

Multiple fires spread across the city, including Annabel's estate. Smoke flooded the basement, causing coughing and panic. Those still asleep were woken by the acrid fumes.

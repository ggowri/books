Chapter 33

The two little puppies had been restless the moment they left the warmth of Lila's hands, but as soon as they touched the soft blanket inside the cage, they settled down again.

Their tiny heads nudged together, and soon they were curled up side by side, sleeping contentedly.

Lila let out a quiet breath and placed the dog cage and the cardboard box of chicks on the floor, about two metres from the fireplace.

There, they could feel the warmth without getting too hot. The temperature was just right.

Once the little animals were finally settled, Lila turned and went to the bathroom.

Before the German retrofit crew had left, they had cleaned the entire house thoroughly. The bathroom in particular was spotless.

When she switched on the light, the room was bright, spacious and immaculately clean.

The washbasin stood on the right, just inside the door. It had a broad marble counter and a large mirror. Behind the mirror were hidden compartments for toiletries, and beneath the counter were two sizeable storage cupboards.

Beyond the basin was the toilet, and farther in was the shower cubicle.

The shower cubicle was separated by clear glass, keeping the wet and dry areas apart. Inside were hooks and a small shelf for shampoo and shower gel.

On the left side of the bathroom sat a luxurious bathtub. Set into the wall at the end were several recessed storage niches, perfect for bath products.

Heavy, textured curtains hung around the tub, giving it a cosy, sheltered feel when bathing.

Whoever had designed this space had clearly put care into it. Every detail suited Lila perfectly.

From the Vault, Lila took out shampoo, shower gel, bath towels, hand towels and the other toiletries she needed. She carried them into the shower cubicle and arranged them neatly on the shelf.

Tonight, she had no energy for a bath. She just wanted to wash quickly and rest.

The bathroom was still cold. Even with the warm air on, Lila shivered the moment she took off her clothes.

Thankfully, the heat from the fireplace had already warmed the bathroom water. The moment hot water poured over her skin, the cold became much easier to bear.

She washed as quickly as she could, wrapped herself in a thick bath towel and hurried back to the east parlour.

The fireplace was still working diligently, red and golden flames blazing inside it.

As soon as Lila stepped into the east parlour, warmth surrounded her. Her whole body seemed to loosen at once.

She dried her hair at an easy pace, then smoothed a thick layer of heavy-duty barrier balm over her face. Only then did she take her usual bedding from the Vault and spread it over the heated hearth bench.

It was the bedding from her bedroom at home, the set she was most used to.

She changed into her favourite pyjamas, switched off the light and dived straight into the warm covers.

The room was dim, lit only by the glow of the fire, which flickered against the walls.

The soft bedding still carried the faint scent of her usual laundry liquid, and it made her feel deeply safe.

Comforting warmth washed over her. Sleepiness soon followed, and before long, she was sound asleep.

Edward, Annabel and the others were having a much worse night.

Half an hour earlier, they had finally driven into the underground car park at Oakwood Estate. Shivering violently, they hurried into the lift, longing to get home, climb under thick quilts and warm themselves up.

With a click, the door lock opened.

They all rushed inside. The flat did still hold a little more warmth than the outside, and in that instant their tight nerves finally relaxed.

Then came the moment everything turned upside down.

Maggie pressed the light switch, and her hand froze on the wall.

For some reason, the flat looked especially bright tonight.

The walls gleamed white. The floor shone pale under the light. The whole place was glaringly open.

And the reason was simple. It was empty.

Apart from dust on the floor, there was nothing left in the flat. For a moment, it felt like an entirely unfamiliar place.

The flat had always been spacious, but under the stark white light, it looked even colder and more desolate.

Annabel quickly backed out of the door and checked the number outside.

Flat 802.

It was right.

She went back inside in disbelief and looked around again.

The number was right. They had not entered the wrong home.

It was their home that was wrong.

"This can't be right. We must have opened it wrong."

With a bang, Annabel slammed the door shut, making the others jump where they stood in a daze.

Only after stepping outside did Annabel realise she did not have her keys. The biting cold in the hallway was unbearable, so she immediately began hammering on the door.

Maggie was the first to react and opened it again.

"This has to be Lila's doing!" Edward was furious. He grabbed his phone and called Lila again and again.

He tried three times, but her phone was still switched off.

Maggie seemed to remember something all at once. With a shriek, she rushed into her bedroom.

Her dressing table held the savings she had scraped together over half her life, five gold necklaces and two gold rings.

The very thing she feared most had happened.

The moment Maggie entered the bedroom, she saw that it was as bare as the living room.

Never mind the dressing table. Even the bed was gone. Not so much as an old sock had been left behind. The room had been stripped clean.

"Mr. Spencer, Mrs. Spencer, this..."

Daniel quietly edged towards the door. All he wanted now was to drive home.

The flat was empty, freezing cold, and did not even have a single duvet. How were they supposed to keep warm here?

He wanted nothing more than to get back to his rented room.

Annabel saw exactly what he was thinking. She tightened her grip on his wrist.

"Daniel..."

Her voice was soft and pleading.

Annabel had already weighed up the situation. Staying at home should have been the best way to get through the cold, but the flat was now completely empty. She needed to take shelter at Daniel's place for a while.

Once her parents had sorted things out here, she could come back.

Daniel only wanted to go home as soon as possible, and taking one more person with him was not a problem. He nodded.

"Come on. The snow's getting heavier. We should leave while we can."

Without another word, the two of them opened the door and left without even telling Edward and Maggie.

After all, Daniel's rented place was small. If everyone went, it would be cramped and miserable.

Besides, someone needed to stay behind and deal with this stripped-out flat.

"Call the police. Hurry up and call the police. Make them find that girl!"

Maggie was close to losing control.

Edward pressed his lips together and thought for a moment before dialling the police.

"Hello. I'd like to report someone missing. It's terribly cold tonight, and my niece has disappeared. Could you please help us find her? I'm worried about her being out alone."

"Her name is Lila Spencer. Her phone number is... Her car registration is..."

"Yes, yes. Thank you. Sorry to trouble you."

Edward ended the call with a stern expression. Only then did Maggie dare to speak.

"Why did you say she was missing? Why didn't you report her for stealing everything from the flat?"

"Use your head. If we report her for theft, we fall out with Lila completely. Do you still want this flat or not?"

Edward still had his eye on both the flat and the savings in Lila's hands. He absolutely would not burn that bridge.

"She's already moved everything out, and you still think she'll hand over the flat and the money? Stop dreaming. Lila is utterly ungrateful."

The more Maggie thought about it, the angrier she became, and she started hurling bitter complaints into the empty room.

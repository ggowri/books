Chapter 16

1 June. Savings: 859,000 dollars.

All the household essentials had arrived.

Lila got up early, drove out, collected every parcel, took them back to Lockridge Storage Unit, and moved them into the Vault.

Today, she was continuing with meat.

There was a chicken farm in a nearby village.

The farm had rented an entire hillside, and the chickens were usually left to roam freely.

In the local way of speaking, they were proper free-range hill chickens.

The meat was firm and tasty, much like chickens raised in village back gardens, but the price was a little lower than true home-reared birds.

Lila decided this was where she would buy the chickens she planned to stew in the future.

She drove straight out of the garage and followed the navigation to the farm.

According to the map, the Luton van eventually stopped in front of a large wooded area.

Inside the trees was a clearing with a sizeable rural courtyard.

Facing the courtyard was a small hill.

The whole hill was enclosed with wire fencing. It seemed the free-range chickens were kept on the hillside, and the fencing was there to stop them wandering off.

At the entrance to the courtyard, a sign said eggs were eight dollars per pound, and free-range hill chickens were eighty dollars each.

The eggs here were much more expensive than those from industrial farms. Wholesale eggs from commercial farms were only three or four dollars per pound.

Still, eggs from free-range hens always felt more nutritious.

Lila decided she would buy both expensive and cheap eggs.

"Hello, is anyone here?"

She called from the entrance to the courtyard.

It was quiet inside. There was no reply.

Lila took a deep breath and shouted, "I'm here to buy chickens!"

There was a sudden clatter from inside, followed by a sleepy female voice.

"Coming, coming..."

A girl about Lila's age hurried out in a fluster, wiping a little drool from the corner of her mouth as she came.

"Sorry. I fell asleep just now."

The girl gave an awkward, honest smile, her face full of apology.

"It's fine. Do you run this chicken farm by yourself?"

From the look of things, it seemed as though the girl was handling the place alone. But with this many chickens, could one person really manage it?

Lila was not usually nosy, but she was genuinely curious.

"Yes, I raise them myself. It's not too tiring."

The girl nodded, apparently guessing what Lila was wondering.

"I'd like two hundred pounds of eggs and one hundred chickens."

Lila did not waste any more time and gave the quantity directly.

The girl's eyes widened in shock.

Her little chicken farm had never had anyone place such a large order in one go.

Usually, someone's family fancied a proper farm chicken and came to buy one.

Restaurants generally thought her chickens were too expensive and bought the cheap supermarket farmed chickens instead.

So she had never really received a major order.

Hearing that Lila wanted two hundred pounds of eggs and one hundred chickens truly stunned her.

She only had a little over two hundred chickens in total. As for eggs, she had not even saved up one hundred pounds.

"I don't think I have two hundred pounds of eggs. Would eighty pounds be all right?"

She had sold the previous batch. These were only the ones she had collected recently.

"That's fine. Give me whatever you have. Do you have enough chickens?"

The eggs were not the main point. If she did not have enough, Lila could make up the rest at an industrial farm. Those places had as many as she wanted, and the prices were cheaper.

The free-range hill chickens were the important part. They were not easy to find elsewhere.

"The chickens are enough, I think... yes, enough!"

The last part of Lila's question had clearly tangled the young farm owner's thoughts for a moment. After thinking it through, she finally answered.

"Can you kill and clean them for me?"

One hundred chickens was a lot to ask of one young woman.

"The price I gave is for live chickens. I'm not very good at processing them, especially not a hundred. How about this? Since you're buying so many, I'll knock five dollars off each chicken. You can take them to the family at the village entrance. They process chickens there. Killing, plucking, gutting, the lot. They charge eight dollars per bird. You'd only need to add three dollars per chicken yourself. Would that work?"

The young farm owner, Elsie Harper suggested it carefully.

The main issue was that she had raised all these chickens herself. Selling them was one thing. Killing a hundred of them in one go was something she could not quite bring herself to do.

"That's fine. Thank you."

Lila did not mind.

When she had visited open-air markets before, live chickens had not been processed there either.

As long as someone could deal with them, paying an extra three hundred dollars was not a problem.

After that, Elsie invited Lila to sit in a wicker chair in the courtyard and rest while she went off to catch the chickens.

Lila reclined in the chair, enjoying the shade and occasionally checking the delivery updates on her phone.

Outside the courtyard, chaos erupted.

There were chickens clucking everywhere, dogs barking in a jumble, and Elsie's startled cries ringing out one after another.

More than an hour passed.

Lila was nearly asleep in the chair when she suddenly heard Elsie's clear voice from outside the courtyard.

"They're caught! One hundred exactly!"

Lila immediately perked up. She pushed herself up from the chair with both arms and walked out.

On the ground lay a large spread of chickens with their feet tied.

"Count them," Elsie said.

There were still a few chicken feathers stuck in her hair, making her look faintly ridiculous.

Lila gave the chickens a rough look. The number seemed right.

Then she began tossing them into the van. Elsie came over to help.

Lila counted as she loaded them, and by the end there were exactly one hundred.

After paying, Elsie said goodbye to Lila, then yawned and prepared to go back inside to sleep.

"Wait a moment."

Seeing her lazy, rather endearing manner, Lila could not help saying, "Raise another hundred, would you? I'll come back to buy them in a while."

Elsie froze for a moment, then smiled brightly and nodded.

"Of course. I'll make sure they're plump and healthy for you."

Lila nodded, then climbed into the driver's seat and started the van.

In her heart, she thought, I hope these hundred chickens, together with the next hundred, will help you survive the Collapse.

Following Elsie's directions, Lila soon drove to the household at the entrance of the village.

The place was easy to find, because a rusty metal sign hung outside the door with red paint announcing that they processed chickens.

Lila knocked.

The courtyard gate opened. Inside, a family of five was sitting together having lunch.

Realising she had arrived right in the middle of their meal, Lila felt a little embarrassed, but she still asked, "Hello. I need some chickens processed. This is the right place, isn't it?"

"Yes, yes. Just wait a moment while I finish this mouthful."

The man of the house hurriedly swallowed his food, answered her, then lowered his head and carried on eating much faster than before.

In only two minutes, he had finished.

"How many birds?"

He wiped his mouth with a tissue and walked towards the gate.

"One hundred," Lila said.

"How many?"

The man could not believe it.

"One hundred."

Lila felt a little helpless and could only repeat it with a smile.

"Oh. Right. Love, hurry up and eat. Come and help when you're done!"

After thinking for a moment, the man turned and called into the house.

He needed reinforcements.

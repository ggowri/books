Chapter 45

"That's enough. Let him speak for himself."

Edward's face reddened with anger as he snapped at Anna.

Even though Anna was upset, she was frightened enough to fall silent.

Now that he had been pushed this far, Daniel could no longer sit there saying nothing while eating their food for free. He straightened on the sofa and said, "All right. Buy enough for four people, then. I'll transfer you my share."

Edward had planned to spend twenty thousand, but Maggie stopped him at once.

"It's only the weather getting colder. Why would you spend that much? Do you think saving money is easy? I don't even let myself buy clothes that cost two hundred..."

"Enough," Edward cut her off impatiently. "Then we'll spend ten thousand. You transfer me five thousand."

"Tight-fisted," Daniel complained inwardly.

Still, he had no choice but to transfer the five thousand.

Edward and Maggie discussed it for ages before finally ordering two bags of salt, one box of instant noodles and eight packs of dried noodles. They bought nothing else.

Callum organised the group-buy orders.

There were so many. Far too many.

He was really going to make a fortune.

When he sent the order quantity to his old classmate, he quietly added double the amount for himself.

He had earned too much from this round of group-buying. If the extreme cold really turned into the Collapse, there would be no point having money if he was not alive to spend it.

Better to keep two thirds of the money and use the remaining third to stock up on more supplies. That way, he could protect his life too.

As for the cold-weather clothing at home, he would not sell another piece, no matter what anyone offered.

That night, once most people had fallen asleep, Callum's old classmate, Gordon Grant, arrived with a lorry full of group-buy food.

The deliveries that evening were for the few households that had bought large amounts. The time for door-to-door delivery had already been agreed in advance.

There were fewer people awake at night, which meant less attention. That protected Callum and the delivery team, and it also protected the households that had bought large amounts of food.

Fully kitted out, Callum went down to the underground car park.

In the lorry loaded with supplies, there was Gordon and two other large men.

They were the two orphans Gordon had taken in and raised from childhood. They were extremely reliable.

Callum stood nervously outside the lorry. When the doors opened and two men a head taller than him stepped down, he was genuinely startled.

"Don't panic. Big Grant and Little Grant. I told you about them before, the two younger brothers I adopted."

Gordon stuck his head out of the cab to say this, then quickly ducked back inside.

"Callum!"

"Callum!"

The two men grinned at him in a simple, good-natured way.

Judging by the look of them, they did not seem especially sharp.

"Come on, then. Let's get to work."

Callum did not dwell on it. There were more important things to do.

They had five households to deliver to that night, all with orders worth more than two hundred thousand.

Holding the list he had prepared in advance, Callum picked out the first order and went to deliver it with Big Grant and Little Grant.

When Callum's message came through, Leo was already waiting by the inside of his front door.

"Press the lift."

When Leo saw the message, he put on a thick hat, stepped into the stairwell, pressed the button, and quickly retreated back inside.

It was even colder outside now.

He did not begrudge Callum the money at all. No ordinary person could withstand this temperature.

The lift arrived packed full of supplies. Callum and the two Grant brothers carried everything into Leo's flat and stacked it all inside.

"Thank you, mate."

Even someone as calm as Leo could not help feeling excited at the sight of all those supplies piled in his home.

If he rationed them carefully, they would last him a very long time.

Callum moved closer to Leo, took a small packet from his pocket and handed it to him.

"You bought a lot from me, so I brought this specially. Keep it for when your life depends on it."

He said it softly, then slipped the packet into Leo's pocket before Big Grant and Little Grant could get a clear look.

Leo had not seen what it was, but from Callum's movements, he knew it had to be something good.

Leo took out his phone and transferred Callum twenty thousand.

"For your trouble."

Callum laughed.

"I knew you were a decent sort. We'll head off, then. I delivered to you first. There are still several more households waiting."

With that, he pulled on his thick face covering and led Big Grant and Little Grant away.

Leo locked the door tightly, then took out the thing Callum had slipped into his pocket.

Ten boxes of matches, five candles and two magnesium fire starters.

That was generous.

These were exactly the sort of things that could save a life.

All of a sudden, he felt as though twenty thousand had not been enough.

After finishing the first three deliveries, Big Grant and Little Grant started howling that their feet hurt so badly from the cold they could hardly stand it.

Callum coaxed them along until they reached the fourth household.

The fourth flat happened to be Sophie's.

Callum swallowed his pride and made a request.

"Sophie, it's freezing out there. These two have nearly frozen themselves solid delivering the supplies. Could we warm up at yours for a bit? Just half an hour. We'll leave after half an hour."

By rights, a young woman living alone should never let three large men into her flat in the middle of the night.

But Sophie looked at the solid, genuine supplies they had brought, and she could not quite bear to send them back out to get ill from the cold.

She nodded.

"All right. Warm yourselves by the radiator for a while. It's hottest there."

Big Grant and Little Grant did not stand on ceremony. After thanking her, they hurriedly took off their shoes.

Their toes had already turned purple from the cold.

Callum sucked in a sharp breath.

These two really were daft. Their feet were frozen like this, and only now did they say something.

After half an hour by the scorching radiator, they finally warmed through.

Callum asked Sophie for a few plastic bags and wrapped them around Big Grant and Little Grant's feet before helping them put their padded shoes back on.

The bags were thin, but they were excellent at blocking the cold.

By the time they finished the final delivery, it was deep into the night.

Callum split the money with Gordon, then everyone returned home.

When Callum got back, he stripped off his thick cold-weather gear. Apart from his wrists and face aching from the freeze, the rest of him was fine.

Those reindeer-hide boots really were warm.

His feet had only felt cold. They had not turned purple like Big Grant's and Little Grant's.

"I'm keeping them. Not selling a single pair."

Callum handled the reindeer-hide boots like treasure.

The remaining pairs would all be kept for himself.

Lila had not been idle that day either. She had wiped down every piece of furniture in the house, large and small.

After moving into a new home, she could only feel comfortable once every corner had been properly cleaned.

Before bed, Lila checked the outdoor temperature.

Minus sixty degrees.

So cold...

In her previous life, this was around the time Lila had begun to realise something was wrong.

Even though the heating indoors was still running, she had already started looking for a new shelter.

While the lifts were still working, she moved her basic cold-weather gear down to the Basement.

The Basement was cold too, but its temperature stayed between minus five and five degrees.

With enough layers, it was completely manageable.

When Lila told Maggie, Edward and the others that they needed to move to the Basement, Daniel started bombarding her with phone calls, acting pitiful and begging to come along.

Lila's heart softened, and she brought him down to the Basement with them.

Remembering what had happened at this point in her previous life, Lila felt anger crawl through her teeth.

This time, she wanted to see how those people would survive without her.

Chapter 52

Edward waved Daniel over. The two of them put the food they had found by the front door, then went back into the bedroom.

Maggie was rummaging through the wardrobe. She pulled out three thick padded coats and four pairs of quilted trousers.

She did not stand on ceremony. She pulled a pair of quilted trousers and a padded coat straight over her own clothes, then put another coat on Anna.

Anna looked at the ugly, worn-out quilted trousers in her hands and felt deeply repulsed, but she still gritted her teeth and put them on.

Old Mr. Finch had dressed under the quilts. When he looked up and saw two women who were practically strangers putting on his clothes without even asking, his mouth opened slightly.

In the end, he said nothing.

He slowly lowered his head, picked up the family photograph from the bedside cabinet and tucked it into his coat.

In the picture were his late wife, his son who worked far away, and himself.

The glass of the frame was so cold it made his chest ache. He patted it gently.

"Don't be cross, love. I'm taking you down to the Basement for a while. We'll come back later."

With that, he slowly bent down and began folding the quilts on the bed. At the very least, he needed to take a quilt with him to keep out the cold.

Edward quietly moved from the bedroom doorway further inside. He saw a trekking pole leaning on the windowsill.

He picked it up, stood behind Old Mr. Finch, and brought it down hard against the back of his head.

Old Mr. Finch's hands froze.

His eyes widened, then slowly closed. He collapsed onto the bed.

Blood from the back of his head stained the pink floral cotton quilt.

That floral cotton quilt had been sewn by his wife herself when she married him.

He had never been willing to use it, afraid that the dirt from his clothes would darken the pretty fabric.

Only after the temperature had dropped so terribly had he finally brought himself to place it on top.

Lying against the floral quilt, Old Mr. Finch let out a faint sigh, closed his eyes, and never moved again.

"Is he gone?" Anna asked uneasily. With her hands still tucked in her pockets, she walked over to the body and nudged him with her foot. When Old Mr. Finch gave no response at all, she finally relaxed.

"You could have told me earlier. I thought you were suddenly trying to be saints."

Anna curled her lip and began searching through the wardrobe.

She found several pairs of thick gloves and hurriedly put them on.

Of the three padded coats Maggie had found, one was still lying by the bed. Anna pulled it straight onto Daniel.

Daniel stood there in a daze. After putting on the coat, he murmured quietly, "I thought we were just taking some food. Why did you have to kill him?"

"At a time like this, what difference does that make?" Maggie said. "Have you not seen how many people are already dead on the news? Even if we left him alone, he would freeze sooner or later. We only spared him the suffering."

As she spoke, she stepped forward roughly and stripped off the padded coat Old Mr. Finch had only just put on.

She handed it to Edward.

"We've all got one now. Put this on quickly. It's still warm."

Edward did not hesitate. He put on the padded coat and the quilted trousers from the foot of the bed.

Then he dragged Old Mr. Finch off the bed and dumped him on the floor.

He threw the bloodstained floral cotton quilt onto the floor as well.

Underneath were three clean quilts. He rolled them up together with some effort, folded them tight, picked them up and headed out.

When he reached Daniel, Edward stopped.

He stared hard at him.

"Not one word about what happened today. Not a single word. Do you understand?"

There was a vicious glint in Edward's eyes. Daniel grew even more frightened and nodded quickly.

Seeing that he was obedient enough, Edward continued out.

Maggie, Anna and Daniel followed him, giving the flat a quick search.

They took candles, lighters, medical supplies and the food by the door.

Carrying quilts, food and everything else they had found, they left Old Mr. Finch's flat.

The stairwell was full of chaotic footsteps.

Everyone was fleeing down towards the Basement.

Maggie and the others carried their supplies and headed downstairs as well.

The first few steps were still manageable, but the farther down they went, the colder it became. The warmth left in their bodies was being drained bit by bit.

Even with gloves on, the hands carrying the supplies were stiff and painful with cold. Before long, they could barely feel their fingers at all.

The air they breathed into their lungs felt as though it was laced with shards of ice, stabbing their windpipes with every breath.

Maggie's steps began to slow. Her eyelids grew heavier and heavier.

Anna, standing behind her, was not much better. The soles of her feet and the tips of her ears ached with a biting pain.

Maggie's mind had started to blur when she suddenly bumped into something. She raised her eyes and saw that the stairs below were packed with people.

"Why have we stopped?" Edward was forcing himself down on sheer willpower. The cold air sliced at his neck and wrists like blades. Now that they had stopped so suddenly, he was afraid he would not be able to hold on.

If any of them fell now, they might never get back up.

Maggie did not speak. She no longer had the strength to speak. It felt as if her throat had been sealed shut by ice.

Edward looked ahead and finally realised that the stairwell from the third floor down was jammed with people.

"Move faster."

"Why is it blocked?"

Weak, strained voices came from behind Edward one after another.

It seemed that after only a few minutes moving through the freezing air, everyone was close to collapse.

The entrance from the ground floor down to the Basement was blocked.

One family had tried to carry a bed downstairs. Their hands had frozen so badly that the bed had become wedged in the doorway, and no matter how they tried, they could not shift it.

Oakwood Estate Management staff, who were directing residents on the lower level, heard the commotion and hurried over.

They wore professional expedition down parkas and thick leather cashmere-lined mittens, which kept them somewhat warmer than ordinary residents.

Two estate security guards came over and kicked the bed through in a few hard blows.

"We told you to come here for shelter. Who brings a bed? You may not care about staying alive, but everyone else does!"

The guards were furious and snapped at the residents.

The couple who had brought the bed were middle-aged. They were already so frozen they could barely open their mouths, as if even their eyelids had turned to sheets of ice.

They offered no argument. They abandoned the bed and, barely conscious, shuffled towards their own basement room.

At last, the stairwell entrance cleared.

Slowly, desperately, people squeezed their way into the Basement.

Outside, the temperature had already reached minus seventy-two degrees. The Basement had dropped to minus three.

Because the door from the ground floor to the Basement had been left open the entire time, cold wind kept rushing in. Before long, the temperature had fallen again to minus thirteen.

"Everyone, hurry up! Get inside! It'll only get colder if the door stays open!"

A security guard shouted through a rechargeable megaphone, but the residents being urged along had no way to move any faster.

At last, it was Edward's family's turn to enter the Basement.

The moment they stepped inside, they felt a fierce wave of warmth rushing towards them.

Yes. When it was minus seventy-two outside, a Basement at minus thirteen truly felt warm.

Edward walked ahead, overtaking Maggie. Maggie, Anna and Daniel followed behind him with stiff, mechanical steps, heading towards the entrance to their own basement room.

There were many neighbours nearby as well. In ordinary times, people would nod and exchange greetings when they met.

Now, however, it was unnaturally quiet.

Everyone wore the same numb expression as they made their way to wherever they needed to go.

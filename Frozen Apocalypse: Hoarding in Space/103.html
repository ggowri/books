Chapter 102

The two men split their supplies into separate piles and began filling the storage cabinets.

There were eight lockers in total.

Four each.

The left side belonged to Leo, the right side to Callum.

Warm clothing and reindeer hide boots went into the upper compartments. Food stores filled the lower shelves.

The problem was that they had brought far too much stuff.

Even eight lockers weren't enough.

Callum scratched his head in frustration.

Leo glanced up at the ceiling, remembering how high the roof had looked from outside.

"That's a suspended ceiling. There's space above it."

He climbed onto the heated bed and pressed on a panel tucked into the corner.

With a sharp click, the board popped loose.

A surprisingly large cavity stretched above them.

"Pass me the supplies."

Callum's eyes practically lit up.

"That's brilliant."

He quickly handed boxes up while Leo stacked them carefully inside.

To avoid damaging the ceiling, Leo placed the heavier cartons directly on the roof beams.

Once his own supplies were hidden away, he moved to Callum's side of the room and removed another panel.

A second hidden storage compartment soon filled with food and gear.

When both ceiling panels were put back, the room looked completely untouched.

"You are a genius."

Callum looked genuinely impressed.

If he weren't carrying an armful of supplies, he might've applauded.

"It's nothing."

Leo dusted off his hands.

His jacket sleeves were now covered in dirt.

Suddenly, a harsh loudspeaker crackled outside.

"Attention all residents. Mandatory meeting tomorrow at 8:00 a.m. in the dining hall. Mandatory meeting tomorrow at 8:00 a.m. in the dining hall. Important announcements will be made. Attendance is required."

The message repeated again and again.

More than enough times for every survivor in the refuge to hear it.

Callum folded his arms.

"What do you think that's about? Explaining the rules around here?"

"There'll definitely be rules."

Leo continued organizing supplies.

"They're not going to let us eat and live here for free."

Then he remembered something.

"Oh, right. The military doctor at the clinic spoke to me yesterday. He wants me working there as a physician. I accepted."

Callum stared.

His mouth practically fell open.

"Hold on. You're a PE teacher. Since when are you a doctor?"

Leo laughed.

"I studied medicine at university."

"What happened?"

"Life."

He shrugged and left it at that.

Callum dropped onto his bedding with a miserable sigh.

"Must be nice. Meanwhile, I'm probably headed straight for manual labour if I want work credits."

"You could always go back to what you're good at."

Leo grabbed a rag and began wiping down the wooden door they'd turned into a makeshift table.

Callum groaned.

"What's the point? All the money from those bulk buying deals is still sitting in my bank account. Might as well be monopoly money now."

Just thinking about it hurt.

Leo chuckled.

"As long as people are trading things, you'll figure out a way to make something happen."

Callum leaned against the wall, bundled inside his coat.

"Maybe. Guess I'd better start thinking about my next career."

The only things left on the floor were their worn-out quilts.

They were ragged and stained, but nobody was throwing away cotton during the Long Freeze.

Leo carried them outside and beat the dust from them.

Two were hung over the front door and bedroom entrance as makeshift insulated curtains.

The remaining two were folded neatly and stacked at the end of the heated bed for emergencies.

With that, the room was finally organized.

Unfortunately, it was still freezing.

Only then did Callum realize neither of them had lit a fire.

Leo had handled almost all the unpacking.

The least he could do was deal with the stove.

Before heating water, they needed to warm the room itself.

Callum tossed firewood into the kitchen range.

Flames slowly came alive.

Soon the firebox was roaring.

Wood crackled and popped.

"Now that's what I'm talking about."

He sat directly beside the firebox and stretched out his hands.

"Come over here."

Leo glanced at the dusty floor.

At this point, being picky seemed ridiculous.

He sat down beside him.

After a while, the room finally began to warm.

The cast iron pot above the stove radiated heat throughout the space.

Pulling on his heavy coat, Callum headed outside and returned carrying a basin full of snow.

He dumped it straight into the pot.

As the snow melted, he grinned.

"No idea when the dining hall starts serving food."

He looked toward Leo.

"How about we treat ourselves first?"

They'd risked everything hauling their supplies through the Winter Exodus.

A small luxury felt justified.

Ever since everyone moved into the civil-defence shelter, neither of them had dared make instant noodles with hot water.

The smell would've given them away immediately.

Now things were different.

Leo nodded.

"Sounds good."

"Oh, and I found a few frozen eggs in my pack yesterday."

Callum rummaged through a cabinet.

"I didn't want to leave them outside, but keeping them indoors isn't ideal either."

"I've got a few as well."

Leo laughed.

"Let's keep them in the kitchen cabinet and eat one each day. Call it nutritional support."

The problem wasn't the eggs.

The problem was privacy.

Back in the shelter, there hadn't been a private kitchen.

Cook anything remotely decent and every person nearby would know.

Soon the water reached a rolling boil.

Each man grabbed a packet of instant noodles.

No holding back.

Seasoning packet.

Oil packet.

Everything went into the bowls.

If they were going to indulge, they were doing it properly.

Each tossed a frozen egg into the boiling water.

By the time the eggs cooked through, the shells had cracked open.

White egg protein bulged through the fractures, making the eggs look even bigger.

The two men attacked the meal like starving wolves.

By the end, they were full enough to start hiccupping.

Next door lived Mrs. Harris along with Lauren and her daughter Emma.

Emma wrinkled her nose as a delicious smell drifted through the wall.

She swallowed.

"Mom."

"What is it?"

"It smells like instant noodles."

Her eyes grew wide.

"It smells amazing."

Lauren looked at her daughter's hopeful expression and felt her heart tighten.

She hesitated before forcing a smile.

"Maybe in a few days I'll make some noodles too."

She still had several pounds of flour among her supplies.

Emma immediately shook her head.

"I'm not asking for any."

She lowered her gaze.

"I was just curious."

Despite her age, she understood their situation better than most adults.

The last thing she wanted was to make life harder for her mother.

Nearby, Mrs. Harris and Lauren were unpacking their sleds.

Like Leo and Callum, they each had four lockers assigned to them.

Mrs. Harris heard the conversation.

Her eyes drifted toward a packet of spicy beef noodle oil she'd tucked away in one of the cabinets.

After a moment's hesitation, she picked it up and handed it to Lauren.

"Here."

Lauren blinked.

"When you make noodles for Emma, add this."

Lauren stared at the seasoning packet.

"Oh, no. I can't take that."

She immediately tried to hand it back.

"You keep it for yourself."

Mrs. Harris waved her off.

"At my age, instant noodles upset my stomach."

The lie came out effortlessly.

"Kids should enjoy things like this."

Before Lauren could refuse again, Mrs. Harris stuffed the packet into her hand.

Lauren's eyes grew moist.

"I keep accepting kindness from all of you and never have anything to give back."

She held the packet tightly.

"When life gets better, I'll repay every favour. I mean that."

"Sure you will."

Mrs. Harris chuckled and continued unpacking.

Living alone had its advantages.

Sophie slept more soundly than she had in months.

Nobody disturbed her.

Nobody needed anything from her.

The knock at the door didn't come until a soldier arrived to deliver firewood.

That was what finally woke her up.

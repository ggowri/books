Chapter 15

"Hello. What are you standing out here for?" the gatekeeper called.

"I'd like to buy some meat," Lila replied.

The gatekeeper almost found it funny. From the way she was dressed and how relaxed she looked, she did not seem like their usual trade customers.

Those people either came in smart suits or hurried in and out as if every second mattered.

This young woman looked more like someone sent by her parents to buy meat who had taken a wrong turn and ended up at a meatworks.

Rosie's Meatworks was busy every day. They did not do retail. They specialised in wholesale.

"We don't sell small amounts here. If you only want a bit, you'll need the market or a supermarket," the gatekeeper kindly reminded her.

"I'd like four thousand pounds of pork, eight thousand pounds of beef, and a few hundred pounds of chicken."

Lila guessed what he was thinking and simply stated the quantity she needed.

"You're not joking, are you?" The gatekeeper took a deep breath, hardly able to believe it.

Their big customers were usually brought in by the factory's sales team. Someone walking in on her own and asking for roughly six tonnes, no, even more than that, was a first.

"I'm not joking. Please find the person in charge."

Lila did not want to keep standing outside. The sun was rather strong.

"All right, all right. Come in with me."

The gatekeeper did not dare delay. He led Lila into the compound while taking a walkie-talkie from his pocket.

"Contact Deputy Manager Cole. We've got a major customer here."

A muffled acknowledgement came through the device.

They had only walked a short distance inside when someone came out to meet them.

It was a slightly plump middle-aged woman.

She hurried over, her hand already extended before she even reached Lila.

A slightly plump middle-aged woman hurried over. This was Deputy Manager Harriet Cole. "Hello. I'm the deputy manager here. I hear you want to buy meat?"

"Hello. Yes, that's right."

Lila shook her hand.

"I'll take you to see the meat first, then we can talk prices."

Harriet had an easy warmth about her, and her voice was full of friendliness.

Lila rather liked that.

"Our meat is all chilled fresh meat, slaughtered and processed on the same day," Harriet said as she led Lila into the cold room.

It was not like the cold rooms shown on television, freezing everywhere and coated in ice.

This one was kept between zero and four degrees Celsius.

The pork inside was not frozen solid. It still looked very fresh.

The quality seemed good too, so Lila decided to buy from here.

"How much would four thousand pounds of pork cost?" Lila asked.

Harriet could not hide her delight.

"Do you want specific cuts, or whole pigs? Specific cuts are priced differently. Whole pigs are cheaper. Cleaned pork with the bone in would be eight point eight dollars per pound if you're taking four thousand pounds."

Lila raised her eyebrows in surprise.

So the wholesale price of pork was this low. It seemed the pork she had bought from supermarkets before had been far too expensive.

"Then I'll buy whole pigs. But I need to inspect them first. After that, please cut them into small pieces and pack the same cuts together."

Lila stated her requirements.

She had no desire to handle greasy, complicated work like that herself.

"No problem. The only issue is that we don't have stock available today. All the pork has already been ordered. Why don't you set a time, and I'll have it prepared for you in advance?"

Harriet asked in a practical tone.

"Tomorrow?" Lila wanted it as soon as possible.

Harriet agreed readily.

"That works. I'll have them slaughter and process the pigs tonight. You can come tomorrow morning to inspect the goods. How does that sound?"

"Perfect."

Lila liked Harriet's decisiveness. No fuss, no dragging things out.

"Do you have chicken in stock? I'd like mid-wings, gizzards, hearts and feet."

Lila did not plan to buy whole chickens from the meatworks. Pale, plucked chickens lined up one after another looked unsettling. She would rather go to a farm and buy free-range hill chickens.

Chicken wings and feet were different.

Whole chickens were for stewing, but these cuts could be made into snacks or used for barbecue.

"We do. They're in another cold room."

Harriet led Lila over to have a look.

They were all individually packed.

Some came in five-hundred-gram packs and others in one-kilogram packs. The mid-wings were sealed in food-grade plastic bags, each printed with the meatworks logo.

They looked clean, tidy and healthy.

The quality was good, so Lila placed an order straight away.

Two hundred pounds of mid-wings, one hundred pounds of drumettes, one hundred pounds of gizzards, one hundred pounds of chicken hearts and one hundred pounds of chicken feet.

These were also sold to her at wholesale price, costing seven thousand eight hundred dollars in total.

Lila then paid the deposit for the pork and agreed to come back the next day to inspect it. After that, she drove to Lockridge Storage Unit with the chicken she had bought.

Once she arrived, she wasted no time. She closed her eyes and moved all the chicken into the Vault.

She had not taken in much today, so her body was unaffected.

Since she still felt fine, Lila also moved another fifty tonnes of coal into the Vault. Even after that, she did not feel tired.

Early the next morning, Lila headed straight for the pork she had been thinking about.

This was an important part of her food supply.

Unfortunately, the meatworks did not sell beef. She would have to try her luck in the countryside later.

The gatekeeper at Rosie's Meatworks already recognised her.

Lila drove her Luton van smoothly into the compound and went straight to the cold room.

After explaining the situation to a member of staff, he took her to see the meat she had ordered.

The meat from twenty large fattened pigs hung from iron hooks.

The exposed pork was bright in colour. One look told her it had been slaughtered not long ago and was extremely fresh.

"This comes to four thousand two hundred pounds altogether. Have a look. If you think it's too much, I can have some cut off."

The voice came from behind her.

Lila turned and saw Harriet.

"No need. Four thousand two hundred pounds is perfect. Have them start cutting."

"Right. Everyone, get these pigs processed. Apart from the ribs, remove the rest of the bones. Sort the meat by cut, and pack it in two-pound bags."

Harriet raised her voice and gave the staff their instructions.

The workers picked up their cleavers and bone axes and began processing the pork with practised efficiency.

Because there were plenty of workers and all of them were skilled, the meat from twenty large pigs was fully dealt with in just over an hour.

All the pork was packed into the meatworks' own bags, each at roughly one kilogram.

The bags were sorted by cut and placed into boxes. Each box was even labelled by category.

The bones had also been chopped into the small pieces Lila wanted and packed into large food-grade plastic bags.

Everything was orderly and pleasing to look at.

Rosie's Meatworks really did know how to do business.

Lila and Harriet calculated the price. After rounding off the small change, the total came to thirty-six thousand eight hundred dollars.

Harriet arranged for a refrigerated lorry to deliver Lila's pork directly to Lockridge Storage Unit.

When they arrived, the staff neatly stacked the boxes of pork inside the unit.

Before leaving, they checked with Lila several times to make sure it was really all right to leave the pork in a room-temperature storage unit.

Lila had grown used to her previous excuse and simply said someone would come to collect it shortly. This was only a transfer point.

After the meatworks lorry had driven away, Lila closed the roller shutter again.

Then she moved all the pork into the Vault.

It was already getting late, so Lila drove straight home.

She needed a proper rest.

Tomorrow, she would carry on.

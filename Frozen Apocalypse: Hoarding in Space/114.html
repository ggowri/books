Chapter 113

After returning to the refuge, some people handed over their firewood in exchange for Survival Credits.

Others carried every bundle straight home.

Sophie kept every single log she had gathered.

All four bundles came back with her.

She hated the cold.

She couldn't spend every hour hiding in the office, so the house still needed to stay warm at night.

The more firewood she stockpiled now, the better.

Callum made the same decision.

He worked quickly and had managed to gather four large bundles as well.

Every piece was going home with him.

Keeping the house heated was his responsibility.

Later, when Leo started contributing rent, Callum would feel a lot less guilty.

Edward trudged along with Anna dragging her feet behind him.

He traded one bundle for Survival Credits and kept the other two.

The family had almost no food left.

If he didn't exchange at least some firewood, they wouldn't have anything to eat.

Back inside, everyone who had brought wood home hurried to light their kitchen ranges.

The moment the fire went out, the rooms became nothing more than frozen boxes.

Even burning two full bundles a day barely kept people alive.

Sophie wasn't willing to waste the firewood she'd worked so hard to collect.

After locking her door, she headed back to the office to stay warm.

Most people weren't nearly as fortunate.

The firewood they gathered today had to be burned today.

One by one, residents crouched beside their fireboxes, stretching frozen hands and feet toward the flames while waiting for their heated beds to warm up.

"I honestly thought life would get easier once we reached the refuge."

"Turns out we've got to climb mountains for firewood, and there's still not enough to stay warm."

Daniel sat beside the firebox with a defeated expression.

He had imagined spending the rest of the apocalypse doing nothing except eating government rations.

Reality had other plans.

"You seriously thought the refuge was going to feed you for free?"

His roommate rolled his eyes.

"This is the Collapse."

"If you don't work, you die."

As evening settled over the refuge, Maggie glanced toward the fading light outside.

It was finally time.

Her punishment had ended.

She gently touched the warm surface of the heated bed before looking at the empty bowls resting on top of the cupboard.

This had been the best day she'd experienced in a very long time.

No work.

No freezing.

Hot meals.

Real warmth.

But she couldn't stop worrying about Anna.

She knew exactly what Edward was like.

Without her around, Anna would have had a miserable day.

"Thank you for taking care of me."

"I should head back."

"My daughter's waiting for me at the refuge."

Reluctantly, Maggie climbed out from beneath the quilt and slowly began putting her clothes back on.

"You're still injured."

"Are you really leaving already?"

"You could stay another couple of days if you need to."

The old woman genuinely worried about Maggie's condition.

At least partly.

Keeping another mouth fed during the Long Freeze wasn't exactly easy.

Maggie hesitated for a brief moment before forcing herself to speak.

"I really can't stay."

"I have to go check on my daughter."

She swallowed hard.

"There's... one more thing."

"Could you spare a little food?"

The room fell silent.

Both old people froze.

Neither had expected her to ask.

Seeing they hadn't answered, Maggie hurried on.

"Just think of it as the food I would've eaten if I'd stayed here recovering for another couple of days."

The old woman's expression stiffened.

After a long pause, she quietly nodded.

"All right."

"Eat well."

"Get yourself healthy."

She filled a small sack with two bowls' worth of mixed grains.

"Thank you so much."

"I'll repay your kindness one day."

"If I weren't desperate, I never would've asked."

Maggie clutched the little sack tightly while speaking with heartfelt gratitude.

The old couple forced polite smiles as they walked her to the gate.

Once she had gone, the old man sighed.

"You and your soft heart."

"We don't even know how long our own food will last."

"And you let her eat here and leave with supplies."

As he spoke, he poured his wife a cup of hot water.

The old woman wrapped both hands around it.

"She was in such a terrible situation."

"If we can help someone, we should."

She had certainly never expected Maggie to ask for food outright.

But once she'd agreed, there was no taking it back.

Outside, Maggie stood looking down the snowy slope.

The brightly lit refuge glowed in the distance.

Ignoring the freezing wind, she tightened her grip on the bag of grain and hurried downhill.

The walk usually took more than ten minutes.

She covered it in eight.

The guards recognised her immediately.

After all, she'd only been punished the day before.

Without much questioning, they allowed her back inside.

Still, a few exchanged puzzled looks.

For someone who had spent an entire day outside, she seemed surprisingly healthy.

Maggie hurried all the way home.

The door was locked from the inside.

She knocked hard.

"Anna!"

"I'm back!"

"Open the door!"

Anna was asleep on the heated bed, now pleasantly warm.

Hearing the knocking, she frowned and slowly sat up.

"Who is it...?"

Maggie stepped closer to the window.

"It's me."

"I'm home."

"Open up."

Edward remained lying motionless at the far end of the heated bed.

Seeing he had no intention of moving, Anna reluctantly climbed down.

She lifted the wooden latch, then hurried straight back beneath the blankets.

"It's freezing."

"So cold."

By the time Maggie entered, Anna had already wrapped herself tightly inside the quilt again.

For a brief moment, Maggie's heart sank.

She had spent the past day hovering between life and death.

Not one person had asked whether she was all right.

Then she forced herself to smile.

Anna was still young.

She didn't know any better.

"Look."

"I brought food home for you."

Ignoring the numbness in her hands and feet, Maggie eagerly opened the grain sack and pushed it toward Anna like it was treasure.

Anna immediately leaned over.

Even Edward, who had been lying with his back to them, shifted slightly to look.

"Huh."

"It's just mixed grains."

Disappointment spread across Anna's face.

The refuge handed out mixed grains.

Now her mother had brought back... more mixed grains.

Why couldn't anyone ever bring home proper rice?

"You silly girl."

"That just means you've never really been hungry."

"When you are, even mixed grains taste wonderful."

Maggie had missed her daughter terribly.

Even after seeing Anna's ungrateful reaction, she couldn't bring herself to scold her.

Anna simply lay back against the wall, disappeared beneath the blanket again, and said nothing.

"Where'd you get the grain?"

Edward asked.

"I..."

"I got it from one of the farmhouses up the mountain."

Maggie answered quickly, smiling nervously.

She was terrified Edward still blamed her for yesterday.

If he abandoned them too, life would become even harder.

"Which farmhouse?"

Edward pressed.

Maggie hesitated.

"I don't remember."

"I was freezing so badly everything's a blur."

Edward gave a cold snort.

"You spend one day causing trouble and somehow that's the only thing you're good at."

He rolled over and ignored her completely.

Without saying another word, Maggie stored the bag of mixed grains inside the cupboard before climbing onto the heated bed herself.

The run back through the snow had chilled her to the bone.

More than anything, she wanted to feel warm again.

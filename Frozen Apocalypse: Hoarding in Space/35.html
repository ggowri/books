Chapter 34

"You have no composure at all. Be quiet!"

Edward roared, and Maggie did not dare make another sound.

Once the two of them had calmed down a little, they realised something even more serious.

They were freezing.

The temperature outside had dropped to minus five. Indoors, it was only seven or eight degrees at most.

In winter, dressed properly, minus five would not have been too terrible.

But right now, one of them was wearing a strappy dress and the other a thin suit. Neither outfit offered the slightest warmth. They were shivering uncontrollably.

Worse still, there was no sofa, no bed and not even a single duvet. They had nowhere to sleep for the night.

Just as they were at their wits' end, Maggie's phone began chiming non-stop.

"Turn that thing off. It's doing my head in," Edward snapped impatiently.

Maggie picked up her phone to put it on silent, only to see notifications flooding in from the Oakwood residents' group chat. She tapped in.

Forever Young: "It's absolutely freezing. Can the heating be turned on? Estate Manager."

Miss Hothead: "They're not going to switch on the heating in the middle of summer. We can't count on management. Best thing is to get under the covers and keep warm."

SecondWind: "Neighbours, anyone with electric blankets should use them now. To be honest, with an electric blanket plugged in and a duvet over you, it's not too bad."

Flat 1302: "I stocked up on a load of supplies at the superstore today. Everyone should go tomorrow and buy what they can. Get more food and drink. What if the Collapse really is coming?"

Keep Calm: "Let's not exaggerate, everyone. We all have duvets and winter coats at home. Minus five is manageable if you dress warmly. Please don't spread rumours and make people anxious."

Forever Young: "We're just chatting. What's there to be anxious about? If you don't want to read it, leave."

Keep Calm: "You can chat privately. Don't say things like that in the group. It's only a bit colder than usual, and now people are talking about the Collapse. I don't want to waste public resources or cause trouble for the authorities, otherwise I'd report you. Spreading rumours is illegal."

The group chat was on the verge of turning into a row.

Then Maggie suddenly had an idea and typed a message into the group.

"Does anyone have spare duvets or warm clothes we could borrow for the night? It's honestly too cold tonight."

The moment the message went out, the tense atmosphere in the group vanished. No one said a word.

Ten minutes later, someone finally replied.

Flat 1302: "I've got one spare thick duvet at home. Come and collect it yourself."

Flat 1302 was the neighbour who had sensed something was wrong when the heavy snow began and had stocked up in advance. He was a warm-hearted sort and thought that, since they were all neighbours, he should help where he could.

After sending the message, he took the duvet out of a cupboard and placed it by the door, waiting for the neighbour to come and collect it.

The moment Maggie saw the reply, she was overjoyed.

"Thank you so much. I'll come and get it now."

With that, Maggie hurried out excitedly. Edward's expression eased a little as well.

No sooner had Maggie left than someone knocked hard on the door.

Edward was puzzled. How had she come back so quickly?

But when he opened the door, Daniel and Annabel were standing outside, their faces red from the cold.

"Weren't you going to yours? Why are you back?" Edward asked, surprised and confused.

"The snow outside is too deep. We tried for ages, but the car won't move."

Daniel drew in what little warmth the flat had and quickly squeezed inside.

"Where's Mum?" Annabel asked when she did not see Maggie.

"She managed to borrow a duvet and went to collect it."

Annabel's eyes lit up at once.

She had thought they would have to get through the night by sheer endurance. She had not expected Maggie to find a duvet.

Just then, Maggie came back carrying a one-point-eight-metre duvet in her arms.

It was fairly thick. Two people could just about make do with it, but for four adults it was far too small.

And without a bed or anything to spread beneath it, they could hardly sit on the freezing floor and cover themselves with it.

After thinking for a while, Maggie had no choice but to pick up her phone again and send another message to the group.

"Our flat was burgled today. All our duvets and clothes were stolen, even the bed and sofa. Could any neighbours please lend us some duvets? We just need to get through tonight, and we'll return them tomorrow."

At this point, pride no longer mattered. Maggie was practically begging.

But no one in the group replied.

This sudden temperature plunge was too strange. Who knew whether it would get colder from here? Who would be willing to lend out their warm bedding now?

"Buy them," Edward said in a low voice. "Offer three hundred pounds for one duvet and see if anyone sells."

But after the message was sent, there was still no response.

"Raise it to five hundred."

Edward refused to believe that people would stop caring about money just because the weather had turned cold.

Yet even at five hundred, no one answered.

"Raise it to one thousand. One thousand pounds for a duvet and a pillow."

This time, the moment the message went out, someone replied.

Keep Calm: "I've only just seen the message. I have a spare duvet at home. I can sell it to you for one thousand."

Spring Blossom: "I've only just seen it too. I'm old and don't hear the phone well. Do you still need duvets? I have one as well. One thousand is fine."

Edward looked at Maggie's phone and gave a cold snort.

"Funny how no one saw anything just now. Now they've all suddenly noticed."

More replies came in one after another.

Maggie added them as contacts one by one, picked the three thickest duvets and paid three thousand pounds for them.

At that moment, Oakwood Oakwood Estate Management tagged Maggie in the group.

"Dear resident, if your home was burgled, we also bear some responsibility. We're very sorry. Oakwood Estate Management has an old sofa in storage. If you need it, please contact us."

Of course they needed it.

With a sofa, at least they would not have to sleep on the floor.

Oakwood Estate Management still had a bit of conscience. Not long afterwards, they brought the old sofa up.

Maggie and Edward had gone upstairs and downstairs to collect the duvets they had bought, so Annabel opened the door for management.

Just now, she and Daniel had been curled up under the duvet. Being forced to get up and open the door left her deeply irritated.

"Hello. We've brought the sofa up for you," said Zack from Oakwood Oakwood Estate Management. His attitude was decent enough.

Annabel frowned tightly.

"Hurry up and bring it in. I'm freezing."

Zack paused for a second but did not refuse. Together with Arthur Bell, the security guard, he carried the sofa into the flat.

Looking at the empty room, even Zack was shocked.

Whoever had done this had been thorough. They had left practically nothing behind.

"Does management not have any beds? What good is one sofa? Bring me a bed as well!"

Seeing the two men set the sofa down and prepare to leave, Annabel called after them.

A trace of impatience appeared on Zack's face, but when he turned around, his professional smile was back in place.

"I'm sorry, but Oakwood Estate Management doesn't have spare beds."

"What are you people even paid for? My home was burgled, so you're fully responsible. Once the weather warms up, I'm absolutely taking this further. Now go and get me two sets of padded winter clothes."

Annabel was beside herself with anger.

Chapter 41

At first, the radiators were only lukewarm. Then the heat built, gradually turning properly hot.

Warmth began to fill the flat. In homes without air conditioning, the indoor temperature finally climbed above freezing.

In the end, the room settled at around twenty degrees.

For people who had been frozen stiff for a whole day and night, twenty degrees felt like paradise.

The radiators could no longer be touched with bare hands. They were scorching. The government really had put some effort into this.

Anna and the others dragged the sofa in front of the radiator. The four of them huddled together under a quilt, basking in the heat.

Their frozen limbs soaked up nearly an hour of warmth before they finally came back to life.

Thank goodness the radiators had not been stolen.

Once the four of them warmed up, they started to feel hot. They peeled off two layers of thermal tops and bottoms.

To celebrate the return of warmth, Maggie took out a small portion of the food they had brought back from the upmarket restaurant, tipped it into the frying pan they had bought the day before, and heated it through.

The four of them sat around the table, eating steaming hot food.

"When the weather warms up a bit, we'll have to spend some money and get this place properly furnished again. We can't go on like this," Maggie said, looking at the lonely iron pan in the kitchen with a long sigh.

"If that won't work, we can rent this place out and move into Daniel's. We'd save money, and we wouldn't have to worry about the furniture being stolen."

Edward did not even look up as he spoke.

Daniel's heart gave a jolt.

How had the plan suddenly landed on his flat?

Each of the four people at the dining table had their own calculations. Just then, Oakwood Estate Management sent another message in the group chat.

Important Notice: Government Supply Distribution Reminder

Dear residents,

We have received notice that during the extreme cold period, the government will deliver basic living supplies to each residential estate every three days.

Due to the severe weather and difficult transport conditions, supplies are limited and delivery may be slow. Please be understanding and wait patiently.

Supplies will be distributed by building in batches, beginning at three o'clock each afternoon. Once you receive notice, please send one person only, dressed warmly, to collect them in an orderly manner from the ground floor of your block.

Residents with special difficulties, such as elderly people living alone, may contact Estate Management privately if assistance is needed.

The weather is extremely cold. Please keep warm, stay safe, and help your neighbours where you can.

As soon as the notice appeared, the residents' group chat erupted.

"Oh my goodness, heating was already brilliant, and now they're handing out supplies as well? This is amazing."

"Bless this country. I'll follow you forever."

"It's so warm now. I don't even need to wear a down jacket under a huge quilt any more. A jumper indoors is enough. And when I think about getting supplies tomorrow, I'm honestly so happy."

"I told you it couldn't be the Collapse."

"I can relax now. No work, no stress, just sit back and wait to be fed."

Leo frowned as he read through the messages in the group chat.

The government had turned on the heating. Supplies were being handed out. But the outdoor temperature had not risen at all.

Temporary heating had not solved the root of the problem.

Something still felt wrong.

Once the government stopped the emergency heating, they would be plunged straight back into an icy nightmare.

What frightened him most was the possibility that the temperature would drop even further. If that happened, even air conditioning units would stop working, and then people would simply be waiting to freeze to death.

He glanced at the thermometer he had placed outside the window.

Minus forty-six degrees.

It had dropped another six degrees.

It was racing straight towards minus fifty.

Even the snowploughs on the road had disappeared.

Was it so cold that even the snowploughs had packed in?

The more Leo thought about it, the more panicked he became. He had to do something while he still could.

He remembered that someone in the group had been selling reindeer-hide boots. There seemed to have been leather mittens and fur hats as well.

He searched for the keyword "reindeer-hide boots" and soon found the person.

Leo quickly sent a contact request. The other side accepted almost at once.

Callum was sitting snugly at home, gnawing on a piece of yak jerky.

He had always been involved in group-buy deals of all kinds, so his flat was almost always piled high with stock.

He still had at least eight large boxes of yak jerky, and plenty of milk tablets, cheese and pasta as well.

But with the sudden plunge into extreme cold, Callum had no intention of selling food. If the Long Freeze really had arrived, and he ate sparingly, those supplies would last him for a year.

Other surplus stock, though, was different. There was no need to hoard everything. As long as he kept what he needed, he might as well make a tidy profit while the extreme cold was driving demand through the roof.

"Do you still have the reindeer-hide boots you were selling in the group? I'd also like to buy mink hats," Leo asked as soon as the request went through.

Callum saw the message and took another hard bite of jerky.

Just as expected.

He had known someone would come asking.

At a time like this, supplies meant survival. People were not going to quibble over the price.

"Yes. But this is my latest stock, so it's more expensive than before. Reindeer-hide boots are thirty thousand a pair. Mink hats are twenty thousand each."

What a profiteer.

That was Leo's first thought when he saw the message.

They had only been a few thousand before. How had they suddenly shot up to tens of thousands?

Still, this was not the time to fuss over money.

"Two pairs of boots and three hats. Can you deliver them to my door? We'll trade face to face. How does that sound?"

The price did not matter. Going outside did.

"No problem. I've also got heavy fur coats for extreme cold. Want two?"

Last time, Callum had sold a few pairs of boots, but not a single fur coat.

He could have sold them when the temperature first dropped, but he had kept quiet, worried he might price them too low. Now the timing was perfect.

"How much?" Leo did not even dare imagine the figure.

"One hundred thousand each."

Callum named an outrageous price.

After sending the message, he waited and waited, but Leo did not reply. So he added another line.

"Now isn't the time to worry about money. Stock is tight. If you don't buy now, in a while you won't get one even for a hundred thousand."

Leo had just come out of the bathroom when he saw Callum's message.

"So he knows perfectly well he's overcharging," Leo muttered to himself.

Even so, he ordered one.

Callum leapt up from his chair and packed Leo's items into a large bag.

Then he pulled on his own reindeer-hide boots, put on his thickest padded jacket and trousers, followed by a layer of cashmere-lined leather trousers and a cashmere-lined leather waistcoat.

Over the top, he added a heavy fur coat, then a mink hat and leather mittens.

Only then did he set off.

Callum's block was right next to Leo's. He only needed to walk a hundred metres outside.

Wrapped up so thoroughly, Callum did not feel too much cold when he first stepped out of his flat.

Only his exposed eyes stung sharply from the freeze.

He blinked hard and moved as quickly as he could.

When Leo heard the knock at his door, he first leaned in to look through the peephole.

He nearly jumped.

For a moment, he thought there was a black bear standing outside.

After only a second's hesitation, he let Callum in.

"My goodness, it's freezing out there."

Callum's voice came muffled through his face covering.

"Check the goods quickly. If everything's fine, transfer the money and I'll be off."

He dropped the huge bag of fur gear and boots onto the floor.

"All right. Thanks for coming over."

Leo crouched down and began to inspect everything.

The quality of the fur coats and boots was even better than he had expected.

Even after spending more than two hundred thousand, it was worth it.

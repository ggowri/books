Chapter 35

"I'm sorry, but Oakwood Estate Management doesn't have any spare padded clothes either."

After saying that, Zack turned and left.

"Get back here!"

Ignoring Annabel's shriek, Zack and Arthur quickly took the lift and went.

"Anna, leave it. The neighbours will hear. It's embarrassing."

Daniel did not want to be away from the warmth of the duvet for even a minute. Seeing Annabel behaving like that, he only frowned, hoping she would stop shouting.

When Annabel realised Oakwood Estate Management had no intention of dealing with her any further, she dropped onto the sofa and pulled the duvet over herself with Daniel.

"That hateful girl, Lila. I swear I'll make her pay."

Annabel gritted her teeth, her eyes filled with malice.

Edward and Maggie came back carrying several duvets and handed one to each person.

It was a shabby little three-seater sofa, but if they squeezed, it could just about fit four people.

Lying down was impossible. Tonight, they could only sleep sitting up.

The four of them sat in a row, each wrapped in a duvet, silent and sullen. At some point, they drifted off just like that.

28 June.

Lila slept until she woke naturally.

She stretched under the warm covers, feeling as though she had slept deeply and sweetly.

The heated hearth bench cooled slowly, and even now there was still some warmth left beneath her. The bedding had stayed cosy all night.

The fire in the fireplace had gone out, but the smokeless coal inside had lasted until just before dawn. On top of that, the room's insulation had been improved during the retrofit, so it was still fairly warm indoors.

Lila was in no hurry to get up. She lay under the covers, staring into space for a while. When she remembered that all her supplies were finally stockpiled and she no longer had to run herself ragged outside, happiness filled her chest.

She lifted her head to look out of the window, wanting to see how heavy the snow was.

Instead, she saw the spotless courtyard and only then remembered that the courtyard had a canopy over it. She could not see the snow at all.

That was fine too.

Lila fumbled around under the duvet and finally found her phone, which had been kicked down by her feet.

It had died on the drive home yesterday and had not been switched back on since.

She held down the power button.

It was time to check the results of her revenge.

The moment the phone turned on, message notifications flooded in.

There were voice messages and voice call requests from Annabel, Daniel, Maggie and Edward.

All of them had come between six and eleven the previous night.

Lila opened Annabel's chat and saw long voice messages, questioning texts and repeated voice call requests. She smiled.

"Lila, you took everything from the flat, didn't you? My parents have treated you so well. How could you do this to us?"

"Lila, don't make me hate you."

Edward and Maggie must have warned Annabel not to fall out with Lila completely. Annabel was still thinking about Lila's savings too, so she did not dare say anything too harsh.

Seeing this, Lila's lips curved.

Still pretending, even now.

She wrapped the duvet more tightly around herself and opened the feed from the pinhole cameras.

She wanted to see what state that family was in now.

On the camera feed, the empty flat contained only a small sofa. Four people were sitting in a row on it, each covered with a duvet, still fast asleep.

Lila frowned.

She had thought they might freeze badly overnight, but somehow they had managed to find new duvets and even a sofa.

No matter.

This temperature was nothing yet. The real suffering was still to come.

Lila glanced at the weather alert. It was minus twenty-five outside, and the snow had stopped for the time being.

Notifications from Oakwood Oakwood Estate Management kept coming in. Lila left the camera feed running in a small window and opened the notice.

"Emergency Notice from Oakwood Estate Management

Dear residents,

According to the latest warning from the Met Office, Ashton is about to experience the most severe temperature plunge in recorded history. Heavy snowfall is expected again this afternoon.

To ensure your basic needs are met, we strongly advise all residents to go to the superstore or market as soon as possible and urgently stock up on essential food, drinking water and emergency supplies.

Please also note the following:

Check all doors and windows, and take precautions against wind and cold.

When using heating equipment, pay close attention to electrical and fire safety.

Keep an eye on the health of elderly people and children, and reduce unnecessary trips outside.

In the event of an emergency, please contact Oakwood Estate Management on our twenty-four-hour line.

Thank you for your cooperation."

The notice had been sent precisely at seven in the morning. Below it, the residents were already chatting.

Keep Calm: "How are we supposed to go out when the snow is this deep? Can't Oakwood Estate Management do a bulk shop and distribute it to us?"

Forever Young: "What do you think they are, your mother? If you can't get out, why do you think anyone else can?"

Flat 1302: "This isn't the time to argue. Everyone needs to find a way to get out and stock up on food. I'm already heading out."

Miss Hothead: "Photo."

The photo showed the inside of the superstore. Even though the snow outside was thigh-deep, the shop was packed with people.

There were so many customers that the shelves were barely visible. All she could see were heads.

SecondWind: "The temperature outside is still manageable for now. Wear several layers and you can endure it. Anyone who wants to stock up should go quickly."

SecondWind had sent a voice message. His voice sounded muffled, so he was clearly wearing a mask.

SecondWind was Leo Turner, the fit young man from the fourth floor. He was twenty-five and worked as a PE teacher.

Yesterday, when he saw the weather turning strange, he had stocked up early, especially on five bags of rice. With the rest of his food, if he rationed carefully, he could last five or six months.

Last night, as the temperature outside kept dropping, he had felt uneasy. So at six this morning, he got up and headed for the superstore.

The snow outside was too deep for cars, so he had to walk.

The distance was only seven hundred metres, but it took him forty minutes. By the time he arrived, the superstore had not yet opened, and there was already a queue at the entrance.

Even so, Leo had gone early enough to be around fifteenth in line.

Miss Hothead had also gone out early to stock up.

She was Sophie Hale, a twenty-five-year-old lawyer.

She left at seven. The snow on the road had not been cleared yet, but she managed to follow Leo's footprints.

By the time Sophie reached the superstore entrance, the queue was already stretching far down the street.

Fortunately, the superstore had plenty of stock. Those who had gone out early had mostly managed to grab a good amount of supplies.

After seeing Leo and Sophie's messages, the residents in the group quickly began asking questions.

"Has the snow on the roads been cleared?"

"What's left in the superstore now? Will there still be anything if I go after breakfast?"

"I want to wait until the council snowploughs clear the road first. It's too cold outside. I only want to drive."

"I can't be bothered. I'll just stay home with the heating on. It can't stay this cold forever."

The diligent ones had already brought supplies home, while the lazy and cold-fearing were still hesitating indoors.

After reading the group chat, Lila glanced at the camera feed again.

Those four were still asleep.

She could not help giving a faint, mocking laugh.

"The Collapse has already begun, and they're still sleeping. Truly hopeless."

There was nothing more worth reading.

Lila turned off her phone, straightened the soft bedding and got out of bed.

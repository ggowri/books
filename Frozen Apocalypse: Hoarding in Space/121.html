Chapter 120

Lila felt a quiet wave of gratitude toward the apartment that had sheltered her. She eased the door shut behind her and continued climbing the stairwell.

The other floors were nothing like the lavish ninth floor. Most of the flats had already been ransacked, with front doors hanging open and anything useful stripped clean.

After searching the entire building, she finally struck gold. Two untouched flats.

The one on the thirteenth floor was empty. Whoever had lived there had left very little food behind.

What caught Lila's attention was the study.

Every shelf was packed with books.

She did not bother choosing. Every single volume disappeared into the Vault.

The solid oak writing desk looked expensive too, so that went in after the books.

The kitchen was filled with battered cookware, but cast-iron pans and heavy frying pans were still valuable. She packed every one of them. Shelter workshops always needed practical gear, and they could easily be traded for Survival Credits.

Food-wise, all she found was half a sack of rice and a collection of random seasonings.

Anything already opened was worthless to her. She tossed the rice and unopened spices into the Utility Sack she planned to hand over later in exchange for credits.

The flat on the sixteenth floor told a much sadder story.

Two elderly residents still lay where they had died.

Oddly enough, they had never resorted to burning their furniture for warmth.

Inside the bedroom wardrobe, Lila uncovered a gold bracelet and two gold rings.

Those were definitely staying with her.

The wardrobe also held five thick quilts.

Into the Vault they went.

The dogs would appreciate proper beds one day.

Six winter coats were bundled into the Utility Sack for the shelter.

A storage cabinet in the sitting room yielded scissors, pliers and several other hand tools. She pocketed every one of them.

The kitchen held a neat stack of stainless-steel basins.

The refuge would gladly recycle those, so they joined the growing pile in the sack.

Before leaving, Lila stripped the flat of every wooden piece of furniture she could fit into the Vault.

The timber could become furniture, repairs, or simply firewood.

Nothing would go to waste.

By the time she finished searching the entire building, nearly two hours had slipped by.

Dragging the bulging Utility Sack behind her, she hurried downstairs.

Using the Vault had spoiled her.

Actually carrying things by hand felt like a pain in the neck.

Still, she could not risk making the sack disappear. If someone happened to walk into the corridor and watched it vanish, people would start asking questions she had no intention of answering.

When Lila returned to the meeting point beside the Luton van, a fire was already blazing inside one of the nearby houses.

Eight or nine members of the recovery team had made it back.

According to the rules, anything recovered during a supply run belonged to the refuge.

No exceptions.

Everyone sat around the fire with their Utility Sacks at their feet while soldiers inspected each haul, assigned a value, and converted it into Survival Credits that were deposited into individual accounts.

Lila was freezing.

Even wrapped in layers of winter gear, her fingers ached from the cold.

She hauled the oversized sack into the warm room and settled beside the fire.

Only moments later, Ethan walked in carrying a rucksack.

He glanced at the woman bundled up until only her eyes were visible before taking the seat beside her.

More scavengers trickled back, and the soldiers continued logging every recovered item.

Soon it was Lila's turn.

"Six winter coats. Assorted seasonings. Five and a half pounds of frozen pork. About six pounds of rice. Two frying pans. One cast-iron pot. One rice cooker. One electric skillet."

Winter clothing and cookware were common finds.

Almost everyone had brought back something similar.

But six pounds of rice.

Five and a half pounds of pork.

And all those seasonings...

The young soldier paused in surprise.

"You're handing all of this over?"

Lila blinked.

"I thought everything we found had to be turned in."

Had she somehow broken the rules by... following them?

"Nah, that's not what I meant."

The soldier laughed awkwardly before continuing his calculations.

Sophie, who had been listening nearby, glanced into Lila's sack.

She leaned close and lowered her voice.

"You don't have to be that honest. Everybody hides some of the food for themselves."

Rules were rules.

But Ethan was not exactly out here strip-searching everyone.

As long as people did not get greedy, he usually looked the other way.

Sophie herself had tucked away a pound of rice and a packet of salt inside her winter coat.

If she had found pork, there was no way in hell she'd have handed it over.

Lila smiled, her eyes curving warmly.

"Got it. I'll remember that next time."

Sophie looked at the sweet but hopelessly naïve girl and silently worried she would get chewed up by the world one day.

If only she knew.

Lila had already hidden almost an entire truckload of supplies inside the Vault.

When the calculations were finished, Lila's total came to forty-two Survival Credits.

Nearly double what most people had earned.

Several members of the team stared.

"Hold up. Why's hers so damn high? We brought back about the same amount of stuff."

One burly man warming his hands by the fire finally voiced everyone's question.

"Because she turned in six pounds of rice and over five pounds of pork."

The soldier shrugged.

"Did you?"

That shut him up.

Food was always worth the most.

Everything else barely counted by comparison.

Winter coats might cost a fortune inside the refuge, but when the administration bought them back, they were only worth one or two credits each.

The refuge had to make a profit somewhere.

Fuel for trips into Ashton did not magically appear.

Someone had to pay for all that diesel.

The big man scratched his head before grinning.

"Well... yeah, fair enough. Can't argue with that."

Callum was not the least bit jealous.

Who in their right mind would hand over rice and pork like that?

Lila ignored the curious looks around her.

People could think she was an idiot if they wanted.

She was not exactly worried about running out of food.

"If you brought your own lunch, throw it near the fire and warm it up."

The soldiers were still recording supplies, so nobody had anything else to do.

At Ethan's order, one of the younger soldiers hung a large pot of water over the fire.

Then he announced that everyone could start heating their meals.

Some people had bought bread rolls from the canteen that morning.

Others had packed mixed grain porridge.

Everything had frozen into solid bricks by now.

Those lucky enough to own aluminium lunch tins placed them beside the flames.

Everyone else made do with whatever odd containers they had.

Lila's stomach had been growling for ages.

She wanted lunch too.

But everyone else's food was still heating.

Pulling hers out now would be far too obvious.

So she simply hugged her black backpack and warmed herself by the fire without moving.

Sophie noticed.

Poor kid.

Those expensive clothes were probably old things she'd owned before the Collapse.

Judging by the fact she had not even brought lunch, food at home was probably running dangerously low.

Sophie glanced at the coarse multigrain roll inside her lunch tin.

Worst case...

She could let Lila have one bite.

Just one.

It was not because she was some bleeding-heart saint.

She just did not want the girl passing out from hunger.

Her compassion and common sense wrestled with each other in silence.

Ethan's lunch was easily the best.

Two soft bread rolls rested inside his aluminium tin, slowly warming beside the flames.

He had noticed that Lila still had not taken out any food.

Wasn't she supposed to be doing all right?

Had she finally run out of supplies too?

Maybe...

In a few days, he should sit down with her and convince her to move into the refuge.

A young woman living alone up in the mountains could not have an easy life.

The fire crackled brightly.

Warmth slowly returned to everyone's hands, and frozen lunches finally thawed.

The soldiers poured each person a mug of hot water.

People poked at their meals with sticks before carefully lifting them from the fire.

Ethan and Sophie each picked up their warm, slightly stale bread rolls.

"Here. Eat something."

"Come on. Have a bite."

Their voices rang out at exactly the same moment.

Each of them held out half a bread roll toward Lila.

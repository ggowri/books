Chapter 31

Daniel understood exactly what Annabel meant. He stood up, planted his hands on his hips and said, "Get your manager."

The waiter had no wish to invite trouble. He nodded and hurried off to the kitchen to fetch the manager.

The restaurant manager had just hung up on his wife. The snow outside was coming down too hard, and she had taken their child to tutoring and now could not get home. She was urging him to drive over and pick them up.

But this table of customers refused to leave, which meant he could not go anywhere.

"Manager, that table out front still won't go. They won't pay either," the waiter said, looking thoroughly fed up.

"I'll handle it."

The manager rolled up his sleeves. He wanted this over quickly.

With a broad, professional smile, he walked over to the table.

"I'm terribly sorry, everyone. It's almost nine now, and we would normally be closing at this hour. On any ordinary day, you'd be welcome to stay as long as you liked, and we would never rush our guests. But the weather really has taken a turn tonight. The snow outside is already piling up, and none of you are dressed for it. For your own safety, it would be best to head home sooner rather than later."

"You say you don't rush guests, but isn't that exactly what you're doing now? Waive the bill and I won't take this further. Otherwise, we're not leaving."

Annabel dropped back into her chair in a huff. She was furious that the manager was trying to throw them out, and even more furious that Lila still had not turned up.

Daniel's brow tightened. He looked down and called Lila again, but the phone was still switched off.

"Let's keep this pleasant," the manager said. "I can give you twenty per cent off today's bill. We'll pack up the dishes you haven't finished and include two of our premium gift sets as well. How does that sound?"

He had a strong suspicion they were trying to dine and dash, but he did not want a scene, so he gave ground once more.

"What, now you think you can fob us off with two little gift bags? I said waive the bill. Did you not hear me?" Annabel's voice grew sharper.

Edward and Maggie kept their heads down and said nothing. Clearly, they had no intention of stopping their daughter.

The manager's smile faded. He took the walkie-talkie from his pocket and spoke to the restaurant security staff.

"We've got a disturbance in the dining room. Come over, please."

"A disturbance? How is this a disturbance? We're just asking for the bill to be waived." Annabel did not know what he had said into the radio, and a flicker of panic crossed her face.

"You're refusing to pay for your meal. I'm asking security to keep an eye on you while I call the police."

The manager took a step back, no longer interested in arguing, and dialled the police.

"Hello, I need to report a group of customers refusing to pay their bill."

His tone was firm. Edward and the others exchanged glances, suddenly realising the situation might be getting out of hand.

The manager was still speaking to the police.

"The amount involved is eighty-four thousand pounds. Yes. If they refuse to settle the bill, that could lead to a criminal detention, correct?"

A criminal record.

The four people at the table froze when they heard that.

Edward shot to his feet and patted the manager on the shoulder.

"No need for that. No need at all. Don't call the police. We'll pay."

Annabel and Daniel scrambled up as well.

"Yes, yes, of course we're paying. Who said we weren't? Tell the police it was a misunderstanding."

"Sorry to trouble you, officer. We should be able to resolve this privately."

The manager did not want the matter to escalate either. He quickly apologised to the police and ended the call.

"Very well. The total bill comes to eighty-four thousand pounds. Will you be paying by cash, card or mobile payment?"

He handed them the bill so they could check it.

"How much? Eighty-four thousand?"

Maggie could not keep her composure any longer. Her attention had been fixed entirely on the police call, and she had not properly heard the amount.

"That's correct. The two bottles of red alone come to fifty-eight thousand, and then there is the full table of dishes."

The manager nodded without expression.

"That's impossible! We only..."

Maggie was about to argue when Annabel cut her off.

"It's right, Mum. Those two bottles really do cost that much."

She had assumed Lila would come and that they could land her with a brutal bill. She had not expected Lila to stay away, leaving them to pay for it themselves.

Seeing them wavering again, the manager picked up his phone once more.

"If you don't pay, then you'll have to explain it at the station."

"No! We'll pay."

Edward stopped him at once.

Annabel had only just graduated and had not started work yet. They had been considering pushing her towards the civil service. If tonight's nonsense ruined his daughter's career prospects, it would be a disaster.

Grinding his teeth, Edward scanned the payment code on his phone and paid sixty-seven thousand pounds after the twenty per cent discount.

Sixty-seven thousand.

That was half a year's salary for him.

Annabel and Maggie had expensive tastes, and over the years their family had only managed to save a little over a hundred thousand. Now more than half of it was gone in one night.

"This place is a rip-off! I'm going to expose you online and make sure this restaurant shuts down!"

Now that the bill was paid, Annabel found her nerve again and started kicking up a fuss.

"Enough! Haven't you embarrassed us enough? We're going home!"

Edward roared and strode towards the door.

"Pack it up. We want it packed up!"

Maggie glanced at her husband's retreating figure, then hurriedly spoke to the waiter.

They had ordered far too much. After taking photos, they had barely eaten anything, and most of the food was still sitting untouched on the table.

This was a meal that had cost them sixty-seven thousand pounds. They were taking every last bit of it.

Since the bill had finally been settled, the waiter came forward at once with the restaurant's elegant takeaway boxes and packed up everything on the table.

It filled three large bags.

Annabel watched her mother fussing over the leftovers and stamped her foot in embarrassment. Then she pulled Daniel towards the exit.

Maggie, carrying bags in both hands, called out, "Didn't you say there would be gift sets?"

The manager's mouth twitched. He pointed to one side.

"Yuna, give her two bags."

"Here you are. These are our freshly baked floral pastries. We look forward to welcoming you again."

Yuna, the waiter, kept her expression neutral. She simply repeated the line she had been trained to say.

Maggie was in a hurry to catch up with the others. She took the two bags of pastries, nodded quickly and rushed for the door.

The moment she stepped outside, a bone-deep chill swept over her.

Edward, Daniel and Annabel were already shivering in front of the lift, waiting to go down.

Maggie had both hands full of takeaway bags, so she could not even hug herself for warmth like the others.

Annabel and Maggie had both dressed far too lightly today. For the sake of good photos, they were wearing thin strappy dresses.

"Daniel, I'm so cold..." Annabel looked at him pitifully.

Daniel had worn a thick coat when he went out.

He tightened his arms around it, clearly wanting to refuse. But when he saw Edward and Maggie looking at him, he reluctantly took it off and draped it over Annabel's shoulders.

Annabel immediately wrapped herself in the coat.

Now it was Daniel's turn to stand there in a short-sleeved shirt, trembling from the cold.

The lift arrived.

The four of them entered in silence and headed down to the underground car park.

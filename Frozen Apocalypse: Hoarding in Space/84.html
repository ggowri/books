Chapter 83

Mrs. Harris gritted her teeth as she sprinkled the last of the Antiseptic Powder over her shoulder wound.

Afterward, she tore apart an old vest and wrapped the injury as tightly as she could.

The pain was intense.

Despite the freezing temperature inside the shelter, sweat beaded across her forehead.

Once the dressing was secured, she pulled on her coat, lowered herself onto the bed, and closed her eyes.

After everything that had happened over the past several days, she had barely slept at all.

For the first time in what felt like forever, she could finally rest.

Outside, icy northern winds carried swirling snow toward the Lower Vale Mansion.

The courtyard walls stood tall and solid, capped with reinforced roofing that blocked the weather completely.

The wind struck the walls, spiraled briefly, then drifted away in search of somewhere else to go.

"Achoo!"

Lila rubbed her stuffy nose.

Ever since she'd woken up that morning, she'd been sneezing and fighting congestion.

Apparently standing outside collecting lightning during a Long Freeze wasn't the smartest decision she'd ever made.

After digging through her medical supplies, she swallowed a pair of cold tablets with warm water.

Her appetite wasn't great, but she still wanted something hot and comforting.

Pulling on a thick winter jacket, she headed for the kitchen.

The kitchen range hadn't been lit yet.

Perfect.

She could warm the heated bed while cooking lunch.

Crouching beside the range, she fed kindling into the firebox and got a flame going.

Then she reached into the Vault and pulled out a piece of fresh beef.

It was the remaining half from an earlier meal, about a pound in total.

The meat was dark red and beautifully firm.

As the knife sliced through it, the fibers separated cleanly beneath the blade.

She cut the beef into chunky cubes and dropped them into a pot of cold water with several slices of ginger.

As the water heated, foam rose to the surface.

She skimmed it away carefully.

A soft whining sound came from beside her feet.

Looking down, she discovered Snowy and Patches had somehow appeared without her noticing.

The two puppies sat staring at the pot with wide, hopeful eyes.

Apparently they'd caught the scent of beef soup.

"Be patient. You'll get some later."

Lila laughed and continued cooking.

Into the pot went a spoonful of rich soybean paste.

A few star anise pods.

A small stick of cinnamon.

No salt yet.

That would come later.

She covered the pot, leaving only a narrow gap beneath the lid.

Almost immediately, fragrant steam curled into the air.

The aroma of simmering beef filled the kitchen.

Snowy and Patches became even more excited.

Their little whines echoed around the room.

Lila dragged over a small stool and sat beside the range, warming her hands near the firebox opening.

The puppies took advantage of the opportunity.

Both immediately pressed themselves against her legs.

Their tails wagged nonstop.

Lila couldn't help laughing.

She reached down and scratched their heads.

Eventually it was time to make something to go with the soup.

After washing her hands, she poured two large bowls of flour into a mixing basin.

Warm water followed.

A pinch of salt.

Using chopsticks, she stirred until rough flakes of dough formed.

Then she kneaded by hand.

Slowly the dough became smooth and elastic beneath her palms.

She covered the bowl with a damp cloth and left it beside the stove to rest.

Next she checked on the soup.

The moment she lifted the lid, a cloud of steam rushed upward and fogged her face.

The broth had turned milky and rich.

Chunks of beef rolled gently beneath the surface.

She poked one with chopsticks.

Tender.

But not quite ready.

A few more pieces of kindling disappeared into the firebox.

Once the dough had rested, it sprang back lightly when pressed.

Perfect.

She divided it into four portions.

After dusting the board with flour, she rolled the first piece into a thin round.

A layer of lard.

A sprinkle of salt.

A handful of chopped scallions.

She rolled it up, twisted it into a spiral, flattened it, and rolled it out again.

The process repeated until all four flatbreads were ready.

A spoonful of oil went into a frying pan set over the fireplace hob.

The heat there was almost as strong as the main burner.

The oil shimmered.

Ready.

Lila slid the first flatbread into the pan.

A satisfying sizzle filled the room.

Instantly, the scent of toasted scallions and hot oil burst into the air.

She flipped the bread occasionally with a spatula, watching it turn golden brown.

The edges puffed slightly and crisped beautifully.

The first one barely made it out of the pan.

Unable to resist, she tore off a corner and popped it into her mouth.

It was far too hot.

She immediately regretted it.

But the crispy layers and fragrant scallions made it worth the burn.

By the time a stack of flatbreads sat cooling on a plate, the beef soup was finally finished.

The broth was creamy and rich.

Golden droplets of fat floated on the surface.

The beef had become so tender it practically fell apart under the chopsticks.

Before seasoning the main pot, Lila set aside several pieces of beef and a bowl of plain broth.

That portion was reserved for the puppies.

Only afterward did she add salt, a splash of light soy sauce, black pepper, and a little dried chili.

In weather like this, a touch of heat went a long way.

Finally, she scattered chopped coriander over the top.

The green flecks made the soup look even more inviting.

Lila ladled her portion into a large bowl.

Several scallion flatbreads went onto a plate.

Then she carried everything back to the east parlour.

It felt especially cold today.

She wanted to eat on the heated bed.

The low table was pulled out from beside the giant dog bed and placed atop the warm platform.

The soup and flatbreads followed.

Lila settled comfortably onto the heated bed and tore off a piece of bread.

Crispy flakes immediately scattered across the tabletop.

She dipped the bread into the soup.

The rich broth soaked into every layer.

The first bite was crispy.

The second became soft and savory.

Beef, scallions, and spices blended together into something deeply satisfying.

A moment later she took a sip of soup.

The heat slid down her throat and spread through her entire body.

Outside, snow and wind continued to sweep across the countryside.

Some flakes struck the reinforced roof overhead with soft rustling sounds.

Inside, the room glowed warmly in the dim winter light.

The soup steamed.

The hearth radiated heat.

Lila picked up a chunk of beef and chewed slowly.

The flavor only grew richer with each bite.

The smell drove Snowy and Patches absolutely crazy.

The two puppies danced around on the floor, stamping their paws impatiently.

Lila ignored them.

Eventually she finished every last bite.

Half a pot of soup remained.

Along with one final flatbread.

She hopped off the heated bed and carried the remaining soup back to the kitchen.

The pot went into the cupboard for later.

The last flatbread was torn into pieces and dropped into the bowl of plain beef broth she'd reserved earlier.

Then she poured the entire mixture into the puppies' food dish.

"Snowy! Patches! Dinner time!"

The response was immediate.

Both puppies came racing over.

Neither was remotely picky.

Whenever they found a piece of beef, they chewed enthusiastically.

Whenever they found bread, they chewed enthusiastically.

Every few bites they lapped up mouthfuls of broth.

Honestly, they looked like they were enjoying it even more than she had.

After cleaning the kitchen, Lila pulled out the wooden target board she used for shooting practice.

Lightning or no lightning, she wasn't about to neglect her other skills.

The more ways she could protect herself, the better.

Interestingly, today's training felt easier than usual.

Perhaps storing so much lightning had strengthened the Vault again.

Her aim felt sharper.

Her shots carried more force.

By the time she packed away the target board, a light sheen of sweat covered her forehead.

Between training and cooking, she smelled faintly of smoke, oil, and sweat.

Not exactly pleasant.

Returning to the east parlour, she grabbed a fresh set of pajamas from the wardrobe and headed into the bathroom.

When she emerged sometime later, she felt completely refreshed.

Clean.

Warm.

Comfortable.

The old pajamas went straight into the washing machine.

Then Lila returned to the east parlour, ready to enjoy the rest of her peaceful afternoon.

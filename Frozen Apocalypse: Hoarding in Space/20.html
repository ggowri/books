Chapter 19

"Heh."

Mrs. Green's husband was a man of few words. Whatever her brother said, he only chuckled.

"Are you bored sitting there, love? Pick yourself a couple of little tomatoes. Mine are lovely eaten straight off the plant."

Mrs. Green looked back and called to Lila.

Lila was indeed getting a little bored. Since Mrs. Green had offered, she stood up and picked two small yellow tomatoes.

She rubbed one on her clothes and took a bite.

The yellow tomato was full of juice, with a faint sweetness and a rich tomato fragrance. It was delicious.

Lila looked at the golden fruit in her hand and found herself wondering, with complete seriousness, why yellow tomatoes were still called tomatoes. Surely something that colour deserved its own name.

"She's a pretty little thing, isn't she? Shame she's already married..."

Mrs. Green's sister-in-law was still thinking about her friend's son.

"Stop staring at the poor girl like that. You look like some nosy old flirt."

Mrs. Green nudged her with an elbow.

The two women immediately laughed together in a rather wicked little way.

Lila could not help laughing too.

These days, she had been stockpiling in a constant rush, her nerves stretched tight. But at that moment, she seemed to relax a little.

If the Collapse ever ended, perhaps she could live like them. Grow a bit of food, have a couple of friends, and pass the days in peace.

"Why is it so hot this year? I don't remember it being this bad before."

After the laughter faded, sweat began dripping even faster from the sister-in-law's forehead, and she could not help complaining.

At the mention of the heat, Lila thought for a moment, then said, "The weather has been rather strange lately. You shouldn't sell all your vegetables. Keep some for yourselves, just in case there's a drought or something. At least then you'll have food."

"Exactly. I've heard things haven't been very settled lately either. We really should keep some grain at home. If something happens, we can stay put and not go anywhere."

The sister-in-law immediately picked up Lila's words, lowering her voice as though afraid someone might overhear.

After that, everyone began discussing all kinds of strange stories and rumours from nearby villages. Lila listened along and learned quite a bit.

She only hoped that after today's conversation, they really would keep some food for themselves.

Time passed quickly when chatting with other people. It felt as though only a blink had passed, but two hours were gone.

Several large sacks of vegetables had already been piled at the entrance of the polytunnel.

Mrs. Green weighed everything. There were two thousand pounds in total, one thousand pounds of aubergines and one thousand pounds of tomatoes.

Without a word, Lila scanned the payment code on the wall and sent over three thousand dollars.

"Oh, you child. We haven't even loaded the van yet. Why are you in such a rush to pay?"

Mrs. Green sounded almost reproachful, as if Lila's quick payment had made her feel embarrassed.

"It's fine. Paying now or later makes no difference. I still need to trouble you to help me load it."

Lila smiled brightly and put her phone back into her pocket.

They were proper farm workers, used to hard physical labour, and all of them were strong.

In no time at all, the full tonne of vegetables had been loaded into Lila's van.

"Let's just do this one load today. Come back tomorrow for the rest. Otherwise, if you come again later, you'll still have to wait for us. We'll work a bit longer tonight and try to have everything ready by the time you arrive tomorrow, so you can just take it away load by load."

Mrs. Green explained the plan to Lila.

"Didn't you say you were stewing meat tonight? If we're working late, what time do we get to eat?"

The sister-in-law immediately sensed that something was wrong and asked in a hurry.

"Give it a rest and ask the girl what she thinks first."

Mrs. Green elbowed her again.

Lila accidentally laughed out loud.

"That works for me. I'll do as you suggest. I'll pay a five-hundred-dollar deposit now and come first thing tomorrow to collect the rest."

Lila paid another five hundred dollars.

Tonight, they might harvest all the vegetables she had ordered. Without a deposit, they would probably feel uneasy.

"You really are straightforward. Come tomorrow, and we'll have everything sorted properly for you."

Mrs. Green truly liked the way this young woman handled things. She did not need reminding at all.

After saying goodbye to everyone, Lila drove away.

She returned to Lockridge Storage Unit and moved all the vegetables into the Vault. Seeing that it was still early, she turned the van towards Lower Vale.

She had not been to the Lower Vale Mansion for several days, and the place had already changed greatly.

The heated hearth bench was finished. The inglenook fireplace had been completed too, and the windows and doors had been fitted.

It finally looked like a proper home.

Compared with before, it looked neater and much more orderly.

When the manager saw Lila, he came over on his own initiative and reported the progress to her.

Lila saw that there were no obvious problems, then drove home.

The next morning, Lila got up early and went straight to the vegetable polytunnels.

When she arrived, vegetables had already been piled outside in small hills.

The people inside heard the engine of her van and quickly came out.

"You're early, love. Look, we've got the vegetables ready for you."

Mrs. Green was very proud. What she admired most about herself was her efficiency. In one night, she had managed to harvest half the vegetables from every polytunnel.

"Is this all of it?"

Although there were plenty of vegetables piled at the entrance, it was still not enough for Lila.

"No, no. Over there, and over there. Two more piles."

Mrs. Green pointed into the distance, and only then did Lila relax.

They called over the couple from yesterday to help, and everyone loaded the van without fuss.

This load weighed a full three tonnes.

Two thousand five hundred pounds of cucumbers, one thousand pounds of green beans, three hundred pounds of courgettes, one thousand pounds of large spring onions, one thousand pounds of peppers, and two hundred pounds of broccoli.

"These onions still have their roots. Didn't you say you wanted to keep some vegetables at home? Plant these when the time comes, and they'll last you quite a while."

Mrs. Green patted the thick roots at the base of the onions. They looked as if they would be easy to keep alive.

Lila thought it was an excellent suggestion.

When the Collapse came and she had nothing else to do, she could take the onions out and grow them.

With a van full of vegetables, Lila headed straight for Lockridge Storage Unit. A round trip took two hours.

In truth, it was rather far, and Lila could not be bothered driving such a long distance again and again.

But to make absolutely sure nothing went wrong, and to make certain no one discovered the Vault, she was willing to go to the trouble.

In her previous life, she had suffered enough because she had told others about the Vault.

In this life, she would not tell a single soul.

The second load was leafy greens.

Five hundred pounds of crown daisy, one thousand pounds of coriander, five hundred pounds of pak choi, one thousand pounds of loose-leaf lettuce, five hundred pounds of chives, and five hundred pounds of celery.

Lila settled the full payment. Including the earlier deposit, she paid fourteen thousand five hundred dollars in total.

She planned to grow some leafy greens herself when she was bored after the Collapse. This amount would be enough for now.

These polytunnels did not grow potatoes. After asking Mrs. Green, Lila learned that she would need to go to the village at the very far end of town to buy potatoes and sweet potatoes.

That was where the specialist potato and sweet potato farm was located.

After moving this load of vegetables into the Vault, Lila followed Mrs. Green's directions and arrived at the innermost village in Sunwell.

The sweet potato farm here was completely different from a family-run polytunnel. Everything was managed in a much more standardised way.

Once Lila explained why she had come, they were able to bring out ready-stocked potatoes and sweet potatoes from the storehouse.

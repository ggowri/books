Chapter 48

Leo wrapped his clothes tightly around himself and made a circuit of basement level one, eventually choosing a distant, hidden corner near parking bay D3077 to use for disposing of waste later.

After walking around, he returned and knocked on Callum's basement storage room door.

Callum did not answer, but a short while later, the door opened cautiously.

The moment Leo stepped inside, he felt how warm the room was.

"Why is your storage room so warm?" Leo looked around, but did not see any heating equipment.

"Heh. The heating pipe happens to run through mine."

Callum pointed towards the very back of the room, where an iron pipe as thick as a forearm ran along the wall.

Leo stepped over the supplies scattered all over the floor and touched it. Sure enough, it was warm, even warmer than the radiator upstairs.

"You really are lucky." Sometimes, luck mattered too.

"I can even get hot water here. If you ever need any, come over and take some." Callum pointed at the valve beside the pipe. It was meant for bleeding the system, but it could also release hot water from the pipe.

"Still, it probably won't last long. Once the upstairs heating stops, this will go cold too," Callum added.

Leo nodded in agreement. If the heating indoors could keep running forever, he would never have come down here to live.

"I picked a spot for dumping waste, near parking bay D3077. You can use it too if you need to."

As he spoke, Leo handed over the walkie-talkie he had been holding.

"Fine by me. When the time comes, we'll go together. Safer that way," Callum said readily.

Leo could not quite put his finger on what felt odd about that, but if Callum wanted to go together, then so be it.

"I'm thinking I'll finish setting up tonight and just sleep down here. Mainly because this place is so warm. It's cosier than upstairs," Callum said, changing his plan on the spot.

"That works."

Before leaving, Leo thought for a moment, then pointed at the chaotic pile of luggage on the floor.

"If you don't mind the trouble, bring a small mattress down. You'll sleep better."

"Oh, right. How is your brain so useful?"

Callum suddenly understood.

The thought of living in a basement storage room had made him uncomfortable, and this was exactly what had been missing.

A bed.

After leaving Callum's room, Leo went upstairs once more.

The cold was brutal, but he kept worrying that he had not brought enough from home. Once the lift stopped working, going back up would be a real problem.

Callum had the same thought. After taking down a mattress, he also went upstairs again.

Both of them checked their flats once more, and each brought down another suitcase and a rucksack full of belongings.

Then they locked their doors tightly. They probably would not be living upstairs again any time soon.

On this trip down, Leo ran straight into Sophie.

Leo remained fairly calm, but Sophie was startled stiff.

Sophie had not expected to meet anyone. She had planned to move secretly into her basement storage room, and after several trips without seeing a soul, she had somehow run into a man on the very last one.

She guarded the things in her arms warily and stared hard at Leo, as though if he made one wrong move, she would bite him on the spot.

"It's fine. We're neighbours."

Leo explained briefly, then turned and went straight to his own storage room with the things in his hands.

Only after Sophie watched him close his door did her rigid body relax. She carried the last box of instant noodles she had brought into her own basement storage room.

Without arranging it beforehand, the three of them all moved into the Basement that night.

Sophie's storage room was in much better condition than the other two.

A couple of years earlier, when she had been studying to qualify as a solicitor, the neighbours next door had been too noisy, so she had prepared for her exams down here.

At the time, she had furnished the room with a second-hand single bed. It had not only a desk and chair, but also wipe-clean carpet for comfort. She had even lined the walls with acoustic insulation.

For her now, in the Long Freeze, that acoustic insulation could also serve as thermal insulation.

As for the tap by the door, every basement storage room had one.

Because everyone had roughly the same instinctive need for security, Sophie had also placed her bed at the very back.

The outer area was separated from the sleeping space with supplies and bookcases.

The only difference was that Sophie had a desk and chair at the foot of her bed, which made it easier to eat and write in her diary.

By five in the morning, none of the three could laugh at the others. They had all stayed up the entire night moving, and now they were each lying on their simple beds, slowly drifting off to sleep.

Callum's mattress was also laid at the very back, right beside the thick heating pipe.

As a result, he slept more comfortably than the other two.

At eight in the morning, Lila woke up and habitually picked up her phone to check the residents' group chat.

But there had been no new messages in the group since eleven o'clock the night before.

She checked the internet connection.

It had gone down.

Lila remembered that in her previous life, the internet had not gone down until half a month into the Long Freeze. It had not happened this early. This was probably only a temporary outage.

She put down her phone and did not dwell on it.

She usually checked online messages only out of curiosity. They had no real effect on her life.

First, she warmed the house. Then she used a clay pot to cook a plain rice porridge and ate it piping hot with pickles.

After breakfast, Lila sat in her chair and stared into space.

Something was on her mind.

She wanted steamed buns.

Not the thick-skinned ones from breakfast shops with that strong artificial flavour, but big, fragrant, homemade meat buns stuffed generously with filling.

There was nothing urgent to do while riding out the Collapse in comfort. Today could be a bun-making day.

She put on her old-fashioned padded jacket and padded shoes, then took her phone and phone stand into the kitchen.

She found a suitable drama to pass the time and set it playing on the stand.

She scooped a large basin of flour, added dried yeast, mixed it with warm water, covered the basin with a pot lid, and placed it on the heated hearth bench in the east parlour.

The bench was warm, so the dough would rise faster there.

Once the dough was set aside, the next step was the filling.

From the Vault, she selected a large piece of fresh pork, nicely marbled with fat and lean meat. It was perfect for steamed buns.

Mince made by a machine never tasted as good. Meat chopped by hand with a kitchen knife was best.

Lila brought over a chair and sat down, watching her drama while slowly cutting the pork.

She cut the marbled pork into small pieces until the chopping board was heaped full, then continued chopping it fine.

With an interesting drama to keep her company, even chopping meat did not feel boring.

Before she knew it, the pork had already become a smooth mince.

She transferred it into a basin, added seasonings to bring out the aroma and remove any richness, then added two spring onions. She stirred clockwise, adding a little water from time to time and working it into the meat.

Buns made this way would steam up with tender, juicy filling.

Once the basin of meat filling was mixed, she covered it with a pot lid.

Then she took out more vegetables from the Vault and made cabbage filling and aubergine filling as well.

When all three fillings were ready, each gave off its own tempting fragrance.

Lila washed her hands and thumped her aching shoulders.

She was not hungry now anyway, so there was no hurry to wrap the buns. She could wait until the dough had risen properly.

She returned to the east parlour and habitually picked up her phone again.

By then, the signal had come back.

Phone in hand, she walked over to the sofa, picked up the tea from the coffee table without knowing whether it had gone completely cold, and drank two large mouthfuls.

Once her thirst was eased, she collapsed into the sofa.

She scrolled through short videos and checked the residents' group chat.

Short videos were dull now. Almost all of them were about the Long Freeze and the Collapse.

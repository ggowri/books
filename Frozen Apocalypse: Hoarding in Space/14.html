Chapter 13

"When can you deliver it?"

Lila wanted it done as soon as possible.

"We'll spend all day loading today. I can deliver half tomorrow morning and the other half the day after. Would that work?"

Gregory asked for her opinion. The main issue was the quantity. Packing that much coal into sacks would take time and labour.

"That's fine."

Lila paid a deposit of fifty thousand dollars. The rest would be settled once the goods arrived.

She gave Gregory the address of Lockridge Storage Unit.

Early the next morning, Lila was already waiting outside the storage unit.

Worried that taking too much into the Vault at once would make her unwell again, she lay across the driver's seat and tried to sleep.

Her logic was simple. If she slept a little more, perhaps the physical backlash would hurt a little less.

At twenty past nine, five large coal lorries arrived.

The coal yard's lorries were enormous. Compared with them, Lila's Luton van looked almost like a toy.

Gregory climbed down from one of the lorries.

"Miss Spencer, this is two hundred and fifty tonnes. Have a look. I picked the best stock for you. Not a bit of slack in it."

Gregory sounded full of confidence.

"All right. Thank you. Ask them to unload."

At Lila's words, Gregory immediately waved his hand and called loudly for the workers to begin.

They had brought proper coal-moving equipment with them. A conveyor belt carried the sacks of coal straight from the lorries to the entrance of the storage unit.

"Wait a moment."

Lila raised a hand, and the workers stopped.

She took a small knife from her pocket, crouched down, and stabbed it into the bottom of one sack.

Pulling the knife free, she lifted the slit and looked inside.

There was hardly any coal dust at the bottom of the sack. It was full of solid pieces of coal.

The coal was black and glossy. Some of the broken surfaces even caught the sunlight.

It was good coal.

Lila picked a few more sacks from the middle and checked those as well. The quality was much the same.

He really had not tried to fool her. There was no slack packed inside.

"You're sharper than you look, Miss Spencer. See? I didn't try anything, did I?"

Gregory did not look guilty in the slightest. For an order worth five hundred thousand dollars, he was not about to ruin things for a petty little gain.

"It is good."

Lila approved the batch.

The workers carried on. Once the conveyor belt brought the sacks to the entrance, they stacked them one by one inside the storage unit.

Two hundred and fifty tonnes was no small amount. Even with the conveyor taking the coal to the door, stacking it neatly inside still took a long time.

Only after everything had been moved in did Lila pay two hundred thousand dollars without hesitation.

Together with the previous fifty-thousand-dollar deposit, that cleared the payment for the first half of the coal.

Gregory looked at the two hundred thousand dollars that had arrived in his account and grinned so broadly his eyes nearly disappeared. He promised that the remaining two hundred and fifty tonnes would be delivered on time the next day.

Lila watched the coal yard's vehicles drive away. Once they were all gone, she entered the storage unit, pulled down the roller shutter, and walked towards the tightly stacked sacks of coal.

She reached out and, with a flicker of will, moved thirty sacks into the Vault.

The coal appeared in the farthest corner of the Vault, a long way from her clothes and food supplies.

Lila moved her arms and legs. There was no discomfort at all.

So her body could handle thirty sacks at a time.

She touched the sacks and quickly moved the coal before her into the Vault.

By the time she had transferred half of it, she could no longer hold out.

She braced one hand against the wall, slowly sat down on the ground, closed her eyes, and breathed carefully.

At some point, still sitting like that, she fell asleep.

When Lila woke, another hour had passed.

She closed her eyes and entered the Vault, wanting to check the coal she had stored. Instead, she discovered that the space had grown larger.

It was twice the size it had been before.

The change left Lila both shocked and delighted.

Could it be that exhausting herself by moving things into the Vault was what made it expand?

She had no time to think too deeply about it. Using her will, she moved the coal even farther away, completely separating it from the food, clothes and quilts.

Once she had fixed the arrangement, Lila stood up and tried to continue taking the coal into the Vault.

She still felt tired, but it was noticeably easier than before.

Gritting her teeth, she managed to move all two hundred and fifty tonnes of coal into the Vault.

Then she forced herself to drive home.

The moment she got in, she collapsed into bed and slept without even eating.

In her dream, Lila complained about herself. Day after day, she seemed to do nothing except sleep.

The next day, Lila woke feeling a little heavy, but she could move without much trouble.

She still went to the storage unit. Today, she had to receive the other half of the coal.

The second batch arrived shortly after nine.

Gregory and the workers stared at the completely empty storage unit, so shocked their eyes nearly fell out.

"You moved it all in one day, Miss Spencer?"

Gregory sounded astonished.

"Yes. It was taken away. They needed it urgently."

Lila simply followed his assumption.

Gregory did not ask any more questions. He directed the workers to move the remaining coal into the storage unit as well.

Lila used the same method as the day before to inspect several sacks. Once she confirmed there was no issue with the quality, she left the workers to their job.

After the second batch had been delivered, Lila paid the final two hundred and fifty thousand dollars. Gregory and the workers then drove away.

Looking at the storage unit packed full of coal, Lila only stored a third of it in the Vault.

The moment she felt a little tired, she stopped.

The day before, she had moved everything into the Vault in one go because she was afraid there would not be enough space in the storage unit. Now that all the coal had already been delivered and stacked, there was no need to rush.

Just then, her phone rang.

"Hello, you have one parcel... no, wait, several hundred parcels have arrived. Can you come today? They're piled up outside the depot. Please come and sign for them."

The courier tried his best to keep his tone calm.

Only then did Lila remember. This was the batch of food she had ordered online.

"Sorry. I'll come over now."

After hanging up, Lila drove her van towards the parcel depot.

The closer she got, the clearer the mountain of cardboard boxes became.

All of them... were hers?

Lila could hardly believe it. She had spent quite a lot when placing the orders, but it had not felt like this much.

"Hello. I'm here to collect my parcels. The phone number ends in 8820."

The courier searched using the last four digits she gave him, and a long string of collection codes appeared below.

Only then did he realise.

"Oh, so it's you. You're finally here. There are far too many parcels. I've been worried sick about losing one or two of them."

The courier was rather talkative. As he spoke, he headed towards the entrance.

Seeing the Luton van parked outside, he turned back and smiled at Lila.

"You were very sensible to bring a van. You really do need one for this lot. Nothing else would fit it all."

Lila laughed.

"Yes, sorry to trouble you. Could you help me load them into the van?"

As she finished speaking, she scanned the payment code on the wall.

A moment later, the automated voice announced that two hundred dollars had been received through the payment app.

The courier perked up at once.

He shouted into the building, "Liam, come and help!"

A strong young man came out from inside.

He answered briskly, then climbed nimbly into the back of the van.

The first courier passed the parcels up from below, while Liam took each box and stacked it neatly in the van bed.

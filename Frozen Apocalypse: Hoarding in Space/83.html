Chapter 82

The crowd at the doorway finally moved.

Someone charged forward and slammed into Luke, knocking him back onto the bed. A hand immediately clamped over his mouth, muffling any sound he might make.

The others rushed in.

Several people grabbed for the knife at once.

Even when the blade nicked their hands, no one made a sound.

The little girl's mother took the weapon.

Her face hardened.

For her child to eat, for her child to survive, there were things she had to do.

She drove the knife downward.

Then again.

And again.

The room blurred into panic and desperation.

When it was over, Luke lay motionless on the bed, his eyes still staring at the ceiling.

The young mother froze.

Only then did she seem to realize what had happened.

The knife slipped from her fingers and clattered onto the floor.

Mrs. Harris stood there breathing heavily, raising her bloodstained wooden stake.

"We did it."

Her voice trembled.

"We've got food again."

Relief swept through the room.

They could survive.

At least for now.

Yet alongside the relief came fear.

A line had been crossed.

Mrs. Harris lowered her voice.

"Who else?"

"Who else stole supplies and killed people yesterday?"

"There are four more."

The young mother answered immediately.

Now that she'd started talking, the memories came clearly.

She remembered every face.

Every one of them.

Mrs. Harris nodded.

"Then we finish this."

Before fear could settle back into their hearts, they moved.

Using the same strategy, the survivors struck quickly.

One room after another.

One looter after another.

Before long, three more dangerous men had been subdued, and their stolen supplies reclaimed.

Only one remained.

When the group approached the final room, they immediately sensed something was wrong.

The lock was broken just like the others.

But this time, the occupant was awake.

He sat on his bedding holding a knife, staring toward the doorway.

Waiting.

He clearly knew what was happening.

Mrs. Harris now carried a large machete taken from one of the previous looters.

Without hesitation, she swung.

The wire securing the door snapped apart.

The man clearly hadn't expected her to make the first move.

He rushed out immediately, knife in hand.

The survivors met him head on.

By now, desperation had overcome fear.

The struggle erupted in the narrow corridor.

The man was strong.

Much stronger than most of them.

But there were sixteen survivors standing together.

Sixteen people with nothing left to lose.

They grabbed his arms.

His legs.

His shoulders.

Even when the knife lashed out and left cuts and scrapes, nobody let go.

Not a single person.

The man shouted in panic.

Moments later, the struggle ended.

Silence fell across the shelter.

Then came cheers.

The last of the violent looters was gone.

The survivors raised their clubs and improvised weapons high into the air.

For the first time in days, hope returned.

Mrs. Harris managed a bittersweet smile.

The people she'd helped bring down today were once people she'd tried to protect.

But there had been no other choice.

If the rest were going to live, something had to change.

One of the survivors pointed toward several closed basement rooms.

"What about them?"

Adrenaline still ran high through the group.

Mrs. Harris glanced at the shut doors.

"The people in those rooms came after dark."

"They never tried to steal anything."

"They stayed out of trouble."

"They're not our enemies."

She knew exactly who was behind those doors.

Sophie.

Leo.

Callum.

Chloe Bennett.

Nathan Brooks.

Quiet people.

Careful people.

People who kept to themselves.

Once the looters had been dealt with, Mrs. Harris recovered all the stolen expedition parkas.

Together, the survivors carried the bodies outside.

Afterward, they searched every room in the shelter.

Every corner.

Every storage space.

Every empty basement.

When the search was complete, Mrs. Harris counted the survivors.

Twenty.

Including the five people living separately, twenty people remained.

She walked over and knocked on each of the closed doors.

"Meeting time."

"Everyone, come join us."

Inside her room, Sophie sat gripping a knife.

She still wasn't sure whether the people outside could be trusted.

Leo and Callum, however, stepped out almost immediately, each carrying a large blade for protection.

If someone wanted trouble, they'd deal with it.

No one could stay locked inside a tiny basement forever.

When Sophie saw them leave their rooms, she tightened her grip on her knife and followed.

If things went badly, she'd have to side with Leo and Callum.

A moment later, Chloe and Nathan emerged as well.

Mrs. Harris visibly relaxed.

"Thank you."

"Thank you for trusting me."

"The troublemakers are gone."

"Let's talk."

The survivors standing behind her looked exhausted.

Many were injured.

Most were elderly or weakened by hunger.

When they saw Leo and Callum carrying large blades, several instinctively stepped back.

Especially from Callum.

The tension was obvious.

Leo glanced around awkwardly.

"We're all on the same side."

That was the best reassurance he could offer.

Mrs. Harris led everyone into the shelter's central area.

The place looked worn and battered after everything that had happened.

She looked around at the survivors.

"Starting today, the people left here are one group again."

"We help each other."

"We look out for each other."

"And we survive this Collapse together."

She paused before looking toward the five independent residents.

"You have a choice."

"You can contribute supplies and join the communal ration meals."

"Or you can continue eating from your own stockpiles."

"If you choose the second option, that's completely fine. But you won't be part of the communal food distribution."

"The decision is yours."

"Thanks, Mrs. Harris. We'll eat separately."

Callum answered immediately.

His supplies could still last quite a while.

There was no reason to share them yet.

Leo didn't object.

"I'll be eating separately too," Sophie said.

Chloe and Nathan made the same choice.

Mrs. Harris nodded.

"Fair enough."

"We won't take advantage of anyone."

"But when there's work to do, everyone pitches in."

Nobody argued.

Forcing people would make her no better than the looters they had just removed.

After discussing future responsibilities and the shelter's new living arrangements, the meeting ended.

The survivors immediately got to work.

Together they repaired the damaged entrance.

When they discovered there was no usable doorframe left, they dismantled one from an unused room and repurposed it.

The final result was ugly.

Crooked.

Patched together from scraps.

But it kept the wind out.

That was enough.

With so many people gone, bedding was no longer in short supply.

Mrs. Harris hung two thick quilts over the entrance like insulated curtains.

The shelter instantly felt warmer.

There was also enough space now.

The remaining survivors were moved into empty rooms.

Including Annabel's family.

The cooking system stayed the same.

Each day, someone would go above ground and prepare food in the large iron pot.

With fewer mouths to feed, the remaining communal supplies suddenly seemed far more substantial.

There was enough food left to last for several more days.

Perhaps even a couple of weeks.

Everyone held onto the same hope.

That before the supplies ran out, another relief shipment would arrive.

Soon, a fresh pot of hot porridge was ready.

The cooks carried it back into the shelter.

For a brief moment, it almost felt like an ordinary day.

As though yesterday's chaos had never happened.

Everyone gathered to eat.

The little girl's mother carefully spooned warm porridge into her daughter's mouth.

The child's eyes lit up.

"Mommy, this tastes so good."

The woman smiled.

A real smile this time.

She gently brushed a hand through her daughter's hair.

The movement pulled at the injury on her arm, making her wince slightly.

Still, she kept smiling.

After the meal, everyone returned to their rooms.

Mrs. Harris headed back to hers as well.

The bedding still carried traces of what had happened there.

She walked to the Underground Car Park and found a clean set of blankets.

The old ones were stripped away and discarded outside.

In the Collapse, nobody could afford to be picky.

A bed was a bed.

Back in her room, she stood before a mirror.

Carefully, she exposed her injured shoulder.

The wound stretched several inches across.

Angry and swollen.

Far worse than she'd allowed herself to admit.

Mrs. Harris stared at it silently.

Tomorrow would bring another fight for survival.

But for tonight, at least, the shelter still stood.

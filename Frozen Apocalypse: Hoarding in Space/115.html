Chapter 114

The soldiers collected the firewood brought back by the survivors and recorded each person's Survival Credits.

The wood followed a strict allocation system.

Just as Ethan had instructed, two thirds of the daily collection went into storage while the remaining third was offered for sale to refuge residents.

The extra reserve was being stockpiled for emergencies down the road.

During the first month, though, almost no one had enough Survival Credits to buy firewood.

Everyone was too busy gathering wood to earn credits in the first place.

The next morning, Maggie got up before dawn, cooked a pot of mixed grain porridge, and headed into the mountains with Edward after breakfast.

Anna insisted she was too injured to work and stubbornly refused to leave the heated bed.

In the end, she stayed behind.

The firewood teams were assigned by room number.

Since Sophie, Maggie, and Edward all lived in nearby rooms, they ended up together once again.

Sophie was bundled up in heavy winter clothing.

A thick scarf covered almost her entire face.

Even so, Maggie recognised her instantly.

The moment she spotted Sophie, her entire body stiffened.

She remembered Sophie's fighting skills.

She remembered her commanding voice.

She remembered her authority.

Maggie immediately lowered her head, terrified Sophie might come after her again.

Right now, she had absolutely no chance of standing up to her.

Sophie was surprised as well.

She hadn't expected Maggie to recover well enough to return to collecting firewood after her punishment.

Still, what was done was done.

She had no interest in dragging the matter out.

The more she got involved, the more trouble she'd create for herself.

She'd rather spend that energy building her own firewood supply.

Today's destination was the same nearby hillside as yesterday.

There were noticeably fewer fallen branches now.

Collecting wood wasn't quite as effortless anymore.

Still, after another twenty minute session, the supervisors called everyone back.

Sophie's pace hadn't changed.

She still finished with four full bundles.

Without Anna slowing them down, Edward and Maggie each managed to collect three bundles.

That was enough to keep their room warm for a while.

Callum also gathered four bundles through sheer hard work.

As they walked back, he glanced over the increasingly barren stretch of woodland.

A spark lit up in his eyes.

With supervisors walking ahead, he couldn't make anything too obvious.

Instead, he quietly spoke to someone behind him.

"Planning to trade your firewood?"

"What?"

The man hadn't heard him clearly.

"Your firewood."

"Thinking about trading some for Survival Credits?"

Callum asked again.

"Yeah."

"I'm probably trading in two bundles."

The man nodded honestly.

He was raising a young son alone.

More Survival Credits meant more food for the boy.

Callum remembered him.

The little kid couldn't have been more than a few years old.

"What about candy instead?"

"I'll give you three pieces for every bundle."

"The refuge doesn't hand out candy."

Callum had plenty of food stored away.

Instant noodles were too valuable to part with.

Candy, on the other hand, was something he could spare.

Trading sweets for firewood seemed like a worthwhile deal.

The man thought for a moment before nodding enthusiastically.

Candy had become a luxury.

Even the refuge wouldn't exchange Survival Credits for it.

If one bundle of wood could earn three sweets for his son, that sounded like a bargain.

As for Survival Credits...

He could always collect more wood tomorrow.

Callum quietly approached several more people toward the back of the group.

Anyone interested followed him home after work.

By the end of the afternoon, he'd traded thirty pieces of candy for ten bundles of firewood.

"Spread the word for me."

"If anyone wants to trade wood for supplies, or supplies for wood, send them my way."

Callum grinned as he spoke.

Everyone left feeling they'd made a great deal.

Naturally, they promised to help advertise.

After they were gone, Callum stacked the ten new bundles neatly against the wall.

This was his starting capital.

Still, he had to keep things organised.

He didn't want piles of supplies making Leo's life inconvenient.

He loaded today's firewood into the kitchen range, filled a pot with water, and soon the room was pleasantly warm.

Leo would be off work before long.

By the time he got home, there'd be a warm room waiting for him and hot water ready to drink.

As the heated bed slowly warmed, Callum straightened both of their sleeping areas.

Just then, someone opened the door.

Leo was home.

The first thing he noticed was the huge stack of firewood piled in the kitchen corner.

"That's... a lot of wood."

He looked genuinely surprised.

Callum hurried over with a steaming mug of hot water.

"Leo, can I run something by you?"

"Sure."

Leo accepted the mug naturally and sat on the edge of the heated bed.

"I want to start trading supplies."

"I'll probably need a little storage space in the kitchen."

"You don't mind, do you?"

"Whatever I earn, I'll trade for food, and I'll split it with you."

Since they were sharing the room, Callum felt it was only right to ask first.

"No problem."

"I told you before that you should start doing business."

"As long as you don't fill the whole place with junk."

Leo drained the hot water in one long drink before climbing onto the heated bed.

It was already wonderfully warm thanks to Callum.

He slid beneath the blankets with a satisfied sigh.

"If the bed's this warm every night, I wouldn't care if the hallway got blocked."

"I'd climb through the window."

Callum broke into a wide grin.

"Thanks, Leo."

The knot in his chest finally loosened.

His excellent roommate truly didn't mind.

After a full day's work, Maggie climbed onto the heated bed.

The instant she stretched out, she winced in pain.

The frostbite blisters on her legs had stuck to her padded trousers.

Pulling the fabric away felt like tearing her skin apart.

Carefully, she rolled up one trouser leg.

Damaged skin clung stubbornly to the cloth.

"Oh, put that away."

"That's disgusting."

Anna happened to glance over.

The sight of Maggie's leg made her gag.

The raw, bloodied flesh was horrifying to look at.

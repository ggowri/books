Chapter 104

Lila pulled on her full set of extreme cold weather gear and stepped outside.

With a pair of binoculars in hand, she surveyed the refuge below the mountain.

The settlement no longer looked empty.

Small figures moved along the roads between buildings.

They clearly weren't soldiers.

Looks like a lot of survivors made it.

Smoke curled from dozens of chimneys, proof that people had already settled into the houses.

After a few minutes, her fingers began to sting from the cold.

Lila lowered the binoculars and headed back inside.

Snowy and Patches had grown noticeably bigger over the past few days.

More importantly, they seemed to be developing minds of their own.

Whenever Lila went outside, the two puppies would scratch frantically at the door, desperate to follow her.

Back inside, she removed her fur hat and heavy coat.

The two dogs were still sitting by the entrance, whining pitifully.

"Alright then. Go ahead."

A mischievous smile crossed her face as she opened the door.

The puppies exploded through the doorway.

Three seconds later, they spun around.

Then they sprinted back inside even faster than they'd gone out.

This time they didn't stop at the entrance.

They bolted straight into their doghouse.

Apparently that was enough outdoor adventure for one day.

Laughing to herself, Lila shut the door.

She headed into the west room to check on her vegetables.

The leafy greens were thriving.

Some of the chrysanthemum greens had grown a little too tall and would soon become tough.

Picking up a pair of small scissors beside the planter boxes, she carefully trimmed them.

The fresh greens were bundled together and stored inside the Vault.

That way they'd stay perfectly fresh until she wanted to eat them.

Once she'd finished tidying the planters, she returned to the east parlour.

It suddenly occurred to her that she hadn't eaten fruit in quite a while.

The problem wasn't a lack of fruit.

The problem was the Vault.

Anything placed inside stayed preserved indefinitely, which made it easy to forget it existed.

She washed a plate of strawberries and sliced open half a watermelon.

Then she settled comfortably onto the sofa across from the inglenook fireplace.

The strawberries were sweet and bursting with juice.

The watermelon was even better.

Both had been harvested at peak season and tasted incredible.

An action movie was reaching one of its best fight scenes when a knock sounded at the door.

Lila quickly grabbed a wet wipe from the coffee table and wiped her mouth.

After putting her cold weather gear back on, she headed into the main hall and stepped outside.

"What can I do for you?"

She didn't open the door.

Instead, she peered through a narrow gap.

"I'm a soldier from the refuge below. There's a mandatory meeting tomorrow morning at eight in the dining hall."

The soldier spoke loudly enough for her to hear clearly.

"There will be announcements regarding rules, benefits, and future arrangements. Registered locals receive the same benefits as everyone else, so attendance is required."

"Understood. Thank you."

Lila replied politely.

The soldier nodded.

Wrapped in layers of winter gear, he turned and began making his way back down the mountain.

Once he disappeared from sight, Lila returned indoors.

She had every intention of attending.

After all, everyone living in the refuge would effectively become her neighbours.

Knowing more about them couldn't hurt.

Besides, with thousands of volts of Vault Lightning at her command, she wasn't particularly worried about anyone.

She grabbed the half-finished watermelon and resumed watching her movie.

That evening, Lila enjoyed a long, luxurious hot shower.

Afterward, she laid out the clothes she'd wear the next morning.

She also retrieved a black backpack from the Vault.

If supplies were distributed during the meeting, she'd need somewhere to carry them.

Using the Vault openly was out of the question.

The next morning arrived quickly.

At exactly 7:40, Lila locked up the Lower Vale Mansion and headed downhill.

The walk to the refuge usually took around fifteen minutes.

Arriving a few minutes early seemed perfect.

She was dressed for serious cold.

A black and white expedition survival suit covered her from head to toe.

She wore sheepskin gloves, reindeer hide boots, a mink hat, and the insulated helmet that had come with the survival suit.

After securing the iron gate with a heavy black padlock, she started down the mountain.

The snow was incredibly deep.

Each step sank.

Then slipped.

Then sank again.

Walking was exhausting.

As she struggled down the slope, she remembered Captain Walker's visit during the population survey.

At the time she'd wondered why he'd looked so miserable climbing the mountain.

Now she understood completely.

The route was awful.

Meanwhile, survivors throughout the refuge were already bundled up and heading toward the dining hall.

Lila descended carefully, occasionally glancing toward the settlement.

The side nearest the mountain contained several large buildings.

Farther out stood rows of neat little houses.

The small houses were obviously for survivors.

The larger buildings were probably public facilities.

After a difficult descent that included two falls, she finally reached the bottom.

Fortunately, all those layers meant she barely felt a thing.

Dusting snow from her coat, she approached the entrance.

Only one soldier stood guard at the checkpoint.

The rest of the area was quiet.

As Lila approached, the soldier studied her carefully.

She looked nothing like the survivors entering the refuge.

Her winter gear appeared brand new.

Professional.

Expensive.

Exceptionally warm.

The tall fur lined boots caught his eye as well.

He felt like he'd seen similar boots on another survivor recently, though he couldn't remember where.

Whatever the case, this woman clearly wasn't someone seeking shelter.

She looked far too comfortable.

Unable to identify her, the soldier immediately raised his rifle.

"What are you doing here?"

The dark barrel pointed directly at her chest.

Lila froze.

One hand instinctively turned palm up.

If things went sideways, a bolt of Vault Lightning was ready to fly.

"She's one of the mountain residents. Don't scare her."

A familiar voice called out.

Captain Walker strode over from inside the refuge.

The soldier immediately lowered his weapon.

"Sorry about that."

"It's fine."

Lila answered calmly.

"You're just doing your job."

Then she looked toward Walker.

"Can I go in?"

"Of course."

His heavily insulated arm gestured toward the entrance.

"The dining hall is that way."

"Thank you."

Lila nodded and headed inside.

Around them, numerous survivors slowed to stare.

Everyone knew Captain Walker.

He was one of the highest ranking officers in the refuge.

But who was the woman standing beside him?

She wasn't tall.

Yet from head to toe she looked immaculate.

Her extreme cold weather suit appeared expensive and perfectly maintained.

The fabric was smooth and matte.

Every cuff and seal fit perfectly.

There wasn't a wrinkle anywhere.

A thermal helmet covered her head.

Protective goggles rested on her forehead.

Even the lenses were spotless.

Not a single scratch.

Her reindeer hide boots still looked new.

The fur trim remained fluffy and clean.

Only a dusting of snow clung to them.

The survivors stood ten paces away.

It felt as though an invisible wall separated them from her.

Their own clothes had long since lost their original colours.

Mud.

Blood.

Frozen grime.

Layers of patched fabric.

Some people had simply wrapped quilts around themselves.

Even families living in the officials' residential quarter weren't dressed half as well as this.

Looking at her, many shared the same thought.

She didn't seem like someone living through the Collapse.

She looked like someone untouched by it.

Lila ignored every stare directed her way and continued toward the dining hall.

She wasn't about to ruin perfectly good clothes or deliberately freeze herself just to blend in.

"Who is that?"

Annabel happened to be standing among the crowd when she spotted the figure ahead.

"How can anyone be dressed that well?"

Grabbing Maggie's arm, she hurried forward.

Certain that the woman had to be related to some important official, she wanted a closer look.

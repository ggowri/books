Chapter 122

Callum chewed aggressively on his coarse multigrain roll while shamelessly inhaling the delicious aroma still lingering around the fire.

Man...

He was so jealous.

How the hell was that girl such an incredible cook?

The smell alone was enough to make him dizzy.

Leo noticed the longing written all over Callum's face.

Without saying much, he picked two slices of pickled radish out of his own lunch and dropped them into Callum's bowl.

Callum blinked.

Leo shrugged.

"Eat them with the bread. Grabbed them from the canteen this morning."

Leo always started work early, so he usually ate breakfast at the canteen before Callum even rolled out of bed.

Naturally, the two had brought completely different lunches.

"Heh. Thanks."

Callum grinned and popped the radish into his mouth without the slightest bit of embarrassment.

The radish wasn't rich by any means, but at least it had some salt.

Together with the dry bread, it went down a whole lot easier.

Seeing Callum happily chewing away, Leo smiled faintly before returning to his own meal.

The rest of the room wasn't doing much better.

Everyone swallowed their plain lunches while secretly savoring the smell drifting through the air instead.

Those who still had a little cornmeal at home silently made the same decision.

Tonight, they were going to splurge.

One big flatbread.

Maybe throw in some shredded potatoes if they could spare them.

Just once.

Just to remember what good food tasted like.

Ever since joining the Homeland Response Corps, the young soldier everyone called Fish had practically shadowed Ethan everywhere.

Watching his captain enjoy that incredible wrap made him grin like an idiot.

It almost felt as if he was tasting it secondhand.

The fire eventually burned down.

As the room began cooling again, everyone headed out for the afternoon scavenging run.

Lila picked another apartment block that no one had searched yet and disappeared inside.

Unfortunately, the afternoon was nowhere near as productive.

Most of the furniture had already been burned for firewood.

Every scrap of food had long since disappeared.

Many abandoned flats had their doors forced open, with even the furniture reduced to ashes.

She only found three apartments worth searching.

The locks had apparently been difficult enough that looters had skipped them.

Every piece of wooden furniture disappeared into the Vault.

Some would eventually become replacement furniture.

The rest would become firewood.

Winter coats and blankets that were too worn for her own use went into the Utility Sack for Survival Credits.

Knives.

Shovels.

Iron pots.

Every metal tool she found.

Electrical appliances.

She took all of it.

Anything in good condition she kept.

The worn pieces would be traded to the refuge.

You could never have too many tools.

And electrical appliances eventually failed.

Spare parts were always worth stockpiling.

Food was disappointing.

Only four pounds of white flour, six pounds of mung beans, and half a packet of salt.

Two hours later, her luck still hadn't improved.

There were no expensive antique furnishings this time.

Still, she wasn't worried.

This certainly wouldn't be her last trip into Ashton.

There would be plenty of opportunities to quietly collect more high quality furniture and equipment.

When Lila returned to the rendezvous point, those who had finished early were already climbing aboard the Luton van.

After the soldiers finished evaluating her haul, she'd earned another nineteen Survival Credits that afternoon.

She climbed into the cab.

The heater was already running.

Pulling off the suffocating thick face covering, she finally breathed comfortably again.

A few minutes later, Sophie, Callum, and Leo climbed aboard as well.

Sophie had earned thirty two credits.

She had quietly kept back two packets of salt, half a bottle of cooking oil, and a pound of rice.

Callum finished with twenty nine credits.

He'd hidden four pounds of flour, four pounds of rice, and two bottles of liquor.

Leo earned thirty six credits after secretly keeping back four pounds of rice and half a bottle of oil.

Lila had earned the most by far.

Sixty one Survival Credits altogether.

Mostly because she had no interest in opened food packages.

The Vault also made hauling supplies effortless, giving her a considerable advantage.

The ride home passed with Lila and Sophie chatting now and then.

They had become... not exactly friends, but comfortably familiar.

Both remained cautious.

Neither ventured into truly personal territory.

Callum, meanwhile, had never met a conversation he couldn't join.

Every few minutes he'd jump in with another comment.

Outside of Sophie and Ethan, Callum was probably the person Lila knew best inside the refuge.

"So..."

Callum grinned.

"Those reindeer hide boots I sold you turned out pretty damn good, huh?"

He proudly looked around at everyone wearing the same rugged boots.

The shipment had originally been one of the biggest business mistakes he'd ever made.

Then the Long Freeze hit.

Now they were worth their weight in gold.

"They're excellent."

Lila glanced down at her boots.

"They're warm, sturdy, and honestly better than anything I could've found online."

Callum scratched his head.

"But seriously... how'd you nail the timing?"

"You bought them just a few days before the temperatures crashed."

"I've been wondering about that ever since."

"Especially after seeing all that top shelf cold weather gear you had."

"It feels like the boots weren't exactly a coincidence."

The moment those words left his mouth, everyone in the cab quietly listened.

Wait.

She'd started buying winter survival gear before the disaster?

Could people really...

Come back from the future?

The young soldier driving the van had devoured apocalypse novels before the Collapse.

His imagination immediately went into overdrive.

Lila laughed.

"Honestly? Pure luck."

"I still can't believe it myself."

"I've always dreamed of visiting Antarctica and seeing penguins in person."

"My plan was to go after graduating university."

"This year I finally graduated, so I was planning to leave this winter."

"I figured buying cold weather gear during the off season would save a lot of money."

"So I started collecting equipment early."

"Then, before I could even book the trip..."

She looked out the frosted windshield and smiled helplessly.

"Antarctica decided to come to us instead."

The explanation sounded casual.

Completely reasonable.

Sophie sighed.

"That's actually incredible luck."

"If I'd dreamed about Antarctica too, maybe I'd have ended up with a mountain of winter gear."

"Oh."

Callum nodded immediately.

"Well, that explains everything."

Not a trace of suspicion crossed his face.

He simply thought Lila had won the lottery.

She certainly had been lucky.

Then again, so had Callum.

When the Long Freeze began, he already owned mountains of cold weather gear and enough food to outlast most people.

Compared to the average survivor, he was miles ahead.

The conversation drifted elsewhere.

Lila quietly let out the breath she'd been holding.

She had known this question would eventually come up.

Someone was always going to remember the reindeer boots and the mink hat she'd bought before everything collapsed.

Thankfully, she'd prepared for this long ago.

She had rehearsed today's explanation several times in private.

Every detail had already been polished.

As they continued chatting, the Luton van bounced its way back toward Lower Vale.

Before they even reached the refuge, Lila pulled on her hat and face covering.

She knew stepping outside would be brutal.

The van rolled to a stop.

"The scavenging team's back!"

Someone shouted across the refuge.

People hurried over from every direction.

Everyone wanted to see what thirty scavengers had managed to bring home.

Callum and Leo climbed down first.

Sophie followed close behind.

Lila, sitting deepest inside the cab, was the last one out.

Ever since her first awkward climb into the truck, she'd developed a healthy fear of the tall step.

Getting down was even worse.

Carefully placing one boot on the step, she lowered herself slowly...

Then hopped safely onto the frozen ground.

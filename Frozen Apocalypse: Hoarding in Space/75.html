Chapter 74

Early the next morning, Lila woke with purpose. She had tasks to complete today and no time to linger in bed.

She cooked a pot of claypot rice for breakfast, the aroma filling the air as soon as it was ready. Two large bowls later, she felt energised and capable of building the table she had planned.

Following the tutorial, she spent over three hours crafting a perfect, sturdy table.

Inspecting her work, she realised something was missing. Comparing it to the illustration in the book, she had an epiphany: paint.

The tables in the guidebook had a glossy finish, making them visually and physically appealing. Her table, even sanded, felt rough.

What could she use as a substitute?

She opened a tin of Crack-Care Balm and coated the table generously. The colour darkened, the surface became smooth, and a subtle scent lingered.

"Perfect," she murmured, delighted. She hurriedly moved the table onto the hearth bench, placing it beside her giant dog bed.

On her little hearth table, she set a packet of tissues, a bottle of cola, and a bag of mixed nuts. Even her tablet could stand on it.

Lila leaned against the edge of her giant bed, snacking and watching shows. More convenient than a sofa, far comfier than lying flat on the bench...it was perfect.

Ever since last night, Annabel had been imagining herself as the estate manager. She envisioned skimming supplies for her own benefit, taking over Mrs Harris's basement for herself.

Morning mealtimes came, and she hadn't even noticed the announcement. Maggie tugged at her. "Come on, let's get breakfast."

Holding her bowl, Annabel followed her. When it was her turn, she asked Mrs Harris, "Any news from the inspector? Any notices yet?"

Mrs Harris paused mid-scoop, a rare moment of attentiveness. "Not yet. Shouldn't be long...probably at the next supply delivery." She ladled a slightly larger portion onto Annabel's bowl.

Annabel's excitement waned a little. She had hoped Cedric's notice would arrive today. Well, patience was required; he had said a few days.

Back in the basement, she opened a can of luncheon meat over her plain porridge. She didn't share it with Edward or Maggie. Yesterday's biscuit sharing had already fulfilled her sense of duty; this luncheon meat was strictly hers.

She ate the entire can in one go, the saltiness making her throat tighten slightly.

Three days later, supplies arrived again. This was the day Annabel had most anticipated.

She had washed her hair with warm water last night and touched up her eyebrows and lips that morning, looking alert and vibrant.

She paced outside the base gate, heart racing. Soon, the familiar knocking signalled the arrival of the goods. Two knocks on the door marked the delivery's arrival.

Two estate security officers, bundled up, opened the basement doors and hauled in the supplies: two sacks of firewood and two large bags of provisions.

Inside one bag was a notice:

Notice
Director of Material Allocation Cedric inspected this estate. Verification shows that actual occupancy is low.
According to the per-capita rationing principle, from now on, monthly supplies will be reduced by one third.
End of notice.

Mrs Harris's eyes went red with anger.

"Bloody hell! We're already starving! How can they cut it further?"

Annabel, who had been imagining today as the day of her appointment, felt her energy drain. Her mind went blank.

Why…? She had expected Cedric's notice today.

Floating through the corridor, she returned to her basement, trying to reason that perhaps the notice was still being processed. After all, he had said a few days.

Back inside, Edward and Maggie watched her expectantly. Annabel cleared her throat and lifted her chin. "The appointment notice is still under review. It'll come, sooner or later."

"Yes, no rush. Sooner or later, it'll arrive. At least we won't starve in the meantime," Maggie said, smiling to ease her disappointment.

Edward nodded. "Mm, let's wait a bit longer."

"Meeting! Everyone, come to the central area!" Mrs Harris's loudspeaker called.

Reluctantly, they left the basement and gathered centrally.

"After inspection, it's clear our basement has fewer residents. Supplies will be cut by a third from now on…" Mrs Harris struggled to continue.

"What? A cut?"
"We're already starving, and now this?"
"That inspector's probably stuffed himself while cutting our rations."
"How are we meant to survive?"

Anger, sadness, disappointment, and fear intertwined among the residents.

"Don't lose heart! Maybe allocations will adjust later. And these supplies are enough to keep us alive. Save what you can...we'll get through this together!" Mrs Harris pressed on, forcing positivity.

Shedding some gloom, the crowd rallied. "She's right! We must stick together!"
"It'll be fine. The earth can't stay frozen forever."
"Right! If we endure, we'll triumph one day." Callum spoke, refusing to believe the Collapse could last forever.

Though a few frowned, none dared contradict. Energised, the crowd looked to Mrs Harris, who, determined, vowed to lead them through the harshest of times.

Chapter 47

Lila carried the two freshly cleaned little pups onto the heated hearth bench, kicked off her padded slippers, and climbed up after them.

The bench was smooth and warm, a sensation the two puppies had never experienced before.

They tried their hardest to open their eyes wide, crawling about in wonder.

After finding the warmest spot, Snowy gave two soft whimpers and lay down. Patches immediately copied him.

Lila moved closer and lay on the heated bench too, gently stroking their little heads again and again.

They were both round, plump little things, and far too satisfying to touch.

Snowy's eyes suddenly opened a little wider.

He studied Lila carefully, his gaze fixed on her without moving away.

This was the first time he had seen his owner so clearly.

No, for Snowy, perhaps this was not his owner.

Perhaps this was his mother.

People used to say that the first thing an animal saw when it opened its eyes, it would recognise as its mother.

Even if that first sight was a person.

Lila did not know that. She was only delighted that Snowy's eyes had opened wider. In a little while, he would be able to run around properly on the floor.

As she stroked them, both little pups fell asleep.

Lila took out all her electronic devices from the Vault and started downloading again.

This time, apart from the storage space needed to keep the devices running, she intended to fill every bit of memory.

Alongside novels, films and series, she downloaded offline games that did not need an internet connection.

If she ever had a craving to play something, she would at least have options.

Before the downloads were finished, Lila began to grow drowsy.

Worried that the two pups might wake up and accidentally fall to the floor, she forced herself to stay awake long enough to put them back in their crate.

Since their eyes were already half open and they could move about now, Lila did not close the crate door.

If they wanted to wander out, she did not want to restrict their freedom.

After settling the puppies, she did not return to the heated bench. Instead, she dropped straight onto the sofa, found a comfortable position, pulled a blanket over herself and fell into a deep sleep.

She no longer had to attend classes. She no longer had to work.

She could let herself fall asleep whenever she wanted.

She did not need to worry that sleeping during the day would mean staying up at night, then being unable to get up early the next morning.

With that thought in mind, Lila drifted into sleep with a smile still on her lips.

As the sky gradually darkened, those with plans began to stir.

Callum had finished sorting all his supplies.

He left only a small amount of food in his flat. The rest went into several large boxes.

As for cold-weather clothing and quilts, he kept only one set upstairs and packed everything else into large sacks.

Leo was even more thorough.

He packed up every bit of food in his home, along with all his cold-weather clothing. He was only waiting for nightfall so he could move everything down to the basement storage room.

At midnight, because of the cold, almost everyone had gone to sleep.

Leo loaded his food into a huge suitcase, stuffed his rucksack until it bulged, and headed for the Basement.

There were two flats per lift landing in this estate, and the flat next door to Leo's was empty, so he did not have to worry about running into a neighbour when he went out.

The lift went all the way down.

As long as no one got in halfway, he would not meet anyone.

Leo watched the floor numbers change with intense nerves.

Only when the display finally reached minus one, without a single person getting in, did he let out a breath of relief.

He dragged the suitcase straight towards his own basement storage room.

Head down, he hurried along. When he saw the door to his storage room, he finally relaxed.

He was turning the key in the lock when he suddenly heard movement behind him.

Leo tensed violently and spun round.

There stood a huge black bear covered from head to toe in fur.

The "black bear" had just stepped out of the storage room opposite. At the sight of Leo, it jolted too.

Neither of them knew who stamped a foot, but the sound triggered the light above them.

As soon as the light came on, they finally saw each other's faces clearly.

"You!"

"Mr. Turner?"

Callum had not expected to run into Leo here while sneaking around with his supplies.

"You scared the life out of me."

The moment Leo saw it was Callum, he relaxed.

After buying from him twice, Leo felt he had a decent sense of what Callum was like.

Greedy for money, yes, but not a bad person.

"You're planning to move down to the basement too?" Callum asked, surprised.

He had not expected someone else to have the same idea.

Leo put a finger to his lips, then pulled Callum into his own storage room.

The damp, mouldy smell of the basement wrapped around them both.

Leo switched on a lamp. As he stacked his supplies in the corner, he spoke to Callum.

"You feel something's wrong with this temperature drop too, don't you?"

Callum nodded hard.

"Of course I do. I was planning to move my things quietly in the middle of the night. Didn't expect to run into you. Still, maybe it's a good thing. We're opposite each other down here. If anything happens later, we can look out for each other."

"Isn't this underneath our block? Shouldn't your storage room be under your own building?" Leo found it rather strange.

"When I bought my flat, all the storage rooms under my building had already sold out. This was the only one left under yours, so I bought it."

Callum answered without hesitation.

The explanation made sense, and Leo believed him.

"When are you planning to move down?" Leo asked.

"I'm thinking I'll come down once the upstairs heating stops completely. You?"

Callum reckoned the heating would not last many more days.

"Probably around the same time. If anything happens later, we can call out to each other. I've got walkie-talkies. I'll give you one in a bit."

If the Collapse really had arrived, being a lone wolf would not be easy. Having someone nearby to look after each other would make things much safer.

"Sounds good. Come find me when you're done."

With that, Callum hurried off to carry on moving his things.

Leo also made trip after trip, bringing down all his cold-weather clothing, quilts and food.

His basement storage room had been renovated back when he bought it. It had running water, electric lights and sockets. The only thing it lacked was a toilet.

Since this was for surviving the Collapse, making do with plastic bags for a while was not impossible.

On the final trip, Leo moved the mattress from the spare bedroom.

The basement was damp. Sleeping directly on the floor would be bad for his health.

The spare-room mattress was one and a half metres wide. It was easier to move than the one in the main bedroom and did not take up as much space.

He used boxes of instant noodles and the original shelving in the storage room to divide the space down the middle.

Apart from quilts and bedding, the rest of the cold-weather clothing and supplies were placed on the shelves.

He separated the outer and inner areas. The mattress, quilts and pillows went right at the back. With the space smaller, it would hold warmth better.

Also, if anyone knocked on the door, they would not see him at first glance. They would only think it was a storage room piled full of supplies, giving Leo enough room to react if he needed to.

He spread an electric blanket over the mattress, then laid a fluffy throw on top.

An extension lead connected to the basement socket, so he could plug in the electric blanket and charge his phone at the same time.

This temporary bedroom was warm and comfortable.

The outer part of the storage room had a tap, which he could use for drinking water and washing.

By the time Leo finished arranging his basement storage room, it was already three in the morning.

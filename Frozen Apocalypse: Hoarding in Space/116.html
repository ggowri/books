Chapter 115

Maggie had long since grown used to Anna's sharp tongue.

She did not take the comment as disgust.

Instead, she convinced herself Anna simply couldn't bear to look because she cared too much.

Smiling quietly to herself, Maggie shifted her injured leg to the other side of the heated bed where Anna couldn't see it.

She carefully cleaned the frostbite blisters and left the wounds exposed to dry.

Just then, the sound of a lorry horn echoed outside.

Captain Walker had returned.

The next day, he would be leading a supply recovery mission into the city.

His return tonight meant only one thing.

He was selecting the people who would accompany him.

Anna had been waiting for this opportunity for days.

The instant she heard the horn, she threw on her padded coat and rushed outside.

She hadn't dressed warmly enough.

The freezing air hit her like a brick the moment she reached the door.

Shivering violently, she hurried back inside, piled on every layer she owned, and dashed out again.

By the time she'd finished all that, Captain Walker had already entered the Military Quarter.

Soldiers stood guard at the entrance.

Anna tugged at the wrinkles in her worn coat, lifted her chin proudly, and walked straight toward them.

She barely reached the checkpoint before one of the guards stepped in front of her.

"What do you want?"

The soldier's voice was blunt.

Anna instinctively slipped into her usual act around men.

Looking up at him with wide, innocent eyes, she spoke in a pitiful voice.

"Could you let me through? I just need to speak with Captain Walker for a minute."

The guards assigned here worked beside Ethan every single day.

Cheap tricks like this didn't impress them in the slightest.

"What position do you hold in the refuge?"

the soldier asked.

Anna hesitated.

"I..."

"I'm a Legal Officer."

The guard immediately snorted.

"Move along."

"You don't get to see Captain Walker whenever you feel like it."

"There are only two Legal Officers in the refuge."

"You think I wouldn't know who they are?"

He could tell instantly she was lying.

Standing firm, he blocked the entrance.

Instead of backing down, Anna puffed herself up with even more confidence.

"I'm going to be a Legal Officer."

"And maybe Captain Walker's girlfriend someday."

"You really think making an enemy of me is smart, idiot?"

The second guard had been silent until now.

Finally he couldn't hold it anymore.

"Lady..."

"Are you living in a fantasy?"

He was about to continue when footsteps echoed down the corridor.

Ethan was walking out.

The soldier immediately shut his mouth.

"You're just an ordinary soldier."

"How dare you yell at me?"

"Believe me, Captain Walker will deal with you later."

Anna's voice grew even louder.

Its shrill pitch cut through the freezing air.

"Captain..."

She suddenly froze.

Ethan had already reached the entrance.

His expression was as dark as a thundercloud.

Her voice instantly shrank to a timid whisper.

"We're not related."

"Don't call me that."

"And the refuge has rules."

"Civilians are not permitted inside the Military Quarter."

"Especially those with no official position and no authorised business."

Ethan frowned deeply.

He had never met anyone so relentlessly persistent.

Women like this irritated him more than anything.

Without waiting for a response, he strode past her and headed toward the administration offices without another glance.

Anna refused to give up.

From her point of view, Ethan had just spent quite a while talking to her.

Obviously that meant he liked her.

Stamping one foot dramatically, she hurried after him.

"Captain Walker."

"Wait."

"I need to ask you something."

"Can I go into the city with you tomorrow..."

She never finished.

One of the soldiers behind Ethan stepped directly into her path.

She was moving too fast.

His solid forearm caught her across the neck, sending her tumbling backward.

She landed flat on the snow.

The back of her head hit the frozen ground.

It wasn't a serious impact, but it left her dazed.

"Ow..."

"That really hurts..."

Even lying in the snow, Anna remembered to stay in character.

She clumsily climbed back to her feet, ready to put on another performance.

But Ethan and his escort had already disappeared.

She shivered violently.

The cold outside was brutal.

After only a few minutes of acting, both her hands and feet had gone numb.

Pouting unhappily, Anna jogged back home.

Meanwhile, after finishing lunch, Lila began preparing for tomorrow's expedition into the city.

It had been a long time since she'd travelled any real distance.

In fact, this would be her first journey away from home since the Long Freeze began.

She was genuinely excited.

Too excited to sit still.

She wanted everything packed as soon as possible.

The first item she prepared was the set of daylight grow lamps she'd promised Ethan.

She packed them carefully into a large carry bag of their own.

Once that was finished, she turned to her personal gear.

She chose the same sturdy black backpack she'd used before.

Inside went a vacuum flask, a utility knife, matches, and candles.

Next came a thick coil of rope.

Then a fire axe.

Food required more thought.

She couldn't bring instant meals.

Those would only make people suspicious.

Whatever she carried had to look like something she'd cooked herself.

After packing the essentials, she still had plenty of time.

So she decided to prepare tomorrow's meals in advance.

Nothing unusual.

Anything eaten in front of others had to be ordinary, shelf stable food.

She reached into the Vault and took out a bag of cornmeal that had remained untouched since the Collapse began.

Then she gathered two potatoes, half an onion, and a tin of fermented soybean paste.

Potatoes and onions stored well.

No one would question seeing them.

She poured the cornmeal into a mixing bowl, added warm water, and began kneading.

The dough turned out much drier than expected.

She usually baked with plain flour.

She wasn't used to working with cornmeal.

After a few failed attempts, she mixed in half a bowl of white flour and added water twice more before finally getting the dough to come together.

She covered the bowl and left it to rest.

Next she peeled the potatoes and shredded them into thin strips.

The onion received the same treatment.

After thinking for a moment, she grabbed a handful of dried coriander from the kitchen shelf and crushed it into fine flakes between her fingers.

The potatoes had to be cooked first.

She seasoned them with only a pinch of salt and a splash of vinegar before stir frying them quickly in a lightly oiled pan.

Once they were done, she sampled a bite.

Crisp.

Fragrant.

Perfect.

She transferred the potatoes onto a plate.

Dusting the work surface with flour, she rolled the dough into a thin round sheet.

Whenever a crack appeared, she simply pinched it back together.

A little oil went into a flat frying pan to preheat over the kitchen range.

As soon as the oil shimmered, she carefully laid the cornmeal flatbread into the pan.

In a small bowl, she mixed the fermented soybean paste with oyster sauce, soy sauce, and garlic chilli sauce, creating her own rich savoury spread.

As the flatbread heated, its pale yellow colour gradually deepened into a rich golden brown.

Once it had firmed up, she flipped it over.

She brushed the sauce generously across the surface.

Then she added two generous chopstickfuls of fried shredded potatoes, a handful of sliced onions, and a light sprinkle of dried coriander.

Carefully, she rolled everything into a tight wrap.

One hearty cornmeal wrap was finished.

The bottom split slightly.

It wasn't pretty, but it would eat just fine.

Fortunately, she'd added that little bit of white flour.

Without it, the cornmeal flatbread would never have held together well enough to roll.

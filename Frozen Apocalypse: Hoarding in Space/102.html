Chapter 101

At first, Sophie couldn't feel her feet at all.

Then, little by little, warmth began creeping back into them.

She let out a relieved breath. At least the nerves hadn't been completely destroyed by the cold.

Once her feet had finally thawed, she examined the frostbite lesions covering them and decided a soak in warm water was probably a good idea.

She carefully washed away the grime, then applied a layer of frostbite ointment to the damaged skin.

A pair of clean cotton slippers she'd dug out of the sled sat on the floor.

She was about to slip them on when she hesitated. The ointment would stain them.

After a moment, she grabbed two plastic bags, wrapped them around her feet, and only then slid them into the slippers.

The water in the pot came to a boil.

Her stomach cramped with hunger.

Forget it.

She might as well eat something.

A full stomach would make sleeping a lot easier.

Digging through her pack, she found her enamel mug. She tore open a packet of instant noodles, broke the noodle block in half, added half the seasoning, but left out the oil sachet. Then she poured in boiling water and let it steep.

The oil packet smelled too strong.

She didn't want the neighbours noticing.

Staring at the mug, Sophie drifted into a daze.

Those noodles.

She'd been craving them for the entire journey.

Tonight, she was finally getting them.

A few minutes later, the steaming noodles were ready.

She devoured them.

Within moments, the mug was empty.

It had been a very long time since she'd eaten a meal that was actually hot.

Even the broth was gone.

Half a packet of instant noodles wasn't much, but it felt like coming back from the dead.

Strength slowly returned to her body.

Her mind felt clearer too.

Since she finally had some energy, she decided to clean herself up before bed.

The remaining hot water in the pot was divided evenly between two basins.

The stainless-steel basins had travelled with her the entire way. They were the only ones she owned.

Bending over, she carefully washed her face and neck.

One basin immediately turned black with dirt.

Using the second basin, she crouched down and quickly cleaned herself as best she could.

Back in the civil-defence shelter, this had been part of her daily routine.

During the migration, there hadn't been any opportunity to do it.

Now that she finally could, she felt human again.

After securing the doors and windows, Sophie stripped off her tattered clothes and changed into clean ones.

Then she climbed onto the heated bed.

She was the last member of Mrs. Harris's group to get in.

The bedding had already warmed up nicely.

A contented sigh escaped her lips.

For the first time in a very long while, she felt safe enough to truly relax.

Within moments, she was asleep.

It was no wonder Sophie preferred living alone.

Among all the survivors in the refuge, she was probably the one who managed to maintain the highest standard of living.

Annabel's family, naturally, were staying together as well.

Annabel remained wrapped in blankets right up until they reached the doorway.

Only then did she emerge, putting on an exaggerated display of pain.

Her face twisted dramatically as she limped forward, allowing others to support her to the edge of the heated bed.

A soldier brought in fresh bedding.

Maggie immediately spread it out for her daughter near the warmest spot.

"Get under the covers, Anna. I'll light the stove. It'll warm up soon."

Before moving in with Lila, Maggie's family had lived in the countryside.

Handling a heated bed stove was second nature to her.

Annabel didn't answer.

She simply slid into the blankets as if it were the most natural thing in the world.

See?

She'd known the journey would work out.

Now she'd arrived safely at the refuge without putting in any real effort.

Aside from being cold, she hadn't suffered much at all.

Edward watched her and snorted.

Spreading out his own bedding, he kicked off his boots and climbed onto the heated bed.

Although he hadn't carried anything during the migration, merely keeping up with the group had nearly broken him.

The cold had drained him.

Even under the blankets, he couldn't stop shivering.

"Maggie," he shouted toward the kitchen, "heat up some water. I need something warm to drink."

"Coming."

Maggie dragged her exhausted body outside, filled a large basin with snow, and dumped it into the pot.

After lighting the stove beneath the heated bed, she untied a small bundle from the sled.

Inside were two vacuum flasks.

She filled both with boiling water.

One went to Annabel.

The other to Edward.

Wrapped in blankets, father and daughter happily sipped hot water.

Meanwhile, Maggie stood there awkwardly.

She wanted some too.

Unfortunately, they'd only brought two flasks.

They hadn't even packed a spare bowl.

Eventually, she returned to the firebox and sat cross-legged beside it.

During the migration, whenever they stayed in abandoned houses, she never got the chance to enjoy a proper heated bed stove.

Now she could finally sit by the warmth as long as she wanted.

She removed her shoes.

The entire sole of each foot had turned dark purple and black.

When she pressed a finger into the flesh, a deep indentation remained.

No rebound.

No pain.

Maggie sighed.

Stretching her feet toward the fire, she warmed them for a long time before her toes finally twitched slightly.

By the time she returned to the room, Edward and Annabel were already asleep.

She spread out her own bedding and crawled beneath the covers.

The blankets had finally become warm.

Within moments, exhaustion pulled her under.

She never noticed the blood and pus slowly seeping from her trouser legs onto the fresh cotton quilt.

Daniel was staying next door to Annabel's family.

Like many of the men in his migration group, he shared accommodation with several others.

People needed someone watching their back these days.

Cold.

Exhaustion.

Fear.

The journey had wrung every last ounce of strength from them.

This was the first truly restful night they'd had since the Winter Exodus began.

Maybe the first restful night since the Long Freeze started.

No one knew what the future held.

But reaching the refuge meant life would probably get easier from here.

Not long after falling asleep, the heated bed grew warmer and warmer.

The blankets trapped the heat.

Everyone slept more deeply than they had in months.

The next morning, survivors gradually began waking up.

The moment they got out of bed, pain exploded through their bodies.

After weeks of relentless travel, they'd finally had a chance to rest.

Now their bodies were reminding them exactly how much punishment they'd endured.

Still, no matter how badly they hurt, there was work to do.

They had to organize their new homes.

Some people had managed to bring food with them.

Leaving supplies outside wasn't exactly smart.

Mrs. Harris's group had arrived in much better shape than most.

Before departure, she'd insisted everyone bring a sled.

While many survivors had arrived with almost nothing, all thirteen members of her group still possessed a decent amount of personal belongings.

Leo and Callum waited until nobody was visible outside before unloading everything from their sleds.

Supplies were spread across the room when someone suddenly knocked on the door.

The two men exchanged alarmed looks.

Callum cleared his throat.

"Who is it?"

"Firewood delivery."

The voice belonged to a young soldier.

Not the same one from yesterday.

Callum gestured for Leo to keep watch before cautiously opening the door.

The soldier tossed a large bundle of firewood inside and immediately turned to leave.

He still had dozens of households to visit.

"Hey, mate," Callum called after him. "Can we take as much firewood as we want?"

The soldier stopped.

His name tag read Xiaoyu, though everyone simply called him Fish.

He shook his head.

"No. New arrivals get free firewood for the first three days only. One bundle per day. After that you'll need work credits."

"Oh. Right. Thanks."

Callum quickly shut the door.

Rubbing his arms, he walked back toward Leo.

"I honestly thought firewood was free here. Turns out we've only got three free days before we've got to earn it."

Leo snorted.

"What did you expect? Some kind of charity camp?"

He continued sorting supplies into neat piles.

"Everybody pulls their weight here."

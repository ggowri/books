Chapter 32

The sight of their own car felt like seeing a lifeline. They hurried over and climbed in as fast as they could.

They had thought the car would be warmer, but inside it was just as cold as outside.

After only that brief spell outdoors, Annabel felt as though her knees had frozen stiff. A numb, needling ache seemed to drill right into the bone.

"Dad, quick. Put the heating on."

Shivering, Annabel found a cushion in the car and hurriedly pressed it over her bare legs.

Edward switched on the heater without a word. Warm air slowly spread through the car, giving the half-frozen group a little relief.

Daniel rushed into his own car as well. He was in no mood to say goodbye to Annabel's family. He started the engine at once and drove off.

All he wanted was to get home and crawl under the duvet.

Once he left the underground car park, he realised the snow outside was already deep enough to cover most of a tyre.

Daniel hesitated for a second, then pressed the accelerator, hoping to make it home in one go.

But his low-slung car jolted violently and came to a stop.

He floored the accelerator, yet the tyres only spun in place and the car refused to move an inch.

Even though it was bitterly cold outside, Daniel gritted his teeth and tried to open the door.

That was not easy either. Snow was piled up against it. He shoved and kicked at it for ages before finally forcing the door open.

Only then did he realise that the snow had hidden the road conditions. One of the wheels had dropped into a deep pothole.

Edward drove up behind him and saw Daniel crouching by the tyre, trying to work out what had happened.

"It's freezing. Get someone to sort it tomorrow. Come to ours for tonight."

Edward lowered the window halfway and spoke quickly.

Daniel could not stand the icy air any longer, so he nodded and hurried into Edward's car.

Annabel shifted across to make room, and Daniel sat down beside her.

This was the first time Daniel had ever stayed overnight at her home. Annabel's heart fluttered with secret delight.

The heater was turned up to its highest setting, and before long, the car began to warm. The four of them gradually recovered.

The outside temperature was now minus two.

In Ashton, winter temperatures could drop as low as minus ten, so in theory, minus two should have been bearable.

The problem was that this sudden temperature plunge had happened in summer. No one had been prepared.

People like Annabel and Maggie were dressed far too lightly and had no warm clothes with them. That was why the icy air had hit them so hard.

"It's terrifying. Snow in the middle of summer is bad enough, but how can it be this cold?"

Even after warming up in the car, Annabel was still shaken.

After all, no one could stand outside in minus two wearing nothing but a thin strappy dress.

"It's all right. We'll be home soon. Once we're back, you can have a hot shower, get under a thick duvet and sleep. Then you won't be cold anymore."

Maggie touched her daughter's chilly hand and could not help trying to comfort her.

Edward, who had been silent all this time, suddenly spoke.

"Why has Lila gone completely quiet?"

"Just wait until I see her at home. I'll make sure she regrets this," Annabel said through gritted teeth.

By this time, Lila had finally managed, after an exhausting drive, to get the car to the gate of the Lower Vale Mansion.

She pulled on her padded hat and gloves, got out, opened the gate, then carried the chicks and puppies inside and placed them on the heated hearth bench in the east parlour.

Then she went back out into the courtyard, looked around carefully and made sure no one was there. With one stretch of her hand, she put the car into the Vault.

After that, she hurried back inside the courtyard and locked the heavy reinforced steel security gate behind her.

The canopy over the courtyard was closed, so there was not a trace of snow inside.

Before the German retrofit crew had left, they had cleaned both the inside and outside of the courtyard. Now the little yard was spotless, completely at odds with the raging snow beyond the walls.

The courtyard and the house were still empty. All her supplies were in the Vault, and Lila had not yet had time to sort them out.

But for the next while, she did not plan to go out again. She would have plenty of time to organise everything.

Since there was no heating yet, the house was cold.

The two puppies were so chilled that they kept whining.

Lila did not dare delay for even a moment. She did not even stop to change her clothes before going to the kitchen range.

From the Vault, she took out a large handful of kindling, a bundle of bought firewood and a lighter, then began to start the fire.

Lila had never done this herself before, but she had watched Agnes and George do it when she was little. She had picked up enough to manage.

She laid the kindling at the bottom of the kitchen range, then packed firewood on top.

The lighter caught the kindling. A small red flame slowly spread, then flared up with a soft whoosh. The fire burned strongly, and as Lila crouched by the mouth of the firebox, she could feel the warmth inside.

Kindling caught easily but burned fast. In less than a minute, most of it had already gone, but that was enough. In that one minute, the firewood above had caught.

The flames grew bright and fierce, crackling constantly.

Lila clapped her hands in satisfaction.

There. She was perfectly capable.

She pressed the burning wood down a little, then packed more firewood on top so it would burn for longer.

She lifted the lid from the iron pot above. She did not plan to add water. Instead, the steady heat from the firebox would spread through the large iron pot and into the kitchen.

Before long, the kitchen began to warm up.

Lila picked up the lighter and went to the east parlour.

Using the same method, she lit the inglenook fireplace.

The fireplace was clearly far better at heating the room than the kitchen range.

After five or six minutes of flames burning in the grate, the temperature in the room began to rise.

Lila took some smokeless coal from the Vault and added it to the fireplace. Sparks flew up at once, and the flames burned even more vigorously.

The flues the retrofit crew had rebuilt worked perfectly. Whether it was the kitchen range or the fireplace, the smoke drew cleanly away, with no choking fumes at all.

Satisfied, Lila closed the transparent fireplace door. Through the glass, she could see the flames burning inside, warm and comforting.

She had been through far too much today. Lila had no intention of continuing to sort the supplies in the Vault. She only wanted to take out what she needed for tonight.

But before washing and settling herself, she had to see to the chicks and puppies first.

As the room gradually warmed, the chicks and puppies, who had been listless the entire journey, became a little livelier.

She could hear the chicks cheeping away, along with the puppies whining and nosing about.

Lila pulled the blankets off the two cages and checked them carefully.

The chicks were all healthy. One fluffy yellow ball after another hopped about inside the cardboard box.

They seemed to be hungry.

She took a handful of millet from the Vault and scattered it into the small bowl in the box. The chicks immediately crowded over, bobbing their little heads as they fought to peck at the grain.

The two puppies were whining more loudly now. They were clearly starving too.

She had spent the whole day finishing her stockpiling preparations, and their feeds had been rushed. The two little things had been hungry for ages.

Lila took out the feeding tools she had used earlier and fed them goat's milk one by one until both puppies were full and quiet.

Only then did she gently place them back into the cage.

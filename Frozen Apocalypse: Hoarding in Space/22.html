Chapter 21

On 11 June, Lila still had $483,000 left.

She had more or less finished stockpiling all the essentials. What came next were the things that would let her keep going long term.

First on the list: seeds.

The moment Lila opened her eyes that morning, she began buying them in bulk.

She ordered every common fruit and vegetable seed she could think of. Thirty packets each of cabbage, pak choi, crown daisy, coriander, spring onion, chives and garlic. Fifty packets each of carrot, white radish, tomato, cucumber, runner beans, pumpkin and winter melon. Another thirty packets each of garlic and ginger for planting.

Then came fruit: thirty packets each of watermelon, cherry tomato, strawberry and blueberry seeds.

She even added flowers: ten packets each of rose, jasmine, English rose and chrysanthemum seeds.

For larger crops, she bought a hundred kilos of sweetcorn seed, plus a hundred kilos each of peanut and sunflower seeds.

The grain stored inside the Vault was enough to last her, so she did not bother stockpiling cereal seeds.

She also bought fertiliser and pesticides while she was at it: twenty sacks of compound fertiliser and ten crates of pesticide.

Lila had no real idea how much fertiliser or pesticide a person needed. She simply thought that, since she would only be growing vegetables rather than fields of grain, this amount ought to last for years.

And if the Collapse dragged on for many years, then by the time those years had passed, she would have to learn how to make her own fertiliser.

Once the seeds and fertiliser were sorted, she still needed containers.

During the Long Freeze, going outdoors would be impossible. Any vegetables or fruit would have to be grown inside.

Lila bought twenty rectangular plastic growing trays, each measuring thirty by fifty centimetres.

She added five hoes and five small trowels.

If the Collapse ever ended and she had the chance to grow vegetables outdoors again, she would need proper farm tools too.

Ten iron pickaxes, ten spades, ten garden rakes, ten axes, ten billhooks, three extendable ladders, three saws, three hammers, ten sickles and five sharpening stones.

She also bought three small hand trolleys for moving heavy loads and one pedal tricycle cart. Each cost $600, for a total of $2,400.

The seeds and farm tools together came to $12,000.

Because she had bought so many tools, the seller threw in a thick stack of sandpaper for free.

After settling the bill, Lila stretched lazily.

Her phone pinged.

She dropped her arms, picked it up and opened the message without much interest.

The voice note was from Annabel.

"Lila, we've been here for twenty days now. Dad's getting anxious to go home. Should we stay a bit longer, or should we listen to him and come back?"

One listen was enough to tell what was going on. Edward had had enough of Seabourne and was desperate to return, while Annabel clearly had not finished enjoying herself and wanted Lila to say a few persuasive words on her behalf.

Lila frowned.

Although her stockpiling was almost complete, there were still odds and ends she had not finished buying.

More importantly, there were still twenty days before the Collapse. If they came back now, there was every chance they would discover her secret and start causing trouble.

With that thought in mind, Lila found Edward's number and called him directly.

The moment he picked up, she said in a bright, breezy voice, "Edward, how's the holiday going?"

"Not bad. Thank you for sending us here, Lila. But it's far too hot, and it's about time we came back."

From his tone, it was obvious he had made up his mind.

"Have you bought any stones yet? Did you have them cut open? How did it go?"

If she wanted Edward to stay put, she had to aim straight at his hobby.

On the other end of the line, Edward gave a heavy sigh.

"Oh, I bought some, all right. Spent $30,000 and didn't get a thing out of them. Lost the lot. Good thing you're footing the bill for me."

Lila laughed lightly.

"Maybe your luck was just off that day. Why don't you buy another piece? Not a gamble this time. Just pick a nice piece of jade you actually like. You buy it, and once my fixed deposit matures, I'll reimburse you for that one too. Keep it under $50,000 and choose whatever you like. I'll just have to trouble you to be patient and let Maggie and Anna enjoy themselves a little longer. It would be such a waste to have gone all that way and not made the most of the flights."

Even while lying, Lila did not dare name too high a figure. Too much money would sound fake.

$50,000 was probably the absolute upper limit that Edward and Maggie would believe she could accept.

Edward fell silent for quite some time, as if thinking it over.

After a while, his voice finally came through again.

"Well… all right, then. I'll go and have another look tomorrow. Let the girls wander around a bit longer."

After a little polite small talk, Lila ended the call.

Her messaging app pinged again.

This time, it was Daniel.

Lila thought for a moment. They had not spoken for quite a while.

Most likely Daniel had been too busy enjoying himself with Annabel and had forgotten to flatter her.

Now that Lila had called, he must have suddenly remembered that he still had a fake relationship to maintain.

"Lila, how's the job hunt going? I miss you so much. When I come back in a few days, I'll definitely bring you a present you'll like."

Lila read the message and curled her lip.

Whatever feelings she had once had for Daniel had been completely worn away during the torment of her previous life. All that remained was hatred.

Now, seeing his message only made her feel sick. He was false through and through.

Still, she replied patiently.

"I miss you too. Take good care of Anna and the others while you're there. When you come back, I'll thank you properly. It's been hard on you."

Putting down her phone, Lila washed, got ready and drove out.

She had proper business to handle today.

She had stockpiled smokeless coal as fuel, but she had not yet stocked up on kindling or wood offcuts.

On the Ashton outskirts, there were many people living in small single-storey houses. Like villagers, they relied on heated hearth benches for warmth, and many still used firewood for daily cooking.

But the people on the outskirts did not make their living from farming. Most had jobs in the city.

The firewood they used for heating was usually bought.

How much for a bundle of kindling, how much for a load of branches, these were everyday transactions around the outskirts.

Lila drove her little Luton van straight there.

There were no dedicated firewood shops in the area. If she wanted to buy any, she had to ask around.

She stopped two people along the road before finally finding out where she could buy kindling.

The household at the far eastern edge of the outskirts made a living by going up the hills to cut wood and sell firewood.

Lila stepped into the shabby yard.

It was a large yard. Bundles of firewood were stacked along the walls, and sacks of kindling were piled neatly beside them.

She knocked on the door.

A small dark head popped out from inside. It was a little girl with her hair tied in two pigtails. Her face was smudged black and white.

"Are you buying firewood?"

Her face was dirty, but her eyes were bright, and her voice was clear.

Lila smiled and nodded.

"Yes. I'm here to buy kindling."

"Then wait a bit. My grandad's gone to buy tofu."

After saying that, the little girl pulled her head back inside and gently closed the door again.

She did not invite Lila in.

Lila was not offended in the slightest. If anything, she felt reassured.

The child had been raised well.

When a little girl was home alone, she should have a proper sense of caution.

Just then, there was movement at the gate.

An old man in his seventies limped back in from outside, carrying a block of tofu.

"You're here to buy firewood?"

The old man looked quite pleased when he saw someone waiting.

Not many people bought firewood in summer. He could not sell much, and the piles along the wall were getting higher and higher.

"That's right. I don't need logs. I'd like to buy some kindling."

Kindling was the easy-burning material used to get a fire started.

Chapter 10

Lila ate the clam noodle soup slowly.

The clams were plump and fresh, the glass noodles were coated in rich broth, and the chilli oil and vinegar came through beautifully. It was deeply satisfying.

She had to buy plenty more.

After the Collapse, there would be no chance of getting anything like this again.

One bowl of clam noodle soup and one pork loin bap came to exactly $20.

Twenty one portions should have been $420.

But Gran Lily only charged her $400, saying the bowl Lila had eaten was on the house.

Lila did not stand on ceremony. She paid the $400 at once and said goodbye to Gran Lily.

She placed the twenty portions of clam noodle soup and twenty pork loin baps on the back seat of her car.

By then, the sky had darkened, and the lights inside the car were off.

In such dim surroundings, no one outside could see what she was doing.

Lila reached out and moved all twenty portions of clam noodle soup and all the baps into the Vault.

Gran Lily was right. If clam noodle soup sat too long, the noodles would swell and go soft.

But if it entered the Vault the moment it was freshly made, it would never soften.

Lila really was a clever little thing.

Inside the Vault, she placed the clam noodle soup and pork loin baps separately, arranging them neatly on two levels of storage racking.

Those two shelves were not full yet.

Next time she stocked up on clam noodle soup and pork loin baps, she would fill them completely.

Lila did not leave straight away. Instead, she drove deeper into the lane.

She stopped beside a rice noodle roll shop.

This shop was another favourite of hers.

It was delicious and cheap. Egg rice noodle rolls were only $6 a portion. With sausage or beef added, they were $8.

"Could I have twenty egg rice noodle rolls, ten sausage rice noodle rolls, and ten beef rice noodle rolls, please?"

There happened to be no one else in the shop, so Lila ordered quite a lot in one go.

The total came to $280.

The owner rarely received such a large order and was delighted. She even gave Lila five bottles of cola for free.

While the rice noodle rolls were being made, Lila went next door and bought ten portions of fried noodles, ten portions of fried flatbread, and ten portions of fried rice.

At $10 a portion, that came to $300.

That was enough for today.

She could not buy any more snacks tonight. Any more would attract too much attention.

Both shops finished almost at the same time.

The owners enthusiastically helped Lila put everything into the boot and back seat.

Lila drove a short distance away.

She stopped in a dark corner with no streetlights, waited until she was sure no one was around, then moved everything into the Vault.

Lila had a slight touch of obsessive neatness.

All the snacks she had bought today went onto the same set of racking, but on different levels.

The clam noodle soup went on the first shelf.

The pork loin baps went on the second.

The rice noodle rolls went on the third.

The fried flatbread, fried noodles and similar items went on the fourth.

Because they were stacked together, none of the shelves were full yet.

She would leave the remaining space for the next time she came to stock up on snacks.

The next day, the first batch of supplies Lila had bought arrived.

She drove the Luton van to pick up the parcels.

The entrance to the parcel depot was piled high.

When Lila gave the last digits of her mobile number, the courier could not help staring at her for a few extra seconds.

So she was the person who had bought that enormous pile of parcels.

Still, the courier kindly helped drag all the parcels onto Lila's van.

"Why did you buy so much? Are you opening a shop?"

The courier could not hide his curiosity.

"No. They're donations for an orphanage."

Lila shook her head.

The courier immediately understood and looked at her with a little more respect.

What a kind hearted girl.

After transporting the first batch of supplies to the storage unit, Lila could hardly wait to pull down the door.

She switched on a bright torch and began unpacking.

No matter when it was, opening parcels was always satisfying.

Whether it was padded coats, hats, bed sheets or duvet covers, she opened and checked every single item carefully.

All of this had cost money. She could not afford to waste any of it.

The clothes that passed inspection were stuffed back into their garment bags, moved into the Vault, and arranged tightly on the racking.

A few items were poor quality, so Lila put them back into the van. She would return them in a while.

By the time she had checked all the clothing, she was so tired that she collapsed into the driver's seat.

After tallying the items she planned to return, she picked up her phone and reordered replacements for the missing quantities from other shops.

Anything missing had to be made up.

Not a single piece could be left out.

After returning the clothes to the parcel depot, Lila drove to Lower Vale to check on the work.

She wanted to inspect the current progress.

The doors, windows and heated hearth benches had already been neatly smashed out, and the debris had been cleared away. The next step should be reinforcing and insulating the whole house.

Seeing no obvious problems, Lila went home.

Once she got back, she did the same as usual.

Eat first, then take a comfortable shower, then lie down on the big bed.

Now that the winter clothing was stocked, the next step was food.

Lila opened BargainBasket again and began buying according to the list she had already written.

Because time stood still inside the Vault, food would not expire in there. Lila bought more than she would realistically need.

At crucial moments, these foods could also be used as barter currency.

Instant staples.

Various flavours of instant noodles, 2,000 packets in total.

Spicy sour rice noodles, extra hot noodles, rice noodles that needed boiling, hot and sour glass noodles, potato noodles, self heating hotpot, and chilled style noodles, 200 bags or tubs of each. Altogether, that made 1,400 bags or tubs.

Ready to cook staples.

Two hundred bags of vermicelli.

One hundred pounds each of wide glass noodles, thin glass noodles and dried rice noodles.

Two hundred pounds of dried wheat noodles.

Thirty boxes of plain instant noodle blocks without seasoning sachets, with fifty blocks per box.

Dried vegetables.

One hundred pounds of dried celtuce stems.

Two hundred pounds of dried kelp.

Fifty pounds of dried green beans.

One hundred pounds of dried black fungus.

One hundred pounds of dried shiitake mushrooms.

Three hundred pounds of dried chillies.

Lila planned to stockpile a large amount of fresh vegetables, so she did not buy too many dried vegetables.

Tinned goods.

One thousand tins of luncheon meat.

Five hundred tins of fish.

One thousand tins of fruit.

Lila would have enough fresh food stored to eat well. The reason she stocked up on tinned goods was so that, in an emergency, she could take them out openly and eat them without drawing suspicion.

Meat.

Five hundred pounds of cured bacon.

Five hundred pounds of cured sausage.

Three hundred pounds of cured ribs.

One hundred cured chickens.

Hotpot base.

Five hundred packets of assorted hotpot base.

Two hundred packets of medium hot Carter's Chilli Hotpot Base.

If Lila ate hotpot once a month, that would be twelve times a year. Seven hundred packets of hotpot base would be enough for almost seventy years.

Seasonings.

One hundred bags of chicken seasoning.

One hundred bottles of oyster sauce.

One hundred barrels of vinegar.

One hundred barrels of soy sauce.

One hundred bags of MSG.

Five hundred small sachets of yeast.

Two hundred packets of mixed spice seasoning.

Lila wanted to buy even more vinegar, but collecting the parcels would be too much trouble. She planned to buy more in person later.

Three hundred bags of various brands of soybean paste.

One hundred jars each of mushroom sauce, beef sauce, spicy sauce and Granny May's Chilli Crisp, making four hundred jars in total.

Fifty pounds each of cumin powder, chilli powder, sesame seeds and ground Sichuan pepper.

Snacks.

Two hundred boxes of ham sausages in various flavours.

Five hundred Old Country Pickled Eggs.

Fifty boxes of spicy gluten snacks in assorted flavours.

One thousand bread snacks in various flavours.

Ten boxes of assorted sweets.

Two boxes of chocolate.

Two hundred jars of spicy boneless chicken feet.

Five boxes of crisps.

One box of compressed biscuits.

Various jellies, biscuits, nuts, meat snacks, crispy instant noodles and similar items, one hundred boxes in total.

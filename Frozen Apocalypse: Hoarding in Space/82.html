Chapter 81

Lila spent the entire day resting, and by the next morning she finally felt like herself again.

After breakfast, she sat in the east parlour staring out at the courtyard.

The walls surrounding the property were so high that she couldn't see anything beyond them.

The lightning she'd stored inside the Vault had remained perfectly stable for an entire day.

She really wanted to test it.

And honestly...

There was no reason she couldn't.

The thought struck her, and she immediately got to her feet.

A few minutes later, she was fully bundled up in her mink hat, reindeer-hide boots, and expedition down parka. She stepped out of the east parlour, crossed the courtyard, and headed through the gate.

The cold immediately pressed against her, though her layers kept it manageable.

She paused and surveyed the landscape.

Bare trees stretched across the frozen countryside.

Some had been shattered during the Lightning Storm, leaving only blackened trunks behind. Others appeared untouched, as though the storm had somehow passed them by.

Lila spotted a nearby tree and trudged through the snow toward it.

Inside the Vault, she focused on one of the medium sized lightning bolts she'd collected.

She extended a hand.

With a flicker of will, she released it.

A sharp crackling sound erupted from her palm.

The moment the lightning struck the tree, a burst of fire flashed across the trunk.

Within seconds, the wood turned black and charred.

Lila jumped in surprise and immediately cut off the flow.

The bolt she'd used had consumed only a tiny fraction of its stored energy.

The rest remained floating inside the Vault.

She stared at the ruined tree.

The power was far greater than she'd expected.

That was all the confirmation she needed.

Not wanting to linger outside any longer than necessary, she headed back to the mansion.

After changing into comfortable indoor clothes, she found herself in an exceptionally good mood.

Thinking about having a power was one thing.

Actually possessing it was something else entirely.

For the first time since her rebirth, she truly had a way to defend herself.

That deserved a reward.

Lila brewed a rich milk tea, pulled a collection of snacks from the Vault, and arranged everything on the low table built into her oversized dog bed nook.

Then she settled in to watch her favorite shows while happily snacking away.

Life felt pretty good.

Unfortunately, things were far less comfortable elsewhere.

Early that morning, Mrs. Harris gathered several volunteers and led them outside.

Together, they carried out the bodies of those who had died from smoke inhalation.

Others had been killed during the struggle over supplies the previous day.

Those bodies were taken outside as well.

The surface was brutally cold.

Apart from the expedition parka Mrs. Harris wore, every other piece of serious winter gear had already been stolen.

No one else could stay above ground for long.

The idea of burying the dead had to be abandoned.

Instead, the bodies were laid in a corner of the snow and left there.

By the time Mrs. Harris returned to Basement Level One, she was completely exhausted.

She dropped onto her bedding and struggled to catch her breath.

A familiar smell drifted through the shelter.

Instant noodles.

Someone was cooking.

A fire had been lit inside one of the basement rooms.

Using one arm to push herself upright, Mrs. Harris followed the scent until she reached the door.

She knocked.

"Don't start fires down here. The air doesn't circulate. It's dangerous."

She had no idea who was inside.

"Get lost!"

The angry shout echoed through the door.

Mrs. Harris froze.

Only then did she realize something.

The authority she once carried as the shelter's manager had disappeared the moment the Lightning Storm tore everything apart.

Silently, she lowered her hand and returned to her bedding.

People watched her from every corner of the shelter.

Hungry.

Desperate.

Waiting.

It was mealtime.

Seeing their faces made her chest ache.

No tears came.

She had none left.

The food was gone.

Everything was gone.

A little girl of about five lay weakly in her mother's arms.

"Mom... I'm hungry..."

The woman forced a smile.

"Just hold on a little longer, sweetheart. Mommy will find something good for you."

It was a lie.

The same lie she'd been telling over and over.

But hope was all she had left to offer.

Nearby, an elderly man with white hair sat motionless on his bedding.

His thin frame looked almost skeletal.

Only his open eyes suggested he was still alive.

Not far away, an eighteen year old boy lay barely conscious.

One side of his face had been horribly injured during yesterday's chaos.

He breathed in shallow, uneven breaths.

The sight was enough to make Mrs. Harris clench her fists.

Those people were monsters.

Everyone was supposed to survive together.

Instead, they had stolen food.

They had hurt people.

They had killed people.

Her voice became low and hard.

"Who took the most supplies yesterday?"

Silence answered her.

No one dared speak.

Finally, a faint voice emerged from the crowd.

"Luke Whitmore."

The speaker hesitated.

"The guy staying in your room. He took almost all the communal supplies."

Mrs. Harris nodded slowly.

"Then we'll take them back."

Her voice was quiet.

But there was no hesitation in it.

She picked up a sharpened wooden stake.

"Who's with me?"

Nobody moved.

Mrs. Harris looked around the room.

"That food belongs to all of us."

"If we get it back, we all eat."

A weak but determined voice answered.

"I'll go."

"Me too."

"And me."

One by one, people struggled to their feet.

They grabbed whatever they could use as a weapon.

Wooden boards.

Metal pipes.

Broken tools.

In one corner, Annabel's family remained silent.

Edward shot both Annabel and Maggie a warning glance.

No volunteering.

No unnecessary risks.

If someone else was willing to take the danger, they weren't about to stop them.

Most people in the shelter were asleep.

Especially the looters.

After stuffing themselves on stolen food, they were snoring heavily behind closed doors.

It was the perfect opportunity.

Everyone who could still walk followed Mrs. Harris through the shelter.

Soon they arrived outside her former room.

The lock had long since been smashed apart.

Luke had broken it when he moved in.

His loud snoring echoed from inside.

Confident in his strength, he had never bothered worrying about the elderly, the injured, or the starving people he'd pushed aside.

Mrs. Harris gently tested the door.

A wire had been wrapped around it from the inside.

Carefully, she slipped her hand through the gap and began unwinding it.

Everyone held their breath.

Some watched her hands.

Others kept watch down the hallway, terrified someone might raise an alarm.

Click.

The wire shifted.

The tiny sound seemed deafening.

Luke 's snoring stopped.

Every heart in the hallway froze.

Several seconds passed.

Then the snoring resumed.

A wave of relief swept through the group.

Mrs. Harris removed the wire completely and eased the door open.

Inside sat two large utility sacks.

The communal supplies.

Their supplies.

Mrs. Harris stared at the sleeping man.

Then she whispered.

"Take him down."

Several people hesitated.

Fear flickered in their eyes.

If they'd been capable of violence before, they never would have been pushed into a corner in the first place.

Mrs. Harris tightened her grip on the stake.

"If we want to live, we don't have a choice."

Then she stepped inside.

Without another word, she drove the sharpened stake toward Luke.

His eyes snapped open.

He sprang from the bed with startling speed and grabbed the knife beside him.

The blade flashed.

Mrs. Harris cried out softly as pain shot through her arm.

Before she could recover, he pulled the knife back and drove forward again.

She clenched her teeth and forced herself to stay silent.

If she screamed, the other looters might wake up.

She raised the stake again.

Luke caught her wrist in a crushing grip.

The strength in his arm was overwhelming.

Mrs. Harris could only watch as the knife moved closer.

And closer.

And closer.

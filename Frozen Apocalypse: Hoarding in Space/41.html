Chapter 40

The temperature gap between the east parlour and the other rooms was still fairly obvious.

Overall, though, the retrofitted mansion's insulation was already excellent.

The weather forecast on Lila's phone was no longer very accurate.

She pressed herself against the window and checked the thermometer in the courtyard. It read minus forty-one.

The temperature was falling fast. In just one morning, it had dropped by more than ten degrees.

It was extreme, but not unexpected. In her previous life, the temperature plunge had happened just as quickly.

After working all morning, Lila was genuinely tired. She returned to the east parlour, placed the kettle on the fireplace hob, then sank into the sofa opposite the fire.

Bliss.

It was warm, cosy and utterly comfortable.

She had spent a long time in the kitchen earlier, and the chill had clung to her clothes. Now, basking in the heat of the fireplace, her whole body relaxed.

It would be even better with a hot coffee.

At that thought, Lila looked at the empty coffee table.

From the Vault, she took out the pale yellow milk tea cup and dark brown coffee mug she often used, placing them on the small table.

Then she found a storage box and set that there too.

The box had once held all her instant drinks.

After starting her final year at university, she had been too busy to restock it, so now only the empty box remained.

She might not have restocked back then, but during her stockpiling she had bought plenty.

Closing her eyes, Lila searched through the shelves inside the Vault and chose a bottle of honey and passionfruit concentrate, ten sachets of instant coffee, ten sachets of plain milk tea powder and a small packet of jasmine tea.

She arranged them neatly in the drink box.

Today, she would have a refined and elegant cup of instant coffee.

She poured the coffee powder into the mug. Less than five minutes later, the water on the fireplace hob boiled.

Steaming water flowed slowly into the cup. The brown coffee granules dissolved at once, releasing a rich coffee aroma.

Lila drew in a deep breath of that scent.

It had been so long since she had drunk coffee.

Ever since her second life began, she had been busy stockpiling and had not once thought to make herself a cup.

Now that she had finally relaxed, the rich aroma made her crave it badly.

Lila leaned sideways into the sofa, her fingertips just able to touch the hot mug on the coffee table.

She picked it up and gently blew away the fine white mist rising from the surface.

One small sip, and the deep roasted flavour spread across her tongue. Warmth slid down her throat and into her stomach. She narrowed her eyes in satisfaction, like a cat basking in the sun.

After a brief spell of doing absolutely nothing, she still reached for her phone.

Sure enough, the moment a young person had time to spare, they wanted to check their phone.

She opened the camera app again.

The four people in the flat were still curled up under their duvets without moving.

Lila shook her head.

It was not truly cold outside yet. If they did not hurry out and stock up now, then once the real Long Freeze arrived, they would suffer properly.

She put the phone down and wrapped both hands around the coffee mug again, letting the last of its warmth seep into her palms.

After she finished the final few mouthfuls, heat spread from her stomach through her limbs, even warming her fingertips.

She set the cup back on the coffee table with a soft tap.

Then she lay down sideways on the sofa and pulled the thick, soft blanket from by her feet, wrapping herself in it.

The fire in the fireplace was burning strongly.

The smokeless coal crackled softly, occasionally throwing up tiny sparks that vanished almost at once in the shifting light.

Against that hypnotic background, Lila soon grew sleepy. Her eyelids slowly became heavy.

Before long, her breathing was deep and steady on the sofa.

The dim room looked especially warm and peaceful.

The flames licked around the firebox, filling the room with flickering amber light.

By the sofa, the two puppies occasionally kicked their legs in their sleep, or let out a muffled little whimper, adding tiny traces of life to the room.

Inside, everything was safe and warm.

In both the city and the villages, people were using every heating device they had.

Air conditioning, electric blankets, fan heaters.

Homes with air-conditioning units had it much better. Even with the temperature outside at minus forty, keeping the heating on indoors could maintain a room temperature of over twenty degrees.

Apart from worrying about food running short, life had not been affected too badly. Some people were even relieved that work and school were suspended for a few days, giving them a proper rest.

Homes without air conditioning were miserable.

People could only rely on electric blankets, fan heaters, duvets and padded winter clothes to keep warm.

At that moment, the difference between having air conditioning and not having it became brutally clear. It was like living in two entirely different worlds.

The snow had started again.

The ground the snowploughs had cleared was soon covered by a thick new layer.

Just as countless people were growing anxious, everyone's phones began receiving messages.

The first was a government notice announcing work and school closures.

The second gave everyone some reassurance.

"Emergency Notice from Ashton Council Heating Resilience Office:

According to the latest warning from the Met Office, our city is currently experiencing a severe temperature plunge.

To protect residents and ensure heating supply, the council has decided to activate the emergency district-heating system from the stated time today, approximately two hours from now.

Before heating begins, please check your indoor radiators and complete radiator bleeding in time to ensure effective heating.

For any issues, please call the service hotline.

Please reduce unnecessary travel and take precautions against the cold."

Residents in the city cheered when they saw the message.

Online forums exploded with discussion.

"We've finally got it! The council's actually done well this time. They're starting heating after just one day."

"Exactly. No more freezing indoors."

"Everyone, bleed your radiators quickly. Don't let it affect the heating."

Others were worried that the Collapse was coming.

"Even with emergency council heating, we still need to plan ahead. This weather is abnormal. Never mind once in a century, this is once in a thousand years. Find a way to stock up on more food and drink. What if it gets even colder, or the freeze carries on? This really could be the Collapse..."

"Stop spreading rumours. Things are bad enough already, and you still want to stir people up and keep everyone frightened? The government has stepped in. It'll be fine."

"The weather really is abnormal. Stocking up where possible is good for everyone. There's no need to be so hostile."

For a while, the two views clashed fiercely online. Some insisted it was just an unusual cold snap, while others believed the Collapse was imminent.

The arguments grew so intense that several forums crashed outright.

When Annabel saw the message saying Oakwood Estate would soon have heating, she screamed.

"Mum! Look! The heating's coming on! Hurry up and open the radiator valves!"

The moment Maggie heard that the heating was coming on, light returned to her eyes.

At last, there was hope.

But she was so cold.

She looked at the three people beside her. None of them seemed willing to move.

In the end, she could only rise slowly and go to open the radiator valves.

Only when the air stopped hissing from the radiators and water began to flow did she close them again.

Under everyone's eager expectation, two hours later, the radiators really did begin to heat up.

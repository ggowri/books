Chapter 51

At six in the evening, Oakwood Estate Management posted a notice in the residents' group chat. Tomorrow would be another supply distribution day.

There would not be much, but for families without much food stored at home, it was still something to look forward to.

Lila watched everyone in the group complain about the tiny amount of supplies and sighed, shaking her head.

"They really don't know how lucky they are. In a few days, even this will be gone."

She added coal to the inglenook fireplace for the last time that night, settled the chicks and puppies, took a hot shower, then slipped into her warm, comfortable bed.

That night, the temperature plunged at terrifying speed.

The empty streets and hills froze beneath a sky so cold the air itself seemed to solidify. Everything was sealed beneath ice.

People living in tower blocks began waking up one after another.

"Why isn't the air conditioning heating?"

"The radiators are freezing!"

The air-conditioning units had frozen completely and stopped working. Outside, the district-heating pipes burst one after another with sharp cracks. Lukewarm water sprayed from the split pipes and turned instantly into columns of ice.

The last trace of warmth vanished from the radiators indoors.

In the middle of the night, lights flicked on across the estate, one flat after another.

People struggled to drag themselves out of bed with duvets wrapped around them, shuffling over to wardrobes and pulling on layer after layer of clothing.

They would have worn every garment they owned if they could.

It was too cold. So cold it felt as though death might arrive in the very next breath.

Any exposed skin stung as if pricked by needles.

It was an unimaginable cold, a cold with nowhere to hide.

Maggie's family had woken long ago as well. They had pulled on every shabby set of thermal vests and long johns they had managed to store, and now they huddled beneath duvets, shaking uncontrollably.

Inside the flat, it was at least minus fifty degrees. Even wrapped in bedding, they could not feel the slightest warmth.

Click.

The lights went out.

In that instant, whole blocks across the estate were swallowed by darkness.

For a moment, everyone fell into blind panic.

"The power's gone! Oh my God!"

A scream rang out from one of the upper floors, and that was enough to shatter what little courage people had left.

"It's so cold! It hurts! Help me!"

"Help! I'm going to freeze solid!"

Desperate cries rose one after another.

Online forums, estate group chats and neighbourhood feeds were flooded with posts saying the Long Freeze had arrived.

The government and military had no choice but to activate their cold-weather emergency plan ahead of schedule.

An official notice was sent to every resident's phone.

To all residents of the city:

According to an urgent warning from the Met Office, our city has now entered an extreme cold emergency due to the impact of a severe cold front. Outdoor temperatures have fallen below historic record lows and are accompanied by heavy blizzards. Due to frost damage to power and district-heating systems, heating has been interrupted across the city. Electrical facilities are under emergency repair, but power cannot be restored for the time being.

The next forty-eight hours will be the most dangerous period. All residents must act immediately and move to a basement shelter at once.

All persons, including elderly residents and children, must immediately take essential supplies and enter their own basement or the basement of their residential building. If your basement is not suitable for habitation, move at once to a communal underground car park, civil-defence shelter, or another enclosed underground space within your estate. Basements can effectively shield occupants from extreme surface temperatures and are currently the safest available shelter.

All residents must comply. This is a life-saving notice. This is not a drill.

Ashton Emergency Management Command

After receiving the message, people stopped cowering in corners and began forcing themselves to move.

Even though their bodies were stiff with cold, they endured the stabbing pain and began gathering essential supplies.

Maggie's family saw the notice too.

They had very few supplies. Aside from the clothes they were wearing and the duvets wrapped around them, they had only a small amount of dried noodles and instant noodles.

The four of them stood there blankly. None of them wanted to take their hands out from under the duvets.

"Mum, you pack it," Anna said when no one moved, urging Maggie on.

Maggie hesitated for a moment, then crouched down. The instant she stretched her hand out from under the duvet and touched the knife-cold air, she jerked it back. In the end, she gritted her teeth and packed the dried noodles and instant noodles from the kitchen cupboard into a carrier bag.

"Go. Quickly."

Maggie tucked the carrier bag inside the duvet as well, ready to head for the Basement.

By now, her face had gone numb from the cold. Even speaking felt stiff and wooden.

A loud, clear announcement blared from beneath the estate.

"Residents, wake up at once! Bring essential supplies and take shelter in the Basement!"

"Look after the elderly and children! Go to the Basement immediately!"

"Neighbours, help one another! Check on elderly residents and disabled residents! Assist them in moving to the Basement!"

The three sentences played on a loop, reaching every ear.

When Edward heard the part about checking on elderly and disabled residents, he stopped.

He looked up at Maggie. Maggie understood at once.

"We should go and remind Old Mr. Finch downstairs," she said.

"Why bother with him? We can barely save ourselves," Anna said. She was numb and aching from the cold, and Maggie's sudden burst of kindness almost drove her mad.

"Come on," Edward said, then took the lead and opened the door.

Outside was more than ten degrees colder than indoors, but they were already too frozen to feel the difference.

With the widespread power cut, the lifts were useless. They had to take the stairs.

After going down only one floor, Edward turned towards Old Mr. Finch's door.

"Mr. Finch! Are you in? Estate Management told us to go to the Basement for shelter! Are you there?"

Edward knocked on the door as if he were genuinely concerned.

Each blow landed on the freezing door with a sharp sting that shot through his hand.

There was no answer from inside.

Edward crouched down and pulled a key from beneath the doormat.

Old Mr. Finch often forgot to take his key when he went out, so he kept a spare under the mat. The neighbours nearby all knew about it.

Enduring the biting cold, Edward unlocked the door.

He stepped in first, with Maggie, Anna and the other family member following behind him.

There was no one in the sitting room, so Edward turned into the bedroom.

The bedroom was still relatively warm. Before the power cut, an electric fan heater must have been running the whole time.

Old Mr. Finch was curled up in bed beneath several layers of quilts, still sleeping with a frown on his face.

The footsteps of the four intruders were loud enough to wake him.

He opened his eyes blearily.

"Maggie? What are you doing here?"

Old Mr. Finch looked surprised.

He had been fairly close to Lila in ordinary times, and Lila would occasionally run errands for him.

But the people now standing in his sitting room were only familiar faces from the building. He did not really know them.

"The notice says we all have to go to the Basement for shelter. We came to remind you," Maggie said.

"Oh. Right. Thank you. You go ahead. I need to get my things together..."

Old Mr. Finch slowly propped himself up. Cold air rushed in through the gap beneath the lifted quilts, and he shuddered.

He reached out and slowly put on the thick padded coat lying beside him.

Edward turned around and went into the kitchen, then began rummaging through Old Mr. Finch's cupboards.

Elderly people often liked to keep food stored at home.

Old Mr. Finch had quite a lot.

There were still four or five pounds of frozen meat in the fridge, along with frozen fresh noodles.

In the kitchen cupboard, there was half a box of shortbread biscuits and several tins of porridge, bought for him not long ago by his son, who worked far away.

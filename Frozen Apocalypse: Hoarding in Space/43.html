Chapter 42

Once the deal was done, Callum retreated at once. All he wanted was to get back to his warm little den as quickly as possible.

Dressed that thickly, the stairwell was not too cold.

But the stretch between the two blocks was different. Threads of bone-cutting wind still found their way through every tiny gap, stabbing into exposed skin like needles of ice.

Quite a few people watching from their flats saw Callum pass below.

By the time he got home, stripped off his gear and recovered from the cold, someone in the Oakwood residents' group chat had already taken a photo of him.

"Who is that? Fair play to him. He actually dared to go outside."

"He's wrapped up properly, though."

Luckily, no one recognised him. Leo was sensible enough not to say a word about it either.

Callum looked at the extra two hundred thousand in his account, and his grin nearly reached his ears.

Money had never come this easily before.

But the cold outside still left him shaken.

Thank goodness he had dressed so heavily. If he had gone out in an ordinary down jacket, he really might have frozen to death in the snow.

Lila woke from a deeply satisfied sleep. The fire in the inglenook fireplace had burned lower.

She got up, and the first thing she did was add coal to the fire. The house had to stay warm.

Out of habit, she glanced at her phone. There were a great many new messages in the residents' group chat.

She skimmed the notices about emergency heating and supply distribution.

She knew about this.

It had happened in her previous life too.

Only, none of it would last very long.

Her stomach suddenly rumbled. Only then did she remember she had forgotten to eat lunch.

Since returning to the Lower Vale Mansion, she had not even cooked her first proper meal here.

Now that her stockpiling was done and everything at home had been dealt with, she ought to spend plenty of time making good food.

She pulled on her old-fashioned padded jacket and went to the kitchen.

First, she washed her hands at the sink.

The fire had been burning the whole time, so there was plenty of hot water.

From the Vault, Lila selected a pack of chopped spare ribs, took out six pieces, rinsed them clean and placed them in a clay pot.

Then she cut a corn cob into short sections and added those as well.

She filled the pot with water, added seasonings, put on the lid, and set it on the fireplace hob in the east parlour to stew.

Back in the kitchen, she lit the kitchen range.

She rinsed the rice and placed it in the steamer basket inside the large pot. While she was at it, she cracked two eggs onto a plate and put that in to steam as well.

When the rice was nearly done, Lila took a piece of beef from the Vault. She cut off a small portion and placed the rest in a basin on the kitchen rack.

The kitchen was on the cool side, so the beef would not spoil there.

She rinsed the small piece she had kept, sliced it thinly, added seasonings and mixed it well to marinate.

From the Vault, she took out three fresh green peppers and four small chillies, then sliced them thinly.

Once everything was prepared, she set the wok on the blazing-hot range.

A ring of cold oil went in first. Then she added the marinated beef and stir-fried it until the pieces separated and changed colour. The aroma rose at once.

Lila swallowed, tipped the cooked beef out, then added all the sliced peppers and chillies into the wok to release their fragrance. After that, she returned the beef to the pan and tossed everything together.

A little seasoning, a light splash of vinegar around the edge of the wok, and it was done.

Afraid the dish would go cold, Lila quickly carried it into the warm east parlour.

By now, another fragrance had filled the room. It was the mellow, savoury smell of the rib stew.

The sweetness came from the corn. It mingled with the rich meatiness of the ribs, fresh and delicious all at once.

Lila was so absorbed in the smell that it took her several seconds to realise there was no dining table in the east parlour.

There was only a little spare space on the floor between the writing desk and the bookcase, so she brought out the dining table from her flat at Oakwood Estate and placed it there.

It was not exactly spacious, but there was room for one chair.

She set the stir-fried beef on the table, added a heatproof mat beside it, then went over to check on the corn and rib stew.

The ribs had already stewed until tender. Because the hob was so hot, fine bubbles were rising through the soup.

It bubbled softly, releasing not only steam, but a deeply tempting aroma.

Holding a cloth in each hand, Lila lifted the clay pot down and placed it on the heatproof mat on the dining table.

Then she brought over the rice and the steamed eggs.

Time to eat.

The steaming, fragrant food gave Lila a fierce sense of belonging.

She found a relaxing show to watch over dinner and began eating.

First, she ladled herself a bowl of rib soup. She blew away the steam and drank it in small sips, driving off the faint chill she had picked up in the kitchen.

The soup was fresh and delicious, with just the right balance of savoury and sweet. It carried the glossy richness of the ribs and tasted wonderful.

Then she took a large chopstickful of quick-fried beef with chillies and ate it with rice.

Fragrant, spicy and deeply satisfying.

Lila had not eaten this fully in a long time.

By the end of the meal, the stir-fry and eggs were gone. Half a pot of corn and rib soup remained.

It was already late, so the remaining half pot could be breakfast tomorrow.

Lila hated leaving chores unfinished. If something needed doing, she had to do it at once, or it would stay on her mind.

She put the remaining rib soup in a cool spot in the kitchen, then washed all the bowls and plates clean.

After that, she went early to the bathroom for a shower, changed into loose, comfortable pyjamas, left her freshly dried hair down, and returned to the east parlour.

She had eaten well. The place was clean. The house was settled. She had showered.

All that remained was to lounge comfortably on the sofa, play on her phone and eat snacks.

Speaking of snacks, Lila suddenly realised the east parlour did not have anywhere to keep them.

She took her snack cabinet out of the Vault, then took out packets of snacks and filled it to the brim before placing it beside the coffee table.

Whenever she wanted something, she could reach out and grab it.

Everything in there was something she loved. Of course, she would never have bought anything she did not like.

The water on the fireplace had boiled again, so she made herself a cup of hot tea.

Then she took every phone and tablet from the Vault, connected them to the internet, and began downloading novels, series and films.

She did not care if there were duplicates. The important thing was that every device had plenty stored on it.

Once the city lost power and the internet went down, she would have to rely on whatever she downloaded now to pass the time happily.

There was no room on the sofa, so she lined the phones and tablets along the edge of the heated hearth bench and left them to do their work.

Only her beloved everyday phone stayed with her, playing a variety show while she grabbed a packet of cucumber-flavoured crisps and drank hot tea between bites.

This was the life.

Two hours later, the films and shows had mostly finished downloading.

Lila put all the devices away and returned them to the Vault.

Time stood still inside the Vault, so the phones would last longer there too.

She was tired of eating by then. It was time to give the two puppies their night feed.

A puppy's personality, it seemed, could be seen from birth.

Patches always fed like he was fighting a battle, gulping everything down in a frantic rush.

Snowy took big mouthfuls too, but somehow still seemed a little more refined.

Still, Lila adored them both.

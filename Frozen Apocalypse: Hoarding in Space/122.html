Chapter 121

"Oh, no, thanks. I'm fine. I've got..."

Just as Lila reached into her backpack to pull a freshly made Cornmeal Wrap from the Vault, both Sophie and Ethan shoved half a dry bread roll into her hands.

Before she could finish refusing, Sophie cut her off.

"Scavenging isn't exactly a walk in the park. You've got to eat something. If you pass out alone in some apartment, nobody's going to find you."

She pressed the coarse multigrain roll firmly into Lila's palm.

Ethan hesitated.

Now that Sophie had already given Lila food, should he still offer his?

Well...

Too late now.

He'd already split the roll.

Without another word, he placed his half beside Sophie's.

Lila looked down at the two dry pieces of bread in her hands, one dark and one white.

At this point, refusing would only make things awkward.

"All right. Then let's trade."

That seemed like the simplest solution.

She smiled, slipped her free hand into her backpack, and quietly retrieved a pale blue insulated lunch container from the Vault.

It was the same lunch box she'd used back in university.

The plastic was scratched from years of use, but it still worked perfectly.

She set her backpack at her feet and balanced the container on her lap.

With one hand, she flipped open the lid.

Four golden Cornmeal Wraps rested neatly inside.

Two on top.

Two underneath.

The instant the lid lifted, a cloud of fragrant steam drifted into the room.

The rich aroma of toasted cornmeal mingled with her homemade sauce, instantly overpowering the smoky scent of the fire.

The glossy wraps looked fresh off the griddle.

Sophie and Ethan froze.

Both of them instinctively widened their eyes.

Ethan cleared his throat.

For perhaps the first time in his life, he silently called himself an idiot.

Seriously, Ethan.

What the hell were you thinking?

When the refuge had been under construction, delicious smells had drifted down from Lila's cabin on the mountain almost every single day.

How had he somehow convinced himself she'd run out of food?

Sophie was not faring much better.

Heat crept into her cheeks.

She had actually pitied someone holding a lunch that smelled like heaven.

And she'd offered... half of a dry multigrain roll.

While the two of them were still processing the embarrassment, Lila carefully placed both pieces of bread beside the lunch box.

She picked up the two wraps from the top layer and handed one to Sophie and one to Ethan.

"Here. We'll swap. Try one."

She smiled sheepishly.

"I made them myself. They're only cornmeal, though. I'm out of white flour."

At a time like this, wraps were a luxury.

But cornmeal made them seem much less extravagant.

During the Collapse, eating mixed grains was perfectly normal.

"No, really. I'm good."

Sophie forced an awkward laugh and took an exaggerated bite out of her bread.

"Go on."

Lila nudged the wrap into her hand.

"Mine's made with coarse grains too. Give my cooking a chance."

Sophie had only brought one bread roll, and she'd willingly shared half.

Lila was not the type to accept kindness without returning it.

Before Sophie could protest again, the warm wrap was already in her hands.

Even after sitting inside the insulated container, it was still almost too hot to hold.

Because, technically, it had gone straight from the frying pan into the Vault.

Sophie stared at the warm, slightly oily wrap.

Something tightened inside her chest.

Ever since the Collapse began, no one had openly shown her this kind of kindness.

Lila was the first.

She was so touched that she completely forgot she had been the one to offer food first.

Since it was already in her hands, refusing any longer would only be rude.

Besides...

The rich aroma rising from the wrap was downright irresistible.

She had not smelled anything like this in months.

Without Lila, she honestly believed she'd never taste food like this again.

Swallowing hard, Sophie took a cautious bite.

The lightly crisped cornmeal flatbread carried a fragrant homemade sauce.

Inside were generous piles of shredded potatoes and sliced onions that still retained a satisfying crunch.

Savory.

Tangy.

Just enough heat.

Everything blended perfectly with the toasted cornmeal.

The flavors exploded across her tongue.

It was easily the best thing she'd eaten since the world fell apart.

Only after Sophie had taken a bite did Lila turn toward Ethan and offer him the other wrap.

Normally, Ethan would have refused without a second thought.

That was simply who he was.

Disciplined.

Reserved.

By the book.

But the aroma refused to leave him alone.

It seemed determined to burrow straight into his nose.

The golden, glistening surface did not help either.

There was simply no way he could pretend he was not tempted.

"...Thanks."

He accepted it, feeling unusually shameless.

Under the longing gazes of everyone gathered around the fire, Ethan took an enormous bite.

The sauce hit him first.

It had smelled incredible.

It tasted even better.

The cornmeal had been fried until lightly crisp, releasing a rich roasted sweetness from the corn oil itself.

On its own it might have been a little rich.

Combined with crunchy potatoes and sharp onions, though, every bite stayed bright and satisfying.

Lila had not skimped on the filling either.

Every mouthful came packed with potatoes that crunched pleasantly between his teeth.

It was so good he almost hated swallowing it.

Around the fire, countless pairs of eyes stared with unconcealed envy.

People silently cursed themselves.

Why hadn't they shared a little food with the girl earlier?

They could have been eating one of those wraps too.

Lila ignored every stare.

It was hardly some extravagant feast.

Just cornmeal flatbread with shredded potatoes.

Anyone in the refuge who had decent supplies could make something similar.

"Wow..."

Sophie's mouth was completely full.

She alternated between bites of the wrap and bites of the bread, chewing with obvious delight.

"It's still hot. Like... it just came off the pan. This is seriously amazing."

She could not help being curious.

The lunch had been exposed to the brutal cold for hours.

It had never been reheated.

So why did it still taste freshly cooked?

Lila laughed and patted the insulated container.

"The lunch box deserves the credit. It keeps everything warm. Food comes out tasting almost freshly made."

"What brand is it? I should buy one."

The words escaped Sophie before she even thought about them.

A second later, she froze.

Then she laughed at herself.

"Listen to me. Buy one where? The world's gone to hell."

She shook her head and returned to devouring her lunch.

Lila smiled.

"When the weather gets warmer, I'll get one for you."

She knew perfectly well that would probably never happen.

Even if temperatures rose, society would not recover overnight.

Still...

Sometimes people needed something hopeful to look forward to.

Sophie grinned.

"Deal."

She somehow enjoyed the food even more after that.

Lila alternated between bites of the wrap and the dry bread roll.

The wraps tasted even better today.

After an exhausting morning of scavenging, every bite was unbelievably satisfying.

Next time, she decided, she'd add a generous layer of spicy sauce.

The onions alone did not bring nearly enough kick.

While the two women chatted over lunch, Ethan had already polished off his wrap.

He smacked his lips, still chasing the lingering flavor.

Damn...

That had been incredible.

He wondered if he'd ever get to eat something like that again.

For the first time, he regretted accepting Lila's Full Spectrum Grow Lights as payment.

Maybe...

He should have asked her to trade him a home cooked meal once in a while instead.

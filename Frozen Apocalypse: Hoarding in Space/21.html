Chapter 20

Three tonnes of potatoes and two tonnes of sweet potatoes cost thirteen thousand dollars in total.

By the time Lila had moved everything into the Vault, she was utterly exhausted.

She had taken in a great deal today, so it was only natural that her body could not quite bear it.

She forced herself to go home and slept heavily. When she woke the next day, she no longer felt quite so tired.

Remembering that she still had not stockpiled enough eggs, Lila ate breakfast, then drove to a wholesale egg supplier.

Egg prices were not high this year. One tonne of eggs only cost six thousand four hundred dollars.

After moving all the eggs into the Vault, Lila drove to the town beside Sunwell.

She had stocked up on enough vegetables. This time, her target was fruit.

Clearwater had many strawberry polytunnels, and the strawberry season was almost over. At this point, the strawberries were at their sweetest and cheapest.

After asking around, Lila had only just walked into one of the strawberry polytunnels when she heard the owner talking noisily inside.

"This is the last crop. Hurry up and find someone to sell the lot to. In another couple of days, they'll rot in the ground."

He was speaking to his wife.

"Hello. Would you sell the whole polytunnel of strawberries?"

The moment Lila heard that, she quickly greeted him.

"Yes! Yes, absolutely!"

For the owner, it was like having a pillow delivered just as he was getting sleepy. His eyes lit up at the sight of a buyer walking in on her own.

"Can you take me in to have a look?"

These would be rations for after the Collapse. Lila had to choose carefully. Sweet ones were best.

As soon as the polytunnel was opened, warm, damp air rushed towards her.

Lila crouched down, casually picked a strawberry, wiped it, and put it in her mouth.

It was soft, fragrant and sweet, filling her mouth with the scent of strawberries.

"Good. The quality is quite nice. How much is there in one polytunnel, roughly?"

Lila nodded and stood up.

"They're not as thick on the plants as they were earlier in the season. This one probably has just over two hundred pounds."

The owner gave an estimate.

"How many polytunnels do you have?" Lila asked.

"Five. All five strawberry polytunnels are ours."

The old couple managed the strawberry polytunnels themselves. Any more would be too much to handle. Five were enough to support them.

"I'll take them all. How much would you want?"

Lila waved a hand, ready to buy the lot.

"Well..."

The owner had not expected his most troublesome end-of-season problem to be solved by one buyer.

"Six thousand dollars. The five polytunnels together should have more than a thousand pounds. That price is lower than what I'd give other wholesale buyers."

Lila roughly calculated it in her head. It came to about eight dollars per pound.

At the market, even when prices were at their lowest, good strawberries could still sell for twelve or thirteen dollars per pound.

Eight dollars was fair.

"All right. I'll take all of them. Please start picking. I'll pay half as a deposit first."

Lila scanned the payment code on the wall, paid the deposit, and drove away amid the owner's repeated thanks.

A village below the town specialised in cherries. The hills were covered with cherry trees.

This was exactly when cherries were arriving on the market in large quantities, and the trees were at their most abundant.

Without much effort, Lila reserved one thousand pounds of cherries.

On the way past other orchards, she also bought two hundred pounds of mulberries.

The cherries and mulberries cost ten thousand dollars altogether.

Strawberries, cherries and mulberries all spoiled quickly. For the next two days, Lila kept going back and forth between the town and the villages to collect fruit.

On the journeys there and back, she kept buying petrol as well. In two days, she stocked up another thirty jerrycans.

The other local fruits were not ripe yet, so Lila had no choice but to go to the wholesale fruit market.

Of course, the remaining fruits were out of season, and some had come from far away, so the prices were not low.

Looking at her remaining balance, Lila no longer dared to buy in huge quantities.

She walked around the wholesale market, comparing prices and tasting samples, and finally bought various fruits from different stalls.

One thousand pounds each of apples, bananas, grapes and mangoes.

Five hundred pounds each of pears, mangosteens, longans, lychees, lemons and another batch of mangoes.

Five hundred pineapples.

One hundred durians.

Eight hundred pounds of premium cherries.

After buying those varieties, Lila stopped. The total came to sixty-three thousand dollars.

As for the other fruits, either Lila did not like them, or they were completely out of season and had not appeared in the market at all.

It took Lila a long time to move all the fruit into the Vault.

Looking over the checklist in her hand, the must-have food supplies were almost complete. Apart from watermelons and maize, which could only be stocked at the end of June, the only thing left was beef.

Since the small meatworks did not sell beef, she could only look for a smaller butcher.

After asking around several times, Lila found a butcher's stall with a good reputation. They slaughtered one cow every morning and sold the meat throughout the day.

Lila went to the stall to ask and learned that the beef was usually twenty-eight dollars per pound, no matter which cut she wanted.

"If I buy a lot, can you make it cheaper?"

This was probably the sentence Lila had said most often since she began stockpiling for the Collapse.

"Depends how many pounds you want."

The butcher smiled as he wiped the grease from his hands.

"Eight thousand pounds."

Beef was Lila's favourite meat.

Grilled beef, hotpot beef slices, big steamed buns stuffed with beef. She loved them all.

Eight thousand pounds would be enough to last her into old age.

"Eight thousand pounds? Then I'll have to slaughter fifteen large cattle specially for you. I can do twenty-six dollars per pound."

The butcher was a little surprised, but not entirely. His stall had a good reputation, and people often came to order whole cattle.

For Lila, twenty-six dollars per pound was naturally acceptable.

"Thank you. Could you separate the beef by cut and pack everything individually?"

If the beef could be properly divided and packed, it would save her a great deal of effort.

"No problem. This is what we do at a butcher's stall. Otherwise, how would we justify earning the difference?"

The butcher was refreshingly direct about his own profit.

Lila paid a deposit and agreed to come the next day to collect the meat.

Seeing that it was still not late, she made another trip to the Lower Vale Mansion.

The renovation was taking shape more and more. According to the site manager, it would be finished in about ten days.

Lila was looking forward to it.

After spending so much money on the refit, she wanted to see just how good the result would be.

The next day, Lila arrived as agreed and collected the eight thousand pounds of beef she had ordered.

The butcher had thoughtfully packed all the beef according to different cuts, and every bag even had a label on it so Lila could recognise it easily.

Even the cattle bones had been cut up and packed into large cardboard boxes.

He had done an excellent job. Lila paid readily, thanked him, and drove the vanload of beef straight to Lockridge Storage Unit.

Only when she had moved things into the Vault from inside the storage unit did she feel truly sure.

After storing the beef, Lila felt increasingly at ease.

Now the only category left was self-defence gear.

She had done plenty of research online, but she still could not get hold of anything more serious. She could only buy other practical tools for self-defence.

Buying them online was unreliable, and the quality might not be good enough, so Lila drove straight to the outdoor sports superstore.

When she came out, she had two powerful compound bows in her hands.

They were the best compound bows in the shop. Since they were for self-defence, Lila did not dare cut corners. The two bows cost thirty thousand dollars.

She also bought two entrenching tools and two pairs of trekking poles.

Altogether, she spent thirty-five thousand dollars.

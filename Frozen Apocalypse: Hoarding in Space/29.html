Chapter 28

Hotpot meatballs, lotus root slices, crunchy celtuce stems, enoki mushrooms, duck-blood curd and other hotpot ingredients. Two boxes of every kind of fruit. From the chilled section, she cleared out the yoghurt, cheese, cold noodles, ready-to-fry rice cakes, packaged sausages and everything else she could use.

Then, as if suddenly remembering something, Lila went to the seasonings aisle and asked an assistant for five hundred bags of brown sugar, two hundred jars of honey, five hundred packets of yeast, and one hundred tins of milk powder.

The sheer scale of her shopping stunned the staff. Even the manager came over and gave her a discount.

It was getting late, but Lila still insisted on driving the van back to Lockridge Storage Unit.

With a flicker of will, she stored the entire van inside the Vault.

Then, holding the two puppies, she got into the car she had left there in advance and drove back to the hotel.

Once she arrived, she wrapped the two puppies in clean towels and placed them in a cardboard box.

By now, the puppies had stopped crying. If their breathing had not been even and their little round bellies had not been rising and falling, Lila might have thought they were already dead.

She took a tub of goat's milk powder from the Vault and made a cup.

When the milk was warm, Lila picked up one of the half-damp puppies and used a straw to drip goat's milk beside its mouth.

One second earlier, the puppy had been fast asleep. The moment it smelled the rich scent of milk, it woke at once.

Its eyes were still closed, but its tiny head began nudging around, searching for the source of the smell.

Lila took the opportunity to slip the straw into its mouth, and the puppy swallowed eagerly.

The moment it tasted the milk, the straw was empty. Unable to get a second mouthful, the puppy began whining in distress.

Lila had no choice but to refill the straw, murmuring softly as she did.

"Coming, coming."

Only when she fed the milk into its mouth again did she manage to stop that noisy little mouth.

Feeding both puppies took more than forty minutes.

Watching the two little things nestle contentedly against the towel and fall asleep, Lila felt reassured too.

Although both puppies had been covered in mud, neither had any wounds. Judging by their condition, at worst they had been chilled through.

For tonight, she would leave things as they were. Tomorrow, she would take them for a check-up and hope they were both healthy.

Lila let out a long breath.

She could finally rest.

To avoid catching a cold, she took a hot shower.

After drying her hair, she lay down on the large bed, picked up her phone, and sent Maggie a message.

"Maggie, I'm coming back tomorrow. Let me treat everyone to dinner in the evening. Anna's always wanted to try that upmarket French restaurant, hasn't she? Tomorrow at six, bring Edward and Anna, and we'll all go together."

Annabel had wanted to eat at that restaurant for a long time. It cost around $2,000 per person, and her so-called friends often showed off about how many times their boyfriends had taken them there.

Annabel was not willing to pay for it herself, and Daniel was a calculating man who always put himself first. Even when Annabel had hinted about it several times, he had turned her down every time.

So Annabel had placed her last hope on Lila, hinting at it openly and indirectly more than once.

Now that Lila had taken the initiative to suggest that French restaurant, there was no way they would refuse.

Sure enough, less than three minutes after the message was sent, Maggie replied and happily agreed.

Early the next morning, Lila could feel that the temperature had dropped even further.

She checked the two puppies first. Both little things were awake, whimpering for milk.

Lila patiently fed them before putting on the thick coat she had prepared in advance and checking out of the hotel at speed.

Today's tasks were heavy.

First, she went to the farmers' market and bought fifteen chicks. Then she bought chicken fencing, cages, feed troughs and waterers.

Ten sacks of chicken feed, fifty sacks of maize meal, and fifty sacks of deep-litter bedding.

Apart from the fifteen chicks, which were packed in cardboard boxes and placed in Lila's car, everything else was delivered to her rented storage unit by the farmers' market lorry.

This was Lila's last trip to Lockridge Storage Unit.

She put everything that belonged to her into the Vault, then drove back into the city.

Her next destination was the pet shop.

She walked in with the two little mud-monkeys.

"Oh dear, how did these two get so filthy? They… they haven't even opened their eyes yet!"

The assistant looked carefully at the puppies. Logically, newborn puppies only a few days old should not have become this dirty.

Thinking of that, she could not help glancing at Lila in confusion.

Only then did Lila explain.

"I found them in the rain yesterday. Please check their health for me. If they're healthy, give them a bath too."

The assistant carefully carried the two puppies towards the vet's examination room.

Half an hour later, what came out with the examination report were two clean little milk-pups, their fur already dried.

Only then did Lila finally see their colouring clearly.

One was pure black, with a slender white mark in the middle of its forehead, like a little third eye.

The other was black and white. Its most distinctive feature was the dark fur around its eyes, with a little beauty spot beside its mouth.

"Well… they do have character."

That little beauty spot made Lila laugh.

"You're lucky. You picked up two very cute puppies, and they're both perfectly healthy."

The vet came out of the examination room and exchanged a few polite words with her.

"Thank you so much. It's been cold these last two days, so I didn't dare wash them myself."

Although the bath cost money, it had saved Lila a great deal of trouble.

"No need to thank us. Do you need any supplies?"

This was an excellent chance to make a sale.

"Yes. I need everything."

Lila was short on time, so of course she wanted to buy everything in one go.

The moment the vet heard that, her enthusiasm rose at once.

She introduced all the vaccines the puppies would need, deworming medicine, and medicines for common illnesses.

Lila bought them all, enough for the dogs' whole lives. She also bought a small dog crate, so it would be easier to take the puppies with her before returning home.

Seeing how generous Lila was, the vet began recommending pet insurance.

That was not necessary.

Lila waved her hands repeatedly and politely refused.

After leaving the pet shop, she noticed a hardware and household shop next door, with a craft shop beside it.

Without hesitation, Lila went in again.

At the hardware and household shop, she bought more useful tools and a collection of small bits and pieces, such as nails and fittings. They would all come in handy for daily life later.

At the craft shop, she bought a large amount of yarn, fabrics in different colours and patterns, as well as cotton wadding and foam, filling the car to the brim.

She turned the car into a blind spot away from the cameras.

One reach of her hand, and the packed car instantly became spacious again.

Checking the time, Lila saw that it was only two in the afternoon. There were still four hours before the time she had agreed with Maggie and the others.

Her stomach was a little hungry.

So, shamelessly, she chose another buffet restaurant.

All kinds of signature dishes and precious ingredients. She ate one portion and stored three. Her stomach was full, and so was the Vault.

After eating her fill, Lila was still in no hurry to leave.

She opened the surveillance feed on her phone.

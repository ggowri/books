Chapter 56

"You must be starving, sweetheart. Go on."

Anna took the bowl Maggie handed her and immediately lowered her head to eat.

Edward and Daniel came in as well.

Seeing that Maggie had not taken a single bite and had given everything to Anna, Edward's face remained cold. He sat back on his bedding and drank the porridge in his own bowl.

Daniel shrank into the corner by the door and hurriedly gulped down his food, afraid Maggie might ask him for some in a moment.

Halfway through eating, Anna frowned in disgust.

"It's so watery. There's no meat. It doesn't taste of anything."

"Just eat, sweetheart. Things will get better once we get through this," Maggie said, swallowing as she looked at the food in the bowl.

Anna complained, but still tipped her head back and finished every drop of porridge.

Maggie had been hungry all night, and her stomach was still empty now. Everyone in the room knew that perfectly well, but no one cared.

She silently took a shortbread biscuit from the box and had just lifted it to her mouth when Anna caught her wrist.

"Mum! The shortbread is meant to be shared! There are only three pieces left. How can you eat one all by yourself?"

Edward's face darkened too.

"Put it back. Save it for when we really need it."

Daniel looked tense.

"How are there only three left? Weren't there seven yesterday?"

Everyone fell silent.

No one answered.

Daniel felt a tightness in his chest. He was certain the three of them had eaten some behind his back.

Just then, Oakwood Estate Management's loudspeaker sounded outside again.

"Hot water will be supplied once a day at seven in the evening. Anyone who wants hot water should queue up."

This was undoubtedly good news.

Sophie set down the unfinished porridge in her hand and found a vacuum flask on the shelf, along with the mineral water bottle she had been using.

The flask could hold hot water for drinking at any time, and the mineral water bottle could be used as a hot-water bottle.

For her, access to hot-water distribution would raise her quality of life by a great deal.

At the same time, Lila had just finished lighting the inglenook fireplace and the heated hearth benches.

Her workload had increased today. In addition to the usual heated hearth bench in the east parlour, she also had to heat the west room once, specifically to provide warmth for the henhouse on the floor.

Once heated, the west room's hearth bench was warm too. Leaving it empty would be a waste.

That morning, Lila had white rice porridge with a packet of pickled mustard greens. It was simple, plain and light, and her stomach felt comfortable afterwards.

After washing the bowl, she put on her padded jacket and went to the main hall.

From the Vault, she took out six rectangular planters. She planned to grow vegetables today and place them on the heated hearth bench in the west room.

It would be warm there. The vegetables would grow well, and the heat from the bench would not be wasted.

But just as she was about to move on to the next step, Lila realised she had no soil.

Going outside was impossible. Leaving aside whether she could withstand the temperature outside, she could not possibly dig into ground frozen at minus seventy degrees.

There was no extra soil stored in the Vault either.

Lila sat in the chair in the main hall and thought for a moment. Then she suddenly remembered a good place.

For the first time, she put on her reindeer-hide boots, expedition down parka, and a very warm hat and gloves. Bundled up awkwardly, she climbed down into the cellar beside the west range.

The temperature in the cellar was similar to the kitchen, so she had not felt much yet.

Lila carefully opened the hidden door and climbed into Rock Hollow.

She had already prepared herself for the extreme cold, but to her surprise, the temperature inside the cave was actually quite comfortable.

Lila pulled off her mask at once and took several deep breaths of the cave air.

The mask was too thick and made it a little hard to breathe.

The air in Rock Hollow was not bad. There was no mouldy smell, only the damp, earthy scent of soil, carrying a natural freshness.

Judging by how it felt on her face, the temperature inside the cave was probably in the teens.

She walked deeper into the cave. As she drew close to the far end, the air grew colder and colder.

Yes, the rocks there had not been completely sealed off. Fresh cold air was flowing in, so the temperature near the end was lower.

Lila turned around without hesitation and went back to the innermost part of the cave.

She set her torch in a hollow in the rock, then took a shovel and the planters out of the Vault.

She chose the places where soil had gathered between the rocks and began digging. The soil here was damp and soft, clearly rich in nutrients. It would definitely be suitable for growing vegetables.

Unfortunately, Rock Hollow was mostly stone, with only a small amount of soil.

After a good deal of effort, she filled two planters to the brim with soil, put everything back into the Vault and climbed out through the cellar again.

After taking off the thick gear, Lila found a layer of sweat on her forehead, and even her hair was damp.

Since the temperature in Rock Hollow was suitable, she could go down there for a wander in future when she was bored.

Lila placed the soil she had dug up on the table in the main hall.

Two full planters of soil were divided evenly among six pots.

She found some seeds in the Vault and planted one pot of chives, one of coriander, one of spring onions, one of chrysanthemum greens, one of cherry tomatoes and one of strawberries.

After planting the seeds, she sprinkled a little water over them and carefully placed the pots on the heated hearth bench in the west room.

Lila looked forward to seeing new green life appear in the house.

Then she suddenly remembered that when she had cleared out the furniture from Flat 802, she seemed to have taken four flowerpots too.

She searched through the Vault and found them.

The pots were not huge, but they were certainly not small either. They were pure white, with carved patterns on the sides.

The flowers in them had long since withered, and the soil inside had dried out a little.

Lila pulled out the dead plants and placed them by the kitchen range. She could burn them directly when she lit the fire that evening.

Then she poured a little water into the remaining soil and loosened it with a small trowel. Before long, the soil was soft again.

She took out the flower seeds she had bought, choosing a few rose seeds and a few jasmine seeds.

Two pots would be roses. Two pots would be jasmine.

Roses were beautiful, and jasmine smelled wonderful.

More importantly, roses could be used for rose pastries, and jasmine could be used for tea.

Lila stared at the four bare pots of soil and almost found herself drooling.

She carried all four pots into the east parlour and placed them wherever there was a gap on the floor.

Then she decided that did not look pretty enough, so she picked out several stools from the Vault and placed the pots on top of them.

Once the flowers bloomed, she would be able to enjoy the view whether standing or sitting.

After washing the mud from her hands, she inspected both the east and west rooms again. Looking at the vegetable pots and flowerpots, she felt a deep sense of achievement.

Lila took the puppies out of their crate and placed them on the bare heated hearth bench.

At some point, both little creatures had opened their eyes.

As soon as they reached the bench and felt the warmth beneath their paws, Snowy and Patches were delighted.

Their four little legs still did not work very well, but they toddled about unsteadily, round eyes constantly looking here and there.

Once the novelty wore off, their attention shifted entirely to Lila.

They stopped wandering and kept trying to burrow against her.

Lila picked up Patches and touched its tiny pink, damp nose.

It was cool, smooth and soft, with a lovely feel.

But before she had stroked it more than twice, Patches stuck out a little tongue and licked Lila's hand.

"Oh, no licking. I'm only touching your nose."

Lila suddenly felt a little troubled by the puppies' enthusiasm. She could already imagine how lively these two would become once they grew a bit bigger.

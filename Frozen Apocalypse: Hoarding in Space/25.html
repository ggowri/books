Chapter 24

On 20 June, Lila had $456,000 left.

She woke early, closed her eyes, and entered the Vault first. Looking at the shelves packed full of supplies, she felt a rush of pure satisfaction.

Then she checked the balance on her phone.

Still more than $400,000.

By this point, all the essential stockpiling had been completed. The remaining money could be used to improve her safety and add a little more comfort.

Feeling pleased with herself, Lila pulled the thick quilt back over her and prepared to enjoy a second sleep.

Just then, her phone rang.

"Lila, Dad says he won't stay in Seabourne no matter what. We've booked flights home for tomorrow. Are you coming to pick us up?"

After reading the message, Lila felt a surge of irritation.

She had been hoping they might last in Seabourne until the Collapse arrived. Then they would never make it back in this lifetime.

But Edward had to ruin things.

Who exactly was waiting for him at home? Why was he so determined to come back?

Still, the situation was not too bad. After all, she had managed to keep them away for a full month. With no one getting in her way, she had already finished stockpiling all the essentials.

Only after getting her emotions under control did Lila reply.

"Oh no, I've just passed an interview for a new job recently, and I'm still in the probation period. I'm currently on a business trip in Bristol with my manager, so I won't be able to pick you up. Once I'm back, I'll treat you all properly to welcome you home, all right?"

It took a long while before the other side replied.

"Is everything fine at home?"

Although the profile picture was Annabel's, the tone of the message sounded exactly like Edward. It seemed Lila's excuse about the business trip had made him suspicious.

"Everything at home is fine. I'll be back in a week at most."

After Lila sent that, no more messages came through.

She counted the days in her head.

On 26 June, the sky would turn overcast and drizzle would begin. On 27 June, there would be a sudden temperature plunge. At seven that evening, the rain would turn to snow, and the Long Freeze would gradually descend. By 10 July, the temperature would reach its lowest point of minus seventy degrees.

In other words, a week from now would be exactly when the Long Freeze began.

That would be the best time to lure everyone away and strip the flat bare.

She could not do it yet.

If they came back tomorrow and found the flat empty, they would definitely call the police. Then the police would certainly contact her.

But after the 27th, there would be no need to worry about that.

By then, the authorities would be busy enough dealing with the extreme cold. Even if Edward and the others reported it, the police would have no time for such a small matter.

Once she had decided to return in a week, Lila packed a few simple belongings and booked a hotel far from Oakwood Estate.

She would stay there for the time being.

Sitting on the hotel bed, Lila booked a driver to bring the Luton van to the hotel's underground car park.

Then she called the site manager in charge of the mansion renovation.

Right now, the most urgent matter was the Lower Vale Mansion.

"Hello, Miss Spencer. I was just about to contact you," the manager said first.

Lila's heart lifted.

Could it be that the house had just been finished today?

"Is the renovation complete?"

The manager gave an awkward laugh.

"Almost. We expect to finish in three days. At that point, we'll need you to come over and inspect the work. If there are no issues, we'll be returning to Germany."

Three days.

That was fine. There was more than enough time.

The two of them agreed to meet at the Lower Vale Mansion at two in the afternoon, three days later.

Once she had confirmed that the driver had brought the van to the hotel's underground car park, Lila headed straight to the shopping centre.

One laptop, two tablets and five mobile phones cost $80,000 altogether.

The price was high, but she bought models with long lifespans and strong performance.

In the long, dull days after the Collapse, she would be relying on them to pass the time.

She also bought four excellent pinhole cameras, which cost another $40,000.

Without pausing, Lila placed all the expensive items into the Vault, then drove back home.

Following the instructions the salesperson had given her, she installed the pinhole cameras inside the smoke alarms.

Smoke alarms already had red indicator lights. With pinhole cameras hidden inside, no one would notice anything unusual.

Once that was done, Lila quickly left.

She was not especially tired today. After lunch, she went to Ashton Flea Market, the most famous second-hand market in the area.

The market was enormous and accepted almost anything. As long as someone wanted to sell something, they could set up a stall there.

In the middle of the market, there was a section dedicated to second-hand books. Lila had no idea where these old books had come from, but they were extremely cheap, usually $1 to $5 each.

She stopped in front of one stall.

The seller was a boy of about seventeen or eighteen, head lowered as he played a game on his phone. When he noticed a customer, he looked up and grinned.

"Pick whatever you like. Left side is $1, middle is $2, right side is $3."

After saying that, he lowered his head again and threw himself back into his game.

Lila crouched down and carefully looked through the titles.

The books were priced by thickness, not by category.

Novels, yes.

Gardening books, yes.

Craft books, yes.

Medical books, yes.

After looking for a long while, Lila found that she wanted most of them.

Practical books could teach survival skills. Entertainment books could help pass the long, boring hours.

Psychology… no.

Success manuals… no need for those either.

She was a homebody whose grand ambition was to stockpile supplies and ride out the Collapse in comfort. What did she need success manuals for?

So Lila picked out all the psychology books, success books and business books. There were more than a dozen in total.

The boy finished a round of his game. Seeing the stack Lila had picked out, his eyes filled with excitement.

"You're taking all of these?"

"No. I don't want these. I'll take everything except them."

When she first said she did not want them, the boy looked disappointed.

The moment she said she wanted everything else, he looked as if he might fly from sheer excitement.

"Everything? You even want this one, Postnatal Care for Sows?"

The boy was clearly surprised.

Lila smiled and nodded.

"Yes, all of it. Work out the price for me, then help me carry the books to my car."

There were more than a hundred books. The boy charged $300, then happily helped Lila load all the second-hand books into the car.

Lila returned to the market once more and chose over a hundred more books from other stalls, most of them practical manuals and novels.

With around three hundred books in total, she would definitely have enough to read.

Ashton Flea Market was simply too cheap. Even after buying the books, Lila still wanted to keep browsing.

And with that casual wander, she found something else she wanted.

One of the stalls had solar panels.

Although the renovation at the Lower Vale Mansion already included power generation, what stockpiler preparing for the Collapse could resist solar panels?

They would be useless during the Long Freeze, but if the Freeze ever passed, they would definitely come in handy.

"How much are your solar panels?"

Lila stepped closer to the stall.

"These aren't solar panels. This is a portable solar power station. It has a large battery pack at the back. You can charge phones with it, cook with it, and even keep a television running."

The stallholder hated it when people mistook his portable solar power stations for ordinary solar panels.

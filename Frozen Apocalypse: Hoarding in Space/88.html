Chapter 87

Lila tightened her grip on the card and nodded.

"I understand."

What she didn't say was that Captain Walker had no idea what she was capable of.

Walker gave a final nod.

"All right. Stay safe."

He turned and waved before starting back down the snowy path.

The drifts were so deep that every step looked like a struggle. He had to pull each leg free before sinking into the next patch of snow.

Watching him, Lila suddenly felt a little sympathy.

These soldiers weren't exactly having an easy time either.

She raised her voice.

"Thank you, Captain. You stay safe too!"

Walker waved again without turning around.

The layers he wore made even that simple gesture awkward.

Soon he and his companion disappeared into the white landscape, shrinking into two distant black dots.

A gust of freezing wind sliced across Lila's face.

She quickly locked the gate and hurried back inside.

After removing her outer layers, she dropped onto the sofa and stared at the card in her hand.

Go to the refuge and collect supplies?

She didn't need supplies.

Not even remotely.

The problem was that the arrival of survivors was no longer speculation.

It was happening.

If everyone knew there was a household up on the mountain, yet that household never collected government rations, people would start asking questions.

And questions led to attention.

Attention led to trouble.

Lila groaned and flopped backward into the cushions.

This was exhausting.

Meanwhile, at Oakwood Estate, Mrs. Harris was supervising lunch preparations on the ground floor.

Suddenly, a distant rumble echoed through the air.

Her eyes lit up immediately.

Supplies.

That had to be supplies.

She hurriedly looked toward the source of the sound.

A helicopter was approaching from afar.

Something seemed to be drifting from its cargo bay, fluttering through the sky.

The distance made it impossible to see clearly.

"Look!"

Mrs. Harris pointed.

"What is that?"

The three people helping her cooking followed her gaze.

"Supply drop?"

"Wait... those don't look like supplies."

"Looks more like paper."

The helicopter continued approaching.

Eventually they could see clearly.

Sheets of paper.

Thousands of them.

"It's flyers!" one of the survivors shouted.

Mrs. Harris felt her heart sink.

She'd hoped for food.

Still, flyers were better than nothing.

The satellite phone had stopped working long ago.

Communication with higher authorities had been impossible.

Deep down, she'd begun wondering whether they'd been forgotten.

The helicopter's appearance answered that question.

Someone still remembered them.

That meant there was still hope.

Mrs. Harris waved her walking stick overhead.

"We're here!"

"There are survivors here!"

Whether the helicopter crew saw them or not, it never landed.

Instead, it circled overhead.

The massive rotors kicked up clouds of snow.

A figure leaned halfway out of the aircraft and threw bundles of flyers into the wind.

The papers scattered across the gray sky like oversized snowflakes.

Mrs. Harris hurried forward and caught one before it blew away.

Bold black lettering covered the page.

It read:

"To all survivors:

The government and military have completed a Permanent Refuge at Lower Vale.

The refuge is now open to surrounding survivors.

Food, shelter, heating, and basic necessities are available.

Please share this information with anyone you encounter and travel to the refuge independently.

Over the next five days, temperatures are expected to rise temporarily, averaging approximately minus sixty five degrees Celsius.

This period is considered an optimal Travel Window.

See the map and information below for details."

Beneath the text was a hand drawn map.

Simple.

Easy to follow.

Mrs. Harris narrowed her eyes.

Their current region was marked with a thick black circle.

Roughly eighty kilometers southeast sat another circle labeled:

Lower Vale.

Additional information appeared beneath the map.

Reasons for selecting Lower Vale as the refuge location:

Proximity to Strategic Reserve Depot No. 7, providing access to emergency food supplies.

Surrounded by mountains containing extensive Regrowth Forest areas. Deadwood and brush are available as fuel sources.

Large River Flats near the village offer space for expansion and shelter construction.

Natural mountain barriers create a warmer valley environment. Recorded temperatures average five to eight degrees Celsius higher than surrounding open terrain.

Access to an Underground Spring Channel that remains partially unfrozen, ensuring a reliable water source.

"Food..."

"Fuel..."

"And it's warmer there?"

One of the survivors stared at the flyer.

His hand trembled.

The sheet of paper suddenly felt heavier than stone.

Mrs. Harris finished reading and slipped the flyer into her pocket.

Her expression remained troubled.

"Pick up as many as you can."

"We'll discuss them after lunch."

The group gathered more flyers before carrying the finished pot of porridge back underground.

As always, food came first.

Only after everyone had eaten did Mrs. Harris distribute the flyers.

Every survivor received one.

Then she asked the question.

"The government built a refuge in Lower Vale."

"They have food."

"Fuel."

"Heated shelter."

"Do you want to go?"

The reaction was immediate.

Eyes lit up across the room.

Several people answered before even reading the flyer.

"Of course we go."

"What is there to think about?"

"Finally."

"Maybe things are turning around."

Mrs. Harris rubbed her face.

Then she delivered the difficult part.

"We have to get there ourselves."

"The refuge is eighty kilometers away."

Silence.

Then chaos.

"What?!"

"Walk?"

"In this weather?"

"Eighty kilometers?"

"We'll freeze before we get halfway there!"

The room exploded with panic.

Nobody needed to explain the problem.

Walking eighty kilometers through temperatures approaching minus eighty degrees sounded suicidal.

Mrs. Harris raised her voice.

"That's exactly why I'm not making the decision for anyone."

"You all decide for yourselves."

Annabel looked down at her injured leg.

Panic flashed across her face.

No.

They couldn't leave.

If everyone left, who would take care of her?

She quickly spoke up.

"Everyone, think about this."

"We have a shelter."

"We're safe for now."

"If we start walking out there, we'll die."

Several uncertain survivors nodded.

Maggie immediately joined in.

If they actually attempted the journey, she'd be the one carrying Annabel.

The thought alone made her stomach churn.

"She's right."

"We're alive right now."

"Out there, who knows what'll happen?"

Mother and daughter worked together, planting doubt throughout the room.

For a moment, it seemed to be working.

Then Sophie stood.

"Don't listen to them."

Her sharp gaze swept across the crowd.

"The government built a refuge."

"That means supplies won't keep coming here forever."

"If we stay, we'll eventually run out of food."

"We'll starve."

"We'll freeze."

She paused.

"But if we make it to the refuge, we're under government protection."

"There will be food."

"There will be soldiers."

"There will be a future."

The room fell silent.

Everyone understood the truth.

Surviving the Collapse wasn't just about food anymore.

It was about choosing which risk offered a chance to live.

Chapter 36

Lila took a little firewood and smokeless coal from the Vault and first lit the kitchen range and the fireplace.

The temperature outside would keep falling. Keeping the house warm mattered most.

Once that was done, she swept the floor clean and went to the bathroom to wash.

The bathroom was too far from the heated hearth bench and the fireplace to catch much warmth, so the temperature inside was noticeably lower. The moment Lila stepped in, she shivered.

Luckily, the construction crew had insulated all the water pipes. Otherwise, she would have been worried they might freeze and burst.

After letting the tap run for a while, hot water did come through. It was the water heated by the fireplace the night before.

After a quick wash, she returned to the east parlour. By then, the room had fully warmed up.

The indoor temperature showed twenty-four degrees.

Lovely and warm.

Just then, soft whining came from the dog cage.

The two puppies were awake and hungry again, searching for milk.

Lila took the kettle from the fireplace hob and made a cup of warm goat's milk formula. Then she carried the dog cage over, sat on the edge of the heated hearth bench and fed the two puppies.

Their eyes were already half open, though still cloudy and unfocused.

Lila was in no hurry. The days ahead would be slow and easy, spent riding out the Collapse in comfort. She had all the time in the world to wait.

Still, it was time to give the puppies names.

Both were little males.

The bigger one was black all over, with a small white spot on his forehead. Lila decided, rather casually, that he would be the elder brother. His name would be Snowy.

The smaller one was black and white, with irregular markings all over, so he would be Patches.

"From now on, you're Snowy, the big brother, and you're Patches, the little brother. You must be good brothers, love each other, and of course, be loyal to your owner. Me."

After saying that, she gently tapped each of their tiny foreheads.

When Snowy was tapped, he actually let out a decent little howl. Patches was stirred up as well and started whining loudly after him, though his call was not quite as impressive as Snowy's.

"Oh, not bad at all. Looks like you might grow up to guard the house properly."

Lila laughed. Since her second life had begun, this was the most relaxed she had felt.

After playing with the puppies for a while, she chose a piece of soft cotton cloth from the Vault and laid it over the cushion inside the cage. Then she put them back.

With the bedding dry and soft, they would be much more comfortable.

Their eyes had not fully opened yet, so letting them roam freely would be risky. Once they were a little bigger, they could leave the cage.

The chicks were far easier to look after. She simply added water and millet, and that was enough.

At last, it was her turn.

Time for breakfast.

The house was not sorted yet, so cooking properly would be inconvenient. Lila simply took two buns and a carton of milk from the Vault and ate them while they were still steaming hot.

Breakfast was a little dull, so she opened the camera feed again.

The four sleeping figures were finally showing signs of waking.

Annabel woke because of the cold.

In her dream, the tip of her nose had grown more and more painful, and her ears had gone icy. In the end, she could not keep sleeping and bolted upright.

It was around nine in the morning. Daylight filled the empty flat, making it look glaringly bright.

Annabel stared blankly at the bare home for a moment before remembering what had happened there yesterday.

"Stop sleeping. Get up. Dad! Mum! Daniel!"

The flat felt even colder than it had the night before. Annabel hurriedly shoved the people around her, shaking everyone awake.

"What? What is it?"

Maggie opened her eyes in a daze, her voice thick and nasal.

She had caught a cold overnight.

Now her head felt heavy and dizzy, her throat was sore, and every part of her felt unwell.

Edward and Daniel were not much better. Both of them looked utterly miserable.

"Didn't we call the police yesterday? Have they found anyone yet? They need to bring Lila back and make her return our things!"

Annabel was frantic.

Edward shook his head, waited until he felt a little more awake, then said grimly, "I'll call and ask now."

He dialled the police, but the line was busy.

Edward called six times in a row before someone finally answered.

"Hello, my name is Edward Spencer. I reported a missing person yesterday. Has there been any progress?"

"We'll deal with missing person reports later. The station is overwhelmed right now. Please don't keep calling unless it's urgent."

The officer spoke sternly, then hung up and went back to work.

Today was the busiest day the police station had ever seen.

There had been multiple traffic accidents the night before, and many officers had been sent out to handle them.

By daylight, crowds had flooded into superstores and shopping centres, fighting over food and winter clothes. Everywhere was chaotic, with arguments, pushing and clashes breaking out constantly. Police were needed all over the city to maintain order.

Who had the time or energy to deal with a missing person report?

"How dare they hang up on me? Is this how the police work now? When things calm down, I'm filing a complaint."

Edward glared at his dark phone screen and vented furiously.

"The police won't do anything?" Maggie asked in disbelief.

She had never imagined the police would ignore something this serious.

Edward refused to give up and called Oakwood Estate Management instead.

"Our flat was burgled last night. The person who stole everything is another resident you know, Lila Spencer. Check the cameras immediately and see when she came here yesterday."

"May I ask if you are the resident of Flat 802?" Oakwood Estate Management asked.

"Yes. Hurry up and check," Edward snapped, finding the question utterly unnecessary.

"I'm sorry, but the owner of Flat 802 is Lila Spencer. Removing items from her own property is lawful. We have no right to interfere, nor can we help outsiders investigate her."

Oakwood Estate Management served the owners. Since the property certificate was in Lila's name, naturally they would not help outsiders.

Even if the matter ended up in court one day, Lila would still be in the right.

"You..."

Edward was so angry that, for a moment, he did not know what to say.

"Enough, Dad. Hang up. We've got something more important to deal with."

Annabel was reading through the Oakwood residents' group chat, and the more she read, the colder her heart became. She urged Edward to end the call.

"Loads of people have gone out to grab supplies. The superstores are nearly empty. Quick, we need to go too."

In a panic, Annabel started putting on her shoes. They had woken up far too late, and she was terrified there would be nothing left to buy.

"It's freezing outside. What are we supposed to wear? We need to borrow clothes from the neighbours first."

Maggie stopped Annabel, afraid her daughter would rush out recklessly and freeze herself half to death.

Wrapped in a duvet, Maggie knocked on more than a dozen doors before finally managing to buy four padded jackets.

They were ordinary short padded jackets, neither too big nor too small, and each one cost two thousand pounds.

If she had tried to borrow them for free, no one would have agreed. Maggie had no choice but to beg people to sell.

"Your dad and I will go and buy food. You and Daniel go and buy warm clothes."

Maggie handed each of them a padded jacket, then prepared to go out with her legs still bare.

The neighbours had only been willing to sell jackets. No one would part with trousers or padded trousers no matter how much she asked.

"I... Maybe I should go home first. I don't want to hold you back."

After saying that, Daniel wrapped himself in the padded jacket and fled in a panic.

Chapter 105

"Everyone, please gather as quickly as possible. The meeting will begin in five minutes. If you haven't arrived yet, pick up the pace."

The loudspeaker echoed across the refuge once again.

The crowd immediately sped up.

Without realizing it, Lila quickened her own pace as well.

Annabel, who had originally come partly out of curiosity, was trying to push through the crowd when someone bumped into her.

She was already limping.

The collision sent a sharp burst of pain through her injured leg.

"Ouch! Are you blind?"

Annabel snapped, clutching her leg.

Maggie hurried over to help her up.

"Anna, are you hurt? Let me see."

She reached toward the injured leg.

Annabel immediately shook her off.

"Stop fussing around. The meeting's about to start."

She glanced toward the dining hall.

"If there are free supplies, they'll all be gone before we get there."

Ignoring her mother's concern, she limped forward as quickly as she could.

The moment Lila entered the dining hall, she once again became the center of attention.

She never intended to attract so much notice.

The helmet felt a little excessive indoors, so she removed it.

The hall wasn't particularly cold, so the mink hat came off as well.

For the first time, everyone got a clear look at her face.

The room grew briefly quieter.

She was young.

Beautiful.

Her features were delicate and clean.

Yet after the initial surprise, most people looked away.

Mystery always drew more attention than beauty.

What lingered in people's minds wasn't her appearance.

It was her hair.

Long.

Dark.

Healthy.

Tied neatly behind her head.

Many women her age couldn't help feeling a twinge of envy.

In the Long Freeze, most families barely had enough hot water to drink.

Yet somehow her hair remained soft, clean, and full of life.

A moment later, Captain Walker entered the hall behind her.

His eyes briefly landed on the young woman standing quietly in the corner.

Her expression remained calm and unreadable.

Even so, she was impossible to ignore.

After a brief pause, he strode toward the front platform.

At exactly eight o'clock, the meeting began.

Whether everyone had arrived or not didn't matter.

Captain Walker spoke first.

"Welcome to Lower Vale Refuge."

His voice carried effortlessly through the room.

"From today onward, we're all part of the same community."

He didn't waste time with formalities.

"Let's get straight to the important part."

His gaze swept across the crowd.

"I'm going to explain the rules of this refuge."

"Remember them."

"Your survival depends on it."

Walker stood well over six feet tall.

Simply standing there created a sense of pressure.

His expression was stern.

His eyes cold.

The noisy hall fell silent almost instantly.

Everyone listened.

Even Annabel.

The moment she'd seen him step onto the platform, her heart had practically melted.

She found herself staring.

Unable to look away.

Somewhere deep down, she thought she might be falling for him.

Walker continued.

"Rule One. The Work Credit System."

"Lower Vale Refuge does not support freeloaders."

"Everything required for survival is exchanged through work credits."

"Housing."

"Meals."

"Firewood."

"Oil lamps."

"Additional supplies."

"All of it requires work credits."

"There are many ways to earn them."

"You'll find job listings available after the meeting."

"I also lead regular supply recovery missions into Ashton. Recovered materials can be exchanged for credits."

"If you possess useful skills such as heating repair, medical training, vehicle maintenance, or similar expertise, you may apply for technical positions."

"Those jobs pay significantly better."

"Individuals who make exceptional contributions to the refuge may also receive bonus credits after community review."

He paused briefly.

"Rule Two. Security."

"This refuge is not lawless territory."

"In times like these, human threats are often more dangerous than the weather."

"Theft, violence, intimidation, and seizure of another person's property are strictly forbidden."

His voice hardened noticeably.

"Stealing food or heating supplies is considered a major offense."

"If you're caught, punishment depends on the severity."

"Minor violations result in credit deductions and mandatory cleaning duty in the communal latrine."

"Serious violations result in confiscation of winter gear and expulsion from the refuge."

He looked directly at the crowd.

"Remember this."

"Expulsion is a death sentence."

The room remained silent.

Nobody doubted him.

"Rule Three. Resource Distribution."

"In addition to supplies purchased with work credits, the refuge will provide weekly survival allocations."

"These distributions exist to ensure basic survival."

"Everyone receives the same amount."

"There will be no exceptions."

"Rule Four. Community Duties."

"The refuge belongs to all of us."

"Everyone must contribute."

"Except for the elderly, children, and those physically unable to work, every adult is required to participate in rotating public duties."

"Night fire watch."

"Snow removal."

"Security patrols."

"If you fail to report for an assigned shift and your absence causes damage or danger, the maximum penalty is expulsion."

"Rule Five. Emergency Mobilization."

"If we face severe blizzards, animal attacks, or any major crisis, the refuge will enter emergency status."

"During emergencies, all residents must follow centralized command."

"Rule Six. Information and Job Announcements."

"Each morning, today's available jobs and work credit rates will be posted on the dining hall notice board."

"You may sign up voluntarily."

Walker folded his arms.

"Those are the current rules."

"If you forget them, ask someone."

Then his voice turned even colder.

"One last thing."

"Here, work credits are life."

"Rules are life."

"Follow the rules."

"Do your share."

"You'll survive."

"Try to cheat the system or hurt others..."

He let the silence linger.

"...and you can go freeze to death outside."

"Understood?"

The six rules were written clearly on the large blackboard behind him.

As people listened and read, most managed to grasp the basics.

His final words hit the room like a hammer.

One after another, people nodded frantically.

"Understood."

"Yes, sir."

"We understand."

Voices echoed throughout the hall.

Walker appeared satisfied.

He nodded and returned to his seat.

Although he held enormous authority within the refuge, his primary responsibilities involved security and resource management.

Today's lengthy speech had actually been a favor for someone else.

The refuge director believed Walker's presence and reputation would help bring order to a population gathered from every corner of the region.

Once Walker sat down, another man stepped forward.

He cleared his throat.

"My name is William."

"I am the director of Lower Vale Refuge."

"Captain Walker handles security and enforcement."

"I oversee daily operations and administration."

He smiled faintly.

"I trust everyone now understands the rules."

"We expect full compliance."

"There is one additional matter."

William gestured toward a stack of papers nearby.

"The refuge currently has numerous open positions."

"We will be selecting qualified candidates from among the residents."

"Employees working directly for the refuge will receive regular work credit salaries and may be exempt from duties such as patrol rotations."

"After the meeting, you may review the available positions at the front desk."

"Salary information and requirements are included."

"Applications can then be submitted through my two secretaries."

"Candidates will be selected based on suitability."

Once both leaders finished speaking, the weekly relief distribution began.

This week's allocation was simple.

Half a pound of grains and pulses per person.

As of today, Lower Vale Refuge housed 937 relocated survivors.

Including Lila and the other two local households, the total population stood at 943.

Residents lined up at the serving windows to collect their supplies.

After receiving their ration, each person had to exit through a separate doorway.

No one was allowed to join the queue again.

Lila took one look at the crowd and decided she wasn't interested in fighting through it.

There were simply too many people.

Instead, she remained where she was.

She could collect her ration after things quieted down.

On the meeting platform sat two stacks of documents.

One contained detailed job openings throughout the refuge, including monthly salaries in work credits and the qualifications required for each position.

Chapter 103

When Sophie woke up, the heated bed was still warm.

The room, however, was freezing.

She quickly pulled on her thick winter clothes.

The first thing she did was feed firewood into the kitchen range, letting the cast iron stove slowly warm the room.

Then she wrapped herself in a quilt and sat at the edge of the heated bed.

Only after the chill finally faded from the air did she feel fully alive again.

Her eyes drifted toward the enormous sled pack sitting on the floor.

Time to unpack.

Compared to everyone else, Sophie had brought practically everything.

Toothbrushes.

Wash basins.

Soap.

A shoe brush.

Scissors.

Needles and thread.

She had even packed several books to pass the time.

Most people had come to the refuge as desperate survivors.

Sophie had arrived prepared to build a life.

She stored her food at the back of the lockers and placed clothes and bedding in front.

The eight lockers suited her perfectly.

She left the doors of the upper three open.

The rest remained shut.

Inside were neatly arranged vacuum flasks, towels, a comb, a toothbrush, cleanser, and other daily necessities.

One locker held only family photographs and books.

There were just five books, but they mattered.

She had also brought her own bowls and utensils, which she carefully placed in the kitchen cupboards.

Once everything was organized, her attention shifted to the two filthy quilts lying on the floor.

Like Leo, she came to the same conclusion.

They'd make good insulation.

She stripped off the stained quilt covers before hanging the quilts over the front door and bedroom entrance.

The covers themselves weren't thrown away.

They only had a few tears.

After washing and mending them, they'd still be useful.

Meanwhile, unpacking was much easier for Annabel's family.

Mainly because they owned almost nothing.

Aside from the small amount of food Mrs. Harris had distributed before departure, nearly everything else was gone.

The only possessions they really had left were the quilts covering Annabel.

Annabel remained sprawled comfortably at the warmest end of the heated bed.

Edward showed no interest in helping either.

Maggie lit the stove and folded several quilts into the lockers.

Otherwise, most of the storage space would've remained empty.

Just then, the loudspeaker outside crackled to life again.

"Residents, please proceed to the dining hall for breakfast. Residents, please proceed to the dining hall for breakfast."

The moment they heard the word breakfast, both Annabel and Edward brightened instantly.

Annabel sat upright and hurriedly put on her shoes.

Each member of the family grabbed a bowl and headed for the dining hall.

As Annabel limped along, Maggie looked worried.

"Anna, are you sure your leg can handle it?"

"My leg's basically fine already."

Annabel sounded irritated.

She immediately quickened her uneven pace.

Daniel was waiting outside.

The moment he spotted her, he rushed over.

"Come on, Anna. We'd better hurry. Looks like half the refuge is already heading there."

The two walked off together.

Edward saw every second of it.

His expression darkened immediately.

Turning toward Maggie, he snapped, "Why are those two together again?"

"What? I... I don't know."

Maggie panicked.

She knew a little about what was going on.

Not much.

And besides, if her daughter liked Daniel, why interfere?

"Idiots. All of you."

Edward stormed off toward the dining hall without waiting for her.

This was everyone's first visit to the cafeteria.

The moment the announcement ended, survivors poured out of their homes.

The road quickly filled with people.

At first they walked.

Then they worried about being left behind.

The pace increased.

Someone started jogging.

Soon everyone was running.

Leo and Callum were no exception.

Seeing the crowd surge forward, they grabbed their bowls and joined the stampede.

Callum was already out of breath.

"You literally just ate. Why are you running like your life depends on it?"

"Because whatever I don't eat today might help me tomorrow."

Leo didn't even slow down.

His eyes were fixed firmly on the prize.

Food.

Sophie moved even faster.

The loudspeaker had barely begun speaking before she understood exactly what it meant.

She grabbed her bowl and sprinted out the door.

By the time most people arrived, she was already inside the dining hall.

The cafeteria was enormous.

Wide.

Open.

The ceiling wasn't particularly high, though.

Six serving stations lined one side of the room.

Every station served exactly the same meal.

Two dishes and one staple.

The rest of the hall was filled with tables.

Around one hundred and fifty of them.

Enough seating space for well over a thousand people.

Curiously, there were no chairs.

Probably a space-saving decision.

Or maybe they simply hadn't wanted to transport thousands of chairs through the Long Freeze.

Sophie studied the room while quietly making guesses.

She joined the queue at Serving Window Two.

A few moments later, it was her turn.

Today, soldiers were serving the food.

Until permanent work assignments were arranged, the military was handling almost everything.

Breakfast consisted of sweet potato and brown rice porridge along with stewed cabbage and radish.

A soldier scooped half a ladle of porridge into her bowl.

Then another half ladle of vegetables.

Together it barely filled a small bowl.

"Thank you."

Sophie accepted it with a smile.

She found a table in a quiet corner and immediately began eating.

Taking it home wasn't worth the risk.

The cold would freeze it solid before she arrived.

Besides, carrying food around openly wasn't exactly smart.

Desperate people noticed things.

Fortunately, she'd woken up late and hadn't eaten breakfast yet.

The sweet potatoes were soft and slightly sweet.

The porridge was hot.

Honestly, it wasn't bad.

The cabbage and radish were another story.

Apart from salt, they tasted like absolutely nothing.

Still, this was the Long Freeze.

Nobody was in a position to complain.

Sophie scraped the bowl clean.

Not a drop remained.

When she looked back toward the serving area, she almost laughed.

The line had become enormous.

It stretched from the serving windows to the entrance, then curled back through the dining hall in long winding rows.

Most of the people inside weren't eating.

They were waiting.

Sophie silently congratulated herself for arriving early.

Carrying her empty bowl, she headed for the exit.

"Excuse me. Coming through."

She politely addressed the people blocking the doorway.

No response.

She looked up.

Then frowned.

Daniel and Annabel.

Again.

Why were they still glued together?

Annabel raised an eyebrow.

"What now?"

Her tone dripped with smugness.

"Trying to come between us again?"

In Annabel's mind, Sophie wanting to squeeze between them in a crowded line somehow translated into romantic sabotage.

After all this time, Daniel had still chosen her.

That meant she'd beaten Sophie.

At least that's how she saw it.

Sophie nearly laughed.

"Not only am I coming between the two of you."

She smiled sweetly.

"I'm about to come between your parents too."

Before either Annabel or Edward could react, Sophie slipped directly between them and pushed her way through the crowd.

For a second, Annabel was speechless.

Then rage exploded across her face.

She stomped her injured leg instinctively.

Pain shot through it immediately.

Unfortunately, she couldn't leave the queue to argue.

All she could do was stand there and fume.

Daniel took the opportunity to move closer.

"Don't let her get to you."

His voice softened.

"If she hadn't kept throwing herself at me back then, none of this misunderstanding would've happened."

He looked at Annabel with what he thought was sincerity.

"You trust me, right?"

Annabel's anger instantly melted away.

She nodded shyly.

Of course she trusted him.

In her mind, she and Daniel were destined to be together forever.

Meanwhile, far away at the Lower Vale Mansion, Lila was only just getting out of bed.

After feeding the pigs and dogs, she lit both the fireplace and the kitchen range.

A steaming bowl of cabbage and shrimp dumpling soup soon sat before her.

The warmth spread through her entire body.

Comfortable.

Peaceful.

Safe.

While she enjoyed breakfast, she glanced toward the mountain below.

Judging by the time, most of the survivors should have reached the refuge by now.

Chapter 22

"Yes, yes, I've got some. You just need pine needles and pinecones for kindling, don't you?"

The old man nodded at once.

Dry pine needles and pinecones burned best. They caught easily and did not give off much smoke.

"That's right. I'll take everything you have."

The old man pointed towards the sacks stacked along the wall.

"Those are pine needles I brought back from the hills, mixed with pinecones. $5 a sack. I should have more than thirty sacks here."

"Good. I'll take them all."

The old man was naturally delighted to hear Lila wanted the lot.

Fewer and fewer people lived on the outskirts now, and fewer still bought firewood. A few years ago, business had still been manageable. These days, selling firewood barely kept food on the table. Making a little over a hundred today meant he could add something extra to his granddaughter's dinner.

There were thirty-six sacks of dry pine needles in total. Lila paid $180, and the two of them loaded the sacks onto the Luton van one by one.

Once they had finished, the old man insisted on stuffing two bundles of firewood into her van.

Lila tried to refuse several times, but he pushed them in anyway.

"You're buying so much, love. These are from me. Take them."

Lila's original plan had been to buy the kindling, then find a furniture works and purchase scrap wood to use as fuel. Later, she could also use some of it for crafts.

But since the old man was so warm-hearted, Lila bought half the bundled firewood stacked along the wall too.

The firewood was $8 a bundle. Lila bought fifty bundles.

After the wood had been loaded, she paid again.

"I'll come back to buy more in a while. Could you collect some more pine needles and firewood from the hills for me?"

The Long Freeze was coming soon. Lila did not want to buy up all the firewood in the old man's house and leave him and his granddaughter with nothing to burn.

"Of course, of course. I'll go tomorrow."

Lila had spent $580 with him today. For the old man and his granddaughter, that was a huge sum.

$580 could buy the child pencils, notebooks and sweets. If he was feeling extravagant, he could even buy a whole crate of milk.

The old man's head was full of thoughts about buying his granddaughter something nice to eat. It did not occur to him how many long hours he had spent collecting the firewood he had just sold.

Only then did his little granddaughter open the door and slip out of the house.

Her bright eyes blinked at Lila.

"Thank you. Now we can buy rice."

Recently, the old man's income had been so poor that the rice and flour at home had to be bought by the pound. With today's money, they could finally afford a whole sack of rice.

The sight made Lila's chest ache.

After a pause, she took another $500 in cash from her pocket.

"Take this as a deposit for next time. The weather hasn't been good lately. Since you've made some money, buy more rice and keep it at home."

She had exchanged this cash earlier in case of emergencies. She had not expected it to come in useful so soon.

"No, no, love. There's no need for that. You can pay me when you come next time."

The old man had never taken a deposit for firewood before. He felt he ought not to accept it.

"Please take it. Cut a bit more wood for me. I'm not sure when I'll be able to come back. If it rains, keep my firewood indoors for me. I'm worried it'll go mouldy outside."

Lila had a reason for saying this.

Everyone nearby knew his family sold firewood. Once the Long Freeze arrived, people would definitely come to snatch it. If the wood was kept indoors, the chance of it being stolen would at least be a little lower.

After saying that, she left amid the old man and his granddaughter's repeated thanks.

That was all she could do for them.

The rest would depend on their own fate.

Lila put the newly bought firewood and kindling into the Vault, then drove to the back entrance of the furniture works.

Outside the back gate, piles of scrap wood and offcuts lay everywhere.

One heap here, another there. There was more than enough.

This was something she had researched online earlier.

People on the forums said furniture works often sold scrap wood very cheaply.

It could be used for carving, DIY furniture, or simply burned as firewood.

The only thing was, you had to make it worth the gatekeeper's while.

Lila was standing in front of the woodpiles, lost in thought, when a man's shout rang out behind her.

"Oi, what are you doing?"

His voice was loud enough to startle her.

An older man in a worn sleeveless vest was standing there with a toothpick in his mouth, as if he had just finished eating.

Lila glanced behind him.

Inside the back gate stood a shabby little booth, probably where the gatekeeper stayed.

Which meant it belonged to the man in front of her.

"I'd like to buy some wood offcuts. How do you sell these?"

As Lila spoke, she walked forward quickly and smoothly pressed two packets of premium cigarettes into his hand.

The old man looked down. His eyes lit up.

Now this was good stuff. For him, these were proper decent cigarettes.

"We don't usually sell these. But since you're a sensible young woman, $300 a vanload. Take what you can carry."

He spat out the toothpick and swiftly tucked the cigarettes into his trouser pocket.

What he said was exactly the same as what the people online had described.

With permission secured, Lila quickly thanked him.

"Thank you. Where do I pay?"

The old man took out his smartphone, then fished a pair of reading glasses from his pocket and put them on.

He tapped at the screen with one finger for a while before finally showing her a payment code.

"Scan this. Work out how many loads you want and pay the amount yourself."

Lila did not hold back. She looked over the heaps of wood in front of her and transferred $1,800.

The old man's phone immediately announced in a loud voice, "Mobile payment received, $1,800."

"What? You want that much?"

The gatekeeper had not expected this young woman to buy so much in one go.

If he had known, he would not have let her pay into his private account.

Fortunately, the timber yard belonged to his nephew. Otherwise, being found out would cost him his job.

"Is there a problem?" Lila asked, feeling that his reaction was a little too strong.

"No, no. Go ahead and load it. There are sacks by my booth. Take some if you need them."

With that, the old man slipped his hands into his pockets, feeling the two packets of cigarettes there, and returned to his little booth.

Lila had paid, so she had nothing to worry about.

Full of energy, she picked up a sack and began loading wood offcuts.

The offcuts had already been piled up like little hills. All she had to do was press down the mouth of the sack and scrape the pieces in. The wood slid neatly inside.

After a dozen or so sweeps, one sack was full.

It was easier than carrying them by hand, but after filling ten sacks, Lila was still tired.

She stood with her hands on her hips and thumped her aching lower back.

It would be so much better if she could hire someone to help load them.

But the moment the thought appeared, she dismissed it.

Hiring help would save effort, but the fewer people who knew she was stockpiling wood offcuts, the better.

For caution's sake, she had to do this physical work herself.

Fifty sacks of wood offcuts filled the van to the brim.

By then, the sky was almost dark.

Just this one vanload nearly finished Lila off. Every bone in her body felt as if it had come loose.

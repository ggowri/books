Chapter 3

The car stopped at Baker's Lane Market near the university.

Lila had studied in Ashton, the city where she had grown up. Her memories were woven through every corner of it.

She knew almost every street.

Especially the food.

Behind the university, there was a lane packed with little eateries. A few of them had been her favourites for years.

Now, walking into Baker's Lane Market again, it looked exactly as it used to.

Colourful signs glowed above the stalls, each one advertising a different snack or meal.

The lane was lively.

The clatter of spatulas against pans, the owners bickering, dogs barking, customers laughing and chatting. All those messy sounds drifted into Lila's ears, yet they made her feel strangely calm.

Grounded.

They made her feel that she was still alive, and that the world was still at peace.

She walked into a familiar little shop and ordered without even looking at the menu.

"One pork loin bap and one clam noodle soup, please. Medium hot."

"You haven't been in for ages, love. I'll get it started for you now."

Gran Lily's kind voice sounded from behind the counter. When Lila looked up, she saw only the woman's slightly stooped back as she got to work.

Gran Lily was quick.

Ten minutes later, a large bowl of clam noodle soup and a bap stuffed generously with tender pork loin were placed on the table.

Lila picked up the bap at once and took a huge bite.

The crust was still crisp. The pork was still soft, juicy and delicious.

She picked up her chopsticks, stirred the soup, and hurriedly lifted the noodles to her mouth.

Gran Lily had added extra chilli oil. It was fragrant, spicy and numbing in the best possible way.

"Slow down, love," Gran Lily said, watching her. "Why are you eating so fast today?"

Lila wanted to answer, but her mouth was too full to open.

All she could do was chew hard, swallowing with such effort that her throat almost hurt.

When she finally managed to get the mouthful down, she said, "It's been too long. I've missed this so much. It's still just as good."

Gran Lily was wiping a table with a cloth. Hearing that, her hand paused.

"You should come more often lately. My husband's legs are getting worse. It won't be long before I close this place and take him back to the countryside to retire."

She looked around the shop she had run for decades, her eyes full of reluctance.

To Lila, however, this was good news.

The Collapse was coming. The countryside would be far easier to survive in than the city.

People in villages usually bought supplies in bulk because it was troublesome to go into town all the time. They tended to keep grain and daily necessities at home. Even when the Collapse came, they would be able to hold on for a while.

"Then I'll come as often as I can," Lila said naturally. "I'll have to eat here a few more times, otherwise I might never get the chance again."

Once the money was sorted, she would definitely come back and stock up on pork loin baps.

"If it comes to that, you can visit me in the village," Gran Lily said. "I'll cook for you there. No charge."

She was fond of Lila too. The girl had loved her baps since childhood. In a way, Gran Lily had watched her grow up.

"The village won't be very convenient, though," Lila said gently. "You should store plenty of rice and grain at home, and keep them in the cellar so you can eat slowly over time. That way, you won't have to keep making trips into town. It'll be less strain on you, and your husband needs you there too."

She could not help giving the warning.

"You're right. That's what I was thinking as well. We're old now. Not as quick on our feet as we used to be."

Gran Lily sighed.

Lila felt a complicated ache in her chest that she could not quite put into words.

By the time she finished eating, it was fully dark outside.

She lit up her phone screen.

8:14 p.m.

With a helpless sigh, she knew she had to go home.

Lila truly did not want to go back. She did not want to see those revolting faces.

But there were still more than two months before the Collapse. Showing her hand too early would only work against her.

Besides, there were still things at home she needed to sort out.

At the very least, she had to finish that first.

As for the title deeds, she would be taking them to the bank tomorrow. Nothing could be allowed to go wrong tonight.

With a flicker of will, Lila stored the title deeds, ID papers and other documents inside the Vault.

When she got home and opened the door, she saw Edward, Maggie and Anna sitting neatly on the sofa.

Their faces were tinged with anger.

But the moment they saw Lila, they all suppressed it and forced smiles onto their faces.

Maggie came forward with a warm, eager smile.

"Lila, why are you back so late? Edward and I were worried about you."

As she spoke, she reached for Lila's hand.

The sight of that hand made Lila feel sick.

It was that very hand that had tied her up with wire.

Lila frowned and avoided her, leaving Maggie grasping at empty air.

"Lila, are you upset about something?" Maggie asked, full of concern, her tone cautious and gentle.

Seeing her like this made Lila's stomach turn.

This was the act that had fooled her. This was the face that had kept her from seeing the poisonous heart beneath.

"No. I've just been under pressure looking for work lately. I can handle it."

Lila gave an excuse. She did not want them to notice anything wrong.

"Then come and have some fruit," Anna said, patting the seat beside her on the sofa, acting for all the world like the lady of the house.

Yet every piece of fruit on that coffee table had been bought with Lila's money.

Afraid of making them suspicious, Lila had no choice but to sit down.

"Lila," Maggie said, pretending to be concerned, "I was tidying your room earlier and saw quite a few bloodstained tissues in your bin. Did you hurt yourself?"

There was nothing to hide about that, so Lila nodded.

"Yes. I scratched my finger by accident while reading. It's almost healed now."

As she spoke, she raised her finger to show the wound.

Seeing the red mark on Lila's finger, Maggie gave a perfunctory nod.

After a brief silence, Maggie spoke again.

"When I was tidying your room, I noticed your title deeds didn't seem to be in the drawer."

Lila sneered inwardly.

So the concern over her finger had been fake. The title deeds were what Maggie truly cared about.

They were watching her far too closely. She had only just taken them out, yet Maggie had noticed already.

"When I went out this afternoon, I meant to take my university certificate and ask a friend whether she could help me find a better-paid job. I left in a hurry and accidentally grabbed the title deeds instead."

Lila had prepared that excuse in advance.

Edward and Maggie's doubts were not completely gone, but they could not say much more.

"You must keep the title deeds safe," Edward said, his eyes full of fatherly warmth and concern. "You absolutely cannot lose something that important."

But Lila knew the cruelty hidden beneath that look.

She met his gaze for only a moment before quickly looking away.

"I know, Edward. Don't worry."

After a little more small talk, Lila used a headache as an excuse and returned to her room early.

She found a padlock in the drawer and locked the drawer where the title deeds had previously been kept.

Then she searched online for a shop that could make imitation documents, placed an order, and sent over photos of her title deeds.

Once that was done, although her mind was full of worries, she forced herself to go to sleep early.

There was not much time left before the Collapse.

This was going to be a hard fight.

She needed proper rest and a healthy body so she could prepare properly for the end.

Early the next morning, Lila dressed neatly, ate breakfast as usual, took her bag and left.

Once she was certain no one was following her, she drove straight to the bank.

"Good morning. I'd like to apply for a mortgage loan."

Lila said the words to the receptionist.

The receptionist saw the title deeds in Lila's hand, and her eyes widened at once.

This was a major case.

"Of course. Please come with me."

The receptionist led Lila to a counter where she did not need to queue.

Lila sat down and handed over the title deeds.

An hour later, Lila walked out of the bank.

The title deeds were no longer in her hands.

There was an extra $1.6 million in her account.

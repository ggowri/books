Chapter 89

"Give it a try. Mommy built this little house just for you. See if it's comfortable."

Worried the size might be off, Lauren had her daughter test it first.

The little girl clumsily climbed into the cardboard box and settled in the middle. Thick blankets surrounded her on all sides, making her feel warm, secure, and protected.

When she leaned back, she could almost recline.

"Mom, I love it! This little house is so cozy!"

Seeing the excitement on her daughter's face, Lauren finally relaxed and smiled.

Daniel had almost nothing except a small bag of food.

He rummaged through abandoned bedding in rooms once occupied by people who were gone now and managed to find two heavy winter coats. He pulled both of them on.

When the time came, he would simply stuff his food inside the layers and tie everything down.

That was his entire worldly possession.

The loudest commotion, however, came from Annabel's family.

With her injured leg stretched out in front of her, Annabel sat on the floor throwing a tantrum.

"I can't walk! I can't walk! I don't care. You two have to pull me!"

Maggie tried to calm her daughter while working on a sled.

She couldn't remove one of the doors, so she used the large wooden serving table instead.

The tabletop was wide, sturdy, and flat enough to drag across the snow.

"And who's supposed to pull you?" Edward snapped.

"Your leg isn't broken off. Walk."

"Eighty kilometers in this weather means at least six or seven days on the road. You trying to kill your mother and me?"

His face darkened with irritation.

How had he ended up with such a selfish daughter?

"Dad won't help me. Mom, you won't leave me behind, right?"

Knowing Edward was hopeless, Annabel immediately turned to Maggie.

"Don't worry, sweetheart. I'll pull you."

Maggie had already tied ropes to the wooden platform.

She spread blankets across the surface and secured their food supplies at the front.

Watching her fuss over Annabel only made Edward angrier.

Without another word, he stormed out of the basement.

 

That night, everyone went to sleep with a mixture of hope and anxiety.

By dawn, they were already awake.

The sky had fully brightened when Mrs. Harris stood outside the basement entrance with her packed belongings, counting heads.

Twenty four people.

Not one missing.

Not one extra.

She took a deep breath.

"Let's move!"

"The road ahead won't be easy."

"But hold on."

"When we reach the refuge, a new life is waiting for us."

Her voice carried strength and conviction.

It gave everyone a little more courage.

Wrapped in layers of winter clothing and carrying her supplies, Mrs. Harris stepped forward first and pulled her sled out into the snow.

Afraid of being left behind, Annabel's family rushed out immediately afterward.

Edward traveled light.

He carried nothing except what he wore.

Outside, Maggie positioned the sled before helping Annabel climb aboard.

Two thick quilts covered her completely.

Maggie grabbed the rope and started pulling.

Nothing happened.

She pulled again.

Still nothing.

Only after straining with everything she had did the sled finally budge.

Meanwhile, the people behind them were trapped at the entrance.

"Come on!"

"Mrs. Harris is already getting away from us!"

"Stop blocking the doorway!"

The journey had barely begun, and people were already frustrated.

"What are you rushing for?"

"Can't you see we have an injured person here?"

Annabel glared at the crowd from atop her blanket throne.

Trying to hurry, Maggie jerked the sled forward too hard.

For a second, Annabel nearly slid off.

Finally, the path cleared and everyone managed to leave.

Sophie's sled wasn't particularly heavy, and she quickly moved near the front of the group.

Leo and Callum deliberately stayed in the middle.

They wanted to avoid both leading and falling behind.

It seemed like the smartest position.

Lauren was the last to emerge.

She carefully placed her daughter inside the prepared cardboard box.

Extra blankets were tucked around every side.

The little girl sank into the soft layers, shielded from most of the cold.

Still not satisfied, Lauren wrapped two more blankets around the outside, leaving only a small palm‑sized opening for air.

"How's that?" she asked.

"I like it here, Mom."

The tiny voice floated out from inside the bundle.

Only then did Lauren finally feel at ease.

By now the group had already moved about fifty yards ahead.

She grabbed her rope and hurried after them.

 

Dreaming about the journey the night before had been one thing.

Actually walking through the frozen wilderness was another matter entirely.

Snow reached above their knees.

Every step demanded enormous effort.

Those with proper winter gear managed slightly better.

Others wore only a couple of coats.

The icy wind slipped through every gap in their clothing, draining enthusiasm as quickly as it drained body heat.

Mrs. Harris led from the front.

After thirty minutes of continuous walking, even she was exhausted.

Her thighs burned.

The sled rope dug into her shoulder like fire.

Finally she stopped and turned around.

"How's everyone doing?"

"Still holding up?"

"I'm okay!"

"I can keep going!"

The slight rise in temperature helped.

Everyone felt cold, but most were still managing.

Encouraged by the responses, Mrs. Harris allowed a five minute break before setting off again.

The brutal trek tested both endurance and survival.

Strength and temperature became equally dangerous enemies.

Another thirty minutes passed.

The pace slowed noticeably.

Every leg felt as though it weighed a thousand pounds when lifted from the snow.

Frozen hands and feet added a constant layer of misery.

"Slow down, Mrs. Harris."

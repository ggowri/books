Chapter 46

That night, some things quietly began to change.

Just as the sky was turning pale, people started noticing something was wrong.

"What's going on? The heating isn't as warm as it was before."

A woman who had got up to use the toilet felt chilled all over. She went over and touched the radiator. Yesterday it had been scorching hot. Today, it was only lukewarm.

"Go back to sleep. It's early. They're probably changing shifts at the boiler house."

Her husband pulled her back into the warm bed, and the two of them soon fell heavily asleep again.

By eight in the morning, heating had dropped in half the homes across the city. Indoor temperatures could only be maintained at around ten degrees.

Online, people were full of complaints. They kept tagging the government's official accounts, demanding that the heating be increased immediately.

The other half, whose homes had not cooled down, took pleasure in the contrast. They filmed videos showing off their comfortable little lives in warm rooms, eating the good food they had stocked up.

By then, Lila had already eaten breakfast.

The fire in the inglenook fireplace had warmed the room beautifully. She was sprawled on the heated hearth bench, playing on her phone and cracking sunflower seeds.

When she saw people gleefully showing off the food on their tables, she clicked her tongue in irritation.

"Never flaunt what you have."

The estate where Maggie and Anna lived was one of the places where the heating had dropped.

The Oakwood residents' group chat was full of complaints.

"Why is it so cold indoors? The heating has only been on for two days and it's already failing?"

"I can't get through to the district-heating supplier at all. What a useless company."

"I just got through. They said a district-heating pipe over on Westbrook Road froze and burst. They're repairing it now."

Leo touched the lukewarm radiator.

Nine degrees indoors.

He sent a message in the group chat.

"Appreciate it while you can. Having even this temperature is already pretty good."

Then he muttered to himself, "In a few days, we might not even have this. It's time to find a shelter."

Leo held his phone and began researching possible shelters. He also had to consider how he would move his supplies.

Over at Callum's, he had already sorted out his gear and was preparing to deliver the orders.

Gordon was still driving, with Big Grant and Little Grant helping Callum with deliveries.

Today would not be as troublesome as last night. They only needed to place the supplies in the entrance area of each block, cordon them off, and let residents collect them by checking their orders on their phones.

By the end of the morning, Callum had delivered every order.

Only Callum's own supplies were left in Gordon's lorry.

There were already too many things in Callum's flat, and he could not fit that much food upstairs. So he asked Gordon to park in the underground car park.

He would quietly move the supplies straight into his own basement storage room.

Both the storage rooms and the car park were on basement level one, though the storage rooms sat beneath the building itself, while the car park spread out under the estate grounds.

Gordon parked as close as possible to Callum's basement storage room.

Big Grant and Little Grant helped Callum carry his supplies inside.

Then they jumped back into the lorry, and the three Grant brothers drove away.

That was the end of this round of business. The weather was getting colder and colder, and they would not risk making a second trip.

The fact that the lorry had not broken down halfway was already something to be grateful for.

Callum's basement storage room had no window. It was small, but fairly warm.

He stacked all the food carefully in the corner, locked the door securely, and went back upstairs.

He did not run into anyone throughout the entire process, and for that, Callum was deeply relieved.

On the day the government heating weakened, everyone was anxious.

Some people kept calling the council emergency hotline, demanding the heating be restored. Some began thinking seriously about escape routes and how best to stay alive. Others prayed desperately, hoping this strange extreme cold would pass soon.

Maggie, Edward and the others were terrified too.

Lila had cleared out almost all of their cold-weather supplies. If the heating stopped, they would have no choice but to wait to freeze.

The residents' group chat would not settle down either. People kept tagging the Estate Management account, asking when the heating would be restored.

The gratitude from the beginning was gone. Their tone was full of resentment.

Some even launched into angry rants, saying the annual estate fees and all the taxes they paid when shopping had been a complete waste.

Estate Management could only reply over and over again that the government was carrying out urgent repairs, the heating would be restored, and residents were asked to remain calm.

Then someone suddenly tagged Callum's shop account.

"AAA Country Furs & Jerky Wholesale & Retail, are you doing another group buy today?"

The people who had held back yesterday and had not bought anything immediately followed under that message.

"I want to buy too."

"I was busy yesterday. Is it still possible to buy today?"

"Do you still have stock?"

"I bought too little yesterday. I want to buy more today."

"AAA Country Furs & Jerky Wholesale & Retail, why aren't you saying anything?"

Callum was holding a pen and planning how to move his supplies that evening when his phone began pinging non-stop.

He had muted the residents' group chat, so if the phone was making noise, it usually meant someone had mentioned him.

He unlocked the screen and saw that people in the group were tagging him.

Callum sighed, then replied in the group.

"No group buy today. There were too many orders yesterday. All the food sold out. I don't even have enough for myself."

The reason he replied was to make everyone believe he had nothing left, so they would not come looking for trouble later.

No matter what people said after that, Callum did not respond again. He focused on making his own moving plan.

Lila had exhausted herself cleaning the day before, so today was a proper day of doing absolutely nothing.

Apart from keeping the fire in the fireplace burning, she had no other worries at all.

At noon, she could not be bothered to cook, so she took a bowl of beef noodles from the Vault.

A steaming bowl of spicy beef noodles warmed her through. By the time she finished, she had even broken into a light sweat.

Lila put the plastic takeaway bag from the noodles into an empty cupboard in the main hall, saving it to use for rubbish later.

She did not waste the takeaway container either. She washed it clean with washing-up liquid and put it on the shelf. It would come in useful sooner or later.

Just as she pushed open the door to the east parlour, she heard the two puppies squeaking.

Their eyes were only half open, but they had already learned to play.

The two little heads kept nudging against each other, and their tiny paws paddled up and down as they played with enormous enthusiasm.

Lila crouched on the floor and watched for a while before deciding she had to be the villain.

She reached out and picked up Patches.

Holding him in one hand, she used a wet wipe with the other to clean him from head to tail.

With no mother dog to lick their fur, the little ones still did not know how to clean themselves. After rolling about for the past two days, they had become a little grubby.

Patches was usually lively, but now that Lila had him in her hand, he did not resist at all.

He did not even squeak. He simply stayed there obediently, letting her wipe him again and again.

"Patches, does that feel good?"

Seeing him behave so well, Lila was sure he must be enjoying it.

Perhaps the wiping made him imagine he was being licked clean by his mother.

Once Patches was clean, she placed him on the sofa to warm by the fire.

Then Lila picked up Snowy and wiped him down in the same way.

It was warm beside the fireplace. The damp fluff on the puppies was wrapped in the heat from the fire and gently dried, so neither of the little ones felt cold.

Before long, their fur had dried fluffy and soft.

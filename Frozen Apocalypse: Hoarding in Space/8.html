Chapter 7

Early the next morning, Lila personally drove the others to the airport.

Unexpectedly, she saw Daniel there.

Lila frowned. She had almost forgotten about him.

"What are you doing here?" she asked.

Daniel was holding a bouquet of flowers. He came forward with a smile and placed them in Lila's arms.

"I heard Edward and Maggie were going on holiday, so I came to see them off."

As he spoke, his eyes instinctively flicked towards Anna.

"I'm at a crucial stage with job hunting and can't go with them," Lila said. "Why don't you go with them for me?"

If she could send Daniel away as well, that would be perfect.

Anna was thrilled.

If Daniel went on holiday with her, and Lila was not around, then the two of them could be together openly.

"Well, I'm not sure that's appropriate."

Daniel had wanted to go with them the moment he heard about the trip yesterday. That way, he and Anna would not have to keep sneaking around.

But Maggie had refused and told him to stay behind to keep an eye on Lila.

"Please," Lila said, deliberately softening her tone. "Edward, Maggie and Anna don't travel far very often. I'm a bit worried since I can't go with them. You can go in my place and keep them company. I'll reimburse all your expenses when you get back."

Daniel had no choice but to put on a reluctant, helpless expression and agree.

Seeing the situation develop this way, Edward and Maggie quietly accepted Daniel joining them.

Only after Lila watched them pass through security and made certain they would board the plane did she finally relax.

She was free.

There was no one left to watch her.

From now on, she could stockpile whatever she wanted, however she wanted. No one could stop her.

She drove straight to the railway station, picked up Markus for the site survey, then headed for Lower Vale.

When they arrived, Markus looked up at the house halfway up the hillside, and a hint of admiration appeared in his eyes.

"This is an excellent location. You have very good judgement."

"Not really," Lila said. "It's just an old family property left by my grandparents. I thought I'd renovate and repair it properly so it doesn't continue falling apart. At least that way, I'll have something to remember them by."

It was a decent excuse.

The reinforcement and renovation were simply to preserve the house better.

Lila showed Markus around the house and courtyard.

After that, she laid out her requirements.

First, the entire house had to receive top level insulation and structural reinforcement.

In particular, the outer sides of the courtyard walls and house walls had to be reinforced with high grade cement concrete.

Second, all doors and windows had to be replaced with insulated, blast-resistant versions.

Third, the roof had to be waterproofed.

Fourth, the two heated hearth benches inside had to be dismantled and rebuilt.

Fifth, the stoves had to be rebuilt as well. The east side stove was to be made into one large stove and two smaller ones.

Sixth, a large inglenook fireplace had to be built against the east wall of the east room, connected to hot water pipes running to the kitchen and bathroom.

Seventh, all bathroom fittings had to be replaced. The toilet, especially, had to be durable and built to last.

Eighth, the courtyard walls had to be raised to three metres, with electrified perimeter fencing added along the top metre.

Ninth, the courtyard gate had to be replaced with a high grade reinforced steel security gate.

Tenth, the top of the courtyard walls had to be covered with a blast-resistant glass roof that could enclose both the entire courtyard and the house. It had to be pitched, so water and snow would not collect, and it also had to be easy to dismantle and reinstall.

In her previous life, Lila had only experienced the Long Freeze.

But she was not certain whether acid rain, sandstorms or other disasters might come later.

Ideally, the whole courtyard would be covered.

That would stop people from climbing in and would give the yard the greatest possible protection.

During the Long Freeze, she would also be able to move around in the courtyard, giving herself more living space.

Markus took out a notebook and recorded each of Lila's requirements.

When he heard the tenth one, he frowned slightly.

"You want blast-resistant glass over the entire courtyard? That won't be cheap. The roof alone would cost $200,000 in materials. Your friend mentioned that you were hoping to keep costs down. Also, I don't have enough stock on hand, so I'd have to arrange more from overseas."

Lila could just about accept $200,000 for the roof.

But from Markus's tone, she sensed he had a better option.

"Do you have another solution? It needs to cover the top completely, let light through, and be strong."

"What about reinforced polycarbonate panels?"

Markus thought for a moment, then gave the answer smoothly.

Seeing Lila's blank expression, he explained patiently.

"High strength polycarbonate panels are the best fit for what you need. The cost is far lower than blast-resistant glass, but their impact resistance is over two hundred times stronger than ordinary glass. Normal sticks and stones won't be able to break through them.

"They also have excellent toughness. Even under heavy impact, they won't suddenly shatter like glass. At most, they'll deform.

"I happen to have a batch of high quality panels in storage. They're multi layer hollow sun panels with a UV coating, so they block ultraviolet light, resist ageing, and can last more than ten years.

"They have excellent insulation and can help retain heat.

"Polycarbonate panels are also lightweight, so they don't require as much from the supporting roof frame. They're particularly suitable for covering a courtyard.

"There are drawbacks, of course. After long periods of direct sun exposure, the transparency will drop slightly, but the light transmission will still be good. You don't need to worry about that."

Markus listed both the advantages and disadvantages.

From what Lila heard, these panels were clearly better value for money.

"How much would it cost in total?"

That was the part she cared about most.

Markus held up five fingers.

"I'll give them to you at cost price. $50,000 will be enough."

There was actually such a miraculous material?

Strong, insulating, and $150,000 cheaper.

At this point, whether it was perfectly transparent or not no longer mattered. She would use it with her eyes closed.

"Is it flammable?" Lila asked.

This worried her too. What if someone could not get in and decided to set fire to the place?

"No," Markus said with a patient smile. "You can rest assured. Polycarbonate panels have very good flame retardant properties. They won't self ignite unless the temperature reaches about 580 degrees Celsius."

This time, Lila was completely reassured.

"Then let's use the polycarb... the panels."

"All right. I've noted down your requirements. I'll need to go back and calculate the exact cost carefully. I'll call you tomorrow morning."

Markus turned off the tablet screen and placed it in the bag he carried with him.

"Good. I'll take you back to the station."

Lila turned, locked up the Lower Vale Mansion, then drove Markus back to the railway station.

By six in the evening, Lila made do with a bowl of noodles, bought a hidden camera detector, and returned home.

The house was empty.

When Lila entered, she did not turn the lights on at once. Instead, she took out the detector and swept it through the rooms.

Only after confirming there were no cameras did she relax, turn on the lights, and wash up.

She had been running around all day, and she was genuinely tired.

After washing, Lila lay straight down on the bed and began planning her stockpiling.

Instant meals, seasonings, clothes and household essentials could be bought online.

Meat, vegetables, fruit and grain would be bought in person.

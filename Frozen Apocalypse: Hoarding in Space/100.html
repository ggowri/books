Chapter 99

After his wife squeezed through the doorway, the heavyset man finally shuffled inside himself.

The man looked surprisingly familiar.

It was none other than Cedric, who had been absent from the story for quite some time.

Following close behind him was a young woman dressed in an expensive extreme-cold protective suit.

The hallway inside the residential wing was pleasantly warm.

She immediately yanked off her hat and unzipped her coat, revealing a pampered face twisted with impatience.

"How do two people manage to block a doorway that wide?"

After complaining, she looked around the corridor and seemed quite satisfied with the temperature inside.

The young woman was Emily Dawson, Cedric's daughter.

She had been assigned a room of her own, while Cedric and his wife were housed next door.

Although the officials' residential compound was one large interconnected building, each unit was spacious and exceptionally well insulated.

Living here felt nothing like a dormitory.

Each residence felt like a private home.

Including Ryan and Evan, there were now four government officials living in the officials' compound.

Once those four were settled in, more survivors began arriving.

The soldiers barely had time to catch their breath.

Ever since the relocation notices had been issued, survivors from surrounding towns had been making their way toward Lower Vale Refuge on foot.

Starting today, arrivals would reach their peak.

At one o'clock that afternoon, Mrs. Harris and her group finally arrived.

They were still dragging their sleds behind them, taking one numb step after another across the frozen wilderness.

Mrs. Harris knew they were headed in the right direction.

She just had no idea how much farther remained.

Suddenly she squinted.

For a moment she thought her eyes were playing tricks on her.

The landscape around them consisted of nothing but white snow and gray tree trunks.

Yet she could have sworn she'd seen a flash of red in the distance.

She didn't have enough strength left to raise a hand.

Instead, she blinked several times and shook her head.

When she looked again, the red remained.

It looked like...

A flag.

"Look!"

"Look!"

"We made it! We actually made it!"

Mrs. Harris shouted with the last reserves of strength she possessed.

The exhausted group of thirteen instantly came alive.

Everyone lifted their heads and looked toward the distant flag.

It was real.

They had actually reached their destination.

Even Annabel poked her head out from beneath her blankets and glanced toward the direction Mrs. Harris was pointing.

"Maybe it's just an abandoned school..."

she muttered before retreating back under the covers.

But hope had already taken hold.

The survivors quickened their pace and headed toward the flag.

As they drew closer, the details became clear.

The refuge was enormous.

Large buildings.

Small buildings.

Rows upon rows of structures arranged neatly across the valley.

Smoke drifted from several chimneys.

Signs of life.

Signs of warmth.

Signs of people.

Mrs. Harris smiled.

For the first time in days, her entire body relaxed.

"Good."

"I got all of you here."

The words had barely left her mouth when her knees buckled.

She collapsed unconscious into the snow.

Everyone panicked.

But nobody had much strength left themselves.

The survivors glanced at Mrs. Harris.

No one said anything.

They simply dragged their sleds downhill faster.

They needed to get inside.

Immediately.

"Mrs. Harris!"

Callum was terrified.

His own legs were nearly frozen solid, but he couldn't abandon the woman who had carried the group this far.

"Help me get her onto the sled."

Sophie stepped forward, setting aside her own sled.

Callum looked toward Leo.

Leo's voice emerged from beneath layers of scarf and frost.

"Do it. I'll pull our sled. You pull hers."

Neither Callum nor Sophie could properly feel their hands anymore.

Working mostly on instinct, they managed to lift Mrs. Harris onto her sled.

The three of them trailed behind the rest of the group.

By the time they reached the refuge entrance, the others were already checking in.

Soldiers stood guard at the gate.

After a brief inspection, survivors were allowed inside.

Hundreds would be arriving over the coming days.

Leo approached one of the guards.

"Where's the clinic? We've got an injured person."

The soldier pointed toward a nearby building.

"Right over there."

After thanking him, Leo, Callum, and Sophie headed straight for the clinic.

The clinic was packed.

Anyone with minor injuries was being turned away.

Only the seriously injured remained inside waiting for treatment.

The refuge's medical services were currently being handled by Mr. Brown, a retired army medic in his sixties.

With no licensed doctors yet assigned to Lower Vale Refuge, he had become the settlement's acting medical officer, treating everything from frostbite and exhaustion to severe injuries. Over the past several days, he had been working almost nonstop.

Seeing the warmth inside, Leo handed both sleds to Callum.

"You go ahead."

He hoisted Mrs. Harris onto his shoulders before turning back.

"Find a place for our gear. Put Mrs. Harris's things with ours for now."

Then he pushed his way into the crowded clinic.

Every hospital bed was occupied.

The benches were full as well.

Left with no alternative, Leo laid Mrs. Harris carefully on an open patch of floor.

He removed his gloves and squeezed close to the stove.

After warming his hands enough to regain feeling, he returned to her side.

Mrs. Harris was beginning to recover consciousness.

Leo immediately began performing a proper assessment and emergency treatment.

Although he now worked as a physical education teacher, he had originally studied medicine in university.

And he had been exceptionally talented.

Years ago, the professor he admired most had died right in front of him.

Unable to save his mentor, Leo had abandoned medicine entirely.

He switched careers and eventually became a teacher.

Yet all those years of medical training remained etched into his memory.

Faced with Mrs. Harris's condition, his instincts simply took over.

"Cough! Cough!"

Mrs. Harris suddenly erupted into a violent fit of coughing.

Her eyes opened.

"How are you feeling?"

Leo crouched beside her.

His concern was obvious.

"I'm alright..."

Her voice lacked its usual booming strength.

She looked around the clinic.

Strangers surrounded her on every side.

"You've just pushed yourself too hard," Leo said gently.

"Now that we're at the refuge, a few days of rest will do wonders."

Looking at her gaunt face, he could barely recognize her.

Was this really the same powerful woman who had led them across the frozen wilderness?

"Thank you, Leo."

Mrs. Harris's eyes grew moist.

"You saved my life."

The gratitude in her voice was unmistakable.

In conditions like these, carrying someone back from the brink of death was no small thing.

"It wasn't just me."

Leo smiled.

"Callum helped bring you here too."

Mrs. Harris slowly sat up.

Warm air surrounded her.

She looked around the clinic and sighed happily.

"Wow."

"It's so warm."

"Everything we went through was worth it."

"We don't have to worry about surviving anymore."

Leo was about to respond when someone tapped him on the shoulder.

He frowned and turned around.

A deeply lined face greeted him with an eager grin.

It was Mr. Brown.

"Hey, young man!"

Mr. Brown's eyes lit up.

"Are you a doctor?"

Inside, he was already celebrating.

At long last, he might have found someone who could help carry the workload.

Leo offered a polite smile.

"I'm sorry."

"I'm not a doctor."

The excitement on Mr. Brown's face dimmed slightly.

"I was watching you earlier."

"Your emergency treatment technique was textbook perfect..."

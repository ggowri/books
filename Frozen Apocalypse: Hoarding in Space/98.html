Chapter 97

Eating a Beef-Filled Bun or Chicken-Filled Bun required strategy.

First, don't burn your mouth.

Second, don't let the broth spill all over your clothes.

The buns were still piping hot.

Lila carefully bit a small opening into one.

Inside sat a huge ball of seasoned meat surrounded by rich, glistening broth.

She blew gently into the opening until the steam eased.

Then she tipped the savory juices into her mouth.

The flavor was incredible.

Rich, meaty, and deeply comforting, with a savory sweetness she couldn't quite describe.

Whatever it was, it was delicious.

Lila polished off two giant buns in record time.

By then, the Dinner Rolls in the steamer were ready as well.

She stored them in the Vault, cleaned up the kitchen, and finally declared the cooking marathon finished.

She was exhausted.

After removing her apron, she collapsed onto the heated bed.

A steaming mug of coffee in one hand and a book in the other, she settled in for some well-earned relaxation.

Then a thought struck her.

Lila sat upright immediately.

She pulled on her heavy winter gear and stepped into the courtyard.

The binoculars slid through one of the observation holes.

The refuge appeared normal.

Only soldiers were visible.

She scanned the surrounding landscape.

Dead trees.

Snowfields.

Nothing but white and gray stretching to the horizon.

Then she noticed movement in the northwest.

Tiny black dots.

Her eyes narrowed.

Survivors?

She adjusted the focus and looked again.

The shapes were still tiny, but there was no mistaking them.

People.

And they were dragging supplies behind them.

The first survivors were almost here.

Fortunately, all the comfort food she'd wanted for the coming weeks was already prepared and stored away.

Hopefully they wouldn't bring any trouble with them.

Truthfully, life had become fairly boring lately.

Eat.

Sleep.

Watch movies.

Read novels.

Repeat.

If survivors arrived, at least there would be something interesting to observe through her binoculars.

The thought actually made her a little excited.

Maybe they would get here soon.

After satisfying her curiosity, Lila returned to the mansion and resumed her carefully cultivated lifestyle of doing absolutely nothing.

Her coffee had gone lukewarm.

She frowned.

Pouring it into a stainless steel mug, she placed it on the fireplace hob to warm.

When steam began rising again, she carried it back to the bed table.

Book in one hand.

Coffee in the other.

Perfect.

At three o'clock that afternoon, construction on the refuge was officially complete.

Cheers erupted among the soldiers.

For the first time in weeks, they could finally catch their breath.

The refuge now occupied almost all of Lower Vale.

There was a massive dining hall capable of serving a thousand people.

A secure grain warehouse.

Supply depots.

Fuel storage facilities.

A boiler house.

There was even a medical clinic and a communal bathhouse.

The bathhouse had hot water, although it would only open once each week.

Twenty soldiers would remain stationed at the refuge permanently.

All were armed and responsible for maintaining security.

The residential section contained two hundred houses.

At maximum capacity, the settlement could hold sixteen hundred people.

For reasonably comfortable living conditions, eight hundred was a more realistic number.

The dining hall sat at the innermost section of the refuge, closest to the Lower Vale Mansion.

The supply depots, grain warehouses, and fuel storage buildings stood on either side.

The clinic and boiler house occupied the center.

Nearby were the soldiers' duty station and living quarters.

The boiler system heated only the public facilities and military buildings.

The survivors' houses were expected to rely on heated beds and individual fuel supplies for warmth.

Heating every home centrally would simply be impossible.

Beyond the public buildings stood the bathhouse.

Everything else was housing.

The entire settlement was surrounded by fencing.

It wasn't especially tall.

Anyone determined enough could climb over it without much difficulty.

Two hours later, the first survivors finally arrived.

They were the same distant figures Lila had spotted through her binoculars that morning.

They had approached from the northwest.

Back then, they had looked like tiny dots moving painfully slowly across the snow.

Now they were close enough to see clearly.

The first group was small.

Only seven or eight people.

They walked single file, all gripping the same thick rope.

No one dared let go.

Together they dragged themselves across the frozen landscape.

At seventy degrees below zero, survival bordered on impossible.

Yet somehow they had made it.

Their faces were wrapped in layers of cloth, leaving only narrow openings for their eyes.

Frost coated their eyelashes and eyebrows so heavily that it was impossible to tell who was who.

At the front walked a woman.

She wasn't tall, but she never stopped moving.

Behind her stumbled a child bundled in so many layers that age was impossible to guess.

Every few steps the child tripped.

The rope pulled them upright.

Then they stumbled again.

For three days they had been walking.

They no longer knew where they were.

Only stubborn determination kept their legs moving.

Then suddenly the woman at the front stopped.

The person behind her nearly crashed into her back.

He wanted to ask what was wrong, but his frozen lips barely worked.

The woman simply stood there staring into the distance.

The first thing she saw wasn't the buildings.

It was the flag.

Against the endless gray sky and swirling snow, that patch of bright red was impossible to ignore.

The flagpole towered above the settlement.

The fabric stretched tight in the wind, snapping sharply with every gust.

It looked alive.

Beneath it stood rows of houses.

Large ones.

Small ones.

All neatly arranged.

Warm yellow light glowed from some of the windows.

Light.

Warmth.

Hope.

The woman's lips moved.

"We made it."

Her voice was cracked and weak, but the people behind her heard it.

The child jerked upright.

"We made it!"

Everyone heard that.

The woman began moving again.

This time she wasn't shuffling.

She was walking with purpose.

Not fast.

She no longer had the strength for that.

But much faster than before.

The others tightened their grip on the rope and followed.

All eyes were fixed on the red flag ahead.

The closer they came, the more details emerged.

A towering chimney.

Red-roofed houses.

Soldiers in uniform.

"People are coming!"

One of the younger soldiers spotted them first.

He shouted over his shoulder.

Three soldiers in heavy winter coats immediately sprinted toward the approaching survivors.

Snow sprayed beneath their boots.

The lead soldier shouted while running.

"Get Old Liu! Tell him to fire up the clinic stove!"

One soldier instantly turned and ran back toward the military quarters.

The other two reached the survivors.

One grabbed the woman by the arm and guided her toward the clinic.

The other lifted the child into his arms.

"Hang in there," he called to the rest of the group. "We'll send more people to help."

Then he hurried toward the clinic.

The child felt stiff as a board.

The padded coat had frozen almost solid.

Yet the child's eyes remained wide open.

They stared at the soldier.

Then at the red star on his hat.

By the time they reached the clinic, warm yellow light already glowed through the windows.

Just looking at it felt comforting.

The woman tried to step inside.

Her legs refused to cooperate.

The soldier quickly caught her and half carried her through the doorway.

A stove had already been lit inside.

Old Liu worked fast.

The fire was burning, though the room hadn't fully warmed yet.

"Get yourself warmed up first. We'll examine you in a minute."

Then he turned toward the young soldier behind him.

"Go get the medic. Now."

Chapter 98

As soon as he finished speaking, the soldier ran out of the clinic.

"We need more hands! Help get the survivors inside!"

"People finally made it here?"

"That's fantastic!"

The soldiers who hadn't yet heard the news immediately dropped whatever they were doing and rushed after him toward the approaching survivors.

The child was carried into the clinic and placed on another bench.

One soldier removed his own military coat and wrapped it around the child.

Someone else brought over two bowls of hot water, steam curling into the air.

The woman reached out to take one, but her hands shook so badly she could barely hold it.

A single soldier remained behind to watch over them.

A frostbite scar ran across his face. The scar made his smile look intimidating, but his eyes were warm.

"If you made it this far," he said, "you're home."

The woman's lips moved.

No words came out.

But her eyes reddened.

Red as the flag flying outside.

One by one, the remaining survivors were escorted into the clinic.

Some were placed on hospital beds.

Others sat on benches.

By now the stove had fully warmed the room.

The chill was finally gone.

"Take it easy. Warm up first."

The voice entered before the man himself.

A veteran in his fifties stepped into the clinic wearing a military coat.

The army didn't currently have a dedicated physician stationed here.

The veteran had enough medical experience to serve as the temporary medic.

He examined the child first.

Checking the eyes.

Testing the hands and feet.

"Pretty severe frostbite, but recoverable. We'll need to look at the toes later. Don't rush taking the boots off. Let everything thaw gradually."

Then he moved to the woman.

After that, he inspected the others being brought inside one at a time.

"How many?"

"Eight."

The veteran nodded.

Turning toward the doorway, he called out loudly.

"Eight people. Prepare eight servings of porridge. Thin porridge. Nothing greasy."

After eating, another soldier entered carrying a notebook and pen.

"We need to register everyone."

One by one, the survivors provided their information.

Gender.

Age.

Place of origin.

Afterward, housing assignments were arranged.

"Can you walk?" the soldier asked.

The woman nodded.

Using the bench for support, she slowly rose to her feet.

Her legs were still weak, but they held.

The others followed suit.

Then they slowly made their way toward the refuge under military escort.

The group consisted of three women and five men.

They were assigned to two residential houses.

The homes were located close to the clinic.

After only a short walk, they arrived.

The houses were simple.

Each contained only two rooms.

A kitchen and a bedroom with a heated bed.

The kitchen held a stove, an iron cooking pot, and a storage cabinet.

The cabinet stood empty.

Half of the bedroom was occupied by the heated bed.

Against one wall sat a large metal storage locker divided into eight compartments.

When occupancy was high, each resident received a compartment.

When occupancy was low, supplies could be organized by category instead.

Since the survivors were still in poor condition, soldiers had already lit the fires and warmed the houses before their arrival.

The rooms were comfortable.

The heated beds radiated warmth.

"Pick a compartment and store your personal belongings," the escorting soldier explained.

Just then, two younger soldiers rushed in carrying bedding.

Three sets were placed in the women's house.

Five sets in the men's.

The soldiers said little else before leaving.

After days of suffering, endless walking, and relentless cold, this was everything the survivors had dreamed of.

Food.

Warmth.

Safety.

Each person claimed a set of bedding.

They spread blankets across the heated bed and crawled underneath.

No one spoke.

A few exhausted sighs escaped.

Then one by one they drifted into sleep.

At last.

They could finally rest.

The eight survivors had barely settled in before another group arrived.

This one was much larger.

Sixty-three people.

The soldiers suddenly found themselves overwhelmed.

There was no longer time for the careful attention given to the first group.

Instead of escorting everyone individually, they guided only the group's leader toward the clinic.

Medical checks were performed one after another.

As long as a person wasn't in immediate danger, they were registered and assigned housing.

Large pots of porridge began cooking in the communal kitchen.

The survivors would be fed shortly.

Before registration of that group was even finished, another team arrived.

More than twenty people.

The entire afternoon became a blur.

The soldiers spent hour after hour registering arrivals and assigning shelter housing.

By seven o'clock that evening, things finally quieted down.

Ninety-seven survivors had arrived in a single afternoon.

Throughout the night, two soldiers remained on watch.

If additional survivors appeared, they would need immediate assistance.

Perhaps everyone understood how dangerous nighttime travel was.

No one arrived.

The next morning, construction duties officially ended.

Only twenty soldiers remained stationed at the refuge.

The rest departed with Captain Walker.

Their new mission was to search the surrounding cities for supplies.

They would return only when it was time to rest and resupply.

Not long after the military convoy left, another vehicle arrived.

This one carried government officials and their families.

They had been assigned to live at Lower Vale Refuge and oversee daily operations.

Ultimate authority still belonged to Captain Walker and the Homeland Response Corps.

However, day to day administration would follow directives from central authorities and be handled by civilian officials.

The first man to step out of the vehicle was in his late fifties.

His extreme-cold protective suit was immaculate.

His expression was stern.

The moment he exited, two tall young men followed closely behind him and escorted him toward the administrative housing section.

The military residential area was divided into two sections.

The eastern side belonged to the soldiers.

The western side housed government officials and their families.

A wall separated the two.

The connecting passage had been completely sealed.

Under normal circumstances, neither side needed to interact much.

Their responsibilities were different.

The soldiers handled security, the grain stores, and the supply depots.

Everything else fell under civilian administration.

As the older man stepped through the entrance, warm air immediately enveloped him.

The brutal cold outside seemed to vanish from his body all at once.

Maintaining his serious expression, he followed a soldier to the innermost residence in the officials' compound.

The official residences were similar to the survivors' homes, though somewhat smaller due to lower occupancy.

Each contained a living room, kitchen, and bedroom.

The older man had no family accompanying him.

Only the two young men.

Both served as his senior secretaries.

Once inside, he removed his felt hat and heavy coat, revealing a gray knit sweater underneath.

His name was William.

Recently appointed director of Lower Vale Refuge, he was responsible for managing the entire settlement.

After settling him in, the two secretaries moved into neighboring residences.

Their accommodations were nearly identical.

The two men were brothers.

The older brother was Ryan Holloway.

The younger brother was Evan Holloway.

Both were known for their integrity, decisiveness, and efficiency.

Once the three men had settled in, another government official finally arrived with his family.

A large, heavyset man climbed out of the vehicle wearing a broad smile.

Supporting an equally heavyset woman beside him, he said cheerfully,

"Honey, we're here. We finally made it. You've had a rough trip. Get some rest once we're inside."

To preserve indoor heat, only half of the entrance door had been opened.

Unfortunately, both husband and wife tried squeezing through at exactly the same time.

A loud thump echoed through the hallway.

Neither one made it inside.

The woman immediately smacked the man on the arm.

"Oh, move already."

Then she pushed past him and entered first.

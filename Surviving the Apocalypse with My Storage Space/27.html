Chapter 27 Rainstorm, Toads: Part 2

Three hours later, with no adverse reactions in her body, Evelyn put her protective suit back on. She went to Claire's place and then to Calvin's home, telling both families what had happened.

After that, Evelyn returned to her apartment, grabbed a bucket, and went out to collect toads. What she wanted was the toxin inside their glands.

Claire and Calvin's family trusted Evelyn completely and immediately joined in. When others in the building saw them picking up toads, they asked Calvin about it and only then learned that these things could actually be eaten.

"These toads crawled out of the water. Are they really edible? That water is disgusting. There are corpses soaking in it."

"Exactly. Did you forget what happened when that bloated body burst a few days ago? My balcony is covered in rotten flesh. The smell is unbearable, and there are maggots everywhere."

"But Evelyn said they can be eaten. That means she's already tried them. If she's not afraid, what are we afraid of?"

"She studied medicine. Her tolerance is higher than ours to begin with."

"If you're not eating them, I will. Meat is meat. Filling your stomach and staying alive is what matters."

Some people felt sick just thinking about it. Others quickly joined the toad-collecting group.

In a rare moment of kindness, Evelyn warned them that toads were poisonous. The head and outer skin had to be removed. It would not be fatal, but as for other long-term side effects, she could not guarantee anything.

After filling one bucket, Evelyn went home. She planned to process them first and see if she could successfully turn them into medicinal ingredients.

Most people were extremely enthusiastic, but toads reproduced quickly, like cutting chives. Once one batch was cleared out, before long another dense wave would crawl out into the hallways.

That night, Evelyn had just lain down when waves of toad croaking erupted outside. The noise was piercing, drowning out even the sound of heavy rain and thunder.

By the next day, the toad-collecting teams had grown even larger. People even waded into the water to scoop them up. Those who had called it disgusting the day before were now the most eager.

Word that toads were edible spread quickly. Within the complex, and even on nearby streets, people drove inflatable boats and cast fishing nets. Sometimes they hauled up unexpected bonuses, like the corpses of rats, cats, or dogs.

That afternoon, a household in the neighboring building suffered poisoning. Panic spread immediately. Fortunately, poisoning was a low-probability event. That family had failed to clean the toad glands properly. They spent a day vomiting and having diarrhea, but no one died.

Because of the water shortage, most people made simple filters, running rainwater through charcoal, then boiling it before drinking or cooking.

Even so, many people developed diarrhea, or began passing roundworms. Not just neighbors from Block D, but patients from the entire Willow Heights started coming to Evelyn for treatment.

Under normal circumstances, intestinal parasites required several courses of medicine to kill and expel them. But Evelyn could not take out her precious drugs. She could only use silver needles and acupuncture to help.

That day, a resident from Block A came to find Evelyn. His wife was about to give birth, and he begged her to come help deliver the baby.

Evelyn did not want to go. But the man's face was filled with desperation. The refusal reached her lips, then she swallowed it back down.

She followed him to the tenth floor of Block A. The moment the door opened, a strong smell of blood hit her. The apartment was damp, with standing water on the floor. The pregnant woman lay in the bedroom, crying out in pain. When Evelyn saw her large, round belly, her eyelid twitched.

"Honey, the doctor is here," the man said, rushing to hold his wife's hand, trying to soothe her. "Hang on."

The man had already told Evelyn on the way over. His wife was carrying twins.

"Dr. Foster, please save my wife. No matter what, you have to keep her alive."

Evelyn looked at the ordinary-looking man, a little surprised. Twins, yet what he cared about most was his wife's life.

The fetal position was wrong. The baby's head and pelvis did not align. The woman was thin, her pelvis narrow.

At this moment, Evelyn pushed all thoughts of supplies out of her mind. She looked at the man and spoke gently.

"I'll try to help her deliver naturally, but this is my first time delivering a baby. I have no real experience. If something goes wrong…"

"Dr. Foster, everyone says you're amazing. I'm begging you. Just save my wife. As long as she lives, that's enough."

Seeing how close he was to breaking down, Evelyn could only usher him out of the room. Only the couple lived here. In the bedroom, she noticed piles of baby clothes, diapers, and cans of formula stacked neatly.

Evelyn realized the contractions were weak. A natural birth would be extremely difficult. She told herself over and over to stay calm. Three lives were at stake. She had to steady herself.

"Can you hear me? Don't be nervous. You're going to be okay."

The woman was already exhausted. Hearing Evelyn's voice, she nodded weakly, then suddenly grabbed Evelyn's arm.

"Save my babies, doctor. Please, help me save them."

Evelyn met her gaze for a few seconds, then squeezed her hand back.

"Trust me. And trust yourself. Now relax. Don't be afraid. You can talk to me. Say anything you want."

After putting on gloves and a mask, Evelyn nodded to her and began manually adjusting the fetal position.

An hour later, as Evelyn looked at the two babies, their bodies bruised and purplish, she found herself unable to describe what she was feeling.

Their cries grew from faint to strong. Evelyn called the man back in to help clean the babies. A wad of cloth was stuffed in the woman's mouth. She passed out from pain, woke up, then passed out again, over and over.

Because there were two babies, the tearing was severe. Evelyn stitched her carefully. There was no massive bleeding. That alone made it the best possible outcome.

It was a boy and a girl.

After briefly cleaning them, the man wrapped the babies up, then collapsed beside the bed, his eyes locked on his wife.

"She's okay now, right?"

"She needs to be observed for a while," Evelyn said. "There was no anesthesia. She passed out from the pain. Postpartum care is critical."

After an hour-long procedure, Evelyn's hands were trembling. All she wanted was a hot shower and sleep. But the woman was not yet out of danger. She could not leave.

After two hours of observation, the woman's condition remained stable. Only then did Evelyn take the supplies the man gave her and leave.

On the way back, she kept asking herself. If she had failed today, could she have borne that responsibility?

Being nosy always came at a price. Yet when the twins were born safely, Evelyn gave herself a silent pat on the back.

Human nature had two sides. She was no exception. Even as she regretted getting involved, she was genuinely happy for the birth of the two children.

Neighbors from Block D were outside collecting toads. When they heard she had gone to Block A to deliver a baby, they hurried over to ask how it went.

"Boy and girl twins. Very healthy."

The neighbors could not help but smile.

"That's wonderful. As long as the children are healthy."

"A big blessing. A newborn means this disaster will pass soon."

"Hahaha, that's great!"

"Children are hope."

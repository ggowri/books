Chapter 33 Rainstorm, Nematodes

Evelyn cut the worm into two pieces. Both halves thrashed violently, refusing to die. After a long while, instead of perishing, they split like earthworms and survived as two separate bodies.

The white nematode was as thin as sewing thread. Its body was extremely soft, with no bones or spine, covered in fine segmented rings. Evelyn studied it under a magnifying glass for a long time and discovered a sharp, protruding head with a suction like mouthpart. It likely belonged to the same family as earthworms, but unlike them, it was far smaller and capable of drilling into human skin and blood vessels to parasitize or consume the human body.

In that sense, it resembled a leech even more.

Cutting it apart was useless. In the end, Evelyn poured a bit of alcohol over it and set it on fire. As it burned, the worm released a sharp, sour stench. It writhed frantically, then secreted a green liquid from its body. Once it shriveled completely, it finally died.

Calvin's lower leg turned out to be fine. After the nematode was removed, the swelling gradually subsided, and the stitched wound showed no signs of infection. As payment for the treatment, Jessica gave Evelyn a brand new thin blanket.

The torrential rain had weakened into a light drizzle, and more people were venturing outside to salvage supplies. Some used wooden planks to build crude rafts, preparing to head out.

No one knew how many unknown dangers still lurked in the murky floodwaters. The temperature had already dropped to minus twenty degrees Celsius, and the cold wave showed no sign of easing or retreating.

In the neighboring building, a family of four sealed their windows and burned fuel indoors. Three of them died from carbon monoxide poisoning. The only survivor, an elderly man, cried out for help. When neighbors went inside, they emptied the apartment of valuables instead. The old man stood in the hallway, cursing and sobbing. During an argument, he was shoved down the stairs by a strong man and rolled straight into the floodwaters.

The eleventh floor erupted into arguments every day. Residents from the sixth and seventh floors despised each other. Just to fight over a patch of sleeping space, they sometimes came to blows.

Evelyn's door was pounded on several times a day. She eventually decided to play dead and stopped answering altogether. As curses echoed outside, she calmly flipped the pork belly on the grill pan, sprinkling chili powder and cumin over it. The aroma filled the room instantly.

Unit 1101 was packed with more than a dozen people crammed into a two bedroom unit. In the north corner of the living room, Dylan from 701 had not moved all day. Someone thought he was dead and quietly pulled back the blanket covering him. A wave of stench rushed out. Dylan jolted in terror, shoved the person away, and shrank back into the corner, shaking.

"Did you soil the bed again? Dylan, there's a bucket in the bathroom. Use it and dump it out the window. How many times do we have to say this? You're a grown man in your forties. How can you be this disgusting? There are more than ten people living here, and there's no ventilation. Are you trying to suffocate us with the smell?"

Dylan did not argue back. He pulled the blanket tight around himself, covering everything except his head.

His wife and daughter were downstairs gathering firewood. Alone, under everyone's disgusted stares, Dylan kept his head lowered and remained silent.

The man who had pulled back the blanket studied Dylan for a second longer. A flicker of suspicion crossed his eyes. Then he noticed a patch of rotting flesh on Dylan's neck and screamed in terror.

"Dylan is rotting. He stinks. He's sick. Don't you remember what Henry said? After his wife and mother-in-law started acting strange, they began biting people. Dylan is mutating. He's going to eat people."

The man stumbled backward in panic. The others retreated into the corners as well, grabbing whatever weapons they could find and staring at Dylan with fear.

Dylan hurried to explain, but the moment he climbed out from under the blanket, the stench hit them head on. The entire room filled with a sharp, putrid smell. Now they could clearly see the rotting flesh on his neck and the backs of his hands. On closer look, the man who once stood over six feet tall was now gaunt, his cheeks sunken, his body little more than skin and bones.

"Don't come near me. Don't bite me."

"I won't bite anyone. I won't infect you. Don't be afraid."

The more Dylan tried to explain, the more terrified they became. Just then, his wife and daughter returned. They rushed to his side, clumsily trying to cover the decaying flesh and block the smell.

"Kick Dylan's family out. He's mutated. His wife and daughter must be infected too. Throw them out."

Once someone spoke up, the others seemed to find their courage. They all raised their weapons and advanced on Dylan's family, hurling curses as they closed in.

"Get out. Get out right now."

"My dad hasn't mutated," Natalie cried desperately. "He's just sick. He won't infect anyone. He'll get better soon."

Before she could finish, someone swung a clothes drying pole and struck her hard across the back.

"Are you trying to get all of us killed? Get out. Dylan is already rotting and stinking, and he still says he hasn't mutated. What if he bites someone? We have children here. We can't let this family stay."

The others immediately agreed. They raised their weapons, ready to strike. Dylan dropped to his knees with a loud thud and slammed his head against the floor in front of his neighbors.

"Please don't drive us out. I won't bite anyone. I haven't mutated."

"We're all neighbors," his wife sobbed as she knelt beside him. "You can't do this."

But the smell and the sight of Dylan's rotting flesh had already pushed everyone past fear and into hysteria. No one could stay calm anymore. No one had room left for mercy.

Their blankets and belongings were thrown out into the hallway. Natalie was kicked to the ground, curling into herself as she whimpered in pain.

Dylan's eyes turned red with rage. He lunged forward to fight back, but someone kicked him hard. His body flew like a kite, tumbling down the stairs. After hitting the bottom, Dylan spat out a mouthful of black blood, his body curling as it convulsed.

Evelyn was jolted awake by pounding on her door. She pulled on her military coat, grabbed an emergency lantern, and went to open it. Claire stood outside, and Evelyn looked at her in confusion.

"It's the middle of the night. What happened?"

Claire sighed and told her what had happened on the eleventh floor.

"Dylan once helped me. A man followed me on the street, and he chased the creep away. I decided to take them in for now. Evelyn, please take a look at him. He's in really bad shape. I don't think he'll make it."

Evelyn stared at her for a few seconds, then nodded.

"I'll go get my medical kit."

After Claire went downstairs, Evelyn glanced up at the door of 1101.

She put on a wool hat and a mask, picked up her medical kit, and went to Claire's place. In the living room, Dylan lay on the floor. The firewood in the hearth burned fiercely. Natalie and Dylan's wife Margaret cried beside him, while Claire stood in the back with her hands tucked into her sleeves.

"Dr. Foster, please save my dad."

When Natalie saw Evelyn arrive with the medical kit, she rushed forward and dropped to her knees. Evelyn quickly pulled her up.

"Let me take a look at your father first."

In fact, they had knocked on Evelyn's door a few days earlier. But during those days, Evelyn had been playing dead at home. No matter who knocked, she never answered.

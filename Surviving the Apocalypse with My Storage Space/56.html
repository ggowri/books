Chapter 56 Heatwave, An Old Acquaintance

At three in the morning, Evelyn hurried away from the club.

The wind was fierce, sand swirling wildly through the streets. She kept her head down and moved quickly. Every so often, a violent gust would force her to crouch in place, waiting it out before she could continue.

The road ahead was still brutal.

She wiped sand from her goggles and hummed an off-key tune under her breath.

"I told you to run. Go on, run. Why'd you stop? I'll beat you to death. Three, grab what's in his arms. Break his legs."

"Yes, Boss."

Evelyn stumbled and fell. Just as she pushed herself up, voices drifted from around the corner ahead.

She froze, holding her breath.

Faintly, she could hear pained groans.

"Boss, he's got a lot on him. No idea where he got it. We hit the jackpot."

"Enough. Take the stuff. Finish him."

"Don't, don't kill me. Take it all. Just let me go."

The pleading voice was met with harsher blows and cruel laughter.

Evelyn frowned.

That voice sounded familiar.

"Please… let me go. I don't want the stuff anymore…"

Her eyes widened.

Wasn't that the man she had run into at the pharmaceutical company before? What was his name?

Eddie… Thompson?

"Let you go?" The leader laughed. "Dream on. I told you to drop it earlier, but you ran. Made me fall on my face. Three, kick him harder."

One of the men lifted his foot, aiming for Eddie's stomach.

Evelyn's gaze sharpened.

She grabbed a stone from the ground and hurled it.

The rock struck the man squarely in the head. Blood burst instantly, and he collapsed without a sound.

"Who's there? Show yourself. Who the hell hit my guy? Three, get up. Damn, you're useless."

The man cursed as he spun around nervously.

Evelyn stood, brushed sand from her clothes, and walked toward him step by step.

"Looking for someone?" she said coldly.

She still held a stone in her hand. Covered head to toe in sand and gear, her features were impossible to make out. Her voice came out low and rough, unused for hours.

"You hit Three with a rock?"

She glanced at the unmoving body on the ground and shrugged.

"Who else?"

The man's eyes flared with rage. He snatched up a knife and charged.

Evelyn threw the stone again.

Perfect aim. The rock slammed into his head. He staggered twice and dropped flat onto the sand.

"You… why are you here?" Eddie struggled upright, then coughed up blood and nearly collapsed again.

"You still recognize me?" Evelyn asked, surprised.

"I'm a researcher," he wheezed. "Sharp nose. Sharp ears."

"Alright. Then I'm leaving. You should go too. They're not dead."

Even Evelyn could not kill a man with a single stone. They were just unconscious.

"Wait." Eddie crawled forward and pulled his bag protectively into his arms before forcing himself to stand again.

Blood covered his face and clothes. It was a brutal sight.

"What?"

"My hero," he said weakly. "This is the second time you've saved me. I don't even know your name."

Evelyn looked at his bloodied face and twitched slightly.

"Evelyn Foster."

"Evelyn Foster. Got it." He coughed again. "Do you remember mine? I'm Eddie Thompson."

She nodded. "I remember. You didn't go to the base?"

Eddie spat out another mouthful of blood. Pressing a hand to his chest, he answered with difficulty.

"Didn't want to."

Evelyn remembered he'd had a cat.

Judging from his current state, he could barely protect himself. The cat was probably gone.

"My cat died in June," Eddie said quietly, as if reading her thoughts.

Evelyn looked into his exhausted eyes. She wanted to offer some comfort, but the words would not come. The air between them turned strangely still.

Without warning, Eddie picked up one of the bandits' knives.

Then, with a steady hand, he slit the throats of the two unconscious men.

Afterward, he stripped off one of their outer jackets and pulled it over himself.

Evelyn watched the entire scene, her expression unreadable. Silently, she took a small step back.

"Bye," she muttered, then turned and walked away.

Five minutes later, she glanced over her shoulder.

Eddie was following her, about fifteen feet behind.

She stopped, exasperated.

"Why are you following me? Planning to repay kindness with betrayal?"

"I have nowhere to go."

"I'm not taking you in." Her tone turned cold.

"I don't need you to. I won't cause trouble. I just… can I at least know where you live?"

She walked back toward him and gave him a slow once-over.

"No."

"If you let me stay somewhere near you, I can tell you a secret."

She laughed in disbelief. "You think your secret is that valuable?"

"It is." He swallowed. "Our company's vice president left the country before everything collapsed. She never came back. I know where she lives. There's a hidden room in her house. Plenty of supplies."

Evelyn studied him without blinking.

Eddie met her gaze calmly.

After a full minute, she let out a soft chuckle.

"Not interested."

"She married a man overseas years ago. Over there, she obtained a firearm license and bought weapons. I know about the hidden room. I just can't open it."

One of Evelyn's brows lifted. "And how do you know that?"

He fell silent.

She snorted and turned to leave.

He panicked. "She… she had feelings for me. Used work as an excuse to lure me to her house. That's how I found out."

Evelyn stared at him.

Was this something she was supposed to hear?

"You look over thirty. And you're skinny. Not that tall either. And your face…" She paused deliberately. "You sure you're not messing with me?"

Eddie sputtered and coughed up another mouthful of blood. Evelyn immediately stepped back two paces to avoid being splattered.

"I'm twenty-nine," he snapped hoarsely. "I was twenty-six when she made a move. I'm five foot ten. And I'm not ugly."

The last sentence sounded practically shouted.

Silence settled between them again.

After a moment, Evelyn cleared her throat and scratched her ear awkwardly.

"Alright. Keep it down. You want to attract more trouble?"

He looked aggrieved. "You just attacked me personally."

She coughed again.

"…Did she succeed?" she asked finally, unable to suppress the curiosity in her eyes.

Eddie clutched his chest like he might actually cough up more blood from sheer frustration.

"No. Of course not. I'm not a gigolo. She was almost fifty." He took a shaky breath. "Anyway, I've laid out my bargaining chip. Are you making the deal or not?"

Evelyn considered for a few seconds.

"Fine," she said at last. "But if you're lying, or if you try anything, I'll kill you."

Eddie raised his right hand solemnly.

"I swear on my face and my height. If I lie or try to hurt you, may I be disfigured or lose eight inches."

Evelyn gave him a long look.

"…You're already disfigured."

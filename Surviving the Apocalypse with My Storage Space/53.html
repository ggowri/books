Chapter 53 Heatwave, The Cellar: Part 2

When they set out again, it was already one twenty in the morning.

Everyone was exhausted. Their legs felt like lead. But the thought of all that grain waiting in the cellar lit a fire under them. Fatigue faded beneath raw determination.

On the return trip, they ran into trouble.

"Drop what's on your backs and I'll spare your lives."

The man at the front leveled a gun at Calvin's head. His narrow, triangular eyes gleamed with excitement.

Four lackeys stood behind him, each gripping a large machete. Their expressions were vicious, twisted. A sour stench clung to them.

"Boss, they're carrying rice. I can smell it."

"And cabbage. Potatoes too. Boss, we just hit the jackpot."

"Boss, there are two kids." A skinny man with shifty eyes pointed his blade at Ray and Evan, his voice brimming with glee.

Then he stepped in front of Evelyn, his gaze sliding over her in a way that made the air turn cold.

"This one smells good too. Boss, it's a woman."

The leader's eyes shifted to Evelyn. He chuckled and walked toward her, gun still in hand.

"Take off her hat and mask."

"Got it."

The skinny man reached out.

In that split second, Evelyn moved.

She yanked the crossbow from behind her back and fired. The bolt pierced straight through the leader's throat. She did not hesitate. A second arrow followed instantly, ensuring he dropped for good.

At the exact moment Evelyn struck, Calvin reacted. He kicked away the man pressing a blade to his neck. The dagger hidden in his sleeve flashed once across the attacker's throat.

Two seconds was all it took for the man to collapse.

Their coordination was seamless. Ray and Evan did not panic or interfere. They retreated to the side without a word, giving Evelyn and Calvin clear space to counterattack.

Five bodies lay motionless on the ground.

Evelyn's expression was ice cold. She picked up the gun from the leader, checked it briefly, then tossed it to Calvin.

"It's real," he said after examining it. "But there are no bullets."

There was faint mockery in his tone. He handed it back to her.

"They must have used this empty gun to commit plenty of crimes."

They grabbed the machetes and kept moving. The incident barely stirred their emotions.

When they reached Block D, Calvin stopped them.

"Looks like plenty of people in Elaris never made it to the base," he said seriously. "Next time we go out, we stay sharper."

"We need to move everything from Everpeace Monastery as soon as possible," Evelyn added. "Before someone else finds them."

Calvin nodded. "Agreed. And tonight, we take a different route."

No one objected. They split up and went home.

After an entire night of hauling, Evelyn was beyond tired. She was starving.

The moment she shut her door, she turned on the air conditioner, unloaded the supplies, then pulled two boxed meals from her spatial storage and devoured them.

Her clothes clung to her skin, soaked through with sweat. Fighting off exhaustion, she forced herself into the bathroom for a quick shower and changed into clean clothes.

Under the bright bathroom light, she noticed deep rope marks carved into her shoulders. Without a proper frame pack, they had tied the sacks with hemp rope and carried them across their backs. She dabbed some ointment on the raw skin.

She did not even bother organizing the supplies.

She collapsed onto the bed and fell asleep instantly. She needed rest. Nothing else mattered.

When Evelyn finally woke up again, it was three in the afternoon.

The humidifier had run dry. The air felt tight and brittle against her face. She quickly refilled it, splashed water on her skin, and put on a hydrating mask.

Only then did she begin sorting through the supplies they had carried back from Everpeace Monastery the night before.

She trimmed away the rotten outer leaves from the cabbages and stored the rest in her space.

For a long time, Evelyn sat there thinking, unsure how to repay Ray and the others for sharing the supplies this time.

Maybe she could stash a few cases of water somewhere, then "accidentally" lead them to it.

She rubbed her chin. It could work. But it would look too deliberate. Better to wait for the right moment.

The next several days passed in nonstop hauling. Five days later, they had emptied the cellar completely.

On the final trip, Evelyn was the last to leave. After the other three climbed out, she turned back and swept the remaining books into her space. Cultural treasures like those were priceless. She had been tempted the moment she first saw them.

Once the cellar was sealed again, the four of them shouldered their loads and headed home.

This time, everyone was smiling.

With food came survival.

That night, they gathered once more at Calvin's apartment for a meeting.

"We have enough supplies to last a long time," Calvin said seriously. "So no more unnecessary trips outside. The fewer risks we take, the better. We don't want to run into bandits again or draw attention."

He paused.

"And one more thing. If you see someone who looks pitiful, stay alert. Don't let having food make you careless. Ray, Evan, remember this. It's not just adults. In times like this, the more helpless someone looks, especially the elderly and children, the more dangerous they can be."

The two boys nodded solemnly.

"Second," Calvin continued, "starting tomorrow, you two train with me. Self-defense. Evie, you're already capable, probably better than any of us. But if you're interested, you can join. What I'll teach you isn't for show. It's meant to kill. If you don't have a weapon, you use speed and precision to end it fast."

Without hesitation, the two boys dropped to their knees and bowed. Just like that, the apprenticeship was sealed.

"I know you've wanted to learn for a while," Calvin said as he pulled them to their feet, patting their shoulders. "Before, we were focused on finding food. Now that's temporarily settled, it's time to build strength and skill."

Thinking of Calvin's speed and lethal efficiency, Evelyn decided she would learn as well.

Over the next few days, they cleared out an apartment on the eleventh floor and turned it into a makeshift gym.

"Calvin, tomorrow night I'm going to Elaris University to look for medicine," Evelyn said one evening. "Do you want to come?"

Calvin knew she had studied medicine. And from what he understood about her, she never acted without preparation. He agreed immediately.

The next night, they left at nine thirty again.

It took more than an hour to walk from Willow Heights to Elaris University. With the roads in ruins, it felt even longer. By the time they arrived, everyone was exhausted.

This time, Evelyn led.

She guided them into the campus.

Looking at the familiar yet alien grounds, her heart stirred despite herself.

The cafeteria. The library. The dormitories. The administrative building.

The last time she had come to collect supplies, everything had been green and vibrant, full of life.

Now there was only desolation. Empty buildings standing like skeletons.

"The laboratory building is up ahead," she said quietly. "If we're lucky, we might find something useful."

The sand crunching beneath their boots echoed through the hallway.

Evelyn led them straight to the top floor.

"Evie, this is your alma mater?" Ray asked softly.

She nodded. "Yeah. It's one of the best schools around."

She pushed open the lab door.

A wave of decay rushed out to meet them.

Thankfully, there were no signs that snakes had invaded this building. Rows of instruments and glass containers filled the room.

A flicker of nostalgia passed through Evelyn's eyes.

Chapter 96 Hunting Together

Evelyn wanted to sharpen her skills. Ever since she drank Rowan's blood, she had the strange feeling her healing ability had improved. Not to his absurd level, but still.

A few days ago, she had accidentally cut her hand while cooking. By the next morning, the wound had already scabbed over.

They agreed to go together. The next day at eleven in the morning, after breakfast, they locked the door and set off.

Evelyn followed behind him as usual. It was tiring, but she could keep up.

Rowan led her over two mountains and into a massive valley.

"Wait here. I'll drive them over."

Evelyn suspected he thought she was too slow. Just days ago, he had said he would take her hunting.

Typical. Men changed their minds faster than the weather.

She did not argue. A short break was welcome. After Rowan left, she took out her crossbow. Since she was here, she had no intention of returning empty handed.

A few minutes later, he came back.

"They'll be here soon."

Evelyn was curious. "What are they?"

"Wild boars."

Shattered Ridge really was overrun with them. After all this hunting, there were still so many left.

"How many?"

"More than twenty. Can you handle it alone?" He suddenly held out his hand.

She blinked. "What?"

"Jerky."

Evelyn rolled her eyes. Eat, eat, eat. Why not just eat yourself to death?

"You're making me handle them alone? You're not helping?"

Rowan tore open a strip of jerky and tossed it into his mouth. "It's only twenty. Do you really need my help?"

She almost challenged him to a fight.

"No. Please step aside gracefully and don't interfere with my performance."

The sound reached her ears. She raised her crossbow.

The herd was massive. There was a huge boar that was clearly the leader, along with several much smaller piglets.

She counted quickly. Twenty eight in total.

The grunting grew louder as they approached. Take down the leader first. Evelyn aimed at the largest boar and fired twice in quick succession.

Perfect shots. Both bolts sank into its neck. It collapsed, squealing and struggling before finally going still.

While the herd fell into chaos, she fired again. She focused on the biggest ones first. The smaller ones would be easier afterward.

The boars shrieked wildly, charging in every direction across the snow. Evelyn's face was calm and focused as she kept shooting.

Behind her, Rowan watched her back. The scent around her felt stronger today. He could not quite place it. Bamboo. Trees. Osmanthus blossoms.

Strange. Why did she carry so many different scents?

Evelyn began moving forward. She felt it too, that rush he must feel when hunting. It unsettled her, yet set her blood on fire.

Her hands were still quick. She had not grown weaker.

If anything, she was stronger than she had been a few months ago.

The herd spotted her and decided to charge together. Evelyn remained steady, even pulling bolts free from fallen boars to continue firing.

Today she wore a red sweater beneath her winter coat. The red scarf at her neck made her flushed cheeks even brighter. She breathed hard, then released the final shot with clean precision.

The last piglet dropped.

Without pausing, she retrieved her bolts and stored all the carcasses into her space.

She turned to Rowan. "Head back, or keep going?"

Rowan finished the last strip of jerky and pointed behind them.

"Keep going. The temperature's about to drop. In a few days, we won't be able to come out."

Evelyn raised a brow. "How do you know it's getting colder?"

"My body can feel it."

Of course it can. Show off.

They continued forward. Evelyn took out some jerky for herself, but the moment it appeared, Rowan claimed more than half.

She shot him a look of pure disdain.

As they climbed higher, they reached the summit. The moment Evelyn stepped up onto the ridge, she spotted several wild mountain goats.

"Get more of those." Rowan's eyes lit up at the sight of them.

She ignored him and raised her crossbow.

The wind was strong at the peak. Evelyn pulled her mask up tighter. Frost quickly gathered on her eyelashes.

Her stamina was starting to fade. Goats were not as clumsy as boars. They were fast, agile, and their horns could do real damage.

She had to dodge their charges while firing as quickly as possible.

This would be the last hunt of the winter. Evelyn wanted to finish strong. She was breathing hard, surrounded and pressured by several goats, yet she remained calm. At one point, she even switched to her handgun to finish a target cleanly.

When it was over, she collapsed onto the snow and did not move.

She was exhausted.

Rowan handled the cleanup. When he was done, he dug the bullets out of the carcasses with a dagger.

"Thirteen in total." From his tone, anyone would think he had killed them himself.

Evelyn laughed softly. She pushed herself up, hands on her hips, catching her breath before storing the prey. She took the crossbow bolts and bullets he handed back to her and prepared to head home.

They moved quickly downhill. Evelyn had to half jog just to keep up with Rowan's pace.

The moment they stepped into the yard, all the tension drained from her body.

"Take them out. I'll process them," he said.

She glanced at the sky. There was no need to rush. "We can deal with it later."

"I'm not cold. You hunted, I clean. Fair."

He was stubborn. She could not out argue him. With a sigh, she released the thirteen goats from her space.

"Start with these. I'll heat water."

The first thing she needed was a hot shower. The smell of boar and goat clung heavily to her clothes and skin. The second she removed her mask, the odor hit her. It was overwhelming.

Sprig and Fallon leaped around excitedly when they saw them return. Evelyn added more wood to the fireplace and lit the stove in the kitchen.

The generator kicked on, powering the water heater. She glanced outside. Rowan had already finished processing one goat.

Was he a butcher in his past life?

She set a pot of ribs to stew. After a full day of hunting, her energy was drained. She was starving.

She had noticed something else lately. Not only was she stronger, her appetite had grown too. Every change traced back to that bowl of his blood.

After washing up, she dried her hair and left off her hat. The cabin was warm, waves of heat rolling from the fireplace.

Dinner was ready. Evelyn opened the cabin door.

"Time to eat."

Rowan immediately set down his knife and followed her inside. After washing his hands, she noticed how thin they were, bones prominent beneath pale, almost yellow tinged skin. There was barely any color in his palms.

As a medical student, Evelyn had heard plenty about gene editing cases.

Years ago, the research had been halted because of the ethical and biological concerns involved.

What were the limits of an edited human body? How long could they live? What were their weaknesses?

Those questions were far beyond her field. In her previous life, she had never connected people with heterochromia to gene edited humans.

No one would believe that altering genes could change the color of someone's eyes.

Evelyn found herself staring at Rowan.

Was he a successful prototype?

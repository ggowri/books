Chapter 3 Stockpile: Part 2

Evelyn suddenly remembered that she still needed cat litter and sand. After the torrential rains, it wasn't just power that went out. Water systems collapsed too. Sewage from the underground pipes had backed up, flooding homes with filth.

Cat litter or sand would be essential for handling bathroom needs once that happened again. She decided to place a bulk order from a local cat litter manufacturer in Elaris and had it delivered directly to her warehouse.

As for sand, she'd get that later from the outskirts, along with cement and red bricks. Every drainage point had to be sealed up, especially the toilets. She would never allow sewage to flood her home again.

When it came to weapons, Evelyn could only sigh.

It wasn't possible to legally buy sharpened knives or controlled blades online. She wanted real tools for self-defense. If she could get firearms, even better. In her last life, she had seen what people with guns were capable of. The things they did... they weren't just cruel. They were monstrous.

Her eyes darkened.

Human nature was built on the foundation of civilization and order. Once that foundation cracked, when order collapsed, the darkness buried deep in people's hearts was far more terrifying than any wild beast.

On the third day, Evelyn got up at five in the morning and went straight to the warehouse.

It was still cold in January. She wore a thick down jacket, mask, and hat, moving with purpose and focus.

Today was meat delivery day. She'd placed orders at three slaughterhouses. Tons of pork, beef, and lamb arrived, along with large amounts of chicken, duck, and goose.

Each batch of fresh meat was packed into foam boxes before being stored in her space. Inside, she organized everything neatly. With just a thought, she could see the full inventory in her mind—clear and orderly.

At noon, she visited multiple pharmacies. Ten in total. After that, she considered her medicine list complete.

Then she went to a herbal shop and purchased a range of medicinal herbs for anything that might prove useful in the years to come.

In the afternoon, the fruit and vegetable deliveries arrived. She packed everything into foam boxes again before storing them in the space.

Just before heading home, the cat litter truck showed up at the warehouse—several tons, all delivered smoothly.

On her way back, Evelyn's phone rang. It was her dormmate, Sophie Miller.

Evelyn hadn't told her roommates about her withdrawal from school. Only her academic advisor knew.

"Evelyn, when are you coming back to school?"

She didn't have a close relationship with her dormmates, but Sophie was the only one who had added her on social media.

Thinking of her belongings still at the dorm, Evelyn paused for a moment, then made a decision. She'd go pack up tomorrow.

"I've withdrawn from school."

There was a pause on the other end. "You dropped out?"

"Yeah."

Sophie lowered her voice. "What happened?"

"Just family stuff. I'll stop by the dorm tomorrow to get my things."

"You're moving out? Are you taking a long break from school?"

Evelyn didn't answer right away. She pulled into the complex's parking lot, grabbed her things, and headed upstairs. In the hallway, she passed a few familiar neighbors. They exchanged polite nods and kept walking.

"Sophie, you're from Elaris, right?"

"Nope, I'm from Altaris."

Altaris? Evelyn remembered now. In her last life, there had been a mid-sized survivor base in Altaris.

"Why do you ask? Planning a trip?" Sophie launched into a cheerful rant about Altaris's famous foods and scenic spots.

"No. Just curious."

After hanging up, Evelyn frowned slightly. Should she warn Sophie?

Once home, she locked the door behind her and kicked off her shoes. Her body instantly relaxed.

The contractor was scheduled to arrive tomorrow to install the extra security doors. Between that and sealing the windows and balcony, it was going to cost a small fortune.

All the windows in Evelyn's home had to be replaced. She chose heavy-duty tempered glass, added security bars on the outside, and applied heat-insulating film and panels on top of that.

For the doors, she picked the most expensive brand—thick and solid. She was turning her home into a fortress. Once it was sealed, no one would be able to force their way in.

Looking at the neatly organized supplies in her storage space, her nerves finally began to settle.

The next day, she went to Elaris University.

She timed her visit while her roommates were in class. Stepping into the familiar dorm, seeing her bed and her belongings still in place, Evelyn sat at her desk for a moment. A complicated wave of emotions washed over her.

At sixteen, she'd scored a near-perfect score on the SAT and earned early admission to Elaris University's prestigious medical program. Back then, she was practically a legend on campus.

She packed all of her things into her space, then turned to look at the dorm one last time. She had no regrets or attachments, just a sense of relief.

Lately, Evelyn had been surviving on only three or four hours of sleep a night. When she wasn't buying supplies, she was at the warehouse receiving deliveries.

Her online orders were now arriving in waves. She used a rolling cart to haul everything inside, batch by batch.

Once the workers came to install the doors, she had the balcony and windows upgraded at the same time. With two layers of steel-reinforced security doors, even the installers joked, asking if she was hiding gold inside.

Evelyn kept things as low-profile as she could, but with so many workers coming and going, it didn't escape the attention of the neighbors.

Most were just curious and minded their own business. A few nosy ones pressed harder with questions, but she deflected them easily.

After a few more days, the home renovations were finished. Evelyn returned to inspect the results—shatterproof windows, reinforced doors, a sealed-off balcony. For the first time in years, she felt truly safe.

She pulled out her notebook to review her list and found there were still plenty of gaps.

Before tackling that, she made a trip to the hardware and trade market. There, she picked up everything she'd been missing: generators, power cords, extension boards, transformers, water pumps, hoses, axes, saws, entrenching tools, coal stoves, a glacier thermometer, sprayers, outdoor shower kits, and a mini tiller.

Out in a small town near Elaris, she bought 20 tons of coal and 20 tons of firewood. Coal was priced at 100 dollars per ton. Dry firewood went for 50. Diesel and gasoline, though, couldn't be purchased in bulk without drawing attention. For those, she planned to visit Cherry Hollow.

Over the next few days, Evelyn split her time between online shopping and collecting deliveries. In her spare hours, she cooked in the kitchen. Everything she made, she sealed into plastic trays and stored in her space. She could make about a hundred meals a day.

Evelyn had decent cooking skills. After the apocalypse, even a hot meal became a luxury. The scent of food could easily attract danger, or death.

People killed for scraps, just for a mouthful of warmth.

Her space couldn't hold living creatures. Anything alive had to be butchered before going in.

Even though she didn't care much for sweets or snacks, Evelyn still stocked up on a wide variety. They'd be useful someday.

Looking at the remaining balance in her bank account, she decided it was time to head to Cherry Hollow.

It was mid-February. The spring festival had just passed, but the temperature in Elaris had already soared to 28°C. Evelyn felt a chill—not from the heat, but from a deep sense of unease. She made a note of it on paper.

Cherry Hollow was an old, narrow street tucked away in Elaris. No one there asked for names or identities, but everyone wore masks, sunglasses, and gloves.

Here, the currency wasn't cash. It was gold.

Evelyn had exchanged for gold bars at the bank in preparation.

In addition to diesel and gasoline, she hoped to get her hands on some self-defense weapons.

The deal went smoothly. In this place, no one cared who you were. If you had the money, you could buy anything.

She secured drums of diesel and gasoline. And she didn't stop there.

She also picked up several boxes of sharpened knives, thirty military-grade crossbows, and three thousand bolts.

By the end of February, Elaris's temperature had broken 30°C, shattering a century-old record.

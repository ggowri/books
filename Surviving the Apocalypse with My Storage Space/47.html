Chapter 47 Heatwave, Scavenging: Part 2

Back home, Evelyn pulled out two safes.

She had absolutely no idea how to crack a code, so she had no choice but to go old-school. With a wrench and a screwdriver, she pried them open by force.

Half an hour later, Evelyn stared at the opened safes, speechless.

Seriously? Do all rich people like hiding gold bars at home?

She picked one up. A bank logo and a serial number were stamped on the surface, shining faintly in the light.

After admiring it for a moment, Evelyn tucked both safes into her space.

To be honest, this wasn't what she expected at all. These were safes from a pharmaceutical company—she thought she'd find guns, ammo, or some rare medicine inside.

She didn't expect both safes to be filled with gold. Not that she was complaining. She liked gold. She just wasn't sure if she'd ever get a chance to actually use it.

Oh well. She'd keep it. Worst-case scenario, it could be part of her burial treasure.

The temperature kept climbing, but Evelyn still went out for a couple of hours every day. She had already picked through most of Willow Heights, so now she had to try her luck in neighborhoods farther away.

Despite the heat, plenty of people were still out scavenging.

Evelyn put on the most ragged sun-protection gear she had and carried a worn burlap sack in her hand.

In another era, she could've passed for the king of beggars.

Up ahead, a little girl had collapsed on the ground. Some people rushed over to check on her, but others looked away and walked on.

"Don't go near her. It's a trap."

Calvin walked over carrying a sack. Wendy clung to his back, her arms wrapped around his neck. He held a battered umbrella over them, but it didn't help much. The heat was suffocating. Wendy looked groggy and worn out.

"The kid's bait?"

Calvin nodded. "I ran into something like this the last time I went out for supplies. There's usually a gang nearby. They use the child to lure people into an alley, then grab them. You can imagine what happens next."

So that explained it. The last time Calvin came back battered and bruised, it must've been because of one of these traps.

But Evelyn wasn't the kind to fall for something like that. She didn't have that much empathy, and her kindness didn't extend to strangers lying on the street. She hadn't even considered walking over.

People like Calvin, though—the ones who carried justice in their bones—were the easiest targets. The ones most likely to get tricked, or guilted into making the wrong call.

The two of them headed to a nearby upscale residential complex. There were already a lot of scavengers there. Two men had gotten into a fistfight over a bag of dried tofu.

"We should try the older complex next door. Too many people here. We won't get in."

Calvin agreed. Wendy, still clinging to his back, peeked shyly at Evelyn.

Evelyn caught her gaze.

Wendy quickly turned her head, embarrassed.

Evelyn reached out and lightly tapped the girl's fingertips.

Wendy grinned, showing off a row of tiny, baby teeth.

This area was on slightly higher ground. The floodwaters had only reached the fifth floor.

Evelyn and Calvin split up, each heading into a different building.

Now that more people were out searching for supplies, Evelyn didn't want to take any chances. She strapped her short-handled Tang blade to her lower back.

If anyone tried anything shady, the sight of the blade would be warning enough. Better to scare them off than get into a fight and cause a scene.

She had just stepped into the building when she noticed two people following close behind.

Evelyn ignored them and began combing through each floor, one by one.

When Evelyn reached Unit 701, she tried the door and found it locked. Thinking there might be survivors inside, she went next door instead. After searching the neighboring apartment, she stepped onto the balcony and saw a skeleton lying on 701's balcony. Realizing something, she turned back.

By now, Evelyn's lock-picking skills were extremely polished. It took her less than five seconds to open the door. She slipped inside and closed it behind her.

The dead snake on the floor had rotted into a pile of black sludge. White maggots writhed inside the decaying flesh, and clouds of red-headed blowflies buzzed loudly above it.

Aside from the skeleton on the balcony, there were no other human remains in the apartment. The owner had likely died during the period of torrential rains. The rice container in the kitchen was still full. Potatoes had sprouted and grown tall, while the other vegetables had completely rotted.

She also found two bags of noodles, two bundles of vermicelli, several packets of salt on the spice rack, and a jar of dried chilies.

She was focused on gathering supplies when faint noises drifted in from outside. Evelyn's eyes turned cold, and her hands moved even faster.

She had collected quite a lot from this apartment. Before leaving, she moved the skeleton from the balcony into the bedroom.

The moment she opened the door, she lifted her foot and kicked the man who had been eavesdropping outside, sending him flying.

She recognized them immediately. They were the two men who had followed her upstairs earlier.

Gripping her blade, she was about to strike when the man who had dodged aside pulled off the sack and rope he was carrying. The other scrambled to his feet and drew a hidden knife, trying to block her.

Evelyn drew her blade. For a brief moment, neither side made the first move.

"So it really is a woman," one of them sneered.

The man with the knife rolled his wrist, then suddenly lunged at her.

As he rushed forward, Evelyn sidestepped and drove her blade straight into his back.

"Ah…" he screamed.

She tried to pull the knife out, but the blade was lodged in his bone.

A strange stench of decay clung to both men. Their faces were twisted, their mouths crooked, their eyes unfocused. Their bodies twitched uncontrollably, and their skin tone looked unnaturally pale.

Evelyn narrowed her eyes. Without hesitation, she summoned her crossbow and fired.

The man struck by the bolt collapsed to the floor. His body shuddered a few times, then went still.

She aimed again at the other man. He was still frozen in shock over the crossbow that had appeared out of thin air in her hands. Before he could react, a bolt pierced his throat.

Both of them showed signs of prion disease and mad cow disease, illnesses that were highly likely to develop after consuming human flesh.

Fresh blood splattered across Evelyn's body. She fought down the urge to vomit, retrieved her bolts, picked up the knife and the sack, and left the building.

On her way back, she ran into several people. When they saw the bloodstains on her clothes, they panicked and hurried away.

The first thing Evelyn did after returning home was strip off her sun-protection jacket and throw it into the trash. The blade and crossbow needed to be disinfected, and she needed a thorough shower as well.

After washing up, water still dripped from her short hair. Her bare face was delicate and striking. Beneath her slip dress, her figure was slim and well-shaped, her calves long and graceful.

Staring at her reflection, Evelyn let out a shaky breath. Going out now meant danger everywhere.

When she thought about those two men, her gaze turned icy again.

Prion disease. Mad cow disease. Just hearing those names was enough to make anyone shudder. And now they had appeared right in front of her.

Remembering the blood that had splashed onto her jacket, Evelyn hurried to the trash can, took out a lighter, and burned the clothes inside.

The next day, the temperature climbed to forty-eight degrees Celsius. It was already impossible to go outside. Evelyn stared at the blinding white sky and carried the flowerpots from her balcony back into the living room.

Calvin had reinstalled the protective gate downstairs. More and more robbers were coming out at night. He had been forcing himself to search for supplies despite the heat, until Wendy collapsed from heatstroke. Only then did he stop going out.

Wood swept down by the floodwaters downstairs would suddenly burst into flames. This phenomenon happened almost every day now.

Once again, Evelyn entered full stay-at-home mode.

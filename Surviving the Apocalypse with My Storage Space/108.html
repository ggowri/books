Chapter 108 Moving into the Apartment Building

“Once the base tapped into groundwater, they built a hydroelectric station and set up wind turbines as well, so electricity isn’t in short supply here. If you move into better accommodation, you can have power all day, every day. Of course, both electricity and water have to be paid for. During the first month, you can use supplies instead. From the second month onwards, the cost is deducted automatically from the points you earn.”

Mercer led the three of them into a lift and took them straight up to the sixth floor. Inside, Evelyn glanced up and noticed there were surveillance cameras overhead.

“Captain Mercer, does the base have internet access?”

He nodded. “Most of the signal towers were washed away after the torrential rains, and then came the earthquake. Later, the base’s research staff built a new network and put up fresh towers. For various reasons the signal is still poor, so at the moment only the internal network is available.”

Evelyn understood.

Once they stepped out onto the sixth floor, she felt as though she had walked into the HR department of a company. Quite a few people were working there, and it was warm inside the building thanks to the heating.

Captain Mercer led them to a door and knocked. A voice from inside called, “Come in.” He pushed the door open, and the four of them entered in turn.

“Director, I’ve brought two survivors to apply for resident permits.”

Director Walsh was a young man of about thirty, ordinary-looking, with black-rimmed glasses. He looked up and studied Evelyn and Adrian for a moment before beginning to ask for their names, ages, and hometowns.

“Where do you want to live?”

“The apartment buildings in the South Court.”

One of his brows lifted. Mercer and Eddie both looked surprised as well. They had assumed Evelyn would choose a single room in the west, not set her sights on the apartment blocks in the south.

Director Walsh entered their details into the computer, then asked whether they wanted to rent one room between them.

“Two.”

The moment Evelyn answered, he looked up at her again. “I thought you said you were a couple.”

She said nothing. It was the sort of question she had no wish to answer.

“What are you paying with?”

Evelyn handed over the bag she had been carrying on her back. He took it, opened the zip, and looked inside, then lifted his eyes to her once more.

“What you’ve brought is enough to rent two flats for three months. From the fourth month onwards, points will be deducted. An apartment costs three hundred points a month. This sheet explains how points are earned and how the system works. Take it back and read it properly.”

Evelyn pulled one sheet from the stack on the top right-hand corner of the desk.

“What can you do?”

“I’ve got medical training. Acupuncture, surgery, even delivering babies.”

Director Walsh gave her another look. “And him?”

“He was in the army. Only recently left.”

“You’ll report to the Medical Division. He’ll go to the hunting crews.”

He handed them another two sheets, each with a route map to the relevant department.

“Your resident permits are ready. South Court, Building 10, sixth floor. Here are your key cards and keys. Your rooms are next to each other.”

“If you lose your permit, replacing it costs fifty points. Also, permits cannot be lent out. You’ll need them to enter or leave the base, and all points you earn will be credited directly to the card.”

The so-called resident permit was essentially like a university meal card or a bank card, a magnetic card that also served as proof of identity.

“You are to report to your respective departments at ten o’clock tomorrow morning. No lateness, no leaving early. Anyone who refuses management or discipline will have their property confiscated and be expelled from the base. And one more thing, dress properly. Torn clothes are fine. Dirty clothes are not.”

“All knives and controlled tools on your person must be handed over. Fighting is forbidden inside the base, and so is any kind of illegal behaviour.”

Everything important Evelyn owned was in her space. Director Walsh checked what they had and confiscated the shovel.

“You may go.”

“Director Walsh, thank you for your trouble.” the Captain stepped forward and shook his hand. Evelyn saw him slip a few sweets into Walsh’s palm.

“Not at all.”

The three of them followed Mercer back downstairs. He looked as though he wanted to say something, though since he did not ask, Evelyn naturally pretended not to notice.

“Quite the generous move, Miss Evelyn.”

When the lift doors opened, he said it in an unreadable tone. Evelyn simply gave him a modest little nod, neither denying nor admitting anything.

“Let Eddie take you over to the South Court. The hunting crews still have matters to deal with, so I’m off. Adrian, when you come to report tomorrow, remember to find me.”

Adrian gave a quiet grunt, and the Captain left. Once he was out of earshot, Eddie clicked his tongue twice and looked Evelyn up and down all over again.

“I genuinely thought you’d had a rough time, but there you are, renting a flat and paying three months up front. Evelyn, what exactly did you give him?”

She smiled. “A secret.”

“Fine, keep it to yourself.” Eddie waved a hand. “Come on, I’ll take you south.”

“You seriously don’t have decent clothes anymore?” Evelyn glanced at the ragged coat hanging off him.

“You’ve no idea how expensive clothes are in the base. I do have better ones, but when you go out on missions, you dress in tatters on purpose. What’s the point of wearing anything decent? And look at you, talking about me. Have you seen yourself? Even beggars look tidier.”

After trading barbs for a bit, the two of them turned at the same time to look at Adrian. He said nothing at all and simply carried on walking.

The apartment buildings in South Court sounded grander than they really were. In truth, they were not much different from an ordinary one-bedroom flat before the apocalypse.

The advantage of living there was simple. No restrictions on electricity, no restrictions on water, a small balcony, and a private bathroom.

When the three of them reached Building 10, an administrator stopped them at once. Evelyn showed her resident permit, and only then were they allowed through.

“I need to register you. Let me have your permits.”

He took both cards, scanned them, then pulled out a ledger and recorded their identities and room numbers before letting them go upstairs.

The apartment block had ten floors in total. Evelyn and Adrian were on the sixth. There was a lift, but the moment the three of them stepped inside, they caught the smell of mildew.

“Where you live now, is there not even a lift?”

Eddie shook his head. “Those rooms are only five square metres, and there’s nothing in them except a bed. There’s a communal bath on the ground floor, but everyone only gets to use it once a week. Each floor has a shared loo. Luckily it’s a large one, otherwise the place would be unbearable.”

“If the electricity is restricted there, how do you manage at night?”

“It’s limited during the day. There’s power from ten at night until eight in the morning. I traded for an electric heater, so don’t worry, I’m not freezing to death.”

Ding.

The lift doors opened.

601 and 602.

Evelyn had 601. Once she opened the door, she found it much better than she had expected.

There was a one-and-a-half-metre wooden bed. No wardrobe, but the place was reasonably clean. The bathroom was tiny, though manageable. Evelyn turned on the tap over the washbasin and found the water somewhat cloudy.

“It’s groundwater. The quality’s terrible. A lot of people’s teeth have already started going bad.”

The so-called balcony was really no more than an iron cage bolted outside the window for hanging clothes.

“Eddie, I’ve still got a few things. I can spare some for you.”

He waved the offer away. “I’m doing all right. Besides, I’m a man. I should earn my own keep. A bit of hardship is nothing.”

“Tell Officer Shaw and the others. If there’s time, we should all meet up.”

Eddie thumped his chest. “Leave it to me.”

“For now, I’ll take you to buy bedding.”

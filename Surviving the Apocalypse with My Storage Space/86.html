Chapter 86 Surviving Alone: Part 10

Evelyn kept inching deeper into the cavern. The farther she went, the colder it became. The air felt thinner too. She quickly pulled off her mask and took a few steady breaths.

She swapped her boots for hiking shoes with anti slip soles. Better traction meant better odds. Once her breathing steadied, she put the mask back on and continued forward.

Damp, icy drafts rolled through the cavern in waves. Even through her insulated coat, she could feel the chill seeping in.

Moss clung to the stone walls. Even with gloves and a mask, she knew better than to touch it carelessly. Mold spores and fungal growth in a place like this could cause serious infections. Not just a cold, but kidney infections or respiratory infections severe enough to kill.

She slid on her night vision goggles. By now, the faint daylight from the cave entrance was useless. After about thirty meters, the ground abruptly dropped away.

It was a massive pit.

Lying flat on her stomach, she peered over the edge. Below, dark water sat unnervingly still. The basin was enormous. On the far side, stalactites blocked her view. She could not see where the water led.

There were natural stone ledges along the wall. She could climb down.

She hesitated for a few seconds, then decided to go for it.

Carefully, she edged down along the rock face. From the cave floor to the water below was about ten meters.

A thick, fishy stench rose from the water. Just before reaching the surface, she released a kayak from her space. It settled quietly on the motionless water. Using the paddle for balance, she stepped onto it and crouched low once she was steady.

She waited a few minutes, watching for any movement. The water remained eerily still.

Only then did she begin paddling slowly toward the far side.

The stalactites overhead were breathtaking. She had seen pictures of caverns online before, but witnessing it in person was something else entirely. Nature's craftsmanship was beyond imagination.

"Hey!"

She shouted again.

This time the echo boomed back louder and lasted much longer.

Adjusting her flashlight, she continued paddling.

A few hundred meters in, the water split into several branches. Each branch seemed to lead into another cavern. She aimed for the widest one.

The deeper she went, the colder it grew. She sneezed, her body shivering despite herself.

The place was damp and eerie. Even someone as bold as Evelyn felt a flicker of unease.

Then she spotted something ahead. A faint green glow.

She narrowed her eyes. It was too far away to make out clearly.

She paddled faster.

As she drew closer, she saw a large stone platform, roughly twenty square meters wide. On top of it lay seven or eight skeletons.

The green glow was coming from them.

She shone her flashlight over the bones, studying them carefully. Judging by the degree of whitening and decay, they had likely been dead for at least three or four years.

Nearby were several backpacks. Using her trekking pole, she dragged one closer.

"Sorry to disturb you, seniors. No offense meant."

She glanced inside the bag.

A rusted dagger. Empty water bottles. A flashlight. A box of cold medicine.

On the ground were life vests and inflatable swim rings. Evelyn studied the still water and guessed they had swum across to reach this platform.

The pool inside the cavern was dead water. No current, no visible signs of life. She had already scanned it carefully and had not seen a single fish or living creature. Swimming across probably had not been dangerous. The real risk would have been contamination. If the water quality was bad, it could have caused something far worse.

Several empty cans lay scattered nearby.

Staring at the faint green tint clinging to the skeletons, Evelyn felt a chill crawl across her scalp.

It was probably just the humidity and temperature shifts that had caused the discoloration. Still, those hollow eye sockets seemed to stare straight through her. The feeling of being watched prickled across her back.

She circled the stone platform with her flashlight, then turned her attention to the rock wall behind it. The stalactites were gone here. The surface was smooth, almost like polished marble.

She removed a glove and ran her fingers across it. It was dry. There was no condensation.

But something felt off.

Evelyn pulled out a cloth and wiped the surface gently. Nothing changed.

A thought flashed through her mind.

She took out a bottle of alcohol, dampened the cloth, and wiped the wall again.

This time, the stone shifted.

Lines began to appear.

A map slowly revealed itself across the rock face.

Her eyes widened. A dozen wild possibilities raced through her head. Treasure. Secrets. Hidden chambers.

She forced herself to calm down and studied it carefully. The map was incredibly complex. Just tracing it with her eyes felt overwhelming.

She set up multiple flashlights, flooding the wall with bright light, then took out her phone and photographed every inch of the map. To avoid missing anything, she memorized each line, each turn.

Was the location marked on this map somewhere inside this cavern?

But this massive cave system branched into countless smaller tunnels. How was she supposed to search them all?

She wiped down the entire wall. No other markings. No hidden switches. No secret doors.

She examined the stone platform inch by inch. Even checked behind the skeletons.

Nothing.

Which meant the location on the map was not here.

Before leaving, she pulled out an oversized bag and carefully gathered the skeletons inside. She would take them out and bury them somewhere proper.

Climbing back into the kayak, she opened a boxed meal and ate quickly. Then she continued her treasure hunt.

After paddling for a while, she noticed small bubbles breaking the surface ahead.

She immediately aimed her flashlight at the water.

Two pale shapes darted past beneath the surface. They looked like worms, but not quite. She caught a glimpse of something like a fish tail.

They moved fast. In seconds, they vanished.

She scanned the water again, but the strange creatures did not reappear.

Evelyn kept paddling until she reached another fork. She chose one at random.

Might as well rely on luck. If she found the place marked on the map, maybe she had been blessed by the gods of fortune today.

Suddenly, from rows of tiny black holes in the ceiling overhead, a swarm of bats burst out.

It was the first time she had ever seen so many. Thousands. Maybe tens of thousands.

She immediately ducked down in the kayak, covering her head and staying completely still.

The bats poured out in a frenzy, their shrill cries echoing through the cavern.

Nearly half an hour passed before she dared to lift her head.

Bat droppings littered the kayak, but she barely noticed. Her gaze was locked on the ceiling.

The clustered bat nests looked like a massive honeycomb.

Her arrival must have startled them, sending the entire colony fleeing in panic.

She guessed they would not return anytime soon.

Raising her flashlight, she studied the nests. The water surface was at least twenty meters below them. There was no way she could climb up.

Instead, she zoomed in with her phone and took several photos of the eerie scene above.

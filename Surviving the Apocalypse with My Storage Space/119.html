Chapter 119 Nematodes and Infestation

“It’s wonderful news. Daisy can finally rest in peace now, and the other victims have had justice too.” Clara looked much better at once.

Something flickered in Evelyn’s expression. The whole business still felt faintly strange to her, though she did not dwell on it. The killers had paid the price, and that gave the story the neatest ending anyone could hope for.

Once the snow had melted, the base began building pig farms and chicken sheds on the surface. Evelyn also saw pigs, chickens, ducks and geese being moved up from the Underground Quarter.

The base had both a canning factory and a noodle factory. Most of the meat was being sent to the canning plant. Clara said the base was worried that other disasters might come later, so they were processing food into canned goods, compressed biscuits and instant noodles that could be stored for long periods.

Evelyn had bought a few tins before. The taste was odd, frankly. A bit fishy. The meat inside was minced too finely and mixed with tiny bone fragments. It was not especially pleasant, but it was still meat, and already cooked, so she had not thrown it away. She simply stored it.

Every pig, every chicken, duck and goose had its own number marked on it. The staff at the farms must have been doing a good job, because the animals were all impressively plump.

Meanwhile, the teams turning the earth and sowing wheat were nearing the end of their work. In the space of just one month, dozens of acres of wasteland around the base had been reclaimed and planted.

Lately, a seasonal flu had suddenly begun spreading through the base. The temperature had leapt from minus thirty up to fifteen above zero, and naturally enough people’s bodies were reacting with all manner of minor ailments, so no one paid it too much attention at first.

But on the third day of the flu, the elderly, those over fifty or sixty, began to show violent symptoms after vomiting. They became delirious, then started vomiting blood and harming themselves. Once the symptoms appeared, they rarely lasted more than three hours before the person stopped breathing. The doctors at the Medical Division repeatedly confirmed that these people had indeed died of the flu.

To prevent infection, the base could only issue masks. But with dozens of people dying every day, panic quickly spread among the residents.

“Attention, attention. Due to a flu outbreak in the base, all residents are to wear masks and drink hot water frequently.”

Hot water?

Evelyn froze.

As the announcement echoed from the loudspeakers, she rushed straight into the bathroom. She removed the filtration device, filled a basin with unfiltered water, then took out a torch. Under the harsh beam, she saw almost transparent white thread-like worms suspended in the water.

She put on a mask at once and ran to the Medical Division. It was her day off, but the entire building was overflowing with people. The bins stank of vomit and blood. Fighting down her nausea, Evelyn went straight to Dr. Hargreaves.

“I’ve found something.”

He had only just finished dealing with an elderly patient and had not yet caught his breath before Evelyn dragged him into the office.

“Uncle Julian, do you drink the base water?”

“Yes, of course. Why?”

Evelyn took the cup from her pocket and poured a little onto the glass-topped desk, then shone the torch onto the puddle.

“Look closely. What do you see?”

Dr. Hargreaves took the torch from her and bent over the desk. A second later, there was a sharp scrape as the chair at his side went over, knocked down by mistake.

His face turned white.

“Nematodes?”

“That’s right. There are white worms in the base water. And the elderly people who’ve died these past few days, first, their bodies were too weak and their immune systems too poor. Once the worms entered them, the symptoms mimicked flu, but in reality the worms may already have been feeding on their organs or blood. Second, they may be like parasites. They probably survive more easily in older bodies. Third, it may be that everyone has them already, only the stronger ones are still in the incubation stage and haven’t shown symptoms yet.”

When she finished, Evelyn looked at him in silence, her face dark and steady.

“I need to report this to the Medical Division.”

Evelyn nodded. He was the right person to bring it forward, and he did not disappoint her. Without a word of hesitation, he took the burden on himself.

She handed him two albendazole tablets. She had no idea whether they would work on these worms, but they were something, if only for peace of mind.

Fortunately, ever since arriving at the base, Evelyn had never drunk the groundwater. Even for bathing, she had only used it after filtering.

Then she thought of Eddie and the others and hurried straight to his floor.

By the time she got there, an old woman had just died. She had vomited all over the floor, blood mixed with sick, and the whole floor reeked.

When Evelyn found Eddie, there was still a large splash of blood on his clothes.

“Come with me.”

Her expression must have been grave enough to alarm him, because he asked no questions and followed her outside at once.

“What is it?”

“There are white worms in the base groundwater. The people dying here are not dying of flu. And these white worms are mutated. Take these two boxes of tablets and give them to Calvin and the others for me. I tested it just now. Hot water won’t kill them. At the moment, there’s still no proper response in place.”

Eddie’s face darkened. “How can this be happening?”

“Come to mine after work. I’ve got another filtration unit at home. Take it to share with Calvin and the others. I’m going back to the flat now to see if I can find any way of dealing with this.”

There were countless books in her storage space, including all the medical texts left behind by her parents.

Eddie tucked the medicine into his coat and watched her go, his whole body seeming to collapse inward.

The sun was shining, yet he had never felt so cold.

Evelyn searched through medical books at home for the rest of the day and still found no answer.

That afternoon Eddie came to collect the filtration unit, and Evelyn gave him an extra packet of Chinese medicine as well.

The next day, when she arrived at work, there were suddenly two children among the sick.

They were rolling on the floor in agony, too consumed by pain to speak. Their parents stood by helpless and desperate.

“Tell me what kind of pain it is. Is it stabbing? Is it twisting? Where exactly does it hurt?”

Evelyn held one little girl still and felt her abdomen. “Does it hurt here?”

The girl nodded through tears.

Evelyn pressed lightly against her chest, and the child screamed.

“Doctor, please, give my daughter something for the pain. She’s in such agony. Last night she was banging her head against the wall, saying there were worms crawling inside it.”

Evelyn was just about to lift the child when the girl suddenly clutched at her chest, her face twisting into something almost unrecognisable with pain. Then she began vomiting blood in great gushes.

In the blood, Evelyn saw the worms.

They were still moving.

Long, thin, pale creatures, almost like centipedes, with feelers and countless tiny legs.

Blood spattered across Evelyn’s protective suit in fine droplets. She carried the girl to the bed in the next room and tied her down with rope. When the parents rushed in and saw the scene, their faces turned white.

“She’s no longer in her right mind. Very soon she’ll start hurting herself. Hold her down.”

Evelyn ran to the medicine store at once, only to be told that supplies were desperately short. Many of the better herbs took too long to grow and were too demanding about soil and temperature. The base simply could not cultivate them.

Her hands were shaking the whole time.

She took out several albendazole tablets, meaning to feed them to the girl, but when she hurried back into the ward, the child was already dead.

Her body was soaked in blood.

The worms were crawling out of her mouth, writhing through the blood as they moved.

Chapter 29 Rainstorm, Toads, Cold Wave: Part 4

This time, the toads that appeared were different.

The bumps on their skin were smaller and packed tightly together. Their color had shifted from dark brown to a murky black-green. Their already bulging eyes seemed even more protruding than before.

Evelyn wasn't sure if these new toads were still safe to eat, or if they had mutated again.

She caught one and brought it home. Using her usual method, she cut off the head, peeled the skin, and removed the poison glands. After boiling it, the broth looked normal. If anything, the aroma was even richer than before.

She briefly considered catching a rat to use as a test subject. But ever since the flooding, most rats had drowned. There were none left to catch.

She ran a toxin test strip on the broth. No abnormalities showed up.

Still, Evelyn didn't dare take risks.

Just then, faint cooking smells drifted in from upstairs and downstairs.

The new toads were edible.

Some people had already tried them, and no one had shown any bad reactions.

As for where the toads came from, no one cared anymore.

For survivors barely clinging to life, nothing mattered more than staying alive. Even one extra day was worth everything.

The next morning, Evelyn was woken by knocking at her door.

When she opened it and saw Claire, she immediately sensed something was wrong.

"What happened?"

Claire shook her head. She took out a potato and asked to trade it for an extra-large plastic bag.

One look at her expression was enough for Evelyn to understand.

Claire's grandfather was gone.

He had taken his own life during one of his rare lucid moments, leaving behind a farewell letter for her.

Claire carefully wiped the marks around her grandfather's neck, dressed him neatly, and gently placed him into the bag.

"He's so thin," she whispered. "I… I can lift him so easily."

She carried him to Unit 601.

That place had become Block D's cemetery.

After that, Claire's apartment felt unbearably empty.

She curled up on the bed, clutching the letter, her eyes completely hollow.

"I don't have any family left," she murmured. "Grandpa, Grandma, Mom, Dad… they're all gone. I don't have a home anymore. I don't have a home…"

Her voice broke over and over.

Evelyn added more firewood to the stove, pulled over a chair, and sat beside her in silence.

She understood too well.

But she didn't know how to comfort her.

Claire's grandfather had chosen death so he wouldn't become a burden. The letter he left behind was short.

To my most precious granddaughter, Claire:

This is the clearest moment I've had in a long time, and I'm writing this while I still can.

Please don't feel pain or guilt over my choice.

Leaving with dignity, without becoming your burden, is my final wish.

Most of all, I hope you live well.

Be strong. Be brave.

"Evelyn… is living alone really that hard?" Claire asked softly.

Evelyn looked at her.

Tears slid down Claire's cheeks without a sound.

"I used to think I was the luckiest person in the world. Grandpa and Grandma, Mom and Dad, they all loved me so much. They worked themselves to the bone, saved every penny, just to buy me an apartment downtown after I graduated.

"From elementary school to college, I was always the top student. I wanted to work hard, become the best lawyer I could be.

"I tried to be a good person. I never did bad things. I donated blood. I gave money. I gave away clothes. I even signed up to donate my body after death.

"I really believed… I was a good person."

Claire clutched her chest, her face twisted in pain.

"Why…" she sobbed. "Why did God take all my family away?

"Why leave me alone like this? How am I supposed to live? I can't… I can't go on…"

Her suppressed sobs finally broke free. She collapsed into hysterical crying, pounding her chest as tears streamed down her face.

After losing so many loved ones in a row, strands of white had already appeared in her hair.

*****

Outside, the floodwaters had risen past the fifth floor.

Some residents on that level had died. Others had fled.

No one knew when the rain would end.

People were growing numb.

Maybe deep down, most of them no longer believed they would survive. Death was just a matter of time.

Claire eventually pulled herself together. She started going out frequently with Evelyn to look for supplies and firewood. In an old hotel, the two of them found plenty of wooden beds and cabinets.

More often, though, Evelyn still went alone.

She carried too many secrets. She was better suited to moving solo. No one would ever learn about her spatial storage.

One day, she docked her motor raft beneath a private hospital.

Evelyn didn't know if any equipment or medicine was still inside, but she didn't hesitate. She climbed in through a window, ready to loot whatever she could.

The moment she entered, she saw footprints everywhere. Drawers had been pulled open, furniture overturned. Clearly, others had already been here.

Anything still clean and usable, she stored away. Hospital beds, cabinets, anything worth keeping.

This was a fairly large private hospital. Compared to the rigid, utilitarian style of public hospitals, this place was almost luxurious. A massive chandelier hung from the top floor, reflecting her small figure.

It didn't feel like a hospital at all.

It felt like a hotel.

The hallways were crawling with toads, packed together just like the venom moths had been before.

In the operating room, she found several pieces of lightly used equipment. After brushing the toads off, she stored them away as well.

In one room, she even found a few frozen, stiff corpses.

Two hours later, Evelyn was ready to leave.

She climbed onto the windowsill, preparing to jump down, when she saw three people speeding toward her in motor rafts.

Since the cold wave, hardly anyone went out scavenging. After scanning the area and seeing no one else, she lowered her raft onto the water.

She hadn't expected someone to try stealing it.

"Holy crap, there's someone here!

"Hey, is that raft yours?"

The voice sounded young. Maybe early twenties.

Evelyn jumped down onto her raft, not bothering to answer.

"Hey, are you deaf or something? We're talking to you! What's it like up there? Is there nothing left? Why'd you come down empty-handed?"

It was three young men.

Their raft was loaded with two large packs. Long knives lay beside them.

One of them pointed his blade at Evelyn, clearly intending to block her way.

"Go see for yourselves," she replied flatly.

"Huh? You're a girl?"

The guy holding the knife sounded shocked, like he'd never seen a woman before.

"Damn, that's impressive. Coming out alone to scavenge? You know how many teams got swept away by the flood earlier? Aren't you scared?"

Evelyn had no interest in chatting.

Unfortunately, they were very interested in her.

"We're students from Elaris University," another guy said eagerly. "You sound young too. Still in school? We've set up a temporary base. Wanna join us?"

"You've got a raft, so we can make an exception and let you in."

Evelyn was speechless.

"No, thanks."

She picked up her crossbow and aimed it at them.

"Out of my way. Move."

"Whoa, you've got a crossbow? That's sick! What's the range? Was it expensive? Do you have extras? Could you sell me one?"

"…" For a split second, Evelyn seriously wondered if she'd just run into lunatics.

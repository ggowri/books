Chapter 51 Heatwave, Vultures: Part 3

Calvin and the other two came back around two thirty in the morning. Half asleep, Evelyn heard their footsteps climbing the stairs.

Half an hour later, a helicopter roared over the residential complex. The deafening noise jolted her awake. She threw off the covers and rushed to the balcony, ignoring the pain in the soles of her feet.

"Attention all survivors. Attention all survivors. Attention all survivors.

"Thirty kilometers southeast of Elaris, in Drakepool Town, the government has established a survivor base. The base is guarded by the military and can accommodate one million people. All survivors are urged to proceed to Drakepool Base as soon as possible. All survivors are urged to proceed to Drakepool Base as soon as possible."

The announcement repeated three times. Evelyn listened carefully. The helicopter moved slowly across the sky, loudspeakers hanging beneath it, taking turns broadcasting the message. It had been more than four months since she had last seen any sign of the authorities.

She stood on the balcony and watched as the helicopter drifted toward the next district.

For people in this country, trust in the government was carved deep into their bones. Even though she had no intention of going to the base, she still felt a surge of emotion she could not quite put into words.

The country had not abandoned them.

The aerial mobilization lasted three days. After that, the skies fell silent again.

The effect was immediate. Calvin and the other two went out every night searching for supplies. The first two nights, they still ran into other survivors. A few days later, the entire city felt empty.

Maybe there were still people hiding in some corner of some building, like Evelyn. But most had already left overnight for Drakepool Base.

When the temperature climbed to 51 degrees Celcius, more vultures began circling outside.

Calvin gathered magnets and wooden boards and built three hand-cranked fans. He gave one to Evelyn and the other two to Ray and Evan. In return, Evelyn brought him three cans of food and two compressed biscuits.

The five of them settled into a fragile kind of harmony. Before they realized it, it was early September.

A few days earlier, when Calvin and the others went scavenging, they had brought back a sack of beans. After dividing it among themselves, they gave the extra portion to Evelyn. She weighed it. About four pounds.

Four pounds of beans was no small thing. In a world where supplies were disappearing fast, even a single dry biscuit was precious.

Evelyn had never liked owing anyone. She immediately brought them several pounds of soil in return and taught them how to sprout bean shoots using packed earth. Over the next few days, the three men stopped going out. They spent hours each day crouched in front of plastic basins, staring eagerly, waiting for the first pale shoots to break through.

The buildings in Maple Haven had already been stripped bare under Evelyn and Calvin's carpet-style scavenging.

Everyone's supplies had grown more substantial. Still, no one dared to indulge. Water especially had reached the point where every sip required calculation.

When September began, Evelyn noticed new faces in Elaris.

They were likely supply teams sent from the base. Moving block by block, they searched every building, every apartment. Whatever they found was airlifted away by helicopter.

After telling Calvin, Evelyn, Ray, and Evan gathered in his apartment that night to discuss what to do.

"Evie, where did you see them?"

"The night before last, I spotted them from the overpass. They were searching the Skyline Plaza next door. Last night they were at the Grand Regency Hotel. Each squad has ten people. Eight search for supplies, two stand guard. Every one of them carries a gun and a blade."

Evelyn spoke as she reached over and gently gathered Wendy's messy hair, tying it back with practiced hands.

"The teams you saw the past two nights, were they different?" Ray asked.

"Yes. And from the way they carried themselves, they were probably soldiers."

Calvin let out a slow breath. "Then we don't need to worry. If they're military, they won't make things hard for us."

"I suggest we stay inside for a while," Evelyn said quietly. "Our complex is old. They might not bother searching it. But if they discover survivors still living here, we won't just be taken to the base. All our supplies could be confiscated."

The moment she said it, the three men stiffened.

"That can't happen. If they take everything, what are we supposed to eat?"

"I'm not going to the base. Even if I die, I'll die here. A place that big, with that many people, you know there will be all kinds. I don't want to get bullied there."

"With things the way they are now, does the base even have food?" someone muttered.

On that point, Evelyn was surprisingly optimistic.

"Every city has strategic grain reserves. When the floods hit, the military would have taken control of those warehouses and transferred the supplies. There were probably some losses, but enough to last for years. The real issue is water. The government is calling survivors to the base not just to gather people, but to build infrastructure. Drill wells, set up experimental greenhouses, grow vegetables and fruit indoors."

The three of them suddenly understood.

"In this kind of heat, they expect people to build a base?" Ray picked at his fingers, fear written all over his face. "Can anyone's body handle that?"

Silence fell over the room.

In the end, Calvin made the call. For the next half month, no one would go out. No cooking fires. No loud noises. Avoid even stepping onto the balcony if possible.

Back in her own apartment, Evelyn sank onto the couch, exhaustion washing over her as she stared blankly ahead.

She unwrapped a piece of candy and slipped it into her mouth. Sweetness flooded her senses. For a moment, all the tension dissolved.

On the third night, a search team passed by Willow Heights. Maybe the place looked too rundown, because they did not come inside.

Evelyn thought the crisis had passed.

She was wrong.

In the middle of the night, two men slipped into the complex with flashlights in hand.

From the balcony, she watched for half an hour. They headed into Block E next door. It had already been stripped clean. They left quickly.

Evelyn's palm was slick with sweat around the grip of her crossbow. Only after their silhouettes disappeared beyond the gate did she force her numb legs to straighten.

For the next half month, everyone except Wendy stayed on edge, nerves pulled tight.

Not until the streets were completely devoid of people, replaced instead by circling flocks of vultures, did they finally relax.

Compared to those disciplined supply squads, the vultures almost seemed harmless.

Those teams carried guns. There were too many of them. Evelyn had no intention of crossing paths with them.

After half a month of poor sleep and worse meals, once the alarm lifted, Evelyn collapsed into bed and slept for two straight days.

On the third day, Ray knocked on her door.

When she opened it and saw the half-dead vulture slung behind him, she thought she was hallucinating.

"Evie, can you eat this bird?"

Evelyn stared at him.

"I have no idea if it's edible. What I really want to know is how you managed to turn it into that."

Ray grinned.

"Evan and I were heading out to check the area. We'd just gotten downstairs when this vulture started fighting a bigger one. By the time they'd both worn each other out, we stepped in and took advantage."

Evelyn arched a brow. "So there were two?"

"Evie, I've got another one."

Downstairs, Evan was struggling up the steps, a massive vulture slung across his back.

Chapter 31 Rainstorm, Toads, Cold Wave: Part 6

Evelyn made a pot of mutton stew with turnips. Mutton could drive out the cold, but ever since her last trip outside, it usually took her two or three days to fully recover her energy.

By evening, Evelyn had succumbed anyway. The chill had seeped deep into her bones. At first, it was just dizziness and nausea. She took some medicine and burrowed under the blankets, hoping to sweat it out.

But after sleeping for hours, she was still shivering, feeling as if she'd been frozen solid. Thankfully, there was a good amount of the herbal tonic she'd brewed last time left. She fetched a bowl from her space and gulped it down.

Soon, cold sweat beaded on her forehead. Her constitution was decent; she'd thought a single night of suffering would see her through to the next day. Yet, by morning, a deep, dull ache had settled into her very bones.

A suspicion crept in: rheumatism. Rheumatism could warp joints. In severe cases, it could even lead to rheumatic heart disease or paralysis.

A little later, her lower back began to throb as well. The coughing intensified, turning into violent, wrenching spasms she couldn't control. It felt like her lungs and heart were about to be torn from her chest. Her throat was parched and itchy, and every swallow or attempt to speak sent sharp stabs of pain through it.

Evelyn took out her silver needles, intending to do some acupuncture. She propped a mirror beside her and slowly inserted the needles into the points on her lower back.

Lying face down on the bed, she eyed her reflection in the mirror—her back bristling with silver needles. She let out a soft, amused sound. Looked like a pincushion. No, a hedgehog.

By afternoon, she felt considerably better. The ache in her back and bones had faded. The cough and stuffy nose were still severe, but manageable. Outside, the downpour had tapered to a steady rain. The cold snap, however, remained terrifyingly fierce, so few ventured out.

She wasn't the only one suffering from rheumatic flares triggered by the invasive cold, but Evelyn had no energy left to treat others. For the next few days, aside from meals and necessities, she barely left her bed. She pulled out her tablet and played a downloaded reality show, watching the celebrities clown around during games. She couldn't help but chuckle.

In her past life, she'd once seen a superstar from before the apocalypse get robbed during a heatwave and later beaten to death by a mob. The end times were a great leveler. Power, status, wealth… all turned to dust.

May 15th was Wendy's birthday. The little girl was turning three, already calling her Ms. Evie in that sweet, piping voice. Calvin's family invited Evelyn and Claire upstairs for the celebration. Times were hard, but they'd still managed to create a birthday with a sense of ceremony.

Claire brought two cans of food. Evelyn brought two packs of instant noodles.

In the Shaws' living room, a homemade iron frame held a large iron pot, inside which a toad hotpot was bubbling away. With Calvin in the household, their situation wasn't the worst—at least they had enough firewood and food. Wendy spotted Evelyn and immediately launched herself into her arms.

"Ms. Evie, are you feeling better from your sickness?"

Evelyn carried her over to sit down, casually setting the noodles aside. Claire exchanged greetings with the Shaw family and settled next to Evelyn.

"Much better, sweetie. Did you miss me?"

"Mm-hmm." Wendy, bundled in a thick cotton-padded jacket, studied Claire with wide eyes.

"Ms. Claire, why is all your hair white? Are you old now?" In Wendy's world, only old people had white hair, like her own grandparents.

"Ms. Claire dyed her hair," Evelyn said smoothly, offering a gentle smile to Claire.

Wendy's eyes grew wide. "Then I want to dye my hair too! Can I dye it red?"

Claire shook her head gently. "Not right now, sweetie. You have to wait until you're all grown up."

Wendy wasn't disappointed. Instead, she looked at Jessica and began to wheedle in her sweet voice. "Mommy, when I grow up, can I dye my hair? I like red."

Jessica shot her a sidelong glance. "Yesterday you said you liked purple. Changed your mind already?"

"I won't change! I'll like red forever."

Everyone laughed at her earnest declaration. Jessica moved a small table over and laid out a few snacks.

"Calvin managed to bring back a bottle of coke on his last trip out. Evelyn, Claire, you two should have it."

"I caught a chill recently, I shouldn't drink anything like that," Evelyn declined.

"I haven't been feeling well these past couple of days, better not have anything cold," Claire added.

Both had refused. Jessica had no choice but to put the can of coke away, then proceeded to press handfuls of sunflower seeds into their hands instead. Gathered around the fire, cracking seeds, the conversation drifted from one topic to another.

"Forty-five days now."

"Yea… who knows when this damned rain will ever stop."

Evelyn gazed into the flames, her thoughts churning.

Yes. Day forty-five. If nothing went wrong, the deluge would end in fifteen more days.

The hotpot was ready, potatoes and chili paste added to the pot. It smelled surprisingly good.

They sat around the fire, celebrating Wendy's third birthday. She clapped her little hands, cheering and bouncing with joy. She asked Calvin when they could go to an amusement park, then turned to her mother to ask if she could go to kindergarten now.

That was when a deafening boom shattered the moment. Evelyn almost dropped her cutlery. Everyone exchanged wide-eyed looks. Wendy, terrified, burrowed into her mother's arms, her expression shifting from delight to pure fear.

"Sounds like… a flash flood is coming," Calvin managed to say, the words heavy.

This was different from the stagnant waterlogging after heavy rain. Flash floods were born from landslides, upstream collapses, reservoir breaches—swift and brutal.

Evelyn and Claire excused themselves to return downstairs. They parted in the stairwell. Suddenly, Claire called out softly.

"Evelyn. We have to survive. Okay?"

A sudden sting pricked at Evelyn's eyes. For a long moment, neither spoke. Evelyn stepped forward, gave Claire's shoulder a firm squeeze, and nodded emphatically.

"We will. We'll both survive."

Back in her apartment, Evelyn drew the curtain aside and stood quietly, watching the world outside.

The roar of thunder, the ominous rumble of the approaching floodwaters, the drumming of the rain, the howl of the gale—all twisted together into a single, frantic death knell.

A flash of lightning split the sky, violet and blue intertwined. The following thunderclap shook the very foundations of the earth.

Evelyn stuffed earplugs into her ears, curled into a tight ball, and hid beneath her blankets.

Half an hour later, the flash flood reached Willow Heights. Inflatable boats were snatched up and tossed in the churning water. The flood surged like a raging sea, waves crashing over one another, sending spray flying high. With a sickening crack, a wall of water slammed against her balcony glass. Stones, swept along in the current, hammered against the pane. The sound of the impacts echoed through the room.

The world beyond her wall had become a nightmarish, surreal landscape. Evelyn had lived through this in her past life; she knew its true horror.

Once a person was caught in that torrent, survival was nearly impossible.

A car, lifted by the water, was flung violently against the seventh floor of the opposite building. The sound of shattering glass exploded, followed by desperate, hopeless screams.

Evelyn felt her scalp crawl. Time dragged on, yet the cacophony outside showed no sign of abating. She crawled out from under the covers and began to pace her living room, restless and anxious.

A corpse, carried by the current, was slammed against her window with tremendous force. Evelyn caught a glimpse of a face, rendered unrecognizable. She covered her head, squeezed her eyes shut, her body trembling uncontrollably.

"Just end. Please, just end. Let it be over…"

Crouched on the floor, arms wrapped around her head, she pleaded silently, over and over again.

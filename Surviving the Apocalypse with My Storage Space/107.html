Chapter 107 Blackwater Base

Eddie had barely finished speaking when Evelyn could still take it in, but Adrian’s face had already darkened.

No meat?

That was practically a death sentence as far as he was concerned.

“Evelyn, I want to live with you.” He sounded utterly serious, almost solemn.

“Only married couples can share a room,” Eddie said with a snort. He still found Adrian’s presence unsettling. The trouble was, the man was a full head taller than him, and Eddie rather suspected he would lose badly in a fight.

“Are Officer Shaw and Wendy in a single room too?”

Eddie nodded. “He handed over a carton of cigarettes. And Ray and Evan too. We scraped some things together so they could have single rooms as well.”

Evelyn looked at Adrian and patted his arm. “We can get two single rooms.”

The moment he realised she had no intention of abandoning him, Adrian was immediately mollified.

“Can I come and eat with you every day, then?”

“As if Evelyn has food to spare.” Eddie was nearly hopping with outrage. “You’re a grown man. Don’t be a parasite. Work and feed yourself.”

Evelyn had to stop him before he worked himself into a proper rage.

“Calm down.”

“How am I meant to calm down? Evelyn, where did you even find this boyfriend? He doesn’t support you and still expects you to feed him. It’s outrageous. Listen, I’ve got food where I’m staying. Don’t worry. I won’t let you go hungry.”

She found it both hilarious and oddly touching.

“He’s very capable. Adrian’s been looking after me this whole time.”

“All right, all right. You always defend your own.” Eddie exhaled through his nose. “Anyway, if you’ve got anything to offer, ask the Captain for help. He’s a decent man. He’s got two children at home, so he works very hard.”

Evelyn understood immediately.

The fishing team had around three hundred and fifty people in it. Captain Mercer was the overall leader, with smaller teams under him. Each team had fifty members, and each person had to bring in an average of eighty or ninety pounds before they could return.

There were still plenty of fish left in Silvermere River, and the team’s equipment was fairly extensive. At eight that evening, Captain Mercer called it a day. They would travel through the night and make straight for the base without stopping.

“Another two days or so on foot, then we’ll reach the vehicles. We drove here, but the roads this side were completely destroyed, so the lorries are parked at Highcrest Hill.”

Evelyn packed away the tent. She carried that, while Adrian took the rest of their things. Vanessa shot Evelyn several dark looks, but Evelyn ignored her completely.

“Evelyn, have you decided what you’re going to hand over?”

She nodded. Cigarettes and alcohol would do. She had more than enough of both in her space, and it was the perfect chance to trade for a single room.

She had also prepared a packet of fruit sweets. Those were for Captain Mercer. Eddie had already reminded her that the man had two children.

Because of the cold, there was no danger of the fish spoiling. They were packed into sacks, tied up, and slung over people’s backs. Everyone carried the same way, some with two sacks, some with one. All of it was carefully recorded. Going out on missions earned points, and points were settled once a month, though they could be carried over. Those points could then be exchanged for things such as clothes, candles, sanitary products, medicine, and the like.

Evelyn watched the others as they walked.

Most were dressed in rags. Their expressions were numb, their faces drawn with exhaustion. For them, the base offered shelter from the wind and rain, a place to sleep, and enough food to stay alive. That alone was more than enough.

She and Adrian stayed near the back of the crowd. Every now and then someone would collapse without warning, at which point Eddie had to go and check on them. Evelyn did not need to examine anyone to know the cause. Malnutrition. Exhaustion. Hunger. Cold.

Eddie said that when the teams set out, the base issued dry rations to everyone. Some people ate half of theirs straight away. For the rest of the trip, they had to work on an empty stomach.

Two days later, the group reached Highcrest Hill and saw more than twenty lorries parked at the foot of the mountain.

Captain Mercer ordered all the fish loaded first. Only once the cargo was on board were people allowed to squeeze themselves in.

Evelyn and Adrian stayed tucked in among the crowd. When others climbed aboard, they climbed aboard. When others sat down, they sat down too.

After five or six hours on the road, they reached Blackwater Base.

It looked much as Evelyn had imagined. Huge, heavily guarded, with patrols at the entrance. Eddie told her that besides the buildings above ground, there was also the Underground Quarter. All vegetables were grown below the surface, and they kept chickens, ducks, geese, cattle, sheep, and pigs there as well.

Those living in communal housing were based in the east. People with single rooms lived in the west. All the leadership class lived in the north, where there were not only luxury flats but detached villas too, with uninterrupted electricity and no shortage of water.

The south was reserved for scientific and technical personnel, and that area consisted of apartment buildings as well.

“My eyes hurt,” Adrian muttered suddenly.

Evelyn shot him a warning look.

“Put up with it. If you get yourself into trouble, I’m not helping you. All the food will be mine.”

He pressed his lips together and stared at her.

“That’s just the kind of person I am.”

Everyone got down from the lorries and queued to enter the base one by one. Looking at the enormous gate, easily ten metres high, Evelyn found the whole thing faintly unreal.

“Evelyn, I’ve already spoken to Mercer,” Eddie said. “In a moment he and I will take you to sort out your resident permits. But you’ll need to have your things ready beforehand.”

Evelyn had prepared them long ago. They were already in her bag.

Once everyone else had gone inside, Captain Mercer gave a few instructions to one of his men, then came over to Evelyn and Adrian.

“Evelyn, Adrian. Both twenty years old. I’m told you have medical skills. Do you have any formal qualifications?”

Evelyn nodded. “I was a student at Elaris Medical University. Both my parents were doctors at Elaris City Hospital.”

Captain Mercer looked pleased. “Good. And him?”

“Adrian used to be in the army. He’s very skilled in a fight.”

At this point in the apocalypse, there was no real way to verify any of that, but Evelyn needed to come up with a plausible explanation for his combat ability.

As expected, Mercer’s eyes lit up.

“That’ll do. Come with me.”

“Captain Mercer, we’d be very grateful for your help. We’re simple people and we’ve only just arrived. We’re rather uneasy.”

Evelyn slipped a small black pouch into his hand. He opened it at once and glanced inside. A packet of fruit sweets and a pack of cigarettes.

“People with ability don’t need to be uneasy,” he said. “Still, there is one thing I should remind you of. There are a lot of people in the base. Keeping your head down and behaving yourselves will do you no harm.”

Evelyn nodded. “Yes, we understand.”

The moment they passed through the gate, she found herself staring at rows of buildings of every sort, along with soaring towers she might once have expected to see only in science fiction films. Less than a year into the apocalypse, and this place had already developed to such a degree. She could not help feeling intensely curious about the people running it.

Behind the Captain, Eddie was pulling faces at her and secretly giving her a thumbs-up. Evelyn smugly lifted a brow in return, only to be caught at it by Adrian.

Pip and Lark were still tucked inside the cloth bag, which at present hung round Adrian’s neck.

As they walked, Captain Mercer introduced them to the scale and development of the base, though he said not a word about the people in power.

“We’re at the Administration Hall now. It doesn’t matter that you haven’t got identity cards. You can apply for resident permits instead.”

The four of them went inside. As Evelyn looked at the spotless white marble floor tiles, she was suddenly seized by the strange sensation that she had stepped back into the world before the end.

Chapter 4 Stockpile: Part 3

Evelyn looked up at the glaring white sun and felt a wave of unease settle in her chest. Something wasn't right. In her last life, Elaris had never been this hot.

Back home, she checked her stockpile again. Over the past few days, she'd added two hundred sets of basic skincare products and face masks, along with lip balms and moisturizers. After the heat waves hit, skin would start to crack and peel—painful, itchy, and unbearable.

Ten of the water storage barrels were now full. Whenever she was home, Evelyn kept the taps running to fill the containers, but the water flow was too slow, and she worried it might draw attention. So instead, she went straight to Greymont Reservoir at night and siphoned water directly. After two nights, every barrel was filled.

But her anxiety didn't fade.

She kept thinking about those massive reservoirs that supplied water to tens of millions in Elaris. If the dams burst, it would be catastrophic. The floods had already made things worse during the last apocalypse. How many of Elaris's thirty million residents drowned in the storms and floods? How many starved or froze to death in their homes?

Evelyn didn't have time to spiral.

She hit the road again, traveling to nearby cities for another round of stockpiling.

Besides food, she gathered hundreds of cotton quilts, cold-weather clothing, bedding sets, folding army cots, collapsible chairs, camping tents, coconut coir bricks for growing vegetables, fatwood kindling for fire-starting, old-fashioned sewing machines, spools of thread, sewing kits—anything that might help her survive long-term.

By the time she returned, it was early March.

The heat in Elaris still hadn't let up. Abroad, some regions were already reporting mass casualties due to extreme temperatures. News of volcanic eruptions, tsunamis, and earthquakes was spreading.

Evelyn kept working, continuing to cook and pack thousands of meals into plastic containers.

She bought dozens of phones, tablets, power banks, and books—covering every subject, every era, from every corner of the world. Books were the fire that kept civilization alive.

She also stocked up on milk, adult formula, chocolate, vitamins—anything that could help supplement nutrition.

With the heat in mind, she added twenty air conditioners and twenty fans to her inventory.

As time marched on, her sense of unease grew heavier. She started losing sleep, her mind torn by indecision.

Should she tell someone?

She wasn't stupid. If she ran around yelling that the world was ending, she'd be dismissed as crazy—or worse, arrested for inciting panic.

Evelyn didn't see herself as a hero. But when she thought about the people around her... her fellow citizens... it didn't feel right to do nothing at all.

The days that followed were just as frantic.

Evelyn continued to shop like her life depended on it. She wasn't just relying on online stores anymore. She went out to every mall and supermarket she could find, grabbing anything useful without hesitation.

When insomnia became too much to bear, she drove up into the mountains at night to dig soil. After the acid rain in her last life, the land had been ruined. Crops had withered. Now, with all those seeds waiting in her space, she knew they would be needed one day. For several nights, she carried a hoe and worked the earth until she'd filled twenty large planting boxes.

She was constantly coming and going, leaving at dawn and returning late. No one knew what she was doing, and soon the residential complex was full of whispers. But Evelyn didn't care. She kept her head down and focused on her mission.

Her space was packed with supplies—so much that even two lifetimes wouldn't be enough to use it all. She'd even bought multiple chainsaws for cutting down trees. From tiny things like nails, trash bags, and calligraphy sets, to large farming equipment and fifty-year calendars, she left nothing out.

The cast-iron cookware she'd custom-ordered from a blacksmith had finally arrived. After taking everything home, she did another full inventory.

But the closer she got to the expected date, the more intense her anxiety became.

She felt like a prisoner waiting for execution—knowing exactly when the blade would fall, how deep it would cut. But the waiting, the anticipation, was the real torment.

Twenty days left.

Evelyn bought a burner phone and a prepaid SIM card from Cherry Hollow. Then, using an anonymous identity, she posted a vague but urgent warning online.

She didn't want to be a savior. She was terrified of getting arrested or having her cover blown. But these people… they were her people. She couldn't just do nothing.

After hitting "send," she tossed the phone into her space and didn't look back.

She ignored the news. Instead, she dove back into her routine—shopping, preparing, checking every detail. There were still several hundred thousand left in her account, and she wasn't going to waste a cent.

She bought frozen foods, hot pot bases, high-calorie chocolate, compressed biscuits, honey, waterproof tarps, insecticide, digital scales, thermal clothing, drinks, milk tea, coffee, tea, canned meats...

In the end, she spent the last of her money on a Unimog, five bicycles, five tricycles, twenty acupuncture needle kits, and twenty sets of surgical tools.

Her anonymous warning, combined with the recent surge of global natural disasters, set the internet on fire. Survival vloggers scrambled to pump out emergency prep content, riding the wave of panic.

Evelyn ignored them all.

She still went out every day to stockpile supplies. When she got home, she either locked herself in the kitchen and cooked nonstop, worked out in the living room, or sat in the bathroom sharpening knives with a whetstone.

Her physical condition was decent to begin with, but after two and a half months of intense training, she had even developed defined abs.

Then, on March 20, the national weather forecast issued an official high-temperature warning.

Knock, knock, knock...

Evelyn stared at the security door for a moment before slowly getting up to answer it. When she opened the door, she was surprised to see one of the residential complex's security guards standing outside.

"Evie, Mr. Richard Grant from unit 101 passed away this morning at Holloway General Hospital. His family will be holding a farewell ceremony tomorrow at 10 a.m. at Fellridge Memorial Hall. Will you be attending?"

Evelyn paused for two seconds, then gave a quiet nod. "Yes, I'll go."

The security guard scribbled her name onto a list and left. Evelyn closed the door behind her and stood in place for a long time, lost in thought. She had been so consumed with stockpiling and preparing since her rebirth, she'd completely forgotten that Richard Grant would pass away around this time.

At 10 a.m. the next day, she arrived on time at Fellridge.

Aside from her, only Claire Brooks from 902 showed up.

Before retiring, Richard had been a calligraphy teacher. When they were younger, both she and Claire had learned beginner calligraphy from him at his home. Once a teacher, always a teacher. Seeing the kind, gentle smile in his photo, Evelyn's eyes turned red.

And just like that, her thoughts drifted back to her parents.

On March 25, Evelyn returned the keys to the warehouse. On her way back, she sent Sophie a message, gently suggesting she return to Altaris as soon as possible, and to start stocking up.

While driving home, she found a spot with no security cameras and quietly stashed her vehicle into the space.

By the 28th, the weather suddenly shifted.

The temperature dropped noticeably. A strong gust of wind left her with goosebumps.

That evening, the sky was painted with sweeping streaks of fiery red clouds. It was breathtaking, beautiful in a way that didn't feel real.

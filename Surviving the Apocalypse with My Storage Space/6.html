Chapter 6 Rainstorm: Part 2

After a simple breakfast, Evelyn changed into workout clothes and began her daily routine in the empty living room. She was tall and slim, her hair cropped short. If she covered her face, most people wouldn't even realize she was a girl.

Around 8:30 a.m., her signal returned—barely two bars. She turned on her phone and was instantly flooded with headlines. Every news outlet was pushing the same message: rescue would come.

But it wouldn't be fast.

This was a global disaster. The rain was everywhere. Rescue operations would be slow, and everyone was being urged to cooperate with self-rescue efforts. Residents on low floors were advised to move to higher ones. Those living in low-lying cities and rural areas were urged to relocate to higher ground—especially anyone living at the base of mountains or near rivers. Rain wasn't the only threat. Landslides and flash floods were on the way.

Evelyn sighed and closed the news app. Every few minutes, her phone pushed another red alert. Warnings about everything from heavy rainfall to flash floods, mudslides, and more.

By midday, people who had ventured out for supplies returned home, grumbling about price hikes. Everything was more expensive—and if you were even a little slow, it was gone.

Still, some had managed to come back with inflatable kayaks, life vests, and lifebuoys. Because of the storm, renovation workers were no longer taking jobs, so those who bought cement had no choice but to patch up their homes themselves.

At noon, Evelyn's phone rang.

It was Sophie.

She'd assumed Sophie had already returned to Altaris. But to her surprise, Sophie was still stuck at Elaris University.

"Evelyn, the water has flooded the entire first floor of our dorm. The local students have all been picked up by their families. You're the only local in our dorm. I, Francesca, and Iris are all still here. The cafeteria's underwater, and we haven't eaten in a day. Could you come save us? We talked about it—each of us will pay you a hundred dollars."

Evelyn didn't answer right away.

Sophie's voice cracked as she continued, clearly on the verge of tears.

"Evelyn, I know you're not as cold as you appear to be. Please, just help us. The dorm is awful. The power's out. The bathroom and balcony are both flooded. We really don't know what else to do."

Evelyn could hear voices in the background. It was Francesca Hale and Iris Weller.

"Sophie," Evelyn said flatly, "I can't help you. You're better off waiting for the rescue teams. Once they arrive, they can take you to the emergency shelters."

"Shelters? Those places are always overcrowded and chaotic. How about this—just tell us your address. We'll ask the rescue team to drop us off at your place instead."

"Sorry," Evelyn replied coldly. "My place is small. I can't take anyone in."

Sophie was stunned into silence.

"Evelyn, you're the only local person I know here. We're friends, right? When this is over, I'll treat you to dinner."

Evelyn rubbed her temple, already feeling a headache forming.

"Why don't you ask the rescue team to take you to a hotel instead? The rooms are better, and they'll feed you too."

"Hotels are too expensive. We can't afford that!"

Evelyn let out a quiet, mocking laugh—and hung up.

A few seconds later, Sophie called again.

Evelyn then blocked her.

Take them in? What did they think she was—an idiot?

Meanwhile, back in Dorm Room 301 at Elaris University, Francesca and Iris exchanged a knowing look as they watched Sophie's disappointed expression.

"I told you she was ungrateful," Francesca sneered. "It's not like we weren't going to pay her. She wouldn't even give us her address. Sophie, I thought you said you two were close?"

"She can't even be bothered to care if you live or die."

"Try calling her again," Iris urged. "Offer her 150 each. The first floor's completely underwater. We've got no water, no power, and no food. We have to get out of here, fast."

Sophie frowned and whispered, "She blocked me."

*****

That afternoon, an emergency bulletin was issued across the city: all public facilities in Elaris—libraries, gymnasiums, theaters, clubs, cultural centers—had been converted into temporary emergency shelters. Residents living on the first, second, and third floors, as well as those in nursing homes, orphanages, and school dormitories, were prioritized for immediate evacuation.

The group chat buzzed with activity. Neighbors who were on friendly terms had already begun asking to stay with those on higher floors.

By 4 p.m., someone knocked on Evelyn's door.

She checked the peephole. It was a couple from the second floor. Evelyn narrowed her eyes.

"Who is it?"

"Evie, it's me from 202. I was good friends with your mom, remember? I even held you when you were a baby. You must be scared, living all alone now. Your parents are gone, and we're all neighbors. You're still young—I've been worried about you these days."

Evelyn didn't reply or open the door.

The woman nudged the man next to her with her elbow. He cleared his throat awkwardly and spoke up.

"Evie, it's me, Neil Jones. Could we ask a favor? The floodwater's rising fast downstairs, and we were hoping to stay with you for a bit. Just until the storm passes. Don't worry, we'll pay. We brought food and water, so we can share meals. And we've got our kids Lily and Kai with us. With them around, you won't feel so alone."

Evelyn's eyes glinted with cold amusement. She twirled the knife in her hand and replied flatly, "Mr. and Mrs. Jones. The water might be rising, but it hasn't reached the second floor yet. And the authorities already issued a notice that low-floor residents should head to the official emergency shelters. As for me, I'm not scared. I enjoy my solitude. Please leave."

The couple froze, stunned by how directly she shut them down.

"Evie, getting to a shelter requires a rescue boat. We don't have one. And it's dangerous outside with the rain coming down like this."

"What does that have to do with me?" Evelyn said, tone cold. Unbelievable. The storm had barely begun and they were already eyeing her place like it was public property. How shameless.

She was the only one living alone in Block D. Clearly, they saw her as an easy target.

"Evie, are you really this heartless? You can't even take us in for a few days? We're neighbors. You're just a girl—don't be so cold."

Now they were trying emotional blackmail.

"Evie, just let them stay," a new voice cut in. It was Fiona from next door, soaked and disheveled, joining in the moral ambush. "You're all alone anyway. Wouldn't it be better to have some company? Young people shouldn't be selfish. You should learn to do good deeds. Your parents were doctors, always helping others. Don't disappoint their memory."

Evelyn raised her voice, sharp and clear. "Mrs. Rivers, aren't your son and daughter still out of town? You've got plenty of space at your place. Why don't you take in Mr. Jones and his family? To get in God's good graces. Maybe that'll bring your kids back safely. As for me, my parents are dead. I'm alone. God isn't going to help me now. But you—you still have children."

Fiona's face turned an ugly shade of gray.

Her balcony had flooded, and she'd been bailing water nonstop for days. Her kids were stranded out of town. She was scared and stressed—and now, hearing people knocking on Evelyn's door, she had come out to stir things up. She never expected Evelyn to flip the guilt right back at her, word for word. And with such venom.

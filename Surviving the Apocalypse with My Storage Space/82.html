Chapter 82 Surviving Alone: Part 6

That night, Evelyn lay on the bed while Sprig sprawled across the pillow. One human, one squirrel, both staring intently at the tablet in her hands.

On the screen played one of the most popular suspense films from before the apocalypse. The color grading was dark and moody. Sprig clearly did not like it. It kept nudging her ear with its head.

When a particularly frightening scene flashed across the screen, the squirrel jumped straight up on the bed. Its tail trembled nonstop.

"Sprig, you're that scared? Alright, alright. Don't panic. I'll put on headphones and watch by myself. You go back to your nest and sleep."

She pulled out her earbuds and put them on.

Sprig immediately leapt onto her again, clearly ready to cause trouble if it was excluded.

They locked eyes for two seconds.

Evelyn lifted it one handed.

"Quiet."

The squirrel squirmed, still trying to act up. She placed it firmly back into its nest.

"Stay there and sleep. No bouncing all over me."

Sprig wilted, staring at her pitifully. When it realized she was not going to soften, it finally curled up obediently.

Evelyn finished the entire movie alone, completely unmoved.

Then she glanced at the time.

It was midnight.

Her eyes widened. She shut everything down and went to sleep immediately.

Staying up this late was a sin.

Sure enough, when she woke the next day, it was already ten thirty in the morning.

Her stomach growled loudly. Sprig had long been awake. Its bowl of beans was empty.

She got up and refilled it generously before stepping into the bathroom to wash up. Outside, everything was blanketed in white.

The wound on her forehead had fully healed. The cut on her arm had scabbed over.

She bundled herself in her coat and rain boots, sealing every gap against the cold before stepping outside.

Today, she needed to process the three wild buffalo stored in her space.

She set up several large umbrellas and spread thick plastic sheets across the ground, weighing them down with bundles of rushes. Then she released one buffalo.

Holding a long blade, she tested its sharpness.

First, remove the head.

With a sharp knife, the work required far less effort.

Gloves on. Apron tied.

Butcher Evelyn went to work.

The head came off without much trouble. Skinning, however, was far harder.

She switched to a smaller knife, slicing a long cut down from the neck, then carefully peeling the hide away on both sides.

Even through her mask, the rising heat and smell were overwhelming.

She severed the hooves along with the hide and set them aside with the head.

After finishing the skinning, she collapsed into a chair and rested for a long while before she could continue.

It was too exhausting.

She had overestimated herself. She thought she could finish everything in a single day. Actually doing it was a different story.

She pulled out several large basins for the tallow, organs, ribs, and cuts from the front and hind legs.

Once she sliced through the connective tissue, the organs slipped free. She worked carefully, making sure nothing ruptured as she transferred them into a basin.

The buffalo meat was dense and firm. There was a generous amount of fat.

She picked up a boning knife and began trimming away the thick white tallow.

After clearing the fat, she split the carcass down the center and stripped the meat from the ribs.

Large chunks clinging to the bones were carved away as well. The bones themselves would be saved for broth.

From a short distance away, Sprig watched Evelyn methodically cutting into the massive animal.

It no longer dared to approach her.

By the time she finished processing the first buffalo, she left the head, hide, and organs to handle later.

She sealed the prepared meat in plastic wrap and released the second buffalo.

From morning until night, she worked without stopping.

By the time darkness fell, all three buffalo had been roughly processed.

The hides, organs, and heads would have to wait until tomorrow or the day after.

Evelyn set up a rack in the yard and hung the buffalo hides to dry.

Staring at the three large basins of organs, she felt a rare moment of uncertainty. She had absolutely no experience dealing with this part.

Her gaze drifted to the wood ash in the fire pit, and suddenly she had an idea.

Still, better to render the tallow first.

After dinner, she decided she would finish rendering the fat before resting.

She soaked the beef fat in water for over ten minutes, then cut it into small cubes. A massive iron pot went over the fire. She poured in two buckets of water. No need to bring it to a full boil. The fat cubes went straight into the pot.

After simmering for five or six minutes, she skimmed off the blood foam and fished the fat back out into a basin.

The smell was strong enough that she added another mask.

The blanched fat had swollen, filling two entire basins. She rinsed it again with clean water.

Then she dumped the water from the iron pot, added a fresh bucket, and poured the fat back in to begin rendering.

The fire had to be high.

She stirred constantly while feeding more wood into the flames, busy enough to feel dizzy.

After about forty minutes, she reduced the fire to low. The fat cubes gradually turned golden and crisp. She added prepared spices and continued stirring.

Five minutes later, she used a strainer to remove the cracklings and seasonings. Once the liquid fat cooled slightly, she ladled it into metal buckets.

From three buffalo, she rendered three and a half buckets of tallow.

The crispy cracklings could be saved for cooking vegetables or tossing into noodles.

By the end, she felt like she was coated in grease. The smell clung heavily to her clothes and hair.

She carried the metal buckets into the wooden cabin to cool. The organs, heads, and hooves had to be brought inside too. If left outdoors, they would freeze solid.

After finishing, she headed back to the RV to wash up and sleep.

It had been a long night of work. Her stomach growled.

She checked the time and decided to cook herself a bowl of noodles.

Plain noodles in clear broth, topped with a spoonful of cracklings, chili, scallions, and cilantro.

Perfect.

Sprig had behaved all day, likely intimidated by her butcher persona. When Evelyn stepped out of the bathroom, she found the squirrel curled neatly in its nest, not daring to move.

She smiled faintly but was too tired to tease it. She climbed into bed and turned off the lights.

In the middle of the night, she heard faint noises.

Half asleep, she opened her eyes. Sprig had crawled onto the pillow and fallen asleep beside her. She did not disturb it. Instead, she slipped on her night vision goggles and glanced out the window.

No large animals in sight.

After a few minutes of watching, exhaustion pulled her back under.

The next morning, she found a bird the size of her palm lying beside the fire pit.

It was injured.

Thankfully, it had landed near the embers. With the temperature so low and snow piled nearly to her calves, it would have frozen overnight otherwise.

Its tail feathers were long and streaked with three colors: black, red, and yellow.

She picked it up gently. One wing and one foot were injured. The bird weakly opened one eye to look at her, then closed it again in pain.

Evelyn carried it into the RV and took out her medical kit.

Sprig crept closer, curious.

So this must have been the source of the noise last night.

She laid a cloth on the table and set the little bird down carefully.

"Alright, little one. You can recover here."

She sniffed and rubbed her nose. She felt slightly congested this morning. Probably caught a chill from working outside all day yesterday.

"Evelyn, get it together."

She planted her hands on her hips and shouted at herself for motivation.

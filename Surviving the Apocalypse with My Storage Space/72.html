Chapter 72 On the Road of Escape: Part 3

Along the way, Evelyn made small marks to avoid losing her path on the way back down.

After nearly forty minutes of walking, she had reached deep into the mountains.

She found a patch of grass and sat down, pulling a bottle of water from her space and finishing it in a few gulps. Then she began studying the surroundings.

Though many trees had withered, the forest was still dense. Animal droppings dotted the ground. Fresh grass and wild greens had begun pushing through the soil again.

After resting for more than ten minutes, she took out her crossbow and continued forward.

When she was young, she had read a news report about Shattered Ridge. Aside from being a botanical park and wildlife reserve, it was also rich in mineral resources. Years ago, a group had illegally mined silver there. They were caught and sentenced to death.

Evelyn had only one purpose for coming deeper into the mountains.

To look for minerals.

The odds were slim, but she wanted to try.

The terrain grew rougher the farther she went. Suddenly, something rustled in the grass ahead.

She reacted instantly.

The bolt flew.

When she pushed the grass aside, she found a plump gray rabbit. Unfortunately, the pelt was ruined.

She retrieved the bolt, stored the rabbit in her space, and moved on.

"Ow."

Something struck the back of her head.

She turned sharply, scanning for the culprit.

On a pine tree branch sat a large brown squirrel, clutching a pine cone shell in its paws. When it saw her looking, it hurled the shell at her again.

She tilted her head. The shell missed and dropped into the brush.

"Chirp chirp!"

The squirrel seemed offended. It scrambled higher, plucked another pine cone, and threw it at her.

Evelyn was not one to take that quietly. She scooped up a handful of small stones and retaliated.

For ten full minutes, they exchanged projectiles.

Eventually, the squirrel conceded. The few remaining pine cones had already been sacrificed.

Evelyn gave a short whistle.

The squirrel's fluffy tail flicked wildly before it leapt down from the tree and cautiously approached her.

She crouched and pulled out a packet of melon seeds from her space, offering it a handful.

"Hey there, little guy. Where are your parents?"

The squirrel twitched its ears and tail, nose quivering, and began cracking seeds.

Evelyn slowly reached out and brushed her fingers across its tail.

It was very soft.

The squirrel glanced up at her but did not move away.

"Since we've met, it must be fate. I'm looking for something in the mountains. Want to be my guide?"

The squirrel ignored her and kept eating.

She smiled and gently pinched one of its ears.

"If you agree, flick your tail. If not, stop eating my seeds."

The tail flicked once.

Evelyn raised a brow. "Fine. I'll reluctantly bring you along."

After finishing the seeds, the squirrel sprang onto her shoulder.

One human, one squirrel.

They headed deeper.

At a small hollow between slopes, the squirrel jumped down and sniffed at the ground. It began nibbling on nearby wild greens.

Evelyn stepped closer.

Beside the plants, the surface of the soil was coated in a thin layer of white crystals.

She pinched a bit between her fingers and smelled it.

No obvious scent.

She hesitated, then lightly touched it to her tongue.

Salty.

With a faint bitterness.

Her heartbeat quickened.

She pulled a hoe from her space and began digging into the slope.

The soil was loose.

About thirty centimeters down, the tool struck something hard.

She cleared away the surrounding dirt and switched on a small flashlight.

Large yellowish white stones gleamed in the beam.

It was saltpeter.

Saltpeter was not only used for making ice. It could also be used to produce black powder.

Evelyn's eyes lit up.

She worked faster, shoveling away the topsoil.

Large chunks of raw saltpeter were exposed. One by one, she stored them in her space. When she finished, a deep pit remained where she had dug.

She circled the area once more but found nothing else of value.

Lifting the squirrel back onto her shoulder, she gripped her crossbow and continued deeper into the mountains.

"Chirp chirp…"

The squirrel flicked its tail across her shoulder. Evelyn reached up and playfully caught the fluffy tail, kneading it between her fingers. The squirrel leaned forward to sniff her clothes and hair.

It recoiled slightly.

Probably smelled something unpleasant.

Evelyn climbed over another ridge but saw no sign of water.

She took out a bottle and let the squirrel drink a little.

"Do you know where to find this?" she asked, making a glugging sound. "Can you take me there?"

The squirrel's dark eyes shifted as if considering her words.

Then it leapt down, scampered up a nearby tree, climbed all the way to the top, and sprang to another tree.

Seeing it move farther and farther away, Evelyn hurried after it.

But she lost sight of it.

So it left.

A small wave of disappointment washed over her. She stood there for a moment, then steadied herself and prepared to search for water alone.

Thud.

A pine cone hit her forehead.

She looked up.

The squirrel sat on a branch not far away, baring its tiny teeth at her.

"You didn't leave?" Her voice carried real delight.

She picked up the pine cone and tossed it back.

"I thought you were gone."

The squirrel chirped toward the distance.

Evelyn ran to the base of the tree. "You know where there's water, don't you?"

The squirrel tilted its head.

She patted her shoulder. "Come on. Lead the way. I'll give you something good."

She pulled out a bag of mixed nuts and poured some into her palm. The squirrel twitched its nose and immediately jumped down.

Half an hour later, Evelyn arrived at a small marshy hollow.

The water pool was not large, about the size of a small garden pond, but it was deep. The water was clear.

She crouched and tasted it.

Sweet and clean.

She pulled empty buckets and tanks from her space.

She had a pump, but it would be too loud and might attract trouble. Instead, she used hoses to siphon the water into the containers, filling them one by one.

Then, on impulse, she took out shampoo.

Using a basin of water, she washed her hair quickly. Thankfully, it was unscented.

Her short hair dried fast in the breeze.

She studied the pool, deciding she would tell Calvin about it later so they could collect water too.

Marshy areas often attracted animals.

She decided to stay and lie in wait.

If she could catch a muntjac or even a deer, this trip would be more than worth it.

She had told Eddie she would return in two hours.

That clearly was not happening.

But after traveling together this long, she trusted Calvin to handle things.

She hid in a patch of rushes, cradling the squirrel in her arms while keeping watch.

Washing her hair made her feel much better. Luckily, she didn't get lice or lose any hair.

Evelyn put on a black hat and a scarf. The squirrel sniffed her hair and seemed far more pleased this time.

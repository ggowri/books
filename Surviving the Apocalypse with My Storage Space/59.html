Chapter 59 Heatwave, The Van

Evelyn's injuries this time were serious.

She would need to rest for a while. And with her period about to start, she did not have the energy to go scavenging anyway.

When she brought Eddie his share of the weapons, he was at home assembling a fan, using leftover materials Calvin had collected earlier.

"You're giving me two?" His eyes widened. "You're really giving them to me?"

Evelyn nodded. "Didn't you say fifty-fifty?"

"I was joking. You risked your life to get those. One is enough. You can give me an extra box of ammo instead."

She let out a soft scoff. "That's all you get. Don't want it? I'll take it back."

She reached for the weapons.

Eddie immediately hid them behind his back, glaring at her defensively.

"You gave it to me. It's mine now. No take-backs."

"Do you even know how to use it? Don't shoot yourself by accident."

That clearly hit a nerve.

"I'm not that stupid," he snapped, face flushing. "You're the injured one. Go rest. I'm busy."

Evelyn glanced at the pile of scrap metal on his floor and shook her head in open disdain before walking off.

Behind her, Eddie stared at her retreating figure, clutching his chest in frustration.

*****

By afternoon, the claw wounds were showing signs of infection.

The trauma had been severe, and without stitches, inflammation was inevitable. The cuts were red, swollen, hot to the touch. Pus mixed with blood seeped from the edges.

Worried about tissue necrosis, Evelyn cleaned the wounds again. She scraped away the discharge, disinfected thoroughly, applied ointment, and started antibiotics.

To avoid worsening the inflammation, she did not even dare lie down. She dozed sitting upright.

Thankfully, by the next morning, the wounds began to improve.

Surprisingly, she never developed a fever.

That was good news.

After several illnesses and injuries over the past months, her body seemed to be adapting to the brutal climate.

Still, before the wounds fully healed, her period arrived.

She spent several more days in bed.

By the time she recovered enough to step outside again, Eddie had already blended seamlessly into life in Block D.

With him added to the team, several supply runs had yielded unexpected gains.

How long would this extreme heat last?

Evelyn did not know.

In this life, too many timelines had shifted. The black acid rain that was supposed to arrive a month after the heatwave had not come.

All she could do now was improve her stamina and prepare at least a year or two's worth of cooked food.

One afternoon, Evelyn was asleep when the sudden roar of an engine jolted her awake.

Thinking it might be intruders, she grabbed her crossbow and rushed to the balcony.

Instead, she saw Eddie and the others pulling into the complex in a van.

She blinked in surprise.

The roads outside were practically destroyed. Even bicycles and scooters were difficult to use, let alone something that size.

Under this heat, tires could soften or explode.

Downstairs, they were already setting up a makeshift garage.

When Evelyn came down, they were buzzing with excitement.

"Evelyn, look at this," Eddie called out proudly. "Found it in a factory warehouse. It was flooded, but it still runs."

Aside from the vehicle, they had brought back a considerable haul of instant noodles and bottled water.

"How did you even drive it back?" she asked.

Eddie wiped sweat from his forehead.

"Most cars wouldn't survive these roads. But this kind is tough. Smells awful. Loud engine. But it's practically top-tier for apocalypse mode."

Calvin laughed as he unloaded supplies from the back and gave Eddie a thumbs-up.

"Eddie did great today. We wouldn't have found half this stuff without him."

Eddie lifted his brows smugly.

"Piece of cake. I couldn't have done it alone though. Good thing we've got Calvin, combat power off the charts. And Ray and Evan backing us up. Plus, I used the map Evelyn drew. So the credit isn't just mine."

Evelyn glanced at him, mildly surprised.

Maybe he really did have a talent for scavenging.

"While we were unloading, I saw movement in a building across the street," Calvin said, looking toward the van with faint concern in his eyes. "There are still plenty of people in the city."

He paused.

"And in Northside, several buildings are already on fire. If the flames spread this way, we might have to relocate."

Evelyn looked outside thoughtfully.

"Supplies are getting harder to find," Calvin continued. "If we can't find water, we won't last three days. If the heat doesn't stop, if the ground keeps cracking and groundwater never resurfaces, staying here is just waiting to die."

The earlier excitement faded instantly.

Everyone knew he was right. This was the problem they would have to face sooner or later.

"Where would we even go?" Ray asked quietly. "Will Elaris turn into a sea of fire?"

"Maybe we should go to the base," Evan suggested, glancing at the others.

"What about you two?" Calvin looked at Evelyn and Eddie. "You're in better shape than the rest of us."

"Don't be so pessimistic," Eddie said, stepping forward. "The fires are still in Northside. Elaris is huge. It won't reach us easily. The trees are gone, and there's plenty of distance between buildings. Without fuel, fire won't spread that fast. Even if it does, that's months away. As long as we're alive, there's always a way."

He went down the line, patting shoulders.

When he reached Evelyn, she stepped back instinctively, avoiding her still-healing wounds.

Eddie grinned and continued.

"Besides, we've got a vehicle now. From now on, we keep an eye out for fuel during supply runs. If the day comes when we need to escape, we hit the gas and all six of us leave together. No one gets left behind."

He shot Evelyn a look, clearly asking her to back him up.

"My thoughts are about the same," she said simply.

She did not know what else to say.

"Enough with the long faces. Calvin, let's divide the supplies. With the water and instant noodles, Wendy can finally get an upgrade."

Calvin steadied himself and handed everything out. Then they all headed upstairs.

Eddie lingered.

"Evelyn," he called as they reached the tenth floor. He did not open his door immediately. "Why don't you want to go to the base?"

She stopped.

"After everything that's happened, would you still trust strangers?" she countered.

"I'd be cautious, sure. And I'm especially afraid of women who cry all the time," he added seriously.

Evelyn's lips twitched, but she said nothing.

"Most of the bad people I've met were men," he continued. "Like those two who robbed me last time. If you hadn't shown up, I'd be dead."

"So you don't want to go to the base because you're afraid of running into bad people?"

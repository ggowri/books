Chapter 17 Rainstorm, Venom Moths: Part 13

"If the rescue team doesn't come soon, are we really going to die here?

"Eddie… do you think we'll make it out alive?"

"I don't know," the man muttered. "Hurry up and find something to eat. Cody and Marcus already raided the upper floors. If we don't find anything here, we'll have to go down."

"But don't you remember? Ethan and the others were attacked by the moths in the cafeteria… I'm scared. I don't want to go down."

"Then starve," Eddie Thompson snapped, his tone sharp and irritated.

Evelyn listened silently from behind the office door, one hand on the handle, her body pressed to the wall. Through the frosted glass, she could make out two figures approaching. They were wrapped tightly in makeshift cloaks made from curtains. The smaller one, a woman, clung nervously to the man's back. The sparks from their torch crackled loudly in the quiet hall.

So there were survivors after all.

That meant there was no point wasting time searching the upper floors. Her goal was already here.

Evelyn stilled completely.

The pair passed her door and entered the office next to hers. She heard them rummaging through drawers and cabinets. After a few minutes, Eddie cursed loudly and kicked something over.

"Eddie, help me! The moths! There are so many! They're all around me! My torch is dying!" the girl screamed.

"Useless idiot. Stop screaming."

"I'm scared, Eddie! Don't leave me!"

"I said shut up!"

A loud rustle followed as Eddie tore down a curtain and lit it on fire. He tossed it toward the girl without a second glance and went back to searching for supplies.

Then, footsteps came, even closer.

Evelyn raised her dagger, steady and silent.

Eddie stopped just outside her office door.

He didn't push or try to open it. Instead—

Knock knock knock.

"Is someone in there?"

"Eddie? What are you doing? Who are you talking to?" the girl called nervously, dragging the burning curtain behind her. She looked around, anxious and jumpy.

"These moths are horrifying. Let's go back upstairs…"

"Whoever's inside," Eddie said smoothly, "we mean no harm. There's just two of us. We both work for this company. I'm from the lab—name's Eddie Thompson."

"Is there really someone in there?" the girl whispered, hiding behind him again, her voice trembling.

"Shut up," Eddie growled under his breath.

She pouted but obediently went silent.

"I know someone's inside," Eddie said calmly. "I'm very familiar with this building. If you need something, I might be able to help."

The girl shot him a glance, clearly not liking the way he was talking.

Evelyn hesitated for a few seconds, weighing the risk. Then she made her choice.

The door opened.

All three froze as they finally saw each other.

Eddie and the girl instantly noticed the cold gleam of the dagger in Evelyn's hand.

"Who are you? What are you doing here?" the girl blurted out first.

Evelyn ignored her, eyes fixed on Eddie. "You said you know this place. Then tell me—what's the situation in this building?"

"…You're a girl?"

They both looked stunned. Despite the heavy protective suit, goggles, and mask covering her face, Evelyn's tall figure and sharp presence had led them to assume she was male.

Evelyn's tone chilled. "Is that a problem?"

She closed the door behind her, arms crossed, still staring Eddie down.

"I never made a sound. How did you know someone was here?"

Eddie scanned the office with his torch, noting that there really wasn't anyone else.

That seemed to put him a bit at ease.

"I told you," he said, "I work in the lab. I've got a sharp nose. I can detect subtle scents easily. Besides, the smell of insecticide on you is strong. When did you come into the building?"

"Two hours ago," Evelyn replied coolly.

Eddie's eyes narrowed slightly. "Were you here to hide from the rain, or… were you here for supplies? You came fully prepared. That means you've got a plan. So, tell me—do you have a rubber raft?"

Evelyn had already guessed his intention. "I do." She traced a finger along the edge of her knife, eyes fixed on him.

"Good," Eddie nodded, quickly continuing. "Here's what happened. After the storm hit on April 1st, a lot of employees got trapped in the building. Some tried to leave through the flood, others stayed hoping it would pass in a day or two. But the rain never stopped. We waited for the rescue team… but no one came. Phone lines were dead. Fire department, police, everything—gone.

"We even wrote SOS on the windows, but no one responded. Then came the power outage, water cut-off, the signal blackout. The floodwaters kept rising, the temperature dropped… and then those venomous moths appeared. The ones that eat human flesh."

A flash of fear crossed Eddie's eyes as he spoke.

"I've seen the bodies," he continued, voice low. "There are skeletons on almost every floor. Two people froze to death early on, and after that, we all moved to the top floors. Before the moths showed up, Cody and Marcus took all the food from the upper floors and hoarded it. The rest of us were forced to search the lower floors for supplies. But then people started getting bitten. The ones who were bit… didn't last long. The moths swarmed and devoured them."

Evelyn's fingers drummed against the hilt of her blade.

"How many people are left upstairs?"

"Including me and Nina, there are twenty-four. Besides Cody and Marcus's group, the rest of us have no food left. Today was our turn to come down and look for some."

Evelyn weighed the information in silence.

Eddie studied her. "So? What are you looking for? If I help you, will you get me out of here?"

Their eyes met. Neither blinked.

Evelyn didn't respond at once. But she didn't deny it either.

That was enough for Eddie.

"You're crazy!" Nina snapped, finally losing patience. "This is company property! You can't just take it! If the rescue team shows up, I'll report you both to the police!"

Eddie sneered, "Sure. Send in the complaints. I don't care anymore—I just want out of this hellhole."

Evelyn cut in calmly, "You want to go home? Where do you live?"

"Kensington Heights. I live on the 26th floor." His voice trembled with hope as he looked at her like she was his only salvation.

"Fine," she said. "I want life-saving medicine."

"I'll take you to the lab. We've been developing some new treatments there."

Evelyn liked dealing with smart people. No nonsense, straight to the point. She nodded in agreement.

She picked up her extinguished pine torch and used Eddie's to relight it. Whoosh! The flame came to life again.

"Let's go."

"No!" Nina stepped forward. "Eddie, you can't take her there! That stuff belongs to the company. It's not yours to give away! What if the rescue team comes? I'll tell them you both stole—"

"Do whatever you want," Eddie said coldly.

As the two prepared to leave, Nina shot Evelyn a venomous glare. "You thief! This is robbery! You're going to prison!"

Evelyn didn't even look at her. She simply raised the torch higher and walked off without a word.

The lab was on the 48th floor. As promised, the "special medicine" wasn't a massive supply—just forty boxes in total. Still, in the current world, that was priceless.

Once inside, Eddie watched Evelyn unzip her protective suit, revealing a black waterproof pouch from her inner layer. Calmly, she packed the medicine away.

He glanced behind them, and his expression darkened.

"Nina is gone," he said sharply. "Hurry. She's gone to the top floor… to tell on us."

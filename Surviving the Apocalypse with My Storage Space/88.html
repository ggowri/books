Chapter 88 Struggling to Survive Alone 12

At that moment, Evelyn noticed another massive trigram symbol in the middle of the tomb passage. The lighting was so dim that it was barely visible.

She stepped onto it.

Nothing happened.

Annoyed, she jumped and stomped hard.

Creak…

The trigram symbol suddenly split open beneath her feet.

"Ah!"

"Oh crap, my leg, my waist, my butt… damn, that hurts."

She crashed onto a pile of wooden chests below, wincing in pain. Gritting her teeth, she looked up at the trigram panel above, which had already sealed itself shut.

She forced down the urge to curse and slowly climbed to her feet.

That fall had to be more than four meters. Her entire body felt like it had been shaken apart. Every bone ached.

This chamber held more than twenty chests stacked together.

She had a sinking feeling they would be filled with the same useless material as before. Still, she opened one.

Her eyes widened instantly.

Inside was a box of exquisitely crafted porcelain bowls. The glaze had not faded at all. Each one was unique, beautifully made, perfectly preserved.

Her heart began to race.

Antiques.

Unable to contain her excitement, she opened the rest of the chests.

All porcelain.

Plates. Bowls. Vases.

Some were single glaze pieces. Others were painted in vivid colors. Not one of them was damaged.

There was no gold or silver, but the porcelain alone was enough to make her giddy.

Before the apocalypse, any single piece in these boxes could have sold for millions.

Now that they were in her hands, they would probably do nothing but collect dust.

She shut the lids and stored everything in her space. Miraculously, her back no longer hurt, and her legs did not feel sore either.

Today had already been a huge success.

Time to head home.

By now, she had figured out the pattern. The exit would either be underfoot or hidden in the walls. As long as she searched carefully, she would find it.

Sure enough, she noticed a patch of stone with a slightly different color.

She scanned the ground inch by inch with her flashlight, leaving no corner unchecked.

Finally, directly below where she had fallen from the previous trigram symbol, she found another one.

The moment she stepped on it, a stone door creaked open.

Beyond it stretched a narrow corridor that seemed to go on forever. After walking for nearly forty minutes, she finally saw another door.

*****

Stepping through, she found herself back at the cavern entrance.

The opening was about ten meters above the exit. After wandering through the cavern, she realized that the entire route from entrance to exit formed a loop.

It felt like she had just stepped out of another world.

If not for the porcelain safely stored in her space, she might have believed everything inside the cavern had been a dream.

The breathtaking stalactites. The dense swarm of bats. The faintly glowing skeletons on the stone platform. The stone doors opened by trigram symbols. The bizarre tomb hidden deep within.

All of it felt unreal.

But what about the map she had found on the first platform?

The structure of that tomb was completely different from what had been drawn.

And the burial goods had only been those dozen or so boxes of porcelain.

Were there other hidden passages?

She had a strong feeling that the cavern held more secrets.

Maybe she would come back someday and see if she could locate the place marked on the map.

Finding a secluded spot, she finally took care of her urgent need, then sat down to rest for ten minutes.

Afterward, she took the eight skeletons out of her space.

She found a relatively flat patch of ground, shoveled away the snow, then used a hoe to dig a large pit.

Carefully, she buried them all together.

It was the least she could do for them.

She scattered a handful of flower seeds over the fresh mound of earth, put away her tools, and prepared to head home.

It was later than she thought. Without realizing it, she had spent the entire day inside.

By the time she trudged back through the snow to her yard, she noticed a few new footprints outside the fence gate. She did not think much of it, simply sliding the latch into place before stepping inside.

Sprig and Fallon were perched by the window watching for her. She had been gone too long. The two little creatures looked restless.

Shaking the snow from her coat, she hurried inside the cabin and locked the door.

The room still held a trace of warmth. Only a few glowing embers remained in the fireplace. She kicked off her boots and coat and quickly added more firewood.

"Sprig, Fallon, you guys okay?"

Fallon flew straight to her hat and settled there. Sprig launched itself into her arms, rubbing against her chest. She soothed them for a long while, then refilled their bowls. Beans for Sprig. A slice of lean raw meat for Fallon, who had developed a taste for it lately.

Once they were fed, Evelyn sat by the fire to warm up.

She took out a single color glazed porcelain vase from her space, wiped it clean, and placed it on the low table by the fireplace. After trimming the dead branches she had picked up in the mountains, she arranged them inside.

The pale celadon vase paired with bare winter branches gave off a quiet, artistic charm.

After resting enough, she began preparing dinner. She was too tired to cook anything complicated. A bowl of ginger soup to drive out the cold, and one of the ready made boxed meals from her space would do.

Once she finished eating, the soreness from her fall began to set in. Every bruised spot throbbed.

She gritted her teeth and took a long hot shower. Afterward, she applied medicated patches to the worst areas.

The sweater still needed work. She climbed onto the warm brick bed, turned on the TV, and resumed crocheting. Sprig and Fallon curled up near her pillow, watching along with her.

The mountain trip had been rewarding, but exhausting. She decided to stay home for a while.

The temperature had dropped another two degrees. Sleet was expected tonight, and the wind had not stopped howling.

At nine o'clock, she turned off the lights and went to sleep, wondering how long this snowstorm would last.

She also wondered how Calvin and the others were doing.

*****

Inside a mountain cave, Calvin was tending a fire.

They had gotten lucky today. He and Eddie had managed to catch a foolish roe deer in the snow. It was even bigger than a goat. Enough to feed five people for quite some time.

After the earthquake, Calvin, Wendy, Ray, and Evan had stuck together. Later, they reunited with Eddie. Thankfully, none of them were injured.

When Evelyn went missing, the group lost their anchor. It was then that Calvin made the decision to continue north toward Drakepool Base.

After the heavy snow began, he found a large cave for them to shelter in temporarily.

At first, Eddie insisted on going back to search for Evelyn.

But Calvin remembered her words. If they ever got separated, they were not to look for each other. Just head north, and they would eventually reach Drakepool Base.

He did not know where Evelyn was.

But he believed she was still alive.

*****

The morning wind was especially biting.

Evelyn glanced at the time. It was ten thirty.

Listening to the wind roar outside her window made her want to stay under the covers forever.

The brick bed had cooled down. She added two more logs to the fire, glanced at the equally sluggish Sprig and Fallon, and made a decision.

It was a lazy day so she pulled the blanket over her head and went back to sleep.

At two in the afternoon, she woke up urgently, needing to pee.

Now she really had to get up.

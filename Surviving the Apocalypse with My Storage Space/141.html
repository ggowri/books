Chapter 141 Toxic Haze 3

After several rounds of trial and error, it became clear that plants really did grow faster in the toxic haze.

So Evelyn and Adrian moved into the empty villa next door and decided to grow vegetables and medicinal herbs there. Elliot and Clara moved into the one behind them. They were living separately now, but still within the same safe stretch of villas.

It was a pity the signal tower and the wind turbine had both been ruined by the acid rain. Otherwise, Evelyn would have taken them into her hidden space. They might have been useful someday.

She planned to grow mostly herbs. The tiles on the ground floor had already been prised up, and fortunately the soil underneath had not been corroded. Evelyn divided the earth into neat squares so different herbs could be planted separately.

She put vegetables and maize around the outer edges. She was gifted at this sort of thing, and the camellia seeds she had planted in flowerpots had already grown into little saplings about twenty centimetres high. Even the cactus she had brought from Elaris was still stubbornly alive.

“Evelyn, be careful these next few days,” Eddie said when he came by. “Someone tried to steal the vegetables last night. There were several of them. Shame they got away.”

He stared at the green shoots pushing up through the soil, open amazement all over his face.

“This doesn’t look like wheat. What is it?”

“Medicinal herbs on this side,” Evelyn said. “Cabbage and maize over there. I’ll give you a spray bottle later. It’s filled with chilli water I mixed up. You can use it to defend yourself.”

Eddie pulled his ragged coat tighter round himself and nodded at once. “Brilliant. Those people have probably been watching us for ages. If they come back tonight, they’re getting a proper lesson.”

After a moment’s thought, Evelyn gave him a few extra bottles of her homemade chilli spray and told him to pass them round to Calvin and the others.

Whitmore’s group got some too.

Once she had moved out, Evelyn found life far easier. Whether it was eating, drinking, or doing things around the house, she no longer had to be so careful about hiding everything from the others.

As more rainwater was collected, the plants began to thrive, and oddly enough life started to improve.

Apart from the heavy black haze outside, of course, which still made it hard to breathe at times.

If anyone came by carrying timber and wanted to barter for food, they would trade a bit.

Evelyn caught sight of herself in the mirror and paused.

She had not washed her face in nearly a month.

She was so filthy she could have joined a band of beggars without anyone blinking.

In a rare fit of indulgence, she heated water and had a proper wash. Naturally the dirty water was saved to water the vegetables afterwards. Adrian did the same. He had not had any extra food lately either, and every day he was hauling and lifting heavy loads.

Since moving out, he had clearly been in better spirits.

“What’s for dinner tonight?” he asked.

“Grilled fish.”

Evelyn clicked her fingers. “Done.”

Each villa stood several metres apart, and since the sour stench left behind by the acid rain still lingered everywhere, the smell of grilled fish was quickly lost in the air. Besides, Evelyn had a smart extractor fan.

Once the doors and windows were shut and food had been put into Pip and Lark’s bowls, the two of them started preparing supper.

“Good thing I stocked so many boxed meals in the hidden space before all this.”

Adrian sat staring quietly at the fish on the grill. Every time Evelyn spoke to him, he just nodded absent-mindedly.

“The cabbages downstairs are ready to pick. Once we harvest them tomorrow, we’ll plant more. The herbs will probably need another month or two.”

Adrian nodded again.

“The haze is likely to last a long time. My instincts are usually right, so we still need to focus on herbs.”

“Can we eat yet?” Adrian asked, dead serious.

Evelyn snorted. “Go on, then. It’s bighead carp hauled out of the Silvermere River.”

“Are we having fish again tomorrow?”

“That depends on my mood.”

Adrian responded exactly as expected. “You rest. I’ll do the work.”

In the dim yellow emergency light, Evelyn pressed her lips together and smiled. “All right then.”

The base no longer had anyone managing it. On the surface things looked more stable, but crimes still happened now and then.

Whitmore and his two companions had no intention of stepping in, and Evelyn’s group certainly had no desire to take on that sort of trouble.

One day, a husband and wife knocked on the door of Calvin’s villa. The second the door opened, they dropped to their knees and begged for help.

Evelyn and Adrian had come by to deliver some herbs and happened to witness the whole thing.

“Our child disappeared last night,” the woman cried. “We’ve looked everywhere. We can’t find him. Two days ago, the child next door vanished as well. His mother searched for two days and found nothing. Over the past fortnight, five children and two young girls have disappeared without a trace. People said those living here are capable. Please, help us find the child. Please.”

When Calvin had gone out digging for soil before, he had once saved a child who was about to be dragged away. He was tall, broad, ferocious in a fight, but he had principles. Because of that, he had built quite a name for himself in the base.

Most people did not dare provoke him, and that was one reason the parents had come to him now.

Calvin glanced at Wendy. After a brief hesitation, he agreed.

“I can help you look,” he said, “but I can’t promise we’ll find anyone. There are still ten or twenty thousand people in the base. Finding one person won’t be easy.”

Even that promise was enough to make the parents overwhelmingly grateful.

Evelyn felt there was something wrong about the whole matter, and she decided to help too.

Once she joined in, the others naturally followed. Whitmore and the other two stayed behind at the villa with Wendy, while everyone else went out with Calvin to search.

So many missing children had already set everyone’s minds racing. They could only hope their suspicions were wrong. Since the apocalypse, fewer and fewer children had been born. No matter how ruthless people had become, surely no one should have been laying hands on children.

Evelyn disliked unnecessary trouble, but this was not something she could watch coldly from the sidelines.

Adrian stayed close beside her every step of the way, so the two of them searched together. They went from the Administration Hall to the Medical Division entrance, then through the residential areas in every direction, the laboratory building, the Underground Quarter, and the market.

They found nothing.

Had the missing people been taken outside the base?

The haze made it difficult even to see properly, let alone search. Spending too long in it wore everyone down.

Several of the missing children’s parents had turned up, but the two girls had no family with them. No one even seemed to know their names.

“They lived next door to us,” one resident said. “We didn’t really speak. I think they were sisters.”

There were very few useful leads.

All they could do was keep searching.

After hours of nothing, even Evelyn began to feel anxious.

“Can we still find them? At this point you can’t even tell whether someone three metres away is a man or a woman. Looking for anyone in this is nearly impossible.”

If only they had a tracking dog.

That thought made something click in her mind.

She hurried home and came back carrying Pip in her arms.

“Calvin, Pip might be able to help.”

Calvin understood at once. He asked the parents to fetch something the missing child had worn or used, then let Pip sniff it.

“Pip, can you help us find this person? Have a good smell. Can you remember it and take us to him?”

Pip flicked his tail with typical arrogance and nosed the grubby little jumper for a while. After a moment, he lifted his head, looked at Evelyn, and rubbed his face against her hand.

“You remember the scent, do you? Good. Then take us to him now. Find the child, and you’ll get an extra meal tonight.”

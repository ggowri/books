Chapter 11 Rainstorm: Part 7

"Oh, is that so? Then poor Zack and Pauline really do have it rough. Mrs. Rivers, why are you still alive anyway? Shouldn't you hurry up and die? Free up your apartment for your kids so they can live in a big house too. You're just taking up space and wasting food. And by the way, stop dumping your trash at my door."

Fiona had expected Evelyn to be ashamed or flustered. Instead, Evelyn shot the words right back at her—smiling sweetly as she casually told her to drop dead.

The moment Evelyn looked at her, Fiona felt a chill creep up her spine.

"Evelyn, what's wrong with you? Throwing garbage at my door like that!" Pauline snapped, storming forward to shove her.

But Evelyn shoved her back without hesitation. Pauline stumbled and fell, landing hard on the floor.

"Pauline," Evelyn said mildly, "I heard you got bitten by a dog a few months ago. Better be careful. Rabies has a long incubation period. As for the trash..."

Pauline winced, sitting awkwardly where she'd fallen, ready to start cursing. But before she could open her mouth, Evelyn picked up the trash bag and stuffed it right into Fiona's arms.

"Returning it to the rightful owner." And with that, Evelyn locked the door behind her and headed downstairs.

"Mom, are you seriously just standing there? Throw that garbage out! That witch Evelyn pushed me and you just stood there doing nothing! And you," Pauline turned and barked at Quinn, "help me up, already!"

Fiona finally snapped out of it and realized her back was soaked in cold sweat.

"Did you see her eyes just now? Like she was ready to kill me."

Pauline rolled her eyes. "Seriously, you're scared of someone younger than you?"

Fiona couldn't explain it. Every time she dealt with Evelyn, she couldn't help tossing out those sharp, passive-aggressive jabs. She hadn't even thought twice about what she said just now—it had just slipped out. But the memory of Evelyn's gaze still made her skin crawl.

Ignoring the trash clutched in her arms, she and Pauline quickly slipped back inside. Quinn turned and glanced once at Evelyn's door before following them in.

Downstairs, Evelyn's tightly clenched fists finally loosened. A tiny prick dotted her index finger—a nearly invisible mark.

The needle, however, was no longer with her. It was now inside Fiona.

Evelyn hadn't wanted to go that far, but after repeated provocations, she figured it was time Fiona got a little reminder.

She grabbed the rake she'd fashioned from a clothes-drying rod and started fishing floating planks out of the water. Everyone else was gathering firewood, so she had to make a show of it too.

Once she'd bundled a pile, she turned to head back.

That's when Zack and Henry came paddling back in their inflatable boat. Both of them looked miserable. Their raincoats were shredded, their clothes soaked through. Lips blue from the cold, they shivered as they unloaded their collection of branches.

Evelyn tied up her bundle with twine, hoisted it up, and hurried upstairs. The water level was already reaching the second floor. Most of the neighbors out gathering wood had retreated to the third floor. No one had time for small talk. Everyone was focused on survival.

Back inside, Evelyn laid the firewood out to dry on the balcony.

The blackout was now total. Signal bars flickered in and out. She barely checked the group chat anymore. It was supposed to be a mutual aid group, but at this point, everyone was too busy keeping themselves alive to help anyone else.

Half an hour later, someone slammed violently on her door. Evelyn instantly recognized Zack's voice.

He was tall, stocky, and had a temper like a time bomb. He was pounding on her door, shouting furiously.

"Evelyn Foster, you damn brat, open the door! You shoved my sister and dumped trash on my mom. I'm gonna kill you for that!"

Evelyn rolled her wrist a few times, then calmly opened the door. Just as Zack's hand came down to bang again, she brought a kitchen knife down in one swift motion.

Startled, Zack jerked back just in time. He was quick, she'd give him that.

"Evelyn, you seriously just tried to hit me with a knife? After mistreating my mom and my sister? You wanna die?"

"It's not me who wants to die," Evelyn said coolly. "It's you."

David stepped forward, his expression heavy with disappointment. "Evie, how could you pull a knife on someone? Fiona and I watched you grow up. Today you cursed her to die, shoved garbage at her, and pushed Pauline. She's bleeding, for heaven's sake. What's gotten into you?"

"Mr. Rivers," Evelyn replied flatly, "you know better than anyone what kind of people your wife and daughter are. There's no need to twist the truth here. Me? I've got no one. So if anyone comes after me, I'll drag their whole family down with me. I've got nothing to lose. So go ahead. Try me."

By then, neighbors from the eleventh and twelfth floors had come down to see what the commotion was about. Even the folks below were peeking up the stairwell.

When everyone saw Evelyn standing there with a knife, facing off against the overbearing Rivers family in the hallway, the looks on their faces said it all.

Fiona burst into tears, adding every exaggeration she could think of to make Evelyn look bad. But people in this building knew each other too well. Everyone knew who was really who.

Evelyn had always been quiet, serious, and polite. And she was a med student, after all. In times like these, who didn't need someone who could treat fevers and injuries?

"No way Evelyn bullied you," someone spoke up. "She's just a kid. And Fiona, maybe focus on your grandson screeching like a banshee at all hours before you start pointing fingers."

"Fiona, you're really saying she cursed you to die? Please. But honestly, death comes for everyone eventually. No need to be so superstitious."

"David, you've always been the nice guy around here. But your wife's picked fights with just about every floor in this building over the years. And now you're ganging up on Evelyn just because her parents are gone? That's low."

Of course, not everyone took her side. Some had to sneak in their little digs.

"But Evelyn really has changed. She wouldn't even let us borrow her boat."

"She's gotten weird, honestly. Hard to get close to."

"Really, Laura?" Claire cut in, pushing through the crowd and shooting her a look of disgust. "Evelyn treated your daughter for free, and you still talk about her like that?"

"I'm just being honest," Laura mumbled, shrinking back. "I didn't mean anything by it."

Evelyn looked up at the crowd, her expression calm.

"Thank you everyone who spoke up for me. I won't forget it. As for what really happened, I think everyone here knows the truth. The Rivers family thinks they can take advantage of me because I don't have any elders. They dumped their trash at my door, tried to push me out of Willow Heights, and planned to steal my home. That's beyond shameless.

"This is my house. No one gets in."

At the sight of Evelyn holding the knife, the more timid neighbors instinctively backed up. A few who thought about stepping in to scold her changed their minds real fast when they caught a glimpse of that blade.

The crowd began eyeing the Rivers family differently. Evelyn's version of events might've sounded a little extreme, but no one could deny it had a ring of truth. The Rivers family looked like they wanted to argue but ended up slinking away, closing their door behind them.

Evelyn made a point of thanking the neighbors and added that if anyone had headaches, fevers, or any other issues, she'd be willing to treat them for a small trade. Water, rice, or firewood would do.

Who would say no to that? In times like these, having a doctor around was as good as having a guardian angel.

Even those who'd just mocked her started to regret it.

Back inside, Evelyn set the knife back on the kitchen counter.

Zack had come to her. She'd simply used the opportunity to establish herself in this building once and for all.

As for the Rivers family...

Evelyn looked down at her hand, her expression unreadable.

Chapter 120 Encircled and Treated

“No, my girl... my little girl.”

The child’s mother could not accept what she was seeing. Clutching the girl’s body, she broke down in great heaving sobs. The moment Evelyn appeared, the woman rushed at her and struck her across the face.

“Why did you leave her? Why didn’t you save her?”

Evelyn could not bring herself to be angry with a mother who had just lost her child. She looked at the girl’s body and spoke in a low, steady voice. “Her body is dangerous. You cannot go near her.”

She tried to wipe the blood smeared across the woman’s face, but the woman shoved her away.

“Lily, Lily, wake up. Don’t leave Mum. Don’t go to sleep.”

Lily’s father had already seen the worms writhing in the blood. In tears, he tried to pull his wife away and get her out at once, but he was too late. She had already started coughing up blood in great choking gulps.

Evelyn reacted instantly. She kicked the man out of the ward, tied the woman down, and forced several albendazole tablets into her mouth. It did nothing. The woman screamed and shrieked, then began smashing her head against the wall.

Evelyn tried to save her. She took out medicine after medicine, using everything she had, but in the end she still could not bring the woman back. At the very last moment, the woman seemed to recover a flicker of consciousness. She struggled to lift a hand, as if she wanted to stroke her daughter’s face one final time, but her arm dropped heavily before she could reach her.

When Evelyn opened the ward door, Dr. Hargreaves came running over and asked whether she was all right. Evelyn shook her head. The man rushed inside, saw that his wife was gone too, and let out a hoarse cry, both hands flying to his head. Then he pulled a rusted dagger from his bag.

For one terrible second, Evelyn thought he meant to come for her.

Instead, he drove the blade straight into his own chest.

The Medical Division had become a sea of corpses. The dead were all being taken to a single ward. There were no body bags, so the bodies were simply piled together.

“Has the Medical Division reported this to Blackwater Base? What did they say?”

Dr. Hargreaves shook his head. “Everyone in the Medical Division is being allowed in, but no one is allowed out. We’ve been sealed in here. They haven’t given me a straight answer. They only told me to do everything I can, but there’s no medicine left. How exactly am I supposed to save anyone? Evelyn, it doesn’t matter if I die here, but you’re still young. There’s a narrow path at the back. Leave through there. I don’t think anyone in here is going to make it.”

Evelyn understood the unspoken meaning at once. The base had given up on treating them. To prevent the situation from spreading, it might decide to wipe out everyone trapped inside.

“But the nematodes only erupt once they get inside the body. This isn’t a virus. It doesn’t spread from person to person.”

Dr. Hargreaves gave a bitter smile. “People outside want to feel safe, and that means eliminating every possible threat. They don’t care whether there are still healthy people in here. Have you noticed who’s been dying the most these past few days? The people living in the dormitories.”

He looked at her meaningfully.

“I’ve seen too much life and death in hospitals. When people are pushed to the brink, plenty of them turn into monsters. And the world now is no longer a normal one.”

The Medical Division was soon surrounded. Outside stood armed guards with rifles. Anyone who fell ill was sent in. Once they died, their bodies were dragged away at once. As for where they were taken, Evelyn had no idea.

Food was still delivered twice a day, though it was nothing more than steamed buns and tins of meat.

The fishy smell rising from the tinned food turned Evelyn’s stomach. She could not force herself to eat it, so she slipped into the supply room and quietly topped herself up with food from her own stash.

She could not help wondering how Eddie and the others were doing.

Adrian had gone out hunting, and likely would not be back for several more days.

At least before leaving, she had put out a basin of food and a basin of water for Pip and Lark.

“Evelyn, Dr. Hargreaves is looking for you.” Clara finally found her, hands braced on her hips as she panted for breath.

“All right.”

Clara lowered her voice. “Apparently someone important has just been brought in. Orders from above say he must be kept alive.”

Evelyn rose and nodded. Before leaving, she handed Clara a chocolate bar.

“Evelyn, thank you.”

“You’re welcome.”

When she returned to the emergency floor, she saw the so-called important guest.

A young man in his twenties. Dr. Hargreaves quietly told her that this was Julian Hawthorne, the youngest son of the Hawthorne family. The family had made it very clear that nothing must happen to him.

Julian was immediately taken to a room on the top floor. Even though there was not a single clean patch left in the whole place, a private ward had to be prepared for him on the spot.

Evelyn wanted no trouble. She was already edging away as unobtrusively as possible when one of the Hawthorne family bodyguards stepped in front of her.

She studied Julian. He bore a faint resemblance to Young Mr. Hawthorne, perhaps five parts in ten. His features were ordinary enough, but his eyes were sharp, with something cold and dangerous about them.

He looked dreadful, though he had not yet begun vomiting blood. His private doctor informed everyone that Julian believed there were worms crawling through his body and inside his head.

So this was all in his mind?

Evelyn thought of the Hawthornes in their villa district, drinking purified water. How could someone like him possibly have become host to the nematodes?

“Get me the best equipment in the Medical Division and examine me from head to toe. Then give me some medicine and kill these things. I can’t stand it anymore. They keep crawling about inside my head.”

Dr. Hargreaves exchanged a glance with Evelyn. Neither of them wanted to be the one to step forward first.

“Mr. Hawthorne, in that case we should begin with a full examination. That would include blood tests, urine and stool analysis, ultrasound, X-rays, CT, MRI, and gastroscopy and colonoscopy.”

“What?” Julian snapped. “That much? Are you trying to finish me off? I’m in pain. I’m suffering. Can’t you understand that?”

His temper flared instantly. He snatched up the nearest thing to hand and looked ready to throw it.

Four-fifths of the doctors in the Medical Division had been summoned here. Downstairs, people were dying by the minute, yet every doctor had been pinned in place by armed bodyguards at the door. Not one of them was allowed to leave.

“Do you believe I can have you thrown out of the base?”

One of the administrators in the Medical Division was so frightened that he dropped to his knees and began apologising at once.

Evelyn stayed tucked away in the corner, watching it all in silence.

“Isn’t there any special medicine?” Julian demanded. “Aren’t there people here from Larkshore Pharmaceuticals in Elaris? Before everything fell apart, didn’t their company develop some sort of wonder drug? Where is it?”

Evelyn’s brow twitched. She lowered her head a little further.

“Mr. Hawthorne, the male employees from Larkshore are dead.”

“How did they die?”

“They were buried in the laboratory during the earthquake.”

Julian hurled another object across the room. No one dared dodge it. The blanket struck one of the doctors squarely on the head, and blood immediately began running down his face.

“I want that medicine. Get it made now. Bring everyone from the laboratory here and make them produce it at once.”

One of the doctors cautiously suggested he try a couple of herbal prescriptions first. Julian answered by slapping him across the face.

“I want the special medicine, nothing else. My head is full of worms. That drug is the only thing that can kill them. Do you understand? Get out. All of you.”

Evelyn got out with remarkable speed. With a tyrant like that installed upstairs, life for everyone else had just become a great deal harder.

There were even more bodies downstairs now. Someone tried to carry away a dead family member, but security guards blocked the way. The man lunged at them and began grappling with them on the spot.

A gunshot rang out.

He dropped dead where he stood, a bullet through the head.

Screams rose one after another. Evelyn looked around at the people fleeing in panic, at the bodies trampled underfoot on the floor, and felt her stomach churn violently.

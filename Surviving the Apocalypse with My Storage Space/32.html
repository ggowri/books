Chapter 32 Rainstorm, Mountain Flood

After a full day and night, the mountain floodwaters finally began to slow. Evelyn was bent over in the bathroom, vomiting nonstop. She had already thrown up bile streaked with blood, yet her stomach still churned violently.

Crying and shouting echoed from upstairs and downstairs. Evelyn squatted beside the litter box, retching again and again.

The water had already swallowed the seventh floor. Residents from the sixth and seventh floors had fled to the stairwell on the twelfth floor, barely escaping disaster.

Evelyn clenched the pocket watch hanging around her neck. When she flipped it open, a family photo stared back at her. It had been taken when she was sixteen. Her face was still childish, her hair cut short at her ears, standing between her parents and grinning foolishly at the camera.

Tears dripped onto the back of her hand. She took a deep breath, closed the watch, and slowly stood up.

There was a knock at the door. Evelyn's face was deathly pale as she dragged her exhausted body over to open it.

"Are you okay?" Claire asked, worry written all over her face.

"I'm fine. What about you?"

Claire shook her head. "I'm okay too. Luckily, I went out earlier and brought back some wooden boards and metal sheets. I sealed up the balcony in advance."

They had just started to relax when a wave of noise erupted from the eleventh floor. After listening for a few minutes, Evelyn and Claire exchanged a look. Both saw the same helpless resignation in each other's eyes.

In the hallway on the eleventh floor, several people were kneeling on the ground, murmuring endlessly.

"This is God's punishment. This is God's warning to us. Humans have destroyed nature, exploited energy, polluted the environment, so God has sent this disaster to warn us. Only by sincerely repenting, changing our ways, and becoming good people can God calm His anger and forgive us…"

"That's Jacelyn from 1102," Claire said with a snort. "Her two daughters live overseas. She stayed abroad for half a year before and somehow came back believing in God. The people kneeling behind her are survivors from the sixth floor. She took them in."

"Impressive," Evelyn said, folding her arms as she watched with interest. "Brainwashing works fast."

"Oh God, please forgive us ignorant humans."

"Forgive us. We were wrong. We don't want to die."

Evelyn felt the urge to vomit again. She waved goodbye to Claire and hurried back inside, rushing into the bathroom. She squatted in front of the litter box and threw up violently.

The prayers and confessions on the eleventh floor continued. Jacelyn proposed a three day fast to prove her sincerity to God.

The most terrified residents now were those on the eighth floor. If the water rose again, they would be finished.

William from 801 went to Calvin's home. He needed to plan ahead and make arrangements for his family.

No one knew what kind of agreement they reached, but Calvin's family agreed to take in William's family. They could only move in once the water reached the eighth floor. For now, residents from the seventh floor were temporarily placed in the stairwells of the eleventh and twelfth floors.

A black dog's corpse was wedged inside the iron railing outside Evelyn's balcony window. The balcony glass was fully sealed, with no opening left. All Evelyn could do was pray that it would fall on its own or be swept away by the water.

That afternoon, someone knocked on Evelyn's door again. She opened it and saw Jacelyn from the eleventh floor. A bad feeling immediately rose in her chest.

"Evie, God guided me to come find you. He wants you to repent with me and confess your sins. Come out and join us."

Evelyn was speechless.

"Jacelyn, I don't believe in God."

"Shut up. Don't spout nonsense and anger God. He will be displeased. You still don't even know what you did wrong. Such a foolish, pitiful human. Come out and kneel in repentance. As long as you fast for three days and truly repent, God will forgive you, and you will be allowed to live."

If she hadn't already been in her seventies, Evelyn's slap would have landed on her face.

What was this, some kind of pyramid scheme? It was already the end of the world, and she was still pulling this act.

"Really?" Evelyn said coldly. "Then have him come out. I'll ask him myself why I need to repent, and what exactly I'm supposed to repent for."

"Why, you! Stubborn and hopeless, Evelyn. Don't say I didn't warn you. If you refuse to repent, the next one to die will be you."

Evelyn rolled her eyes. "I think that'll be you."

With a loud bang, she slammed the door shut, then sprayed disinfectant all over herself, as if afraid stupidity was contagious.

Evelyn had no idea when the mountain floods would finally stop. In her previous life, they had lasted two or three days, followed by endless flooding.

But once the mountain floods began, the toads seemed to disappear again.

The floods carried down a lot of wood. Brave people started scavenging planks at the stairwell entrances. Sometimes they even found clothes. After being rinsed clean by the rain and dried over a fire, they could still be worn.

Of course, people sometimes got injured by debris swept in suddenly. The floodwater was murky, and it was impossible to see what was inside. Once there was a wound, it had to be cleaned and disinfected immediately, or it would get infected very quickly.

On the second day, the current slowed again, but the water level rose by more than a meter. Everyone was on edge. No one knew what kind of disaster would come next.

Evelyn felt like she was turning into a walking medicine bottle. Fortunately, after two days of taking medication, the nausea finally faded. Jacelyn's repentance group gained two more members, Calvin's mother and their next door elderly neighbor, Julie.

They knelt in the stairwell like people possessed, praying frantically. Evelyn felt nothing about it, but the others were tormented by the noise. Calvin tried to persuade them countless times. When it didn't work, he gave up.

That night, Jacelyn went to meet God personally, all for the sake of repentance.

Everyone from the seventh floor moved into her apartment.

After that, the repentance group officially disbanded, and the mountain floods finally stopped. They had simply turned into regular flooding.

Starting on May twentieth, the rainfall began to decrease. But with the toads gone, basic survival and food became a serious problem.

While salvaging wood, Calvin was bitten by something unknown. His right calf swelled up instantly, thick as a tree trunk. By the time Evelyn saw him, he had already passed out from the pain.

"Evie, will Calvin be okay?" Jessica asked anxiously. "Was he bitten by a leech?"

Evelyn examined the wound. There was a tiny red dot, no bigger than a pinprick. If you weren't looking carefully, you wouldn't even notice it.

"I'm not sure yet," Evelyn said. "We may have to cut it open and take a look."

"Cut it open?" Jessica went pale.

She looked utterly exhausted. A few days earlier, her mother-in-law had joined the repentance group, brainwashing them daily about how humanity was sinful and needed to atone to God.

After Jacelyn's death, her mother-in-law finally woke up. But now her husband was injured. Watching her father-in-law, who had been worn down to skin and bones by chronic illness, her constantly crying mother-in-law, and her young daughter, despair was written all over Jessica's face.

"Go ahead," she said, forcing herself to stay steady. "I'll hold him down."

Evelyn had no time to comfort her. She took out a scalpel and cut into the wound. Calvin woke up screaming in pain.

From inside the wound, Evelyn pulled out a white worm. Clearly, this was the culprit.

"What is that?" Jessica asked shakily.

"The insect that bit Calvin," Evelyn replied. "It was just about to burrow into his blood vessel."

After biting, it immediately drilled into the skin, then into the bloodstream. It could even reach the brain.

Evelyn pressed her lips together.

It seemed the next disaster had already arrived.

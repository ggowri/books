Chapter 146 Toxic Haze 8

With coal to burn, life in the Safe Zone became noticeably easier. Still, because of the cold, some people shut every door and window to keep the heat in, and cases of poisoning cropped up from time to time.

Fortunately, the survivors all seemed to have developed stronger constitutions after the acid rain. As long as treatment came in time, it rarely turned fatal.

Evelyn and Adrian also went out and brought back several cartloads of coal. Evelyn had a tricycle in her space, but it was far too new. To avoid drawing attention, Adrian had to rough it up first so it looked properly used.

While they were out digging, they took the chance to make a circuit of the hills. Any trees left standing after the corrosion had already been hauled away by the survivors in the Safe Zone. Several hills had been stripped bare, reduced to bald mounds.

Evelyn stored a few cartloads away in her space, enough that she would not need to come back for quite some time. She did not want to take too much. Other people still had to live.

Adrian drove his spade a few metres down into a hollow, but it was still just wasteland beneath the surface. The springs on those hills had dried up long ago, and not a blade of grass remained. The whole place looked like the surface of the moon.

The new arrivals had come to dig coal as well. They had no tricycles, so they could only bundle the coal into bed sheets or scraps of cloth and drag it back that way.

In the Safe Zone, a tricycle was one of the most valuable things a person could own. No one would willingly lend one out.

As soon as Evelyn returned to the villa, someone came to ask her to see a patient. The woman held out two eggs as payment. Evelyn took them, picked up her medical case, and went with her.

The patient was a young girl. It was her first period, and there had been so much blood that her family had been terrified.

Evelyn inserted a few needles for her, then explained about the acid-rain aftereffects. Only then did the girl and her family calm down.

The aftereffects of the acid rain were especially obvious, and often more severe, in women.

On her way back, Evelyn passed one of the houses where many of the elderly lived together. Someone waved to her from inside, and she raised a hand in return.

“Doctor Evelyn, I don’t think I’ve many days left.”

An old man coughed into his hand. Evelyn saw the smear of blood in his palm.

“Let me take a look at you.”

“No, no need, Doctor Evelyn. I won’t last more than a few days. When I die, could you pass a message to the security guards who deal with the bodies? Ask them to cremate me. Scatter my ashes in the direction of Seabourne. I want to go home and have one last look. Thank you. And thank them for me as well. I’m sorry to trouble everyone.”

For a long while, Evelyn could not speak.

The old man stood by the window inside the house. Evelyn stood outside. After a long silence, she gave a small nod.

“Is there anything else?”

“No. Nothing else. My son will come for me. They’ll all be waiting for me at home.”

He took out a piece of cloth and unfolded it. Inside were several strips of dried meat. Evelyn could tell from the smell that it was rat meat.

The old man held it out to her, giving her a shy little smile.

“Go on, Doctor Evelyn. Eat.”

Evelyn accepted the meat he offered. Very carefully, she tore off a tiny piece and put it into her mouth.

It was hard. Tough. Not at all pleasant.

On the walk back, she fought for a long time to force the tears down. Instead of going home, she went to find Mayor Whitmore and told him what the old man had said.

“His name is Walter Moore. He lived in Seabourne. His body won’t hold out for more than three days. He wants to be cremated, and he wants his ashes scattered in the direction of Seabourne. That’s all.”

Whitmore’s eyes reddened too. “All right. I’ll remember.”

Evelyn nodded. For all his faults, Whitmore was a decent leader. If he said he would do something, he did it.

When she got home, she saw Adrian shaping coal briquettes. She took a slow breath and tucked all her feelings away.

“What’s for supper?”

“Stewed hen in the iron pot.” Adrian pointed to the corner. Only then did Evelyn notice the hen tied up there.

“What did it do?”

“I was working. It came over and got in my way, then flung coal dust in my face. So now we’re eating it.”

Evelyn clicked her tongue. “You didn’t frame it on purpose, did you?”

“If I were framing one, I’d have picked the fattest bird.” Adrian pulled off his gloves after finishing the last briquette.

Evelyn glanced at Pip and Lark. The two of them had been shedding badly lately, and Pip seemed to have reached maturity. He howled at all hours of the day and night, which was enough to drive anyone mad. Lark hated the cold and had hardly flown out at all over the past year.

“Where on earth am I supposed to find Pip a mate? Not likely. Pip, maybe I should just neuter you. I’m quite good with my hands.”

Pip, clever creature that he was, bolted the second he heard that, tail flying behind him.

“You wouldn’t howl all day if you were neutered. No urges, no longing for a wife. Pip, my special package is exactly what you need.”

Pip ran even faster. Some of the gloom in Evelyn’s eyes lifted.

She and Adrian had not cooked together in ages. Lately everyone had been busy mining coal, leaving early and returning late.

Adrian had the chicks to look after, and Evelyn still had patients to treat. Most of the time, the two of them simply filled their stomachs with boxed meals from her space.

“I’ll boil the water. You kill the chicken.”

Adrian nodded. “Right.”

After more than an hour of work, supper was finally ready. They had only just sat down when Wendy suddenly started calling for Evelyn from outside.

“Doctor Evelyn, help! My dad’s about to be beaten to death!”

Evelyn and Adrian exchanged a glance, put the food away in the space, and went out together to see what had happened.

“How on earth has your dad ended up in a fight?”

Wendy was five and a half now, sharp as a tack and far too clever for her age.

“One of the aunties wants to be my stepmum, but my dad said no. Then her family said he ought to take responsibility, and Dad got angry and hit someone. But there are loads of them, so I think he might be losing. That’s why I came to get you.”

Evelyn shook her head with a sigh. Calvin was famously popular in the Safe Zone. Widows and unmarried girls alike all had their eyes on him.

This sort of thing was hardly new. By now, Evelyn was no longer surprised.

“Come on then. Let’s go save your father before he really is beaten to death.”

Wendy marched ahead carrying a stick over her shoulder, with Evelyn and Adrian following behind. Halfway there, they also ran into Eddie, Ray and Evan.

Eddie had shaved his head not long ago, and Evelyn spotted the shine of his bald scalp from several metres away.

“Wendy, your dad’s got a remarkable love life. Aren’t you worried he’ll actually find you a stepmother one day?”

Wendy shook her head. “Dad says everyone’s half-starving, so there’s no point wasting time on that sort of nonsense. And he only likes my mum. He looks at her photo when he thinks no one’s watching, then cries on his own. I know he does.”

That made Evelyn think of Jessica. She had been an exceptionally gentle, kind-hearted woman.

The moment they reached Wendy’s house, they heard cries of pain coming from inside. Calvin was standing at the door with an iron shovel in his hand. Several men were sprawled on the ground.

“Dad, thank goodness you’re all right. I thought you were about to be beaten to death, so I went and got help.”

The men on the ground had the same thought: who exactly was beating whom to death?

In the corner nearby, a young girl was sobbing quietly.

“Calvin Shaw, you’re marrying my sister today. A few days ago you put your arms round her. You have to take responsibility.”

Calvin was so furious he looked fit to burst. “She fell into a pit and couldn’t climb out. I pulled her up out of basic decency, and this is how you repay me?”

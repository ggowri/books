Chapter 41 Heatwave, Snake Infestation: Part 5

In high temperatures, it was best to eat cooling foods. Evelyn took out a bag of pea starch, a bag of mung beans, and a bag of coix rice. She planned to make cold noodles and mung bean soup.

She first prepared two small bowls, adding the right amount of starch and water to each, mixing them at a one to one ratio to make starch water. Using the same bowls, she poured five bowls of clean water into a pot and heated it. When the water was just about to boil, she slowly poured in the starch mixture, stirring steadily as she poured. The liquid gradually turned transparent and thick. She turned off the heat and poured it into a prepared basin. It needed four to five hours to cool completely. Once fully cooled, the cold noodles set.

Evelyn started preparing the toppings. She sliced cucumbers into thin shreds, chopped scallions and cilantro, cut the cold noodles into strips, then drizzled chili oil and dressing over it. Finally, she added the herbs and cucumber shreds.

Mung bean soup was even simpler. She rinsed the mung beans and steamed them for half an hour until soft. Then she washed the coix rice and candied dates, placed them in a small bowl, and steamed them together with the mung beans. She diced green plums, kumquats, and citron, boiled them in water, then poured the liquid into a bowl. After that, she added the steamed mung beans, coix rice, and dates, and finished with a sprinkle of osmanthus sugar. The mung bean soup was done.

Mung bean soup cleared heat, detoxified the body, and boosted appetite. It was perfect for hot weather.

Evelyn had not eaten rice for two days. The heat had gradually taken away her appetite. The air conditioner was fully installed now. She set it straight to seventeen degrees. Cool air washed over her as she ate cold noodles and drank mung bean soup, almost forgetting about the snakes lurking outside.

Most of the time, Evelyn passed the days watching movies and TV shows, or exercising. At night before bed, she recorded every small detail of the day in her journal. Then she would randomly open a book, read one page, and go to sleep.

She did not know how long she would have to hide here. But Evelyn believed that one day, she would leave this place. The outside world was just as dangerous. She needed to keep a clear, rational mind and a healthy, responsive body.

Of course, surviving in this cruel world also meant enduring loneliness. Evelyn had never been afraid of being alone. What she hated was the snake swarm trapping her outside, and the unpredictable, ever changing dangers beyond her door.

Late at night, she sometimes wondered if there were still people outside. She had not heard any movement upstairs or downstairs for days. Were Calvin's family, the Rivers family next door, Claire and Natalie and her mother downstairs still alive?

Sometimes, Evelyn imagined herself as another Robinson Crusoe. Robinson had survived twenty-eight years on a deserted island. With food and water secured, Evelyn believed she would not lose to him.

Occasionally, she still pulled back the curtain to bask in the sunlight. But most of the time, all kinds of snakes were coiled outside the window. The plants in her flowerpots had grown much larger. She set up a table by the window and placed the pots on it, letting the greenery absorb what little sunlight it could.

After long periods of training, Evelyn had sculpted a clean six-pack. Thankfully, she still had curves where she was supposed to. She had not turned herself into some muscle monster. After trimming her hair, Evelyn stood in front of the mirror admiring herself for a long time. Seeing that her overall condition still looked decent, she snapped her fingers in satisfaction.

The more hardship and adversity you face, the more you have to affirm your own worth. Only someone who has died once truly understands how precious it is to be alive.

Every morning when Evelyn woke up, the first thing she did was encourage herself.

Once she started using the air conditioner, the generator began burning fuel much faster. Even though she had plenty of gasoline and diesel stored in her space, it still hurt to watch it disappear. She regretted not stockpiling potassium nitrate. If she had, she could have made ice to cool down.

With the AC running, it burned half a liter of fuel per hour. After using it for a few days, Evelyn ultimately chose to switch back to fans.

She charged the batteries with solar panels, then used the batteries to power the fans. That way, she saved both fuel and electricity.

Using electrical wires, Evelyn rewired the apartment and installed light bulbs. From then on, she no longer needed emergency lamps for lighting.

After washing a plate of fruit and piling snacks on the table, Evelyn turned on the projector. With her headphones on, she ate while watching TV to decompress and kill time. Before the apocalypse, she never watched melodramatic family soap operas. Now, they had become her mental sustenance. Trivial family disputes, mother-in-law drama, domineering CEOs and Cinderella style love stories full of chasing and heartbreak. To her own surprise, Evelyn watched them with great interest.

Once the temperature passed thirty degrees, Evelyn had to use hydrating face masks every day. Otherwise, her skin could not handle it. If she did not slather on a thick layer of moisturizer, her face would quickly feel tight and start peeling.

Her body was the same, especially her calves, where the flaking was particularly severe.

The most dangerous time was during her period.

Snakes had extremely sensitive senses of smell. Even though Evelyn masked the scent with medicated oil, plenty of snakes were still drawn to her windowsill. After the temperature rose, she felt that her metabolism had become frighteningly intense. Almost every hour, she had to change again. Not only did her period stretch to seven full days, the bleeding was heavy. She ended up lying in bed for the entire week, completely unable to move. Any movement caused blood to flow heavily.

By the time she finally endured those seven days, her face had lost all color. Even her lips were pale. She felt weak and unsteady when walking, and her weight had dropped by five pounds.

Only after a full week of intensive nourishment did Evelyn feel like she had come back to life.

That day, Evelyn pulled back the curtain to check outside. After the snakes watching her retreated, she quickly opened the window and glanced out. The water level had dropped to the sixth floor. Cracks had appeared along the outer wall of the building across from her, and the sight made her heart sink.

There seemed to be even more snakes in the water. They coiled together, hissing nonstop. The sound was like fingernails scraping across sandpaper. Listening for more than a few seconds made her physically uncomfortable.

There were still countless snakes clinging to the walls. In the high heat, their reaction speed had become even faster. Thirty-five degrees was the most comfortable temperature for them.

Just as Evelyn was closing the window, two brown cobras lunged at her at the same time.

They struck toward her head and neck. In that split second, Evelyn slammed the window shut with all her strength. One cobra's head was caught in the window frame. As it sprayed venom, Evelyn rolled two meters to the side, barely avoiding it.

She had no time to catch her breath.

She immediately pulled out her military knife and chopped off the snake's trapped head.

Fortunately, it was only a juvenile. Otherwise, Evelyn would never have been able to win with speed and strength alone.

After cleaning up all traces, Evelyn collapsed into a chair. Thinking about the cracks in the building across the way, she grabbed her hair, a powerful sense of unease and irritation rising in her chest.

The only good news was that the water level was still dropping.

Once the floodwaters were gone, and the temperature climbed past forty degrees, the snake crisis would reach its turning point.

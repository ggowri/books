Chapter 1 Rebirth

Evelyn Foster woke up with a pounding hangover. It took a long time for her to fully come to her senses, but when she did, the realization hit her like a lightning bolt.

She had been reborn back in time, to three months before the world fell into chaos.

Her hands trembled as memories from her past life flooded in. After a certain island nation released nuclear wastewater, a torrential global rainstorm began. Power grids and water supplies collapsed. Countless people lost their homes, their lives. The relentless rain lasted for two whole months, triggering landslides, dam failures, submerged cities, seawater flooding inland, buildings crumbling...

And that was just the beginning.

When the rain finally stopped, what followed was even worse: devastating floods, toxic black acid rain, scorching heat, water contamination that mutated animals into monsters. The deadliest of all was a week-long corrosive acid rain. It melted human flesh on contact, leaving behind a sickening stench of decay.

In her past life, Evelyn barely survived until the insect plague. That was already five years into the apocalypse.

She took a deep breath, trying to calm the storm inside her. No point thinking about the past. She looked around at the familiar room and thought of her parents. Her chest tightened painfully.

Evelyn had been abandoned as a newborn. Someone tossed her into a trash can, umbilical cord still attached.

Her adoptive parents were both doctors. Her mother couldn't have children because of health issues, but when she was forty-three, she found the barely breathing Evelyn in that trash can and brought her home.

When Evelyn was seventeen, her mother died of stomach cancer.

Two years later, her father followed.

She had never once gotten the chance to care for them. That regret still crushed her every time she thought about it.

Evelyn wiped her tears and glanced at her phone. Her heart suddenly pounded hard in her chest.

It was January 1st.

She had three months.

In her last life, after her father passed away, she fell apart. She dropped out of school and barely held herself together. She hadn't even moved her things out of the dorm yet. She'd have to go back for them.

Walking into the room, her eyes reddened as she took in every familiar detail. After five years at the end of the world, her heart had long turned to ice. But her parents... they were still her softest spot.

On the vanity sat a family heirloom her mother had set aside for her. It was a jade bracelet. Evelyn slipped it onto her wrist.

Suddenly, a wave of heat and pain shot through her arm. She gasped and looked down.

The bracelet had vanished.

All that remained was a thin, red mark encircling her wrist.

And in her mind, a massive storage space had appeared.

Her heartbeat raced. She raised her arm and bit down hard. The sharp pain, the clear bite mark—it was all real.

She wasn't dreaming.

Evelyn picked up a comb and whispered, "Store." It vanished from her hand and reappeared inside the space.

She tried again, gathering more items—her watch, hot water, anything nearby. They all vanished into the space with a thought. The items stayed untouched, as if time had frozen around them.

Suddenly filled with purpose, she pulled out her parents' bankbooks and cards and began calculating everything they'd left behind.

In her last life, the disaster hit too fast. No one had time to prepare. She'd been too consumed with grief to do anything, and their house hadn't stocked nearly enough food. She'd barely clung to life.

Not this time.

Her fingers brushed the red mark on her wrist. Her eyes sharpened with determination.

This time, she would live longer, and stronger.

Her parents had spent their lives frugally. The home they left her was in an old complex built twenty years ago, already transferred to her name.

Between their savings and her own accounts, she had 1.8 million dollars.

Evelyn grabbed a pen and paper, sketching out a rough plan.

First, she needed to hire workers to renovate the apartment. Top priority was reinforcing the doors. She wanted two extra layers of protection installed, plus sealing up the balcony and all the windows. The memory of someone breaking in during her last life was burned into her mind. She couldn't risk it happening again.

She had no intention of joining any survivor base. She didn't trust anyone. This building only had twelve floors, and her unit was on the tenth. Last time, the floodwaters only reached the eighth floor. That made her apartment one of the safest places to be.

Next came supplies. She needed to prepare everything in detail—food, clothing, medicine, protective gear, weapons, household equipment... every category had to be covered.

Her hand trembled as she wrote down the list. Every cent had to be used wisely. There was no room for waste. If she made a bulk purchase all at once, it would definitely attract attention. She'd have to buy from different cities and keep a low profile.

Once her chart was complete, Evelyn pulled a pair of scissors from under the coffee table and headed into the bathroom. She stared into the mirror, forcing a smile that looked worse than crying.

After five years in the apocalypse, living every second in fear and survival mode, it felt surreal to see herself alive again. Like she'd stepped into someone else's dream.

With one cut, her long hair fell away, leaving a clean, sharp bob that stopped just above her neck.

Evelyn exhaled deeply.

She grabbed her phone, rented a warehouse online, then threw on a down jacket, picked up her keys, and left the apartment.

"Evie, you're home?"

In the hallway, her neighbors David Rivers and his wife, Fiona had just returned from grocery shopping. Everyone in the complex knew each other. David was a chef at a five-star hotel, and Fiona worked as a floor manager at a clothing factory.

Evelyn froze for a second, then gave them a polite nod.

"Evie, sorry about your loss," David said gently.

She didn't respond. After so long avoiding people, conversation didn't come easily anymore.

"Heading out? It's almost lunchtime. Come eat with us?"

"No, I've got something to take care of. I'll be back later." She turned and walked away quickly.

Behind her, she could still faintly hear Fiona muttering.

"Orphaned again, and still impossible. Always walking around with that grumpy expression."

"Enough," David muttered.

"What? Can't even talk about it now? Everyone knows she was picked up out of a trash can. Cursed her mom and dad to death, what a jinx. I heard Dr. Alex Foster and Dr. Susan Carter made over a hundred thousand a month together. That's not even counting bonuses. Imagine how much they saved. People with an education sure have it easy. It's just not fair."

"Keep your voice down. What if someone hears you?"

…

Evelyn stood at the stairwell landing, listening to every bitter word. Her lips twisted into a cold smile.

In the parking lot, her father's old commuter car was still in its usual spot. She opened the door and sat down. Hanging from the mirror was a safety charm, still swaying gently.

Her vision blurred.

"Mom. Dad. I'm going to live. Really live. So don't worry about me."

She started the engine and drove straight to the warehouse.

The owner was already waiting outside. When she said she wanted it for three months, he happily signed the contract.

With the key in hand, Evelyn left immediately. She had more urgent things to handle.

Next stop: the wholesale market.

She grabbed a quick bite of some buns then began making her way through the shops, comparing prices, checking inventory.

After visiting several vendors, she found that prices were about the same across the board. She focused on shops with steady supply and high volume.

"The wholesale price for rice is 43 cents per kilogram," one shop owner said. "But that's by the ton. How much do you need, miss?"

"Ten tons. Do you have it?"

The shopkeeper broke into a wide grin. "Of course I do. This is the biggest wholesaler in Elaris."

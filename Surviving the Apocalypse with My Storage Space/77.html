Chapter 77 Surviving Alone

After drying Sprig's fur, Evelyn finally examined her own injuries.

She had not felt it while running, but now the pain made it obvious.

Her right lower leg was fractured.

The bone had snapped, likely when that rock slammed into the back of her knee. Gritting her teeth, she found two flat pieces of wood and secured them tightly along her calf to immobilize it.

The cut on her arm, the one from the man's knife, was deeper than she had realized. Blood had clotted along the edges, but the wound gaped when she cleaned it.

The rest were scrapes and bruises. Nothing fatal.

She wiped the blood from her face and used a mirror to inspect the gash on her forehead before disinfecting it carefully.

After the earthquake, she actually heard birds.

They perched on branches, chirped a few times, then flew away.

Life in the mountains had resumed, as if nothing had happened.

Sprig shivered from the cold. Evelyn added more firewood to the flames. After an hour and a half, all her wounds were cleaned and bandaged.

She took out a boxed meal and devoured it in minutes. Then she grabbed a shovel from her storage space and began clearing the cave.

If nothing unexpected happened, she would be staying here for several days. With her leg like this, she could not travel.

After clearing out the dead grass and leaves, she set up a small tent. She had memorized the instructions long ago. Even with one bad leg and an injured arm, she worked quickly and efficiently.

First, she laid down a moisture proof mat.

She spread the tent flat and separated the inner and outer layers. She assembled the support poles and threaded them through the inner tent's sleeves, raising and securing it to the ground. Then she fitted the outer layer over it and fixed it down with stakes.

Inside, she placed a thick pad, then a sleeping bag and pillow.

The cave entrance was narrow. Evelyn took a reinforced door from her storage space and installed it to block the opening. She left the ventilation window open for airflow.

Next came a battery pack and a standing lamp.

The cave lit up instantly.

This would have to do.

A cramped little shelter for the next few days.

Since leaving Elaris, Evelyn had barely slept well. Every day had been spent clearing roads and driving. On top of that, her period had hit a few days ago, leaving her exhausted. Dark circles and heavy shadows framed her eyes.

After the earthquake, even someone made of steel would have reached their limit.

She made a small nest for Sprig beside the fire, placing a cushion inside. A plastic bowl nearby held water and sunflower seeds.

She planned to rest.

Even if the sky fell, she would not care right now.

She desperately needed a few hours of sleep.

"Sprig, don't sneak outside, okay? If you need to use the bathroom, go in that basin over there."

She pointed to a litter tray she had placed near the inner side of the reinforced door, then limped into the tent.

Thankfully, the cave was dry with no standing water or leaks.

She slid into the sleeping bag.

Less than ten seconds after closing her eyes, she was asleep.

It was not a peaceful rest.

Almost every hour, she jolted awake. Each time, she checked that the fire was still crackling and that Sprig was curled up nearby, fast asleep. Only then would she close her eyes again.

A while later, she would wake once more.

Though the mountains were quiet, she kept feeling as if the ground was still shaking. After lying still and concentrating, she realized it was only an illusion lingering in her body.

Her crossbow and military knife lay beside her pillow, within arm's reach.

When she checked the time, it was noon.

The rain had lightened. Birds were chirping more brightly outside.

"Sprig, what are you doing?"

The little squirrel was circling outside the tent, chirping anxiously because it could not find the entrance.

Evelyn pulled the zipper wider and scooped Sprig into the tent.

"You clingy little menace. You really can't stay away from me for even a minute, can you?"

She set it beside her pillow and started chatting with it out of pure boredom.

At one in the afternoon, she forced herself up. The fire had almost died out, so she quickly added more wood. Taking out a thermometer, she checked the temperature.

Seventeen degrees.

No wonder it felt so cold.

She could not stop thinking about Calvin and the others. The thought dragged her mood down again.

"Chirp…"

Sprig leapt onto her shoulder and peered out through the ventilation window with her. Evelyn stroked its head.

"You want to go outside?"

The squirrel immediately jumped down and curled up tightly in its little nest, looking like it would refuse to leave even if she kicked it out.

Evelyn smiled faintly and sat beside it.

At two in the afternoon, she crawled back into the tent for another nap.

She did not wake until six in the evening.

The rain had stopped. Only droplets clung to the leaves outside, falling one by one.

She opened the reinforced door to step out and relieve herself.

Sprig followed her almost step for step. Even for this, it refused to stay behind.

Under a tree, woman and squirrel stared at each other.

Evelyn felt helpless.

It was just a small animal, but being watched like that made it impossible to relax.

"Hey, buddy. Could you not stare at me like that?"

She picked up a pebble and tossed it aside. Sprig immediately scampered off to investigate.

Back inside the cave, Evelyn picked the squirrel up and decided it was time for a serious talk.

But first, she needed to confirm something.

"You're a male, right? If you are, nod."

Sprig turned its head away proudly and ignored her.

Evelyn felt a wave of exhaustion.

The evening wind carried a sharp chill. She lectured the squirrel for quite a while, but it clearly absorbed none of it.

She let out a bitter laugh.

Had the apocalypse driven her insane? Sitting here, rambling at a squirrel.

She wore a fall and winter tactical jacket on top. Because of the splint on her broken leg, she only had on loose athletic shorts below.

The fire popped softly.

She took out a small calendar booklet and marked an X over October 20th.

After dinner, she checked the reinforced door again and braced a long stick against it. In truth, the door was only useful for blocking wind and rain. If a black bear or wild buffalo showed up, it would take almost no effort to knock it down.

She added more wood to the fire.

She had once lived alone in Block D without fear. Yet now, alone in the wilderness, unease crept in.

There might still be other survivors in the mountains. After the earthquake, aside from those missing, several hundred had survived. Some of them might have fled uphill as well.

Staring into the firelight, Evelyn could not help but mock herself.

These hands had taken lives without hesitation.

She had not been afraid when killing.

But now, hiding alone in the mountains, she was.

Night fell. Occasionally, a bird call echoed in the distance.

Evelyn held a stick and idly poked at the glowing red embers.

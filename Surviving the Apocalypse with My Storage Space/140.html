Chapter 140 Toxic Haze 2

Evelyn looked at the bony hands stretched out in front of her, then at the numb, vicious expressions on their faces. She drew the long knife from behind her waist.

The children froze for a split second, then scattered at once.

The woman was still sprawled on the ground. Evelyn turned to leave, but the woman grabbed hold of her trouser leg.

“Please, just a little food. The children really can’t go on. Have pity on them.”

Her sunken eyes were full of desperate hunger. Evelyn glanced at the children who had backed away. They were still staring at her from not far off.

“I don’t have any food.” She tried to see the child on the woman’s back, but it was wrapped up in a filthy quilt, impossible to make out.

“Then water. Just a little water. The child’s dying of thirst.”

Evelyn pinned her down and yanked the bundle from her back. The woman went mad, thrashing wildly, but Evelyn held her to the ground without much effort.

She shook out the quilt.

Inside was a lifelike doll.

There was no child at all.

The woman let out a shrill scream. Evelyn flung the thing back at her and left at once.

After she was gone, two men came out from behind a corner and dragged the woman away.

Today Evelyn was heading for the Medical Division building. There was an underground storeroom there that ought to contain a few tools and some timber.

The others could not go out without gas masks. Adrian had another task to deal with, so Evelyn was on her own that day.

She had been to the Medical Division basement before. There had never been much left there anyway, only a few storage racks and tools. By now the base had been scavenged almost clean. Evelyn was glad she had not left blindly with the others. If they had gone before the haze rolled in, they would only have suffered on the road. At least here they still had some shelter from the wind and rain.

It took her nearly an hour to get there.

The place had been stripped bare too. Only some broken doors and windows remained, along with rusting hospital beds.

Several people had tailed her along the way. She had wasted a fair bit of time shaking them off.

The survivors still left in the base fell into two groups now: the old, the weak, and the sick, or the truly capable. Best not to run into either if it could be helped.

When she reached the basement and saw the shelves turned upside down and ransacked, she shut the door and quietly stored them away in her hidden space. In the end, she only bundled up about a hundred kilos of timber to carry back on her own shoulders.

Once the basement floor had been prised open, there was still some usable soil underneath. Over the past two days Calvin had planted a handful of maize seeds they had come by unexpectedly.

Oddly enough, although humans had been left with acid-rain aftereffects, plants seemed to grow far faster in this environment. Seeds sprouted just hours after being planted. At times Evelyn felt as though she had stumbled into some strange fantasy world.

She was halfway back when she ran into Adrian.

He took the bundle of wood from her back, and the two of them walked the rest of the way to the villa together.

“Did you get it?”

“Yes. I brought it back for them.”

Secretary Bennett, who had barely spoken for nearly half a month, had suddenly come to Evelyn earlier and told her there was something hidden beneath the floor tiles in the men’s lavatory on the ninth floor of the Administration Hall. He had asked her and Adrian to fetch it. Since Evelyn had been busy collecting timber, Adrian had gone instead.

“What was it?”

“A few crates of seeds. He saw Calvin planting maize and decided to bring them out for everyone.”

Evelyn was genuinely taken aback. “Seeds? They really are old foxes. Seems everyone keeps a way out in reserve.”

Adrian nodded.

By the time they returned to the villa, Calvin and the others were already waiting outside. Everyone helped unload the timber, and Eddie took a few planks straight over to the fire.

Once the toxic haze appeared, the temperature dropped from thirty degrees to fifteen. The sudden swings between heat and cold, on top of the poison in the air, had brought nearly everyone down. Apart from Evelyn, Adrian, and Calvin, the others had all fallen ill.

Evelyn glanced at the sacks of grain and seeds piled on the ground floor. There was an impressive variety: rice, wheat, beans, and all sorts of vegetable and fruit seeds besides.

“Secretary Bennett already divided them up,” she said. “Everyone gets one bundle, about two kilos. If we’re ever forced to split up, then with these, and a patch of clean soil, we might still survive.”

Evelyn picked up her share. Adrian handed over his bundle too. She looked at him and laughed softly, then took it and stored it away.

Elsewhere in the base, some people had begun organising themselves to dig for soil, search for seeds, and grow things inside the villas.

A fair number stayed behind, though every day plenty still chose to leave.

A week after the haze arrived, people’s bodies had started to adapt to the new conditions. They were still dizzy, still coughing, but with masks on they could at least go out again to dig for soil and search for supplies.

Fortunately, it rained once every three days with almost perfect regularity. The rain meant water, and water meant hope.

“Could I trade for some seeds? I’ve got a clean padded coat here. I only want a handful.”

More and more people started coming to the door to barter.

After riot and disaster, the people left behind still had to keep living somehow.

Calvin accepted the coat and gave the man a handful of the wheat seeds he had scavenged.

Others came with children, hoping to exchange them for food. One scrawny little girl was shoved out in front and presented almost like an item for sale, while her worth was listed aloud.

“She’s a girl at least. There are fewer people now. She can have children later.”

The girl’s father cast a glance round the room, a fawning smile plastered on his face.

“You’ve got plenty of men in here. There’s another family living next to me, and they’ve got several girls. If you’re interested, I can take you over to have a look. Just give me a little finder’s fee.”

Eddie exploded. Grabbing a stick, he charged at the man.

“Get out. What’s wrong with you? Are you really her father? You’re trying to trade your own child for food?”

The man stumbled backwards in fright, but he still did not leave. Instead, he pushed the girl farther in front of him.

“What else am I supposed to do? We can’t survive like this. There are loads of you. You’ve clearly got what it takes to live. Be decent and take the girl. Use her as a servant if you like. I’m not asking much. Just ten kilos of grain.”

Eddie pointed the stick straight at him. “Get lost. If you don’t, I’ll crack your skull.”

“If you don’t want her, there are still two young blokes behind you. Nine kilos then. Eight would do.”

Calvin could not bear to watch any longer. Holding Wendy, he turned and went inside.

Eddie looked at the painfully thin girl, then threw her half a dry biscuit. Her father snatched it first and wolfed it down on the spot.

The girl stood there sobbing quietly.

Eddie looked fit to burst with rage.

“Eddie, shut the door,” Evelyn said.

He had no choice but to obey.

The man still kept shouting outside, and every so often he struck the child to vent his anger.

And it was becoming more common.

Sometimes women came to the door offering themselves, asking only for a bowl of rice or a single flatbread.

When Evelyn went out, she ran into all sorts too.

Women.

Men.

Yes, even younger men had started using the same method to trade for food.

Inside the abandoned buildings, there were also frail old people crouched by the doorways, peering out longingly.

Some were already dead.

Others, when someone passing by tossed a stone at them to frighten them, scrambled back inside at once.

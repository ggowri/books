Chapter 65 Scorching Heat, The Refugees: Part 3

"N-No," the woman stammered, licking her cracked lips as she awkwardly pulled her clothes back together.

Evelyn noticed the whip mark slashing across the back of her rough, chapped hand. Her brow lifted slightly, but she said nothing more about it.

"Please, help us," the other woman begged, dropping to her knees in front of Evelyn.

"How?" Evelyn asked calmly.

Tears pooled in the woman's eyes. "We know you're capable people. Just give us shelter. We'll do anything."

"Do you know why so many people died last night?" Evelyn asked.

The woman's face drained of color. "You…"

Evelyn nodded. "I killed them."

She made a quick slicing gesture across her throat.

The woman shrieked and collapsed onto the ground, scrambling back in terror. She grabbed her companion's hand and the two of them stumbled away in a panicked run.

With a loud clang, Evelyn shut the security door and watched their retreating figures with a cold smile.

Anyone who had survived this long, could they really be weak?

One by one, Calvin and the others returned. Evelyn told them what had just happened.

"There's probably a group backing those two women," she said. "They're being controlled. Sent out to seduce men in exchange for shelter or food. Or worse. Lure in idiots and turn them into food."

She paused, her eyes dark.

"The way those people ate last night, that wasn't their first time."

Calvin frowned. "You think the woman's child is being held by that group? They're threatening the child to force them into this?"

Evelyn shook her head slightly. "That's one possibility. Another is that the child is already gone. I saw a lot of whip marks on both of them. They might cooperate just to avoid being beaten. When I asked if she'd recently given birth, she looked terrified."

Eddie rubbed his temples. "Feels like there aren't many sane people left in this world. Just a bunch of lunatics."

*****

The two women returned to the entrance of Block E. They hesitated for a long time before daring to go inside.

The door suddenly opened. A man stepped out, glanced around to make sure the coast was clear, then yanked them in.

"Failed?" A shirtless man sitting in a chair lazily raised his eyelids. A belt dangled from his hand, slapping rhythmically against his palm.

"Bruno, they wouldn't even look at us," Caitlyn said quickly. "They all had knives. Every one of them looked vicious."

"Caitlyn, you don't want your son anymore?"

At his words, the man who had opened the door disappeared into a room and returned dragging out a little boy, no more than seven or eight years old.

Caitlyn dropped to her knees and began banging her head against the floor. "Bruno, please don't hurt my son. I'll go back. I'll try again. I'll finish the job, I promise."

Bruno tapped the boy's head lightly with a knife. "First, tell me. How many of them are there?"

"Six. Three adult men, two teenage boys, and a child."

She had clearly mistaken Evelyn for a man.

"Only six?" Bruno narrowed his eyes. "More than ten people in Block C died from poisoning last night. That was their doing, wasn't it? Sounds like they've got some valuable supplies."

He leaned forward slightly.

"Caitlyn. Faye. I'm giving you two days. If you can't take them down, you know what that means."

Caitlyn glanced at her son's pale, frightened face. With tears in her eyes, she nodded.

Two men stepped forward and dragged the women into a side room.

Bruno patted the little boy's head and signaled for his men to take him away.

"Caitlyn," Faye whispered once they were alone, curling into a corner with fresh injuries marking her skin. "Maybe we should run."

Caitlyn stared at the ground, her voice hollow.

"We can't. My son is in their hands. I can't escape."

Faye looked up at her. "Caitlyn, don't you want revenge? Don't you hate those monsters? They killed your baby girl who was barely a month old, and…"

She could not force herself to finish the sentence.

"I don't have a choice," Caitlyn said hoarsely. "I don't have a better one. Trading her life for my son's… I failed her. I hope she doesn't hate me. I'm useless. I couldn't protect her."

Faye stared at her in disbelief. "She was your child too. Just because she was a girl, you handed her over to that animal to be eaten?"

Caitlyn's eyes turned bloodshot as she glared back. "What was I supposed to do? They were going to eat my son. He's my life. That baby had just been born. She hadn't even seen this world yet. She wouldn't feel pain."

Faye looked at her for a long time. There was nothing left in her eyes but despair.

"You're insane," she whispered. "You're just like them. Animals. Monsters. All of you are insane."

*****

More and more refugees poured into the city.

The fire in Northside had already spread to downtown. Leaving was no longer optional. It was urgent.

But how to leave had become their biggest problem.

With so many refugees crowding the streets, the moment they drove out, they would become a target. Being surrounded and attacked would be inevitable.

Anyone who had managed to survive long enough to reach Elaris had some ability. In the apocalypse, you could never underestimate anyone.

Carelessness only led to a worse death.

"I've been watching," Eddie said. He had borrowed Evelyn's binoculars over the past two days to scout the streets. "Some of them drove here. I've seen three wheeled trucks, vans, motorcycles. If we wait until their main group leaves and blend in, we won't stand out."

The information he brought back was solid.

"They're probably staying to scavenge more supplies before moving on. But the city's been picked clean. It shouldn't be long before people start leaving."

He paused, then added, "Oh, and I saw those two women downstairs again today. They're still not giving up?"

Calvin handed out freshly sharpened knives and nodded. "They're probably still trying to infiltrate us. Whoever's behind them must think we're sitting on valuable supplies."

For once, Calvin had hit the nail on the head.

"Same rule as before," he continued. "On the road, don't show kindness to anyone. Especially children, pregnant women, the elderly. The more harmless someone looks, the more dangerous they might be."

Evan hesitated. "If someone's in danger… should we help?"

Calvin reached over and ruffled his hair, then slowly shook his head.

"It's better not to. Our job is to protect ourselves. As for others, we can't save them. We're not heroes. We don't have the power to save everyone."

Even with Wendy, Calvin repeated the same warnings every day. No shouting. No crying. No calling out. Never go with strangers. He hung a whistle around her neck so that if she ever found herself in danger, she could blow it and he would rush to her immediately.

As the number of refugees grew, the wild greens and giant rats outside disappeared completely.

They locked the security door and stopped going out altogether, waiting for the refugee convoy to move on so they could slip into the line of vehicles unnoticed.

Meanwhile, Caitlyn and Faye, still unable to complete their task, were beaten every single day as punishment.

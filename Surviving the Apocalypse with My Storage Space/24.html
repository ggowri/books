Chapter 24 Rainstorm, Venom Moths: Part 20

That afternoon, Evelyn trimmed her slightly overgrown hair. She even indulged herself with a rare face mask. Then she pulled out two potatoes and placed them on the wood-burning stove to roast.

She unfolded a cot in the living room, laid a quilt over it, and crawled under. The radio was still silent, just a decoration now, unable to pick up any signal.

As the smell of roasted potatoes filled the room, a wave of nostalgia hit her. It reminded her of childhood.

After eating, drowsiness crept in, and Evelyn drifted off to sleep.

When she woke the next morning, the temperature had dropped another two degrees. A sinking heaviness filled her lower abdomen.

She knew immediately—this wasn't good.

Maybe it was the weather, but this time, her period hit her hard. Even after taking painkillers, the cramps were so bad she couldn't get out of bed. She pressed a hot water bottle to her belly, waiting for the warmth to ease the pain. She even stuck heating pads to her socks, but her body still felt cold. Sweat beaded on her forehead like she'd fallen into an ice cave.

By midday, Evelyn forced herself up and brewed a pot of herbal medicine. Drinking it brought slight relief, but not much.

Back in bed, she curled under three thick blankets. The thunder outside rumbled endlessly as her body shivered between chills and fever.

She checked her temperature.

It was 41°C. A high fever.

On top of the period cramps, she now had dizziness, nasal congestion, sore throat, coughing, and a runny nose.

It was like everything had hit her at once.

Of course it had.

After pushing herself so hard, so long, she had finally relaxed a little last night—and her body had collapsed under the weight of accumulated exhaustion.

She slapped a fever patch on her forehead, downed a handful of fever meds, and curled back up, so weak she could barely lift her arms.

Sounds echoed from the stairwell—footsteps, arguing, shouting.

Evelyn winced. Even though her apartment had three doors installed, the walls were still paper-thin. This was an old building after all. Being sick made her ears feel hypersensitive.

Her hair was soaked with sweat. Her thermal underwear clung uncomfortably to her skin. It felt like she'd just been dragged out of a river.

Half-conscious, she drifted in and out of sleep. Two hours later, she jolted awake, immediately trying to sit up.

Her stomach felt like someone had shoved a block of ice inside. Every movement brought sharp pain. She plugged in the hot water bottle again and changed into a fresh pair of thermal pants before collapsing back into bed.

In the late afternoon, there was a knock at the door.

Evelyn forced herself up, biting back the pain. She looked through the peephole and saw Jessica, fully geared up.

Evelyn grabbed a can of insecticide and cracked the door.

Jessica gasped when she saw her pale face and drooping posture. "Evie, what happened? You look awful."

"It's nothing," Evelyn said hoarsely. "Just a small issue. You two just got back?"

"Yes. We went to a lot of places. There are so many people out looking for supplies now. We also ran into some trouble on the way back. But luckily, it was a good haul."

She handed Evelyn a bag. "This is for you. Please take it. Are you sure you're okay?"

Evelyn shook her head. "I'm fine."

She accepted the bag without hesitation.

"Have the venom moths decreased?"

"They have," Jessica nodded. "Once the temperature dropped below minus ten, their numbers started thinning out."

Evelyn gave a faint nod. Her cramps were still stabbing at her. After exchanging a few more words with Jessica, she politely ended the conversation and closed the door.

But not two minutes later, another knock came.

Evelyn sighed, gritted her teeth, and made her way over again.

When she opened the door, Jessica was still standing there, holding out another item.

"Evie, this is for you too. Take care of yourself."

Jessica looked at her with a mix of concern and pity. Evelyn's eyes landed on what was in her hands—a piece of brown sugar and a pack of sanitary pads—and for a moment, she froze.

"We got lucky this time," Jessica said softly. "Found a good stash of these. Still sealed. No water damage."

She quickly shoved the items into Evelyn's hands and turned to leave without waiting for a response.

Evelyn stared after her, feeling a knot twist in her chest.

Inside the bag Jessica had brought earlier were two bottles of water, two small loaves of bread, and a 200-gram bag of salt.

Evelyn tucked them away in the kitchen, then slowly made her way back to the bedroom, still bent over from the pain. She pulled out a knit hat and put it on. Sitting on the edge of the bed, she caught her reflection in the mirror.

She looked terrible.

Her face was pale from illness, heavy dark circles hung under her eyes, and red veins laced her glassy pupils.

She forced down a few bites of a boxed fried rice meal, but quickly lost her appetite. With a grimace, she gulped down another bowl of thick, bitter herbal medicine.

After all the back and forth, the cramps started to ease a little.

That night, Evelyn climbed back into bed and stuck another fever patch to her forehead.

By the next morning, the bleeding had slowed. It no longer felt like her body was flooding every time she moved. She breathed a sigh of relief… only for her stomach to let out a loud growl. A sharp cramp followed right after.

Great. Now diarrhea.

For the hundredth time, Evelyn thanked herself for hoarding several tons of cat litter.

Yeah, the idea of scooping her own waste was disgusting. Embarrassing, too. But once you got used to it, it was just survival. Especially now—having diarrhea without cat litter? She couldn't even imagine.

After five trips to the bathroom, Evelyn was completely wiped out. Her gut was empty, but every cramp still hit like a battle.

She lit a stick of mugwort incense in the bathroom and sprayed nearly half a can of air freshener before collapsing into bed, staring blankly at the ceiling.

At noon, she managed to eat two buns and drink another bowl of medicine. She was about to go lie down again when a thought struck her.

She walked to the balcony and moved the insulation panel aside. Peering through the thick glass, she looked out.

Sure enough, barely any venom moths were clinging to the surface now.

But the rain was still acting insane—one moment pouring, the next quiet. Then thunder. Then violent wind.

People had mostly stopped going out to scavenge. Thunder and wind were far scarier than rain. No one wanted to get struck by lightning.

Corpses floated through the neighborhood, bloated beyond recognition. To Evelyn, they looked like grotesque ticking time bombs. At any second, she felt like one of them might burst open.

The thought alone made her stomach churn.

From next door, John's crying echoed again. He'd opened the door and was yelling out into the hallway.

"Your mom and grandma turned into monsters," someone from the eleventh floor shouted back. "They bit people in the neck and drank their blood. Your dad and grandpa kicked them out."

Then came the threat.

"If you keep yelling, we'll throw you out too. Let the monsters bite your neck and turn you into one of them."

Henry and David rushed out to confront them, but when they saw the neighbor holding a metal shovel, they backed off.

John slumped to the floor, shaking his head in denial. He couldn't accept that his mom and grandma had become monsters. He was terrified of becoming one too.

After Henry carried him back inside, John cried the entire night.

The next morning, he was different.

His eyes were vacant. Drool dripped from the corners of his mouth.

All he did was sit and laugh stupidly while calling out for his mother.

Evelyn wouldn't have known about any of this if Claire hadn't come upstairs to tell her.

"John… that poor kid. He got scared so bad, he went dumb," Claire said.

Was it sad? Maybe.

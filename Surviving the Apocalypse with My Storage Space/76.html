Chapter 76 Earthquake, Torrential Rain

In the stampeding crowd, Evelyn caught sight of Faye and Caitlyn.

Both women carried even more wounds than before. When Faye fell into a massive collapsing pit, she clutched desperately at a man trying to flee past her and dragged him down with her.

Caitlyn tripped and fell. She screamed her son's name, reaching out for him to pull her up. The pale faced boy turned back and looked at her.

For a brief second, relief washed over his face.

Then he exhaled and disappeared into the fleeing crowd.

The earthquake raged for twenty full minutes.

By the time Evelyn reached the small plain to the east, the shaking had finally stopped. Survivors were scattered all around her. Many dropped to their knees and sobbed uncontrollably. They cursed the black bears, cursed the earthquake, cursed the heavens, and cursed their own helplessness.

Evelyn leaned against a rock and pulled off her coat, draping it over her head. The torrential rain continued. The hail had stopped, but water pooled beneath her feet, merging into streams that flowed downhill.

She took Sprig out of the canvas bag and checked it carefully.

Thankfully, it was unharmed.

When Sprig saw that Evelyn was covered in blood, her face smeared red, it began chirping frantically. Evelyn stroked its head and quickly covered its mouth.

"I'm fine. Don't make a sound. Don't be scared."

The little squirrel stared at her with watery eyes.

Evelyn let out a bitter laugh. What kind of twisted luck did she have, wandering into the mountains and coming out with a strangely perceptive squirrel?

She was just about to open her backpack and swallow a painkiller when the aftershock hit.

Evelyn dropped into a crouch and hugged Sprig tightly against her chest. Cries rose again all around them. Some people bolted in blind panic.

She could not run anymore.

So she stayed where she was, crouched low.

A few minutes later, the shaking stopped.

Then it came again.

Over and over.

By five in the morning, the tremors finally subsided. Only the rain continued, relentless and heavy, with no sign of stopping.

The convoy of thousands had been reduced to mere hundreds.

Evelyn walked the length of the small plain, searching for Calvin, Wendy, Eddie, Ray, and Evan.

None of them were there.

A sharp ache rose in her chest.

For the first time, she wanted to cry.

What if they were all dead?

She called their names, her voice hoarse. At first softly. Then louder. Then until her throat felt like it was tearing apart.

Around her, nearly everyone bore injuries of some kind.

She pulled out her folded trekking pole, swallowed two painkillers, and decided to return to where the vehicles had been parked.

Most of the cars had been hurled off the road into the ravine below. Others were buried under landslides and falling boulders.

She put on a raincoat. Her hands would not stop shaking. She could not tell if it was from the cold or the pain.

"Help… please help me…"

A hand stuck out from beneath an overturned vehicle, waving weakly.

Evelyn hesitated for two seconds before walking toward it.

But before she reached it, the hand went limp.

The person beneath it stopped moving.

She stood there, staring.

Her eyes were bloodshot, almost crazed, locked on that lifeless hand.

The rain poured down, washing the grime and blood from her body. Her face felt warm. She raised her hand and wiped at it, but she could not tell whether what she touched was rainwater or tears.

Another small aftershock rippled through the ground.

Evelyn collapsed onto the wet earth.

Her tears vanished into the rain.

She was so tired.

Her eyelids grew heavy, impossibly heavy.

"Chirp… chirp…"

Sprig crawled out of the bag. Its once fluffy, beautiful tail was soaked flat by the rain. Now it looked more like a drenched little rat than a squirrel.

It nudged her with its head.

It slapped her lightly with its tail.

Trying to wake her.

Evelyn's eyes fluttered open through the haze.

The moment Sprig saw she was awake, it burrowed into her chest, trembling with excitement.

Evelyn stared up at the dark, heavy sky. The sharp, metallic scent of wet earth filled her lungs. Gritting her teeth, she forced herself to sit up.

Around her were collapsed slopes, overturned vehicles, and silent bodies of strangers.

It felt as if the world had been stripped bare.

As if only she and Sprig were left alive.

The road ahead had completely caved in. After pushing herself to her feet, Evelyn continued forward with Sprig tucked against her. She still wanted to search the convoy for Calvin and the others.

If she could not find them, then maybe that meant they were still alive somewhere else.

The rain had eased slightly. The air was damp and colder by several degrees. When she reached the center of the convoy, she found the buried van.

She called out each of their names but there was no answer.

She walked toward the very front.

The RV was still intact.

Its exterior and doors were smeared with blood.

Evelyn pulled the door open and stepped inside. The stench of blood hit her immediately.

Hayden was hiding under the table.

Both his legs had been bitten off.

Hearing footsteps, he opened his eyes.

They locked eyes for several seconds.

There was another woman in the vehicle, trembling violently. When she saw Evelyn, she scrambled for a gun on the floor and pulled the trigger.

Click!

No bullets.

She glanced outside.

In the pouring rain lay scattered limbs, cracked earth, bodies crushed beneath boulders, vehicles twisted together in ruin.

For a moment, she seemed dazed.

Then she suddenly lunged at Hayden.

Grabbing his hair, she smashed his head repeatedly against the side of the vehicle.

He looked at Evelyn with pleading eyes.

Within seconds, he went limp.

The woman ignored Evelyn completely. Laughing and crying at the same time, she stumbled out of the RV and ran into the rain.

Evelyn scanned the interior.

Aside from bloodstains, the equipment was intact. Several refrigerators still held bear meat.

She dragged Hayden's corpse outside and tossed it into the rain. After confirming no one else was around, she stored the RV in her spatial storage.

Unfortunately, the other off road vehicles had been shaken into the ravine.

She still had not found Calvin and the others.

There was no road forward or back.

Evelyn lifted her eyes to Shattered Ridge.

Then she turned and walked into the mountains.

Between the eighteen towering peaks were small plains and sheltered hollows. She needed a place to recover first. Once her injuries healed, she would decide her next move.

Either head north to Drakepool Base, or go farther west, southwest or northwest.

Shattered Ridge could never be a permanent home. It was too close to Elaris and Havenport, both of which had nuclear power plants.

The mountain paths were treacherous even on clear days. In the rain, they were nearly impossible. Every few steps, she slipped and fell.

She aimed for a mountain hollow that would take several hours to reach.

After an hour and a half, she finally found a small cave.

It was barely enough to shelter from the rain, but it would do.

The moment she set Sprig down, the little squirrel became restless, sticking close to her side.

"Sprig, I'm not leaving. I won't abandon you. Don't be afraid, okay?"

She poured out a handful of sunflower seeds for it, then took dry firewood from her storage space and built a small fire inside the cave.

Outside, the rain intensified again.

Evelyn had no energy left for grief.

She stripped off her filthy clothes, took out gauze and iodine, and began cleaning and bandaging her wounds.

Drip. Drip. Drip.

Rainwater struck the fallen leaves outside the cave, splashing into small bursts.

Evelyn crouched beside the fire, gently drying Sprig's soaked fur.

The cave was tiny, no more than five square meters. A vehicle would never fit inside.

All she could manage was to set up a very small, makeshift tent.

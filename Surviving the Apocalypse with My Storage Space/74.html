Chapter 74 On the Road of Escape: Part 5

"But I still have to find my parents. I can't stay here." Ray looked at Evelyn, anxiety written all over his face.

Eddie let out a short laugh when he saw how worried the boy was and clapped him on the shoulder. "Relax, I was joking. Out here in the middle of nowhere, what if a wolf shows up and eats us? Besides, who knows what tomorrow will bring. If there's suddenly an earthquake, living in the mountains means we might not even get a chance to run."

"Chirp, chirp…"

The little squirrel suddenly poked its head out of the canvas bag, its bright black eyes studying Eddie and Ray with open curiosity.

"What is that?" Ray stared at it in amazement.

"A freeloading squirrel," Evelyn said calmly. "From now on, it's sticking with me."

"You even managed to tame a squirrel? Impressive." Eddie leaned closer and tried to tap its tiny head. The squirrel instantly ducked back into the bag, peeking out warily with guarded eyes.

"This little thing's pretty smart. But Evelyn, if you take it with you, you'll have to feed it. Can you really afford that? The forest is where it belongs."

Evelyn understood that better than anyone.

"But it refuses to leave me. It's fine. As long as I have a bite to eat, it won't starve. Let's head down."

"Have you given it a name yet?"

She shook her head. "It's just Little Squirrel."

"You're taking it with you. At least give it a real name."

Evelyn thought for a second. "Then I'll call it Sprig." She glanced back at the two of them. "What do you think? Not bad, right?"

Eddie nodded. "It's decent. Sounds almost like a person's name."

Evelyn tapped the squirrel lightly on the head. "You've got a name now. From today on, you're Sprig. When I call you, you better respond. Got it?"

Sprig opened its mouth and tried to nibble her finger again. Evelyn flicked its ear gently.

"Behave. Once we're back down the mountain, no making noise. Or some bad guy might grab you and skin you." Whether it understood or not, she decided a little warning would not hurt.

She secured the water container inside her backpack and led the two men down the mountain.

When they returned to the convoy, Calvin and Evan hurried over.

"Something happened just now," Calvin said in a low voice.

"What happened?"

"A group ran into a black bear up in the mountains and started calling for help. After that, a lot more people went up. They're thinking of hunting it together and splitting the meat."

Evelyn barely reacted. She had already seen wild buffalo. A black bear was not surprising.

"Don't worry about them. Calvin, I'll take you up. I remember the way. Evelyn's already gone twice. Let her rest," Eddie volunteered to lead this time.

Evelyn shot him a skeptical look. "You remember the path?"

"I do. Trust me. My memory's pretty good."

She did not insist. She was genuinely tired. After hiding the water container back in her luggage and locking the car doors tightly, she stayed behind.

Eddie left with Calvin and Evan. Evelyn sat by the fire with Wendy and Ray to rest.

All around them, fires burned. People were heating food and warming themselves. Most of the able bodied adults had gone up the mountain to hunt. The ones left behind were elderly people, women, and children.

"Evie, I smell blood," Ray whispered as he leaned closer.

At that moment, a commotion broke out ahead. Someone was being carried down the mountain.

Evelyn grabbed Wendy's hand, rushed to the car, and slid into the driver's seat. After Ray climbed in, she deliberately moved the vehicle slightly to the side, lowering its visibility as much as possible.

"Evie…" Ray stared at her, nerves tight in his voice.

"Shh," she murmured quietly. "Someone's dead."

The men who were carried back were gravely injured. Two were already dead. The other two screamed in agony while their families frantically searched for a doctor. The physicians who had treated Hayden earlier were dragged over to help. One doctor muttered, "There's no saving him," and was immediately punched in the face.

Evelyn watched it all without expression. Sprig wriggled out of her arms, trying to see what was happening, but she pressed it back down.

"His leg's been bitten clean off. How am I supposed to save him?" the doctor shouted, furious.

None of the four men brought back were whole. Even the ones who had carried them down wore looks of pure terror.

"There were two huge black bears. A lot of people got hurt. We should leave. The bears might come down the mountain."

When the others heard there were really two black bears, fear flashed across their faces. But instead of fleeing, many grew excited.

"With this many people, how could we not kill two bears? I'm going up there. Even a pound of meat would be worth it. I haven't had meat in half a year."

"Don't go. Those bears are massive and vicious. Everyone ran."

Inside a nearby RV, Hayden had an arm around a scantily dressed woman. A topographic map of Shattered Ridge lay spread across the table. His fingers pinched her waist, drawing a coquettish laugh. Their giggling drifted out through the vehicle.

A bodyguard knocked and entered, handing him a camera.

"Mr. Hayden, two adult black bears have appeared in the mountains. These were just taken."

He waved irritably. The woman stood and slipped out.

"Most of the people who went up to hunt the bears are injured. Four are confirmed dead," the bodyguard continued.

"Only four?" Hayden scoffed. "Didn't hundreds go up?"

"Many ran when they saw the bears. Several hundred still haven't come down. If there are black bears in the mountains, there must be other animals too. If we hunt some and bring them back to the base, Mr. Wilson will be pleased."

A cold smile curled on his lips. "The old man's not young anymore. He can't even chew meat like that. It'll probably end up in someone else's stomach. Then what would I be working for?"

"Sir, there's no need to worry about that. Establishing yourself at the base is what matters. If too many of these people die, we'll have fewer hands. Even commoners are useful. One more person means another pair of hands for labor."

Hayden lit a cigarette and took a slow drag. After a moment, he nodded. "You're right. Bring some men. And weapons. We'll deal with the bears."

"Yes, sir."

*****

The four dead were quickly buried. The rest of the injured fled back down soon after.

Then several roars echoed from the mountains. The sound sent many scrambling into their vehicles to hide.

Evelyn checked the time. One hour had passed, and Eddie and the others still had not returned.

It was one in the morning. The night wind cut through the camp. Evelyn put on an extra coat. Wendy kept staring out the window. Evelyn knew she was worried about Calvin, so she gently rubbed the girl's hair.

Hayden's men came around to reassure everyone. They claimed they would head up the mountain to rescue the remaining people and handle the black bears.

The frightened crowd instantly found someone to rely on. Gratitude poured out as they praised Hayden's generosity.

"Evie, is that Hayden a good person or a bad person?" Ray asked softly.

Evelyn tugged at the corner of her mouth. "What do you think?"

"I don't know."

With so many people in the convoy, who could truly be called a good person?

Even Evelyn did not consider herself one.

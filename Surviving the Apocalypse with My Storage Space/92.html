Chapter 92 The Dog Training Manual

Ever since 0018 moved into the cabin, Evelyn had been living in constant chaos.

At first, she even dreamed about ways to kill him. After realizing he simply would not die, no matter what she tried, she had no choice but to accept reality.

Still, she had never been known for her patience.

At dinner, over a single pheasant drumstick, they fought again.

This time, 0018 started it.

He did not just stare at the meat in her bowl. He reached out and tried to grab it.

Evelyn slammed him to the ground and punched him twice while gripping his hair.

He swallowed the drumstick in two bites, lifted her clean off the floor, and threw her into the snow outside.

"I have to kill him. I swear I have to kill him."

She was so furious she could barely think. She used to be calm and composed. Now she had been reduced to this.

That night, she mixed up a bottle of chili spray and crept toward him while he was asleep.

She sprayed it straight into his face.

The next second, she was back in the snow.

The following morning, she refused to cook.

She lay in bed all day.

By afternoon, hunger drove him into a frenzy. He went out and came back dragging two wild boars.

Evelyn stared at the sharp tusks on their snouts and sighed regretfully.

Why had they not skewered him?

"They're yours."

Was that… surrender?

She said nothing, just watched him.

He pressed his lips together and left again.

Two hours later, he returned with two pheasants in one hand and a roe deer slung over his shoulder.

Evelyn stared.

"That's enough. You're going to wipe out the entire mountain."

"All yours."

"So. Do you know what you did wrong?"

He shook his head.

Evelyn sighed.

Dogs did not understand constant scolding. You had to reason with them.

"You are not allowed to steal food from my bowl. Remember that."

"Mm."

He nodded this time.

That was progress.

She felt oddly satisfied.

"Clean them up. We're having barbecue tonight."

His eyes lit up instantly. He swallowed hard.

Evelyn went back inside to nap, fully intending to come out only after he finished processing everything.

She felt no guilt exploiting him.

To her, 0018 was a dangerous freeloader. A walking threat who ate her food and lived under her roof.

Which meant she was now the creditor.

At first, he really had been like an idiot.

Now, though, something was changing.

He could string together complete sentences. His reactions were sharp. His memory might even be better than hers.

A quiet suspicion formed in her mind.

0018 was not some cave zombie.

He was likely a genetically edited human.

Only someone who had spent most of his life inside a lab, rarely seeing sunlight, would have skin that unnaturally pale.

Everything about him pointed in that direction.

He was violent, but trainable.

Another thought surfaced.

In her previous life, the heterochromia bearer captured by the base…

Could it have been him?

Maybe someone had caught him, trained him, and turned him into something manageable.

For easier access to his blood and flesh.

She glanced at 0018, who was standing nearby swallowing repeatedly at the smell of meat.

Her emotions tangled unexpectedly.

He might be terrifying.

But he was also… pitiful.

"Hungry," he urged again.

She shot him a glare.

He bared his teeth right back at her.

They almost started fighting again.

"If you keep flashing your teeth at me, I'll knock them out."

"I'll bite you to death." He snorted and bared his teeth again.

Evelyn calmly pulled a red brick out of her space and hurled it at him.

He sprang clean out of the yard.

"Go on. Bite me. Let's see what's harder, my brick or your teeth."

He still looked unconvinced.

So she deliberately picked up a skewer of grilled meat and took a slow bite.

He panicked.

"I want meat."

"Who was wrong?"

"0018."

"Who is 0018?"

"…Me."

"And?"

"I was wrong."

She crooked a finger at him like she was summoning a misbehaving wolf.

"Self awareness is important."

The snow stopped for a few days. After a light rain, another blizzard rolled in.

0018 went out hunting again. When he came back, he was carrying a fish in one hand and a pheasant in the other.

Evelyn stared in surprise.

"There are still fish? Where did you find that?"

"Back there."

She looked at his punchable face and tossed him a piece of candy.

"The pond behind the woods?"

He nodded.

"Only one fish?"

He shook his head. "Many."

"Then why did you only bring one?" Before, he used to haul back piles of animals.

He looked at her like she was an idiot.

"I only have two hands."

She picked up the brick again. He shut up instantly.

"I'll take you."

"Tomorrow," she said lazily. "Go clean the fish. I taught you already. Remove the scales. Take out the guts. I'm going back to bed."

He accepted the order like an imperial decree and went to work immediately.

Evelyn hugged Sprig and dozed off again.

Half an hour later, he knocked on the door. She woke up.

"How do you want it?"

"Braised."

Of course. He only knew one cooking method.

She had tried teaching him to cook. He nearly set the kitchen on fire.

To keep feeding this oversized menace, she dragged herself up in the freezing wind to cook.

For the hundredth time that day, she considered killing him.

Maybe tomorrow she would push him into the pond and drown him.

*****

Beating roe deer with sticks and scooping fish from ice holes, she finally experienced the fun of winter ice fishing.

The pond was far from the cabin, hidden deep in a mountain hollow, surrounded by towering trees and thorny brush.

0018 ran ahead at full speed on the way up. She could barely keep up.

"This is the hole you broke yesterday? How did you even do it?"

He walked over, lifted a huge rock, and smashed it down on the ice.

Crack!

Then he jabbed at it with a stick until chunks fell through, leaving a rough opening.

Simple. Brutal. Effective.

"I catch. You collect."

He picked up a wooden spear to stab at the fish.

She quickly stopped him and handed him a fishing net instead.

"Use this."

She demonstrated once and scooped up a fish effortlessly.

Then she passed the net to him and shoved her hands into her pockets, standing off to the side.

Should she just kick him into the water?

Probably would not even kill him.

"You're thinking about killing me, aren't you?"

She stared at him.

How did he know? Could he read minds?

"No."

"I felt killing intent," he said seriously.

She laughed dryly. "Your life is just too stubborn. I can't beat you."

"But you stabbed me many times."

He looked at her quietly.

"Ten times," she corrected.

"You broke my arm. Threw me into the snow. What, want to fight again?"

He snorted. "You can't beat me, so you use knives."

Oh, the nerve!

She wanted to slap him so hard his face twisted.

"You deserved it."

"You fed me bitter things."

That was poison, idiot.

"Was it tasty?"

"I threw up."

She went silent for a second.

"Just work. Watch out for my brick."

"I want braised pork tonight. And barbecue. And fish."

"Go to hell!"

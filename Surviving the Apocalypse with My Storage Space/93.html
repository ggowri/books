Chapter 93 A Showdown Between Two Lunatics

Once upon a time, Evelyn had been a very polite, well mannered girl.

Life had sanded that away.

Especially after meeting 0018. Every day, her anger broke the previous day's record.

At its core, what truly irritated her was that she could not beat him.

After scooping up nearly thirty fish, she finally called it.

"That's enough. Let's go home."

He grabbed two full buckets and sprinted ahead effortlessly. Evelyn trudged behind with the net slung over her shoulder.

"You're slow."

She let out a cold laugh. "Oh? And?"

"I can carry you."

"What are you angling for this time? Pork trotters? Braised beef?"

He thought about it seriously. "Both."

"Get lost."

She took a deep breath and suddenly broke into a run.

He blinked, then immediately chased after her.

"I can carry you."

"No."

"You're slow."

"Shut up."

"You're wearing too much. You're as clumsy as the black bear I saw before."

Evelyn whipped out a brick and flung it straight at the back of his head.

He had gotten a lot more talkative lately.

And a lot more annoying.

Today's assassination attempt had failed.

She was not pleased.

*****

With this many fish, they would be eating seafood for weeks.

She decided to keep ten fresh and salt the rest.

As usual, 0018 handled the killing and cleaning. She cooked.

Sprig and Fallon were no longer afraid of him. They even circled around him curiously while he worked.

To deliberately provoke him, Evelyn always made uneven portions.

Three pork trotters. He got one. She got two.

He was visibly unhappy.

The moment he showed the slightest sign of irritation, she would grab the brick.

She had not landed a hit yet.

But she was patient.

One day, she would crack that still annoyingly intact skull.

"I want half."

She took a bite and looked at him provocatively. "I cooked. I get more."

"I hunted. I get more."

She shook her head. "No. This is my house. I make the rules."

Training step one.

Teach obedience.

He started to bare his teeth again.

Her hand slid toward the brick.

Today, she would teach him why flowers were red.

But then—

He stopped.

"Fine."

She blinked.

Was it really this easy?

She celebrated too soon.

The next second, he grabbed her, tossed her onto the roof, and pulled the ladder away.

She was about to curse him out when something in the distance caught her eye.

Several pairs of green lights flickered in the dark.

She quickly put on her night vision goggles.

It was a pack of wolves.

Her body reacted instantly. She crouched low on the roof, hiding herself.

0018 had seen them too.

And instead of caution, he looked excited like a rabid stray dog.

Without hesitation, he charged straight toward the wolf pack.

This was the first time Evelyn truly witnessed his brutality.

He fought more than a dozen wolves barehanded.

That red eye of his looked like it might bleed.

She did not know how long the fight lasted. Time seemed to stretch and blur.

When he returned, his clothes were soaked. If she wrung them out, blood would drip onto the snow.

He leapt onto the roof and looked at her calmly.

"Hungry."

Evelyn stared at him.

Unbelievable.

"I'll cook."

He grabbed her by the back of her collar and lifted her down from the roof like she weighed nothing.

This time, she lost.

And she learned something.

When faced with absolute strength, you either break or you bend.

She bent.

In the kitchen, she glanced through the window at him outside.

He had dragged the wolf carcasses back and was skinning them with clean, efficient movements. Against the endless white snow, his cold, sharp features looked especially ruthless.

Strangely, someone like him only ate cooked food.

For a fleeting second, a strange thought crossed her mind.

What did his blood taste like?

Sweet?

Bitter?

Metallic?

"You were watching me."

He appeared at the window suddenly, locking eyes with her through the glass.

The barrier softened the hostility between them.

"They'll come back for revenge," she said quietly.

"Then I'll kill them all."

He turned away as if it were the simplest thing in the world and went back to stripping wolf hides.

Her hand trembled slightly.

Looking at the pot of meat, she did not add poison this time.

"Put the meat away," he said after a while, then walked off.

When spring came, she would leave this lunatic here to starve.

*****

Wolf meat was surprisingly edible.

She kept some and stored the rest in her space.

Consider it rent.

Over the next few days, their relationship shifted.

From constant clashes and near murder attempts to cold silence.

They stopped provoking each other and stopped talking unless necessary.

Evelyn found she preferred it this way.

She stayed in her bedroom reading and watching shows. 0018 lingered in the small living area, doing who knew what.

By mid January, her period arrived.

Even with painkillers, she felt miserable.

Cooking was out of the question.

She stepped outside her room and saw 0018 flipping through one of her books in the living room.

"For the next few days, make your own food. Don't bother me."

"You're injured."

He appeared in front of her instantly. It wasn't concern but curiosity.

He leaned closer and sniffed.

She nearly died of embarrassment.

"I'm not injured. There's meat in the kitchen."

She tried to shut her bedroom door.

He blocked it.

"You're injured," he insisted, and kept sniffing.

"Say one more word and I'll hit you with a brick."

The words slipped out automatically.

They both paused.

She had not said that in days.

The fragile truce shattered.

Without warning, he turned and walked into the kitchen.

Curious despite herself, she followed.

He took out a bowl.

Then, without hesitation, he sliced open his own wrist.

Blood streamed into the bowl.

Half full.

The wound closed before her eyes.

He carried the bowl to her.

"Here."

She stared at it.

"Drink it."

Evelyn was stunned.

But her first reaction was not refusal.

She swallowed.

His blood smelled strangely sweet. Almost addictive. Like something deliberately engineered to lure her in.

No wonder he had been captured and killed in her previous life. With that kind of intelligence, he would not survive a week in human society.

Exposing your weakness like that, especially in the apocalypse, was suicide.

"I'm not drinking it."

"Drink it. You'll get better. Then you can cook."

The tiny flicker of emotion she had felt vanished instantly.

"I said no."

"Drink."

If she drank it, would the pain really disappear?

She had no patience left for this.

She turned to go back into her room—

But in a blur of motion, he pinned her against the wall, grabbed her jaw, and forced the bowl to her lips.

Half the blood poured into her mouth.

She choked violently, gagging.

Some of it went down.

The sweetness had been an illusion.

It was metallic. Thick. Bitter. Reeking of iron.

She shoved him away and bolted into the bathroom, retching until her stomach cramped.

What kind of karma had led her to this?

She rinsed her mouth again and again, gulping water, but the memory of that red liquid sliding down her throat made her nauseous.

"Are you better?"

He stood a few steps away, expression blank, tone steady.

She let out a strangled sound of rage and stormed toward him, shoving him hard.

"You lunatic. I'm going to kill you."

She knocked him down and grabbed his collar, shaking him.

Then—

A sharp cramp twisted through her abdomen.

Her face went pale.

This was the blood of a genetically edited human.

Had she just swallowed a biological weapon?

The urge to kill him burned even hotter.

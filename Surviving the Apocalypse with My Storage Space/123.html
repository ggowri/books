Chapter 123 The Wonder Drug

On the eighth day of being trapped in the Medical Division, Evelyn was resting in the utility room when she unexpectedly saw Adrian.

The moment he pushed open the glass window, she drew her knife and slashed at him. If Adrian had not reacted quickly enough, he might well have lost a hand.

“So it’s you. How are you back already?”

Adrian jumped into the storage room, shut the window behind him, and sat down on the floor as if he had every right to be there.

“Now that I’ve seen you’re still alive, I can stop worrying.”

Evelyn gave him a crooked smile. “How kind. Miserable as it is in here, I’m annoyingly hard to kill.”

“Pack your things. I’m getting you out.”

She looked him over from head to toe. “Did you just get back? You still smell of blood. How did you know I was here?”

Adrian lifted his eyes to hers. “Where else would you be?”

Evelyn remembered what he had once told her: that she carried a scent he could pick out and follow. So that was how he had found her. He had tracked her here by smell.

“Adrian, so many people have died at the base these past few days. Even with a terminal illness, you’d expect some time to treat it, some hope at least. But we’ve found nothing. Nothing at all. We can only stand there and watch them die.”

“Natural disasters can’t be fought,” he said quietly. “Don’t torture yourself over it.”

“What’s happening outside?”

“They’re checking every building. Anyone who feels unwell is being sent here by force. The base has started purifying the water.”

So they were not doing nothing after all. That eased her mind more than she wanted to admit.

Without warning, Adrian drew his dagger and sliced open his wrist. As she watched the blood well up, Evelyn shamefully swallowed.

“Go on,” he said. “Drink.”

“I’m starting to feel like a vampire.”

“That’s all right. I’ve got plenty of blood.”

She glanced at his eyes. Fortunately, his contact lenses were still firmly in place.

“You really must have died of stupidity in your last life.”

If anyone discovered his blood had healing properties, he might end up dragged off and turned into a test subject or a living cure.

Evelyn bent her head and drank from his wrist until the wound began to close. Only then did she pull back.

A smear of blood lingered at the corner of her mouth, which made her look even more like a vampire. Adrian reached up and wiped it away with rough fingers, then leaned closer as if checking her scent.

Evelyn felt a stab of embarrassment. She had not washed properly in days.

“Aren’t you leaving?” she asked.

“I’m taking you with me.”

They were on the fourth floor. With his skills, getting her down would have been easy enough.

Instead, Evelyn took out ten boxes of the wonder drug. Eddie had once helped her find them when they were scavenging supplies from Larkshore Pharmaceuticals. She had no idea whether the drug would work, and had wrestled with the decision for days before finally choosing to bring it out.

Too many people had died already.

The bodies were piling up into hills.

These people were her own kind, her own flesh and blood in the broadest sense. She could not simply watch without caring.

“Take these eight boxes and put them in the base’s water reservoir,” she said. “I’ll keep these two. I’ll think of some way to get the people here to take it.”

She did not know whether it would work, but Julian’s words had reminded her of it. This was supposed to be a wonder drug. Perhaps it really could kill off the mutated worms. She still had half the stock left in her space, kept aside as a final safeguard for herself.

“All right.”

Adrian took the box without asking questions.

“If it doesn’t work, we leave the base. If it does, then once this is over, I’m leaving the Medical Division too.”

Whenever Evelyn closed her eyes, she saw the dead. She saw the slick pools of blood on the floor.

No matter how much she could endure, she could not stay here forever.

After Adrian left, she caught a little sleep in the utility room.

The room where the herbal medicine was brewed was on the fifth floor. By the time Evelyn got there, the staff member tending the pots had dozed off in the corner. She took out five tablets and dropped them into the huge cauldron of simmering medicine.

That evening the herbal brew was distributed to the patients. Half an hour later, every one of them had diarrhoea and began vomiting. By the next morning, though, their spirits had improved, and colour had returned to their faces.

“Have they somehow recovered on their own?” Dr. Hargreaves said, astonished.

Everyone examined them again and again, but no one could work out exactly why they were improving.

When Evelyn saw that the wonder drug was effective, she let out a quiet breath of relief.

Then Julian, up on the top floor, suddenly fell ill.

Just like the earlier patients, he began convulsing, vomiting blood, and smashing himself against the wall.

Summoning all his courage, Dr. Hargreaves had the medicine forced into him. Julian vomited for a while, then his complexion improved as well.

Earlier, when he had claimed there were worms inside him, everyone had assumed he was lying simply to torment them. It turned out he had been telling the truth.

“His incubation period was much longer,” someone said, horrified. “And the worms he brought up were as big as earthworms. Ghastly.”

The Medical Division had finally brought the parasite outbreak under control. The guards surrounding the building were withdrawn. Once the water had been purified, the food sent in improved too. There were vegetables now, and stir-fried meat.

People were still being brought in, but every patient who arrived was successfully treated.

The despair hanging over the base finally began, little by little, to lift.

What Evelyn had not expected was for Iris to be sent back again.

This time Sophie came with her. It was obvious Sophie already knew Evelyn was here, because she hurried over, eager to greet her, only to be met with cold indifference.

Iris had only just been brought in when she started vomiting. In the short few days since Evelyn had last seen her, she had grown even thinner, little more than skin stretched over bone.

Then Evelyn saw her bring up a lump of rotten flesh.

It was crawling with worms.

The people nearby recoiled in terror, and even Evelyn felt a jolt of shock.

“How did this happen?”

“What did she eat? What did she drink?”

Sophie was so frightened by Evelyn’s icy stare that she could barely speak. Evelyn barked at her again.

“Did she drink untreated water again?”

“No,” Sophie said at last. “After she went back, she ate two tins of food. She didn’t drink anything.”

Evelyn’s face changed at once. A thought struck her with brutal force.

“Get the medicine into her.”

The dose contained the wonder drug, but it did nothing for Iris. She vomited up more shredded flesh, then died with her eyes wide open.

Evelyn turned and ran for the door, but Sophie clutched at her desperately. Evelyn kicked her off, ignored the administrators and security staff moving towards her, and charged straight out of the Medical Division building.

More than ten days.

She had been trapped in there for more than ten days.

All the way back she passed countless people. She was still wearing protective gear streaked with blood, and at the sight of her everyone gave her a wide berth. Evelyn ran as fast as she could towards the apartment block, with the building manager shouting abuse after her.

“Adrian. Adrian...”

She pounded on the door of 602.

A moment later Elliot Hayes from next door came out first. The moment he saw her, he was so overcome he could barely speak.

“Evelyn. Where’s Clara? Has she come back too?”

“No. She’s still in the Medical Division.”

The disappointment on Elliot’s face was immediate, but Evelyn kept knocking.

“Adrian.”

“I saw him go out about an hour ago.”

Evelyn’s legs nearly gave way beneath her.

What now?

Adrian was not here.

Wendy had eaten the tinned food too.

The tins had parasites in them as well.

And the wonder drug had just failed.

What was she supposed to do about Wendy?

How could she save her?

Then Evelyn thought of herself.

She had eaten the tinned food too, yet nothing had happened to her.

Of course.

Her blood.

She had drunk Adrian’s blood.

Could it be that her own blood had gained healing properties as well?

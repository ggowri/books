Chapter 125 There Is Always a Way Through

“Can I take the contact lenses out now? I can hardly see properly with them in, and they’re irritating my eyes.”

Evelyn had already guessed part of it. Adrian clearly believed he had killed whoever knew his secret, perhaps one person, perhaps several, and that he was safe now.

“No.”

Adrian frowned slightly, plainly displeased. Even so, he had already peeled one lens out and was blinking in discomfort.

Evelyn looked at his mismatched eyes, her feelings rather complicated.

“You really do lack basic sense, don’t you? There are too many people here, and far too many watching eyes. Even if no one knows what you can do, they’d still decide you were some kind of monster. In times like these, when people are crushed by life, they need something to vent their fear on, and you’d be first in line. Do you know what happens to monsters? I’ll tell you. They’re beaten to death, or tied up and burned, or drowned. Right now most people are hanging on by a thread. If someone stood up and said killing you would bring good weather and peace back, do you really think you’d survive? However strong you are, could you fight off ten thousand people? Could you withstand bombs and guns?”

He really had spent too long shut up in a laboratory. Ruthless, yes, but still spectacularly lacking in common sense.

Without a word, Adrian put the contact lens back in. Evelyn gave a small snort.

“I think you may need them too.”

Evelyn took the mirror and glanced into it. Her own irises had indeed gone much lighter. Still, light brown was hardly an unnatural eye colour, so she was not overly worried.

“I don’t.”

Fair enough. Whatever she said went. Adrian wisely kept quiet.

“You can go back to 602 now. I’m going to sleep.”

He stared at her in disbelief. After a moment, he managed, “I haven’t eaten.”

Evelyn said nothing.

By the time she had fed His Lordship and sent him on his way, she was too exhausted to move. She collapsed onto the bed and was asleep in less than five seconds.

“Inspection. Inspection. Open up and come out for inspection.”

In the dead of night, a whistle suddenly shrilled outside. Evelyn jerked awake and scrambled up.

“Inspection. Everyone out. Open your doors.”

The patrol unit had electric batons and knives in hand. Evelyn noticed they were no longer carrying guns. The base had probably taken them back.

Someone knocked on the door. Evelyn guessed it would be Adrian and hurried to open it.

“Hide Pip and Lark.”

The moment he finished speaking, another shrill whistle cut through the building, and doors all along the corridor began opening.

Evelyn put Pip and Lark into a bag, soothed them quickly, then tucked them into the bedding.

By then the patrol had reached the sixth floor. Evelyn and Adrian both stepped outside, and the patrol came straight over.

“Open the rooms. Inspection.”

The doors of 601 and 602 were opened. The patrol went inside, took a quick look round, found nothing suspicious, and came back out.

“Now blood testing.”

He produced two test strips and told Evelyn and Adrian to drip blood onto them. If the blood spread, they would be taken away.

Something had been smeared onto the strips. Evelyn caught a strong herbal smell from them. They were obviously being used to test for parasite infection.

Ingenious, really. Though she had no idea how accurate the method was.

The patrolman handed her a knife, but Evelyn ignored it and took a small dagger from her own pocket instead. She made a shallow cut on her finger and let a drop of blood fall onto the strip. The blood stayed in place. It did not spread.

Adrian did the same, and his blood did not spread either. The patrolman opened a notebook and ticked off their room numbers.

“You can go back inside.”

Then he moved on to 603. Evelyn and Adrian exchanged a glance before returning to their own rooms.

The next morning, they went to the West Wing together. Evelyn had been worrying about Wendy and had rushed over as soon as she had eaten breakfast.

When they arrived, Eddie, Ray and Evan were already in Calvin’s room.

“Evelyn, pick me up.”

The moment Wendy saw her, she struggled to sit up. Evelyn crossed over and gathered her into her arms before checking her over again. Wendy still looked a little weak, but her colour was much better.

“Have you been sick today?”

Calvin nodded. “Twice this morning. But there wasn’t any blood, and no worms either.”

At last, Evelyn felt properly relieved.

“Looks like the worms in her body have been cleared out. By the way, did they test your blood?”

Calvin still looked shaken at the memory. “They did. It was past four in the morning when a whole group came round. They went room by room and tested everyone.”

“Evelyn, why were there worms inside me?” Wendy asked.

Evelyn smoothed her hair back. “Because you ate the tins, and there were worms inside them. Once you swallowed them, they got into your body.”

Wendy did not seem frightened. She simply nodded with solemn concentration.

“So I killed them all now, didn’t I?”

“Yes,” Evelyn said with a smile. “You did brilliantly.”

Wendy gave a shy little grin and flung herself into Evelyn’s arms.

“A family of four lived next door to me,” Calvin said quietly. “Both children died. The husband and wife have both gone half-mad. They were screaming and crying in the corridor and the patrol took them away.”

“On our floor,” Evan said, “only me and Ray are left. Everyone else is dead.”

“Someone on this floor went too,” Calvin added. “A very young lad. He worked with the fishing crew.”

After that, no one said a word for a while.

“I saw someone being carried away just now,” Eddie said at last, taking a tin from his pocket. “Covered in blood. I overheard a bit of talk. Something’s happened at the pig farm. Several pigs went mad and bit a man to death.”

He held up the tin and looked round at them all.

“There were worms in the water. The worms got into the pigs. The pigs went mad and bit someone to death. Then the pigs were turned into meat and eaten. The worms got into people. Then people started vomiting blood, losing their minds, and dying.”

He fell silent for a moment before asking softly, “Where have all the dead gone?”

“Dr. Hargreaves told me the base has a cremation furnace,” Evelyn said. “Once the bodies are cremated, the ashes are taken to the Burial Grounds. It’s basically one huge pit, and all the ashes are tipped in.”

Eddie shook his head. He did not look convinced.

“Or perhaps there isn’t any cremation furnace at all. Perhaps there’s only a pit. The dead become fertiliser, and from the soil it all gets back into the groundwater.”

Evelyn pressed a hand to her temple. People had been dying every day. Hundreds every day. One furnace would never have been enough. So where had all the bodies gone? The answer was obvious enough.

“This isn’t a base,” she said quietly. “It’s hell.”

Eddie glanced at the door and lowered his voice.

“The base is sealed now. Locked down. The people inside can’t get out, and no one outside can get in. Last night I heard a helicopter overhead. Someone couldn’t keep control of the situation and slipped away.”

“So what do we do?” Ray asked softly from where he stood in the corner.

Since coming to the base, neither he nor Evan had found their parents or any other family members. However much they hated to admit it, they both knew the truth. They were probably never going to find them.

“Whatever happens, we stay alive,” Calvin said, holding Wendy close and looking at everyone in turn with grim seriousness. “There’s always a way through. We stay alive.”

A way through.

Evelyn lowered her head and said nothing.

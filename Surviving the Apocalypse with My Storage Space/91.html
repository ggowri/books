Chapter 91 Clinging On, 0018

He grabbed the braised pork with filthy hands and shoved it straight into his mouth. Evelyn half expected him to swallow strands of his own hair along with it.

His clothes were so dirty their original color was impossible to tell. Thin fabric. No shoes. Bare feet on snow.

And yet, he did not seem cold at all.

Evelyn dragged out the porcelain she had taken from the tomb and set the boxes on the ground.

"I'm giving these back. Can you leave now?"

He tilted his head at the porcelain, glanced at it once, finished the last piece of meat, and looked up at her.

"Hungry."

Evelyn narrowed her eyes.

"Fine. I can give you food. But you answer some questions first." She studied him carefully. "Are you some kind of zombie that came out of that cave?"

He shook his head.

She blinked.

If not that, then what was he?

"Why did you come to find me? Are you trying to kill me?"

"Hungry." His tone carried a trace of impatience.

She snorted.

"Are you human?"

He shook his head again.

She immediately raised the blowtorch toward him.

"Then what are you?"

"0018."

She froze.

"What did you just say?"

"0018."

Was that his name? Or some kind of code?

Silence stretched between them.

Her mind raced through possibilities, comparing each theory against the thing standing in front of her.

Not a robot. No android could move like that. And robots did not bleed. She had seen the wounds herself.

"What's your name? Your name."

"0018."

He looked calmer now, almost docile.

She did not relax.

"Hungry."

He picked up one of her crossbow bolts and casually bent it in half.

Just like that.

Evelyn inhaled slowly and forced a tight smile.

"I'll cook."

She turned toward the cabin.

He followed immediately.

"I said I'll cook. You stay out here."

His eyes darkened.

Before she could react, he lifted her off the ground with one hand.

"What the hell is wrong with you?"

She pulled out a blade and slashed at him.

They clashed again.

Five minutes later, the yard was a mess. He had two fresh stab wounds. Her arm was dislocated again.

On the surface, she surrendered.

What choice did she have?

She went into the kitchen to cook. He stood in the doorway, blocking it, watching her without blinking.

And suddenly it hit her.

She had pulled weapons from her space multiple times in front of him.

Her biggest secret was already exposed.

For a split second, she almost grabbed the cleaver and tried to take his head off.

Instead, she steamed a whole pheasant.

Watching the way he swallowed, eyes fixed on the pot, sent a chill down her spine.

Forty minutes later, she handed him the plate.

He tore into it with his hands, eating without the slightest trace of restraint.

When he finished, he did not move or leave.

Irritation flared inside her.

She could not possibly let a creature like this stay here.

"You can go now."

He ignored her.

His cold and predatory gaze shifted toward Sprig and Fallon.

She met his stare evenly.

There had been poison in the pheasant.

A strong dose.

He showed no reaction at all.

Frustration gnawed at her.

What would it take to kill him?

Cut off his head?

If she cut his head off… would it just grow back?

That night, Evelyn did not sleep.

She and 0018 sat across from each other, staring until dawn.

Two days later, after failing to shake him off or kill him, she had gone from panic to despair to numb acceptance.

When she went into her bedroom, he tried to follow.

He ate like a monster. One meal required an entire basin of food.

She once lured him deep into the mountains, thinking she could lose him there. Before she even made it home, he was already back.

Dragging a wild boar.

The moment she stepped into the yard, he pointed at it.

"Hungry."

"I really want to kill you," she muttered through clenched teeth. Being unable to win and unable to escape was suffocating.

"Fine. If you want to stay here, I have conditions."

She crossed her arms and stared him down.

"First, you are not allowed in my bedroom. Second, you bring your own food. You go hunting every day, and you pay rent. Rent means half of whatever you catch belongs to me. Third, wash your face and cut your hair. You stink."

She dug out a clean set of clothes from her space and shoved them at him. Then she grabbed a pair of scissors and stepped closer.

"Cut your hair short. And if you're living here, you clean. You get along with Sprig and Fallon. If you don't work, you don't eat."

She had discovered his weakness—food.

As long as she threatened his meals, he compromised.

Though most days, they still ended up fighting over something. He healed fast. She did not. She lost every time.

So she changed tactics.

If she could not kill him, she would domesticate him. Turn the savage creature into something manageable.

He grabbed the scissors and hacked at his hair randomly.

She stared. His intelligence level might not even match a three year old.

He had zero self care skills. Like a giant, violent toddler.

"Even a dog could cut better than that. Give it here. Sit still."

She snapped the scissors near his face.

"You may be strong, but in this house, I make the rules. When I say one, it's one. When I say two, it's two. Got it?"

"Hungry."

She smacked the back of his head.

He bared his teeth at her, eyes flashing with feral hostility.

"Wanna to fight again?"

She raised the scissors.

"Hungry."

"Starve, then."

After she finished cutting his hair properly, she forced him into the bathroom.

The smell coming off him had been unbearable. She would not have been surprised if he had fallen into a manure pit at some point.

The clothes she gave him were brand new, part of the supplies she had collected long ago.

He was tall, so she handed him an XXL set.

An hour and a half later, he emerged.

It was the first time she saw his face clearly.

And it stunned her.

He looked very young. He had pale skin, a high nose bridge, deep set eyes, and full lips.

If not for the mismatched eyes, she would have thought he was someone else entirely.

"Hungry."

"Go chop wood. When the pile outside is done, you eat."

He started to walk out barefoot.

She threw a pair of sneakers at him.

"Put them on."

He shook his head.

"Don't know how."

She pulled out a knife.

"Put them on."

Under her threat, he obeyed.

"That corner is your bed. Don't squat outside my door anymore."

She pointed to the folding cot she had placed near the fireplace.

He looked displeased.

She waved the knife casually in front of him.

He healed quickly, yes.

But pain was still pain.

And he felt it.

Her intimidation worked.

In record time, 0018 finished chopping the firewood.

"Good."

Evelyn closed the book in her hand titled The Beginner's Guide to Training Dogs and pulled out a piece of candy.

She handed it to him.

He put it in his mouth.

His eyes widened slightly.

An almost innocent expression crossed his face, if one ignored the dangerously sharp features of that face.

Chapter 46 Heatwave, Scavenging

Evelyn smoothed the paper flat and slipped it into one of Claire's law books.

A photo frame lay fallen in the corner. She turned it over. Thick grime coated the glass, and it took a long time to wipe clean.

It was a family portrait of Claire's family. In the photo, Claire held a university admission letter, her smile bright and radiant.

She should have had a brilliant future.

When Evelyn went upstairs, Calvin was coming down. Judging by his look, he was heading out to scavenge.

He was tall, dressed in ragged clothes. His hair was long and greasy, stubble covering his face. His eyes looked sharp and unfriendly—someone you wouldn't want to mess with.

"I saw a snake down below," he said.

Evelyn understood what he meant. Even a small snake was meat.

"It's too hot during the day. Going out at night will be safer."

Heatstroke wasn't a joke. At temperatures like this, once it hit you, survival was almost impossible.

"I know," Calvin said. "In a couple of days, I'm planning to go farther out and take a look. I'll lock Wendy inside. I'd like to ask you to keep an eye on her for me."

"No problem." Evelyn didn't plan on going out for now anyway. She still had a fever, so going out into the blazing sun would be suicide.

Wendy was a very well-behaved child. Evelyn was more than willing to look after her.

Because of the fever, Evelyn had little appetite, but her stomach was aching with hunger. After some hesitation, she decided to make crucian carp tofu soup to stimulate her appetite.

She took out a crucian carp, a block of tofu, and a small piece of pork. After cleaning the fish, she scored it on both sides. The tofu was cut into cubes and blanched in boiling water. The pork was minced, mixed with scallions, ginger, and salt, then stuffed into the fish's belly.

She heated oil in a pan and fried the fish until golden on both sides, added ginger shreds and cooking wine, then poured in hot water. Once it boiled, she added the tofu and simmered it over low heat for thirty minutes.

The milky white fish soup was rich and savory. Evelyn's appetite returned immediately. With a bowl of rice, she finally felt alive again.

*****

On the second night, Calvin left under cover of darkness. After he was gone, Evelyn went to check on Wendy. He'd said Wendy was already asleep, but when Evelyn went in, she saw the little girl standing on a chair by the window, her small body pressed against the glass, tears streaming down her face as she stared outside.

She was very young, but she understood everything.

Evelyn didn't say anything. She simply stood beside her, keeping her company.

Calvin didn't return until the third day.

During those three days, Evelyn took Wendy to nearby buildings to scavenge. Tables, chairs, books, pots and pans—anything usable was worth taking.

Wendy wore a sun-protection outfit Evelyn had altered for her and trudged along behind her, puffing with effort. Occasionally, they ran into the five people from Block E. They were like startled birds as any small sound made them tremble in fear and panic.

When they went to Block A, Evelyn went to Block B.

They coexisted like that—peaceful, yet distant.

"The roads outside are even worse now. A lot of places have collapsed, and snakes pop out every so often. The worst are those red-headed flies. The moment they see a person, they swarm around your head. You can't chase them off. They stink and they're loud. This time, I ran into a lot of people out there. Some of them just collapsed and died while walking."

Calvin let out a long sigh. "When I passed the city library, I went in to take a look. It used to be a shelter. There wasn't a single person left."

Evelyn's eyes lit up. "What about the books in the library?"

"Gone. Snake corpses everywhere. The place was filthy, not a clean spot in sight. The books were burned for heat."

Evelyn couldn't help feeling disappointed, but the feeling passed quickly.

After Calvin returned, he didn't rest for long before heading to the neighborhoods farther back to search for supplies. This time, he took Wendy with him.

Wearing a mesh hat, Evelyn carried a steamer she had scavenged and headed back to Block D. The buzzing of red-headed blowflies filled her ears. These insects lived off rotting flesh. Even getting close, you could smell the sour stench coming off them.

Snake corpses were everywhere in the complex. Aside from the rot, these flies were the most annoying thing of all.

In Block C, on the twelfth floor, Evelyn found a generator and two bottles of engine oil. In the study, she also discovered a hidden compartment. After fiddling with it for over an hour, she finally managed to open it and found a safe inside.

She remembered that a long time ago, when she'd scavenged an abandoned pharmaceutical company, she'd also found a safe. She had thrown it into her storage space and never bothered to open it.

She knocked on the safe in front of her, a sudden spark of curiosity rising.

It needed a password.

Evelyn sighed. Forget it. She would study it at home.

There were other unexpected gains in this apartment. More than ten clean, intact books. She picked up a couple and flipped through them. They were all about architecture. The owner must have worked in construction.

In the bedroom, she found a very beautiful lamp. After checking it, she confirmed it was still in perfect condition. She put both the lamp and the small table it sat on into her storage space.

Clothes and shoes littered the bedroom floor, men's and women's alike. In a box on the top shelf of the wardrobe, Evelyn found several unopened bottles of shampoo and body wash.

After finishing this apartment, she went next door.

The situation there was much worse. A skeleton lay right by the entrance. The apartment was full of red-headed blowflies. Between the heat and the stench, Evelyn struggled to breathe more than once.

Five people had lived here.

Evelyn gathered their remains into one room and covered them with a cloth before beginning her search.

There was no food left. The pot in the kitchen held leftover snake meat, now covered in green mold. But food wasn't her goal anyway. Tables, chairs, bookshelves, books, knives, those were what she wanted.

There had been children in this family. In the study, Evelyn found two unopened cans of baby formula. In a cabinet drawer, there was a stack of damaged banknotes and two marriage certificates.

She didn't touch them.

She closed the drawer and went into the next bedroom.

This room had likely belonged to an elderly person. A simple vanity stood against the wall, with several small boxes on top. Beside it lay a severely decayed snake corpse.

Evelyn took a clean box, opened it, and found a vivid green jade bracelet inside.

The search continued.

The first apartment on the tenth floor belonged to an elderly couple. The handkerchiefs and fans they used for square dancing were placed neatly in the most visible spot by the door. On the bed in the bedroom, two skeletons lay side by side. A wheelchair stood next to the bed.

On the balcony, several flower pots had fallen over. A shoe rack had collapsed, with shoes scattered across the floor.

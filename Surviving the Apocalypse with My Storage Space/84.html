Chapter 84 Surviving Alone: Part 8

Evelyn stomped the snow off her boots before pushing open the cabin door. A wave of warmth rushed out to meet her. She quickly slipped inside and shut the door tight behind her. Kicking off her boots, she changed into cotton slippers and headed straight for the kitchen to start dinner.

The charcoal in the stove was still glowing. She grabbed a few logs from the pile in the corner, sat down, and picked up the ax resting beside the stove. The steady rhythm of chopping soon filled the room.

When the firewood was ready, she fed it into the stove. Sparks crackled and popped. Evelyn filled the kettle to the brim and set it on top to boil.

She pulled out a bag of hazel mushrooms, scooped a handful into a bowl, and covered them with cold water to soak for an hour.

The radio sat outside. She switched the tape to an old collection of nostalgic folk songs from the last century. A lazy, melodic tune drifted through the air. With a broom in hand, Evelyn swept away the snow she had tracked in by the door.

Sprig and Fallon circled around her the entire time. She played with them for a bit before the kettle began to whistle. Then she brought the pheasant in from outside. With practiced ease, she slit its throat and drained the blood, then placed it into an iron basin and poured in scalding hot water.

Carrying the basin back out under the eaves, she started plucking the feathers. Not far from the yard stood an iron barrel she used for trash. When it filled up, she burned the contents.

The cold wind sliced through the air. Evelyn sniffed and hurried back inside with the cleaned pheasant.

She gutted it and chopped the meat into small pieces, placing them neatly into a bowl.

After checking the time, she set a clay pot over the stove and poured in some oil. When it heated up, she tossed in peppercorns, dried chilies, scallions, and ginger. The fragrance bloomed instantly. Finally, she added the pheasant and stir fried it.

The chilies were so strong they made her sneeze over and over. Sprig and Fallon sat at the kitchen doorway, staring at her like concerned little supervisors.

When the pheasant was about seventy percent cooked, she poured in water and added the soaked hazel mushrooms. A pinch of salt, a sprinkle of pepper, a splash of cooking wine. She covered the pot and let it simmer for twenty to thirty minutes. After that, she stirred in a spoonful of chicken bouillon and let it cook for a few more minutes before taking it off the heat.

Evelyn carried the clay pot to the low table in front of the fireplace. The tape had already played through all ten songs and shut off automatically. After washing her hands, she grabbed her tablet, picked a favorite comfort drama, and started dinner.

"Hahaha…"

The show was ridiculously funny. Even Evelyn, who rarely laughed out loud, could not help herself. Sprig and Fallon ate their own dinners nearby. Everything moved in its usual rhythm, calm and orderly.

The pheasant was not very fatty, but the meat was tender and flavorful. Evelyn honestly wished she could find one every single day.

The ancients waited by a tree stump for a rabbit. She waited by hers for pheasants.

The pot was far too much for one meal. She stored the leftovers in her space for tomorrow.

Full and satisfied, she washed the dishes, then pulled out a ball of red yarn. Time to continue her grand crochet project.

Red was perfect for sweaters and scarves. Just looking at it made her feel warmer.

She added more wood to the fireplace and began sketching out ideas in her head.

"Chirp chirp chirp…" Sprig scampered over again, eager to join in. Evelyn reached down and rubbed its ears.

"Sprig, how about I make you an outfit?"

She decided to alter one of her old clothes. There was an old fashioned sewing machine stored in her space. After measuring Sprig carefully, she got to work, setting up the machine and starting on a tiny custom outfit.

It was her first time making something like this. The stitching was clumsy, and the finished piece looked… questionable. She slipped it onto Sprig anyway. Judging by its expression, it seemed pretty pleased.

Encouraged, Evelyn made two more so it could rotate between them.

"Fallon, do you want clothes too?"

Fallon flapped its wings and flew off.

Evelyn stared after it, speechless.

Making three tiny outfits for Sprig had taken up more than two hours. She glanced at the ball of yarn and decided she would deal with it tomorrow.

Why was she taking this so seriously anyway? She did not have to study. She did not have to work. She did not have to get married. For all she knew, Earth might suddenly explode one day and that would be the end of her.

Forget it. Shower and sleep.

*****

That night, the temperature outside dropped to five degrees below zero. Snow continued to drift down in soft flurries. After washing up, Evelyn sat on her bed and gazed out at the snowy world beyond the window.

It was beautiful.

It felt like the whole world had gone silent. No birds in the sky. No footprints in the snow.

She had long since turned off her alarm. Her nerves had finally relaxed. She could sleep as late as she wanted. Somehow, the unknown tomorrow did not feel quite as frightening anymore.

The heated brick bed was wonderfully warm. As someone who had grown up in the south, Evelyn was experiencing the magic of a northern brick bed for the first time. No air conditioner, no floor heating, yet the warmth lasted all night.

Fallon's injuries had completely healed, but it showed no sign of leaving. Apparently it had decided to stick with her. Free food, free shelter, and Sprig as a playmate. Even a bird knew how to find a reliable backer. It was becoming too clever for its own good.

Lost in her wandering thoughts, Evelyn drifted off to sleep. Overnight, the snow in the mountains grew even deeper.

The lantern by the door swayed in the wind. Small animals that ventured out to forage passed by the yard, caught a whiff of something unfamiliar and dangerous, and quickly scurried away.

*****

In the morning, Evelyn lay in bed with her eyes open for nearly an hour before finally dragging herself up.

Outside, the wind howled. Not far away, a fir branch snapped with a sharp crack and slammed heavily to the ground.

She pulled on her military coat and quickly tugged a thick winter hat over her head, wrapping her ears and face snugly against the cold.

Sprig and Fallon had already woken up. They only slipped outside after Evelyn opened the door.

Breakfast was yesterday's leftover chicken and mushrooms. She added two large steamed buns before she finally felt full. Sure enough, when the weather turned cold, people needed extra fuel. Her appetite had grown noticeably.

She made a quick round outside and found nothing unusual. Just as she was about to head back in to continue crocheting, she noticed a string of footprints beyond the fence gate. From the shape alone she could not tell what animal had left them, but the paws were small. Probably something small and furry.

After tossing out the trash and finishing her patrol, Evelyn returned to the cabin. She added more firewood to the fireplace, took out her yarn and crochet hook, and resumed yesterday's work.

She decided to start with a scarf to get back into the rhythm. She had crocheted before back home, but after months without touching a hook, her hands felt rusty.

Still, practice made perfect.

By noon, she had successfully finished a scarf. Red really was flattering. It brightened her whole complexion.

After warming up with the scarf, the sweater went much faster. By afternoon, she had already finished the high collar.

Smoke billowed steadily from the cabin chimney. In the silent depths of the mountains, Evelyn was once again busy preparing dinner.

"What should I make tonight?"

She hesitated for a long time, flipping through her cookbook before finally settling on braised pork with vermicelli.

It was a famous northeastern dish she had always wanted to try, but had never made herself.

Today was the day.

She soaked a handful of sweet potato vermicelli in water until soft, then cut pork belly into chunks and set them aside.

Following the recipe step by step, she worked at an unhurried pace.

"If I keep eating like this every day, will I get fat? Haha…"

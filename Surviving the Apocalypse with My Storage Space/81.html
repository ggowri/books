Chapter 81 Surviving Alone: Part 5

Light rain tapped steadily against the RV windows.

Evelyn opened her eyes. She sat up and wiped the fog from the glass. Outside, a thin layer of snow had already blanketed the ground. The rain was light, but steady. The fire beneath the large umbrella had gone out.

She glanced at Sprig, who was curled tightly in the blankets, then stretched her stiff limbs. Pulling on her military coat and rain boots, tugging a knit hat over her head, she prepared to head outside.

Forget washing her face. It was too cold.

Hands shoved deep into her coat pockets, shoulders hunched, she climbed down from the RV.

She reignited the fire beneath the umbrella, then did a quick set of stretches in place to warm up.

Today's tasks were still heavy. She had to build the fence gate and clear the pond.

The fence posts she had installed were tall, about one and a half meters, made from thick fir logs. Solid and sturdy.

There was more than enough leftover wood to build a gate.

First, food.

She sat by the fire and pulled out a bowl of noodles from her storage space, adding a heaping spoonful of chili paste. It was so spicy she kept sucking in air, but warmth spread through her entire body.

Fed and warmed, she grabbed a saw and hand planer and got to work.

"Don't run around. Stay here and play."

She poured a generous pile of beans into Sprig's bowl.

The most important thing about the gate was durability. It did not need to withstand a bear, but at least it should not collapse in strong winds.

After cutting the fir boards, she used the planer to smooth the surfaces, shaving off splinters and rough edges.

When Evelyn did something, she did it thoroughly. Either she did not do it at all, or she did it perfectly.

Three and a half hours later, the gate was finished.

She installed it carefully and added a lock.

Perfect.

Snowflakes and rain dotted her clothes. She caught one snowflake on her fingertip and tasted it.

It was cold and clean.

The marsh lay about a hundred meters past the yard. It was overgrown with rushes, hiding the pond beneath.

Pulling on gloves, she cut down the rushes and stacked them to one side. The cut grass could be used to cover muddy paths so she would not have to step directly in the muck.

Her mood was bright despite the sleet.

Humming a made up tune, she cleared all the rushes around the marsh and pond.

The pond's surface was littered with fallen leaves. She took out a net from her storage space and skimmed away the debris and dead stalks.

“Why am I so awesome? Why am I so good at everything? Honestly, how do I live with myself?”

She sang as she walked back.

The pond was clean now. No need to alter it further. Let it stay natural.

"Damn it!"

Just as she approached the umbrella again, her foot slipped. She landed hard on her backside.

Happiness had turned into disaster in a blink.

"Oh my back…"

She sat there rubbing her lower back, her face twisted in pain.

"Chirp, chirp…"

Sprig darted down from the vehicle, trying to leap onto her.

"Don't jump. If you bounce on me right now, I won't be able to work for three days."

The squirrel stared at her pitifully.

"I'll play with you when I'm done. Okay?"

She tossed more wood into the fire, shrugged off her coat, and began massaging the sore spot. After a while, the pain eased enough for her to straighten slowly.

"That almost killed me."

She looked at the patch of ground where she had slipped. Mud and snow had mixed together, turning it slick.

No wonder she fell.

Evelyn decided she would fix that stretch of path next.

She gathered stones and laid them over the worst stretches of ground. She had considered using wooden planks, but they might shift underfoot. That would be even more dangerous.

It was nearly two in the afternoon before she finally went back to make lunch.

Sprig had been waiting patiently the whole time. Evelyn scooped it into her arms and rubbed her cheek against its head. Instantly, the squirrel perked up, its tail whipping back and forth in excitement.

She switched on the radio and slid in a cassette tape, turning the volume down low. Sprig's attention was immediately captured.

After eating, Evelyn treated herself to a pot of tea.

She had collected the tea leaves during one of her supply runs but had never opened them.

She sat by the fire for an hour, warming herself, then went back to laying stones along the path.

“Little Evie once had a life down in Elaris town,

"Warm bed, full fridge, nothing weighing her down.

"Then the storms rolled in and the world burned cold,

"Now she’s out here surviving, stubborn and bold.”

She huffed and puffed, picking up stones one by one. The drizzle stopped without her noticing. The light flurries turned into heavy snowfall. Within minutes, her hat and coat were dusted white.

She quickened her pace.

By five in the afternoon, the paths inside the yard and the stretch leading to the pond were fully covered with stones. She hurried back to the vehicle to change out of her damp clothes.

Her hands, feet, and face were stiff from the cold. She heated water to wash her face and soak her feet. Sprig was still sprawled beside the radio, listening to music like it had no worries in the world.

Looking at the cactus on the table, Evelyn suddenly decided she should plant more flowers and vegetables.

It was practically instinct, growing things. She could not let that go.

But with a snowstorm coming, she would need cold resistant plants.

After soaking her feet, she pulled her coat back on and walked into the wooden cabin. She added firewood to both the heated brick bed and the fireplace, checking carefully for drafts or leaks. Fortunately, the structure was solid. Not a single weak point.

She lit a bundle of mugwort incense to clear out the earthy smell of sand and timber.

In the bedroom, she could fit a small wardrobe. There was no need for a bed frame since she had built the heated brick platform.

The bathroom still needed a water heater. Luckily, she had salvaged several and stored them away. A compact one would do. The generator could sit in the living area. With the heated brick bed in the bedroom, she would not need an air conditioner.

Since the cabin could be stored in her space and taken with her if necessary, she wanted it to be as perfect as possible.

It was her home.

Even if she was alone, it still needed to feel like one.

Suddenly remembering something, she searched through her storage space with her mind and finally found several lightning rods.

She smacked her forehead.

How had she forgotten to install those on the roof? Never mind. She would handle it tomorrow.

The windows still needed curtains. That would be easy.

Tomorrow she would also run a pipe from the pond into the bathroom. The pipe would have to be buried underground. If it lay exposed, freezing temperatures might crack it.

Even if the pond's surface froze, the water beneath would remain liquid. That was why she planned to draw water from the bottom.

There was still so much to do.

She took out a notebook and began listing tasks one by one. Every time she completed something, she marked it with a check.

The wind picked up in the hollow that night. Bundled in layers, she moved a bit clumsily.

When she reached the RV, she turned back quickly to the umbrella and fed more wood into the fire.

All wild animals feared fire, especially wolves.

As long as flames burned bright, they would not dare come too close.

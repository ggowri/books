Chapter 37 Heatwave, Snake Infestation

The temperature kept climbing. The bodies in the water rotted faster and faster, and even from the balcony you could smell the sharp, nauseating stench. Fewer people were willing to go out. It wasn't just that supplies were hard to find. It was the fear of robbers outside, waiting for prey.

Evelyn hadn't left her apartment in half a month. She set her solar panels on the balcony and kept the battery pack fully charged. In her spare time, she took out the toad venom she had saved earlier and mixed it into the medicine she'd formulated, just in case she needed it later.

When the temperature rose to fifteen degrees, the air started to turn humid and oppressive.

The next morning, someone knocked on Evelyn's door. Claire stood outside, holding an oval egg. Like she'd found treasure, she thrust it toward Evelyn.

"Evelyn, look at this. I found it in the hallway. There are a bunch in the water too. This one's for you. You can eat it. Natalie accidentally cracked one earlier, and she didn't want to waste it, so she ate it raw. She said it tastes like a chicken egg, just a little fishy."

Evelyn glanced at her and took the egg. The shell was thin and cool to the touch. Holding it up to the sunlight, she couldn't see anything inside. When she gently shook it, she could feel liquid sloshing.

"Are there a lot of them?" she asked.

Claire nodded. "Tons. The water's full of them. I haven't told anyone else yet. Evelyn, let's hurry and go collect them."

Evelyn's brows tightened slightly. When something strange happened, it was never good. Eggs didn't appear in huge numbers out of nowhere. Not like this.

"You go first," Evelyn said. "I'll be down in a bit."

Claire hurried off downstairs, excited. Evelyn closed the door, went back inside, and pulled a chicken egg from her space. She set it beside the oval egg and studied them carefully.

This was definitely not a chicken egg. It looked more like a crocodile egg, a turtle egg, or a snake egg.

She went to the balcony and lifted her binoculars to the water below. Just as Claire had said, plenty of pale eggs floated on the surface. They didn't seem to sink, just bobbing there like little white stones.

An hour later, people in the building noticed the eggs too. Excited voices filled the stairwell as everyone rushed down to salvage them. Some people cracked the eggs the moment they picked them up and poured them straight into their mouths.

Evelyn cracked hers open. The moment she saw the thick, yellow, cloudy fluid inside, she panicked. She sprinted to the balcony, but when she saw the people below, laughing and splashing as they gathered eggs, the warning on her tongue died before it could escape.

They were snake eggs.

Snake eggs didn't have distinct yolk and egg white like chicken eggs. Inside was a murky yellow fluid. If the egg was fertilized, you could sometimes even see faint embryo patterns under light.

Evelyn was terrified of snakes. Not mildly scared. She was deeply, violently afraid.

Even seeing a photo could make her break out in goosebumps, every hair on her body standing on end, her skin crawling.

And there were so many eggs in the complex. It was like cockroaches. The moment you saw one, there were probably thousands hidden in the shadows.

A wild thought took hold of her. With the warming temperatures, snakes that had been dormant were waking up. There were too many of them. And in every corner of this city, they were leaving eggs behind.

Evelyn dumped the snake egg into the trash, then grabbed a flashlight and began checking every inch of her apartment, corner by corner. If all those eggs hatched, the consequences would be unimaginable.

Calvin's family upstairs also went to collect the eggs. When Evelyn saw them haul back two buckets filled to the brim, goosebumps instantly rose along her arms. Her mind filled with the image of countless snakes, packed together in writhing masses.

In the middle of the night, Evelyn quietly slipped out and scattered some snake repellent powder she had mixed herself along the stairwell.

In the damp, shadowy levels of the building, snakes were tangled together in dense coils. They flicked their tongues, hissing softly.

Floating corpses in the floodwater were hooked by snake tails. The mass of snakes surged forward, crawling onto the bodies and tearing into them, biting and devouring frantically.

Beneath the murky water, massive pythons lay coiled, resting. Sensing the change in temperature, crimson eyes slowly opened.

Evelyn had a nightmare.

In it, countless snakes slithered into her home. They crawled under her blanket, wrapping around her ankles, her knees, her waist, her neck, her head. She could not fight back at all. One snake coiled around her throat, opened its foul, gaping mouth, and revealed sharp fangs. Thick saliva dripped down, carrying a choking stench. The snake's head crept closer and then lunged straight at her eyes.

"Ah—"

Evelyn gasped violently, sucking in air. She snapped on the emergency light and scanned the bedroom in panic. When she saw the door still intact, she collapsed back onto the bed, her whole body weak, as if she had narrowly escaped death.

The survivors who had gone out to collect snake eggs were still full of enthusiasm. Evelyn took one glance and pulled the curtains shut. The smell of fried eggs lingered in the air. With food in their stomachs, the neighbors' footsteps going up and down the stairs had grown noticeably lighter.

The water level outside the eighth floor had dropped slightly, but William's family, who had moved into 1201, still could not move back. The most hardworking people in the building were William and Calvin. Each of them carried a bucket of snake eggs upstairs. When they passed the tenth floor, Evelyn called out to Calvin.

"These eggs are probably snake eggs. After you take them home, boil them thoroughly as soon as possible. The temperature is rising. If they're fertilized, they'll hatch very quickly."

Calvin's expression changed. He had suspected they might be snake eggs, but William insisted that snake eggs were not this large. He thought they were crocodile eggs or eggs from some other animal.

"No wonder you didn't go collect any," he said.

Evelyn smiled faintly. "Snake eggs aren't poisonous, but once they hatch, they're a huge problem. And snakes are very vengeful creatures. Once the temperature hits twenty degrees, I suggest everyone stop going outside for now. It's best to seal up all the gaps in your homes."

Calvin had always trusted Evelyn's judgment. After thanking her seriously, he hurried home and told everyone.

Over the next few days, Evelyn noticed far fewer people going out to collect eggs. Calvin was not a selfish man. Anything he knew, he shared. Word spread from one person to another, and fear slowly took hold in the complex.

Claire came by once to confirm the information about the snake eggs with Evelyn.

But with drinking water extremely scarce, everyone could only roast the eggs instead of boiling them.

By the end of June, the temperature had reached twenty five degrees. Daylight stretched to eighteen hours, while night shrank to just six.

Quinn had not appeared for half a month. Evelyn found it strange, but she had no desire to meddle. In the days that followed, strange sticky residue began appearing in the stairwell.

Late one night, Evelyn heard something squirming outside. Sleep vanished instantly. She held her breath and listened carefully.

After a long while, she confirmed the sound was coming from outside the window. Evelyn picked up her flashlight, gripped her military knife, and pulled back the curtain.

The moment she saw a snake clinging to the glass, her mind went blank. Her body froze.

It was a red spotted keelback, commonly known as a water snake. Mildly venomous, edible.

The snake was nearly a meter long, thick bodied, with black blotches along its body and stripes running down its back.

Evelyn locked eyes with it.

The snake slammed its head against the glass, making dull thudding sounds. Its tail hooked around the iron railing outside the window. As its scales scraped against the metal bars, they produced a faint, chilling noise.

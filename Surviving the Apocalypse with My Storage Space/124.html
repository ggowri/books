Chapter 124 I’m Safe Now

Evelyn unlocked the door to 601. The moment she stepped inside, she saw that Pip and Lark had been well looked after. The room was tidy, the stove still held a trace of warmth, and the two little creatures threw themselves at her the instant they saw her.

She locked the door behind her, soothed them both for a moment, then took out a dagger and a cup. Drawing the blade across her arm, she winced as it bit through the skin. Blood ran down her wrist and into the cup below.

Once she had collected nearly half a cup, she dropped in a few albendazole tablets. Then she cleaned the wound, wrapped it quickly, and left 601 with the cup tucked away.

Elliot was still outside. The second he saw her, he hurried over to ask about Clara.

“She’s all right. I expect she’ll be back soon enough.”

Elliot let out a long breath. “Evelyn, could you take a few things to Clara for me?”

Evelyn glanced into the bag and saw several tins of food. Her expression darkened.

“Have you eaten any of these?”

Elliot shook his head. “They were for Clara. I haven’t touched them.”

“I’m not going back to the Medical Division for the moment, so I can’t take them in for you. Throw them away. There are worms in them. They can’t be eaten.”

There was something so sharp in her gaze that Elliot nodded at once.

“I’ll get rid of them now.”

“Wait. Leave them in your room for the time being. If you throw them out, someone else might pick them up.”

The moment she finished speaking, Evelyn left the apartment block and ran at full speed towards the single rooms in the West Wing. Eddie had once told her Calvin’s room number: Building 13, fifth floor, room 502.

Before leaving, she had taken off the protective suit. She had been wearing the same clothes for more than ten days, and she did not need to sniff herself properly to know she smelled dreadful.

She looked up at the sun, bright enough to sting the eyes. A patrol unit was wandering about ahead, so she slipped down a side path and avoided them entirely.

When she reached Building 13 in the West Wing, she saw no administrator about and went straight up to the fifth floor. After checking the room number once more, she raised her hand and knocked.

“Calvin? Wendy?”

The door opened almost at once, and Calvin looked startled to see her.

“Come in, quickly.”

Only after stepping inside did Evelyn realise just how small four square metres really was. There was a narrow bed, and with two grown adults standing in the remaining space, there was barely room even to turn around.

Wendy lay on the bed, limp and listless, her complexion tinged faintly blue. Evelyn had no time for pleasantries. She gently prised open Wendy’s mouth and checked her over.

“How has she been these past two days?”

“She’s been weak the whole time. Won’t eat, won’t drink, and she hasn’t been sick.”

Evelyn took the small cup from her pocket and poured the blood into Wendy’s mouth.

Calvin caught the smell of blood and stared at Evelyn in shock, but he knew she would never harm anyone, so he did not question her straightaway.

“There are worms in the tins. It’s probably another kind of mutated nematode carried by the animals raised at the base. Worse than the ones in the water.”

Wendy opened her eyes and tried to spit the blood back out, but Evelyn held her steady and made her swallow.

“Evelyn, I missed you,” Wendy murmured weakly.

Evelyn smoothed a hand over her hair, her eyes suddenly stinging.

“I missed you too.”

She wiped Wendy’s mouth gently. “Do you have any clean water?”

Calvin immediately rummaged through his bag and handed her a bottle. Evelyn let Wendy drink some water to wash away the taste of blood.

“I’ve got some compressed biscuits here. Share them out. Don’t eat the base’s food for now. Adrian said the base has already started purifying the water, so things should settle down after a while.”

Evelyn slipped the rucksack from her back.

Just then Wendy suddenly clutched her stomach and began crying. Calvin was so frightened his mind seemed to go blank, but Evelyn reacted at once. She grabbed a bag, patted Wendy’s back, and urged her to throw up.

There were tiny worms in the yellow fluid and fresh blood Wendy brought up. They writhed for only a few seconds before dying in the blood. Wendy retched until she was completely spent. Only when there was nothing left in her stomach did Evelyn finally breathe again.

“Take this half box of tablets,” she said. “Give Wendy one every few hours. I have to go now, but I’ll come back tomorrow.”

Calvin looked at her worriedly. “Don’t let anyone know you’ve got medicine.”

Evelyn nodded. “I know.”

Wendy fell asleep again, but her colour was already much better. The bluish pallor had faded quite a bit.

Evelyn left the West Wing and hurried back. Patrols were spread all over the base now. If they saw anyone, they stopped them for inspection, and anyone showing the slightest problem was sent straight to the Medical Division.

On the way, Evelyn saw a lorry carrying live pigs rumbling past. Fat white pigs were packed inside the cages. The moment she noticed their pale, clouded eyes, gooseflesh prickled over her arms.

“Oi, you there. Stop.”

Several patrol guards had spotted her and were coming over with electric batons in hand.

Evelyn stood where she was, her fists clenched tightly beneath her sleeves.

“Take off the mask.”

She did not resist. Calmly, she removed it.

The man looked her up and down. “Which department?”

“Medical Division.”

He gave her another long look from head to toe, then waved her on. Evelyn said nothing, put her mask back on, and walked away as quickly as she could.

When she got back to the apartment block, Adrian still had not returned. She frowned. Had he gone hunting again?

That did not seem likely.

Evelyn took a shower and changed out of her filthy clothes. She had no intention of going back to the Medical Division.

The cut on her arm had already started healing. It ached, and itched too.

She took out some meat she had bought earlier from the villa district market. After checking it carefully and confirming it was not pork raised by the base, she relaxed a little.

Then she took out an egg, cracked it, and held a torch to it. Thankfully, there were no worms in the egg either.

At eight that evening, she heard a faint sound outside the door. Evelyn got up at once and opened it. Adrian was standing there with his key in hand, just about to unlock the door. Their eyes met, and Evelyn let out a heavy breath of relief.

“You’re finally back.”

The moment Adrian came inside, he seized her arm and tore off the bandage.

“Why did you bleed yourself?”

“To save Wendy. There’s a tougher mutated nematode in the tins. The wonder drug doesn’t work on it anymore. Blood was the only thing I could use to save her. Luckily, mine worked.”

Adrian lowered his eyes to look at her, his expression darkening.

“I should have come back sooner.”

Evelyn said nothing. Instead, she looked at his sleeve. A large patch of blood stained the fabric, and her sharpened senses told her at once that it was human blood.

Adrian had his secrets. Whatever had brought him to the base, it was likely for some purpose of his own. Since he chose not to speak of it, Evelyn had no intention of asking.

“Is she all right?” he asked, meaning Wendy.

“She was much better by the time I left. I’ll go and see her again tomorrow.”

Adrian gave a small nod. “I’ll come with you.”

“You’re not busy tomorrow?”

He pulled a chair over, sat down, and looked up at her. When Adrian looked at someone, his gaze was so steady and intent that it was almost impossible to look away.

“No. I’ve finished what I had to do.” He paused. “Evelyn, I’m safe now.”

Chapter 25 Rainstorm, Venom Moths: Part 21

On the twenty-fifth day of nonstop rain, the radio finally picked up a broken, patchy signal.

[Global catastrophic rainfall intensifies… All citizens urged to protect themselves… Reports of toxic insect species… Rising floodwaters… Landslides…]

The signal was too weak to catch everything, but Evelyn could just barely make out these fragments.

Another elderly person in Block D had frozen to death. Hypothermia victims often passed with a faint smile on their faces, as if lost in a peaceful dream. His family was devastated. He had just turned eighty a month before the disaster began.

The neighborhood's greenery had long been stripped away. Survivors had chopped down every available tree. Calvin still went out every other day, though not every trip was successful. Sometimes, he came back bruised and bloodied.

Eventually, several residents from Block D came to him. They'd all realized the truth: in this new world, you couldn't survive without skill or strength. But even the strongest person was powerless alone. So they formed a small team, heading out together to search for firewood and supplies. With more people, they didn't have to fear raiders as much.

Now that she had finally recovered from her illness, Evelyn gave the whole apartment a thorough cleaning. She decided to treat herself.

For the first time since the blackout and water shutoff, she cooked a real meal.

She sat in the kitchen as juicy meat sizzled on a grill tray over the fire. The scent was mouthwatering.

Feeling a sudden impulse, Evelyn went to the bedroom and pulled out a tablet. It booted up slowly due to the freezing temperature, but thankfully, she had downloaded thousands of movies, TV shows, and novels ahead of time.

She picked a lighthearted animated series and settled in.

For once, she allowed herself to enjoy the moment. Roasting meat and watching cartoons—who cared if the world outside was falling apart? For now, it had nothing to do with her.

After dinner, she even managed to take a hot shower.

Satiated and clean, Evelyn sank into bed with a rare sense of contentment.

But as sleep took her, a vision from her past life emerged of her starved, desperate self rummaging through piles of garbage for food. Evelyn jolted awake, her legs kicking out in fear.

*****

The next day, Claire came to visit. She looked worried.

"Evelyn… my grandpa hasn't been well these past two days. I don't know what's wrong. No matter what I do, he won't eat."

Evelyn followed her to the apartment. The old man lay in bed, staring off into space. His eyes were blank.

Dementia, Evelyn thought. It was a cruel disease with no cure. She wasn't a doctor. She didn't really know how to help.

"Is it a loss of appetite, or… is he refusing to eat?"

Claire blinked, startled by the question. "Now that you mention it… it does seem like he's refusing food."

Evelyn gestured for her to step into the living room. Once they were away from the bedroom, she spoke gently, choosing her words with care.

"Your grandpa might still have moments of clarity. He knows your grandma, your parents… they're all gone. He's grieving. He might be trying to starve himself. Or maybe, he just wants to save the food for you."

Claire stared at the door, stunned. Then she sank to the floor and curled in on herself, letting out a quiet sob.

"He knows, Evelyn. He knows everything…"

Evelyn knelt down and gave her a reassuring pat on the shoulder.

"Don't blame yourself."

After leaving Claire's apartment, Evelyn felt heavy. Her mood sank again.

This damned apocalypse… it wasn't just trying to destroy the world. It wanted to wipe out the human race.

Later, Evelyn geared up and went out—not far, just a few kilometers to a nearby park. The ground there was higher, so the flooding wasn't too deep. Because of the rain, there were few people around.

She found a quiet spot and cut down several trees, storing most of the wood in her spatial space. She left just a small bundle on her motor raft.

As she prepared to leave, a group of people arrived, armed with chainsaws and equipment. They were there to harvest the trees too.

Evelyn slipped away without making a sound.

She unscrewed her thermos, took a sip of hot tea, and stared up at the gray, oppressive sky.

Then she muttered a curse under her breath.

After leaving the park, Evelyn made a stop at the nearby commercial street. Even though it had already been thoroughly picked over by other survivors, she stuck to her usual principle of never letting anything go to waste.

In a nearly-collapsed corner shop, she managed to scavenge two full cotton comforters.

Time was running out. Though the haul wasn't impressive, she could feel it—after this relentless rain, the next wave of disaster would be fierce winds. Likely over level ten. She needed to get home, fast.

The water had already risen past the fourth floor.

Back at Block D, she slapped the venom moths off her protective suit, then unloaded the comforters and firewood. It took two trips to carry everything, including her motor raft, up to the tenth floor.

Just as she reached her door, Jessica came rushing down the stairs, clearly panicked. The moment she saw Evelyn, she let out a breath of pure relief.

"Evie, thank goodness you're back."

Seeing her like that, Evelyn didn't ask questions. She simply followed her to the twelfth floor. She had expected it to be Calvin's injuries acting up again. But the person in trouble this time was Wendy.

"Evie, something's wrong with Wendy. She started acting weird this morning. She's burning up and has red spots on her body. Do you think it's a high fever and an allergic reaction?"

Calvin and his parents stood nearby, their faces tight with worry as they watched over the little girl. Evelyn stepped closer and examined the red spots.

"These symptoms…" she said slowly. "They look a lot like chickenpox. I remember when I had it as a kid—it was exactly like this."

The moment she finished speaking, Calvin's father Max slapped his thigh with a heavy sigh. "It must be chickenpox! There's a few days of incubation. She's been a little off the past few days already. It's my fault. If only I'd caught it sooner…"

"Dad, don't blame yourself," Calvin said quickly. "Chickenpox isn't fatal."

Still, his jaw was tight. His eyes never left his daughter.

"Evie… chickenpox can heal on its own, right?"

"Yes. It's self-limiting. But it's also contagious. If any of you haven't had it before, you could catch it."

The family exchanged nervous glances, trying to remember their childhoods. Fortunately, everyone except Jessica had already had chickenpox before. Once it scabs over, you gain immunity, and relapses are rare.

"Self-healing takes time though, right? And it's miserable. Should we give her something to ease it?"

"You can apply ointments like an antiviral cream, or give her some herbal meds. Things like traditional cold and flu tinctures, immune-boosting granules, or natural anti-inflammatory drops help too."

Jessica quickly grabbed the first aid kit and began rummaging through it.

"I've got some of that herbal cold medicine! Should I give it to her now?"

"Yes. And once the pox fully breaks out, don't let her scratch. It can leave scars, and with all the bacteria floating around in this environment, infections are a real risk."

Calvin took out a pen and paper, carefully jotting everything down. As Evelyn turned to leave, he handed her a small bag of supplies.

This time, she only accepted one potato.

Back in her apartment, Evelyn quickly removed her protective gear, then switched on the electric stove and poured herself a cup of hot water.

Once her hands and feet were warm again, she unpacked her haul from the day.

The firewood was left to dry on the balcony. The cotton comforters had been tucked away in a burlap sack in a warehouse—completely unused and surprisingly clean.

From her spatial storage, Evelyn pulled out one of the apples she hadn't touched yet, washed it, and took a bite. Then she opened her tablet and picked up where she left off in the animated series.

Chapter 117 Miss Hawthorne Dies

The work of turning the soil was in full swing. By the time Evelyn’s second rest day came round, Clara had spent three days being scolded by Dr. Shaw and had finally undergone something of a transformation. She was working hard now, properly hard, and she had grown noticeably braver too.

Perhaps that was simply what growing up looked like. Fear gave way to courage. Clumsiness slowly became competence.

As usual, Evelyn took Adrian shopping with her. This week his points had reached three thousand, which meant she could buy far more.

“Let’s get twenty trays of eggs. I can make soy-braised eggs and tea eggs.”

“All right.”

A tray held thirty eggs. At four points an egg, twenty trays came to 2,400 points. Evelyn used the rest on baby greens and small cabbages.

Once again, they returned loaded down with supplies. On the way back, Evelyn saw Dr. Hargreaves and Dr. Shaw being dragged into a snow vehicle by several men.

It was not until the next day that she found out why. The two doctors had been taken to treat Miss Hawthorne.

Apparently her mouth had begun to ulcerate. The moment she opened it, a foul stench came out. The Hawthorne family had shut her up in the house and were summoning doctors every day. If anyone managed to cure her, they had promised a luxury flat as a reward.

Dr. Hargreaves asked whether Evelyn wanted to try her hand.

She refused.

“I’m afraid I won’t cure her and will only bring trouble on myself instead. You know what my temper is like.”

Dr. Hargreaves thought it over and decided she had a point.

“That Miss Hawthorne has the foulest temper imaginable. Yesterday she slapped both me and Dr. Shaw, then had her bodyguards throw us out.”

“You’ve had a trying time of it.”

He waved the comment away. “I’ve no wish to go back there and suffer again. Though I doubt she’ll last much longer.”

Something flickered in Evelyn’s eyes.

“Large patches of her body are ulcerating. Her limbs are swollen. Her hair’s falling out. Really, people ought to know how to keep themselves clean. Look at the number of young people she ruined. Now the reckoning’s come. The Hawthorne family may have power and money, but they still can’t buy life.”

There was no reckoning. Only someone acting in Heaven’s stead.

The corner of Evelyn’s mouth lifted slightly, and she went back to work.

“The sun’s been staying out longer these past few days. I reckon the snow will start melting soon.”

“There’ve been more animals coming out to forage too. I brought down two wild boars today.”

“I think you ought to keep things as they are. If you stand out too much, you’ll become a target. Trouble is best avoided whenever possible.”

“Then beat anyone who objects until they stop. Why should we creep about being cautious? If weak people have the nerve to cause trouble, why should strong people worry so much?”

Adrian had his own logic, and Evelyn had hers. She preferred moderation in all things. She liked to stay somewhere in the middle, not too high, not too low. To her mind, that was the safest and most comfortable way for anyone to live.

In the past, when they disagreed, they would simply start fighting. Things were better now. They no longer fought. Instead, they stopped speaking to each other.

Evelyn did not accept Adrian’s way of thinking, and Adrian did not approve of hers either.

Two days later, the snow on the ground began to melt.

Evelyn asked Eddie to pass on a message to Calvin and the others. The thaw might look like the return of life, but moments like that were precisely when people needed to stay most alert.

Clara had done well over the past few days. As a temporary nurse, she was now passable. Just as Evelyn had said, in the right circumstances people’s hidden abilities could be forced to the surface. So long as they worked hard, there was very little they could not learn.

What Evelyn had not expected was for Miss Hawthorne’s bodyguards to come to the Medical Division for doctors again.

This time Evelyn did not avoid them. She and Dr. Hargreaves were taken together to the villa. They had not even entered the house when Evelyn caught a sour, rotting smell in the air.

In the space of only half a month, a young and beautiful woman had been reduced to this. Even Evelyn found it shocking.

Miss Hawthorne lay on the bed, though not on a mattress. Beneath her was a sheet of plastic. Yellow pus seeped from the pores of her swollen skin, and the plastic had to be replaced again and again.

“Gavin’s dead. He only just stopped breathing, then his body collapsed like a punctured balloon. Should we have the doctors look at him too?”

Two people nearby were whispering. Evelyn caught enough of it to start doubting the mixture she had made.

All she had wanted was for Miss Hawthorne to suffer for a while, to itch, vomit, and be miserable. These after-effects were far beyond anything she had intended.

Miss Hawthorne was swollen to giant proportions now. The patches of skin that remained intact were shiny and taut, as if she might burst at any second.

Dr. Hargreaves looked ghastly. Evelyn could tell he wanted to be sick, but under the circumstances, the moment he did, the bodyguards waiting outside would almost certainly beat him for it. So he had no choice but to force himself to endure.

“Hurry up and treat our young lady. Use the very best medicine you have. If anything happens to her, neither of you is leaving today.”

Evelyn hated being threatened.

She had not been planning to do anything at all, but the moment she heard words like that, her fingers began to itch.

Then Miss Hawthorne suddenly brought up a mouthful of black blood with a wet gurgle. Evelyn saw thin white worms writhing in it, and her scalp went cold.

“Uncle Julian, get back.”

She caught hold of Dr. Hargreaves and dragged him several steps away.

Miss Hawthorne’s eyes bulged as she went on vomiting blood, as though she meant to bring up every last drop inside her. Evelyn noticed several young men huddled in the corner, two of whom looked no older than sixteen or seventeen.

They watched Miss Hawthorne in numb silence. But when they saw her retching blood, delight and excitement flashed unmistakably in their eyes.

After bringing up several more lumps of blackened flesh, Miss Hawthorne finally stopped breathing.

Her corpse began to rot almost at once. Within minutes, she had dissolved into a heap of flesh.

The worms living in her blood crawled out excitedly and began feeding on what remained.

Pressed flat against the wall, Evelyn and Dr. Hargreaves could only stare. Both looked utterly horrified.

The Hawthorne family’s only daughter had died in such a hideous fashion. It was a scandal.

Evelyn and Dr. Hargreaves were given hush money and a warning. As for the young men Miss Hawthorne had dragged home and tormented, Evelyn never saw them again. Perhaps they were truly set free. Or perhaps they were granted a different kind of freedom.

“Evelyn, in all my years as a doctor, I’ve never seen anyone die like that. I’ve never seen anything so revolting.”

The moment they left the villa, Dr. Hargreaves bent over and was violently sick several times. Even when he tried to walk, his steps were unsteady. His face was pale and his hands shook.

“When you get back, throw away everything you wore today. There may be bacteria on it.”

Evelyn nodded in agreement. Dr. Hargreaves then staggered off in another direction.

Back in 601, Evelyn took a bath, changed, and threw away the clothes she had worn.

When Adrian came back, she told him what had happened.

To her surprise, his face remained completely blank.

“Good.”

“Did you put something else on my sewing needles?”

Adrian raised his eyes and met hers.

For a long moment they simply looked at one another, each weighing the other up.

“Yes,” he said at last. “I added something.”

“Didn’t she deserve to die?”

Evelyn could not argue with that.

Miss Hawthorne had deserved death.

What Evelyn had not expected was that Adrian would prove even more unfathomable than she had imagined.

How many secrets he was still carrying, she had no way of knowing.

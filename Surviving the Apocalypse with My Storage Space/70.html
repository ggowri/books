Chapter 70 On the Road of Escape

"Any doctors? Is there a doctor?"

The convoy had barely moved ten minutes before it stopped again.

The men who collected protection fees were now storming through the line of vehicles with whips in hand, shouting for a doctor. One of them had blood smeared along his sleeve.

Evelyn thought of the girl from earlier.

She stared out the window without speaking.

The men went from car to car, demanding answers. After a while, they dragged a middle-aged man toward the front. Everyone waited in uneasy silence.

Inside the RV, the girl who had been washed clean was curled into a corner.

A blood-stained dagger trembled in her hand.

On the bed, the young man she had stabbed stared at her with cold fury.

"Mr. Hayden, the doctor's here."

The men shoved the doctor into the RV. Panting, the doctor glanced at the knife wound on the man's arm and visibly steadied himself.

"The wound needs stitches. He'll also need antibiotics to prevent infection. Where are the surgical tools?"

"You're the damn doctor. Don't you have tools?" one of the men barked.

The doctor looked helpless. "I'm a doctor, yes, but I don't carry surgical kits with me. Without suturing tools, I can only give hemostatic medicine and painkillers, clean the wound, and bandage it."

"Find another one. I don't believe there's only one doctor in a convoy this big. Throw him out."

With that order, the doctor was dragged away.

"Got some nerve, huh?" Hayden said lazily from the bed. "Dare to stab me."

His eyes flicked toward the girl.

"Find her family. Teach them a lesson. As for her, feed her to Midnight."

Midnight was the wolf dog kept in the off road vehicle behind the RV. Brutal and vicious. It ate only raw meat.

The girl shook uncontrollably as someone stepped forward to seize her. She slashed wildly with the dagger, trying to drive them back.

A gunshot cracked through the air.

She collapsed.

Blood spread beneath her.

The man who fired stepped forward, picked up the fallen dagger, then dug into the bullet hole in her chest and retrieved the round.

They dragged her to the vehicle in the back.

Inside the cage, the black dog caught the scent of blood and grew frenzied.

Back at their vehicle, one of the men pounded on the window.

He shoved a gun against Calvin's head.

"Any doctors in here?"

Everyone in the car shook their heads in perfect unison.

The man was not satisfied. He yanked open the door and grabbed Eddie.

"I'm not a doctor, I swear," Eddie protested.

He was kicked hard in the stomach. The man then stomped on the back of his hand, grinding his boot down viciously.

"Damn it."

After venting his anger, the man moved on to the next vehicle.

Evelyn jumped out and dragged Eddie back inside.

The air in the car felt suffocating.

No one spoke.

After a long while, Ray began to cry softly. He wiped the shoe print from Eddie's hand, but the wound beneath still bled.

"Shh. Don't cry. Don't make a sound." Calvin quickly covered Ray's mouth and shook his head firmly.

Evan sat beside them, silent tears sliding down his face.

Every one of them felt the weight pressing down.

Only when the men dragged several more people from the rear toward the front did the tension ease slightly.

"Eddie, does it hurt?" Ray whispered.

Eddie forced a grin through clenched teeth. "Doesn't hurt. I'm fine. Just need a little rest."

"Don't lie. It has to hurt."

Evelyn handed Eddie a painkiller.

He didn't refuse. He tossed it straight into his mouth.

"Let me say this again," Calvin said quietly but firmly. "Every one of us needs to shrink our presence as much as possible. Don't let the people up front remember us. Don't make eye contact. Don't get close."

His gaze swept across the group.

"We don't know how many weapons they have. But one thing's certain. They're ruthless. The only reason they tolerate us following behind them, aside from collecting protection fees, is probably another motive. Expanding their manpower once we reach the base."

Evelyn and Eddie both nodded.

"Drakepool Base might not be the safe haven we imagine," Calvin continued. "There could be deep waters inside. The people at the top fighting for power, and the ones at the bottom becoming expendable pawns."

Eddie gave a bitter smile. "We can't be the ones sticking our heads out. Staying alive is what matters. I'd rather take a beating than go up front and treat that guy."

The lesson of surviving by staying low etched itself deeper into their bones.

An hour later, the detained doctors were released. The issue must have been resolved.

The convoy resumed.

At six in the morning, they finally stopped to rest.

"Everyone out."

The command rang out.

People hesitated for a few seconds, then opened their doors and stepped down one by one.

"Our convoy has over a thousand vehicles," one of the men announced loudly. "That includes motorcycles and three wheelers. More than four thousand people. Those who've been walking behind us can continue following to Drakepool Base."

He paused.

"For those on foot, since you've been trailing our convoy from Elaris, Mr. Hayden is generous. No protection fees from you."

Murmurs rippled through the crowd.

"But remember this. Once we arrive at Drakepool Base, don't forget who cleared the path for you."

At that moment, a young man stepped forward, surrounded by bodyguards.

His clothes were spotless. His hair was slicked back with gel. His right hand was wrapped in bandages.

When he removed his sunglasses, sharp triangular eyes were revealed. His features were not ugly, yet something about them felt unsettling.

"We're already more than halfway to Drakepool Base," he said smoothly. "In a few days, we'll arrive. The disasters have made everyone suffer. I want to reach the base quickly, just like you."

He gave the crowd a slow look.

"However, space is limited right now. Entry conditions are tight. But don't worry. With me here, I guarantee every one of you will get inside."

With that, he turned and left under heavy guard.

"What does he mean by that?" someone muttered.

"What do you think? He might as well have said you need to report his name to get in. If we want entry, we'll probably have to work for him."

"I'm not doing that," another voice said angrily. "Did you forget last night? His men shoved a gun against your head asking if you were a doctor."

"So what's the alternative? Wherever you go, you need backing. If we don't attach ourselves to him, what if we're assigned to the worst, most broken area in the base?"

"Calvin called it," Eddie said quietly, his mood sinking. "Even under disaster, people are still playing power games. Those of us at the bottom, without power or status, we're always stepping stones."

"I thought Drakepool Base had military protection?" someone asked.

"Can we even trust the authorities anymore?"

Evelyn rubbed her temples.

Compared to the others, she was much calmer.

In her previous life, something even more absurd had happened. There had been a group that specialized in hunting people with heterochromia. It was said those with mismatched eyes possessed mysterious abilities. Their blood and flesh were rumored to be as precious as immortality medicine.

In the fourth year of the last apocalypse, Evelyn had heard that the base captured one such person.

As for what happened to that individual, it did not need to be said.

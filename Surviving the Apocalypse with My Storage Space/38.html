Chapter 38 Heatwave, Snake Infestation: Part 2

After locking eyes with it for several minutes, Evelyn was the first to give in. She took out a bottle of medicated oil and placed it near the window gap, gently fanning it twice. The sharp, distinctive scent drifted outside. The water snake immediately began to writhe. Its tail slapped against the glass with loud smacks as it hissed irritably, the sound cutting sharply through the night. Two minutes later, it finally slid off the windowsill.

There were many things that could repel snakes. Realgar, sulfur powder, snake repellent powder, medicated oil, garlic.

Even though she had driven it away, Evelyn was still shaken. She grabbed a flashlight and carefully checked every corner of her apartment. Only after confirming there were no gaps did she finally relax.

Water snakes were mildly venomous and not especially dangerous to humans. But if it were a highly toxic and aggressive species, without antivenom, a single bite would almost certainly be fatal.

Highly venomous snakes included sea snakes, sharp nosed vipers, kraits, bamboo pit vipers, cobras, and short tailed vipers. These were the main venomous species within the country. But with the climate changing so drastically, mutations in snake species were not impossible.

If it were any other kind of creature, Evelyn might still be willing to go out and fight it to the death. But when it came to snakes, avoidance was her only strategy.

Later that night, several more snakes crawled up onto the outside of her balcony glass. At first, Evelyn diligently drove them off with medicated oil. Eventually, she completely gave up. They could not get inside anyway. If they wanted to cling there, let them.

The floodwaters stirred faintly. One snake after another swam out of the water and slithered into the building. Catching the scent of humans, their tails lifted, heads raised high, pupils narrowing as crimson tongues flicked in and out.

That night, Evelyn only slept two or three hours. When she pulled back the curtain again and saw several snakes coiled outside her window, she felt nothing at all.

The others in the building were not so lucky.

Many apartments had left their balconies and windows open. Some people reached out in their sleep and touched something cold and slick. When they opened their eyes, a thick, slippery snake was lying beside them, head raised, staring back.

Others were bitten in their sleep and never woke up again.

Of course, there were also fearless types like Calvin, who actually wanted to catch snakes and eat them.

Evelyn finished breakfast and was just about to go back to sleep when violent noises erupted upstairs, downstairs, and right outside her door. Overnight, it felt like the entire complex, even all of Elaris, had turned into a massive snake den.

The water level outside had dropped by about twenty centimeters. And Evelyn made another disturbing discovery. All the corpses that had been floating in the water were gone. If they had not sunk, there was only one possibility.

They were inside the snakes.

Downstairs, Claire and Natalie each held a kitchen knife, staring in terror at the snakes coiled on the windowsill. The night before, Margaret had complained that the room felt stuffy and opened the window on her own. When Claire woke up that morning, she found five or six snakes inside the apartment, with three more hanging from the window.

Natalie was shaking violently, clutching the knife and hiding behind Claire.

As for Margaret, she had already fainted from fear.

"Ms. Brooks, what do we do? Why are there so many snakes? I'm scared. I can't kill snakes."

Claire was barely holding it together herself, but she could not retreat. She had to protect this place. She swung the kitchen knife wildly, chopping a snake that lunged at her clean in half. Warm blood sprayed across her face. Without thinking, she licked her lips. The stench hit her instantly, and she bent over and vomited.

After dealing with the snakes on the windowsill, Claire rushed to shut the window, then carefully checked the balcony. Only after confirming there were no gaps or cracks did she return to the living room and stare at Natalie with a grim expression.

"Natalie, I told you yesterday not to open doors or windows. Why didn't you listen? And when those snakes lunged, why did you push me in front of you?"

Natalie bit her lip and stayed silent. Claire took a deep breath, her expression turning cold.

"When the snakes are gone, you'll move out."

"Ms. Brooks, I'm sorry. I was really terrified just now. I won't do it again. My mom and I are truly grateful for you taking us in all this time. We'll move out. Because of me, Dr. Foster seems distant toward you now. I can tell she doesn't like me very much. I don't even know why. We've never had a conflict. I'm sorry. I feel like I've caused you so much trouble."

"This has nothing to do with Evelyn," Claire said flatly. "She's always taken good care of me. I don't care what you're thinking, but don't say anything bad about her in front of me again. I don't like hearing it. When your mom wakes up, tell her the tent is still available for you to borrow. These snakes should all be edible. I'll clean them up. You wipe the blood off the floor."

"Ms. Brooks, you misunderstood me. I didn't mean anything like that."

Claire waved her hand. "Enough. Stop talking and get to work."

Natalie watched Claire's back, lips pressed tight, and said nothing more. At that moment, Margaret slowly regained consciousness.

"Natalie… the snakes. Are they all dead?"

Natalie's frustration exploded. She lowered her voice and snapped, "Mom, can you stop dragging me down all the time? I'm taking care of you and looking for food. And you? You faint the moment you see a snake. I still have to protect you. Do you even understand our situation? We're living off someone else's kindness. This isn't our home."

Margaret listened in silence for a long time. When Natalie finally vented enough, Margaret grabbed her hand and spoke slowly, word by word.

"Then we'll make this our home."

Natalie stared at her in shock. Margaret looked toward Claire, who was busy in the kitchen, and repeated herself.

"If we make this our home, no one will be able to drive us out."

*****

After an elderly resident on the eleventh floor was dragged away by a swarm of snakes, all that remained was a floor soaked in blood. Suppressed sobbing echoed outside the door. Evelyn took out red bricks and cement from her storage space, planning to seal off the balcony with a brick wall. The glass was thick, but she no longer trusted it.

She spent the entire day watching documentaries about snakes. After learning about the attack power of giant pythons, she knew she could no longer leave her safety in the hands of a single pane of glass.

She saved all the water from washing up. It turned out to be useful for mixing cement. Besides the balcony, the bedroom windows also needed to be sealed. Leaving just one window was enough. Evelyn poured cement into a metal basin, added fine sand, then mixed in water. It could not be too thin, or the bricks would not hold.

Putting on an apron and gloves, Evelyn got to work.

As a beginner, the wall she built was not pretty. It was slightly crooked, even misshapen. Still, Evelyn was satisfied. After letting it dry for two days, once the cement fully set, this home would finally be a real fortress.

While Evelyn was busy building walls inside her apartment, everyone else in the building was busy fighting snakes.

Chapter 113 Adrian at His Most Dangerous

Watching the swift, rough efficiency with which he plucked out the contact lens, Evelyn nearly laughed.

“What are we having tonight?”

“Braised pork ribs, red-cooked pork, and boiled greens. Sound good?”

Adrian nodded. “That sounds excellent.”

The staff in the Surgical Unit were familiar to Evelyn by now. There was Dr. Julian, forty-five, once an intern under her father. The other two doctors had both come from Seabourne, Dr. Warren Reid, thirty-seven, and Dr. Lionel Shaw, thirty-eight. The nurse was Daisy Yates, twenty-five.

The patients were as numerous as ever today. By the afternoon, they brought in a middle-aged man whose left forearm had been torn clean off by a wolf.

Another man who looked rather like him stood beside the bed, weeping the whole time. “Doctor, please save my brother. He can’t die. His wife’s already gone, and his daughter’s only seven.”

Dr. Hargreaves called Evelyn over, and the two of them treated the wound together. The severed arm was gone, so all they could do was clean the injury and dress it properly.

“Stand back a little. Don’t crowd round here.”

The man could not take in a word of it. His hands and legs were shaking uncontrollably, while the injured man on the bed had already fallen unconscious.

Evelyn cut away his clothing with a pair of scissors and began cleaning the wound.

The arm had been ripped off by the wolf’s jaws. Shreds of flesh still hung from it.

Evelyn worked without the slightest change of expression. Dr. Hargreaves saw that she could manage perfectly well on her own and went off to deal with other patients.

“Doctor, save him. It’s all my fault. If he hadn’t tried to save me, he wouldn’t have been bitten. What am I supposed to do? How am I meant to explain this to Beatrice? Brother, please wake up.”

Still crying and shouting, he lurched forward as though to grab at the injured man.

Evelyn kicked him away without hesitation.

“Shut up. If it’s all your fault, what are you crying for? Why not take responsibility instead?”

The man stared at her in stunned silence, one hand pressed to his chest. Everyone else nearby looked at her with a kind of horrified awe. A flicker of amusement crossed Dr. Hargreaves’ eyes. Through Evelyn, he could almost see his old mentor again, sternly telling him off in exactly the same way.

The injured man’s name was Ryan Hayes. He had once been an executive at a publicly listed company. After the apocalypse came, one family member after another had died until only he, his younger brother, and his daughter were left.

At the base, the points he earned with the hunting crews had at least been enough to keep them fed and clothed. Then this accident had taken one of his hands.

When he woke, the very first thing he asked Evelyn was whether his brother was all right. Once he realised he had lost an arm, he began wiping at his eyes in secret, terrified he would no longer be able to raise his daughter.

There were countless people like him.

They had lost their homes, their families, everything. And even when they fought desperately just to stay alive, fate showed no mercy to those already suffering. If anything, it ground them down harder.

That was the true horror of natural disaster. You could never fight it and win.

Even after work, the sorrowful cries in the treatment room stayed with Evelyn. The despair in those eyes, the exhaustion on those faces, all of it lingered in her mind.

Dr. Hargreaves told her that the Medical Division worked six days on and one day off, and said she could take tomorrow as a rest day if she liked. Evelyn declined.

By the time she returned to the apartment building, she had already put herself back in order. Never bring work home with you. It was something her parents had taught her when she was little.

She opened the door and found Adrian already back. Evelyn immediately sensed that something was wrong.

Had he failed to bring back any prey?

Or had the others in his team bullied him, shut him out?

“What’s with the face?”

Adrian looked up at her, hesitating as though unsure whether to speak.

“If you’re going to say something, say it. Don’t dawdle.”

“I had a woman come earlier with two men. She told me to hand over the two little ones. I threw them downstairs.”

Evelyn went silent.

She gave a small cough. “When you say downstairs, are they still alive?”

Adrian shook his head, and Evelyn’s heart jumped.

“I don’t know. No one saw me throw them, anyway.”

“I didn’t see anyone downstairs when I came back. Where exactly did you throw them?”

Adrian pointed at the window.

Evelyn opened it and looked down. In the dented section of the snowbank below lay three figures, motionless, their condition impossible to tell.

“If no one finds them tonight, I’ll go down and put them in the storage space, then dump them outside. I expect they’re already dead.”

Adrian looked perfectly calm. “Works rather well.”

“Try not to be so impulsive. There are cameras everywhere here. And honestly, who goes round killing people in broad daylight? Have you lost your mind?” Evelyn rubbed at her forehead, then paused. “Though I do know who that woman was. Back at Silvermere River, she took a fancy to Pip and tried to snatch him. Clearly she never gave up. She somehow even found out where we live. Those two men looked like bodyguards. There’s no way she hired them herself.”

Adrian looked at her darkly. “They said they were acting on Miss Hawthorne’s orders. I’ll kill Miss Hawthorne. You needn’t worry.”

That only made Evelyn’s headache worse. “Did you say Miss Hawthorne?”

“Yes.”

“I heard this Miss Hawthorne only went around snatching handsome men. What on earth is she doing trying to steal animals as well? There’s something seriously wrong with her.”

Evelyn glanced at the time. She needed to get dinner started. Calvin and the others would be here soon.

“Do not do anything rash. I’m guessing this Miss Hawthorne is the darling daughter of the Hawthorne family. A place like that will be guarded like a fortress. If you go there, you’ll just be throwing your life away.”

Adrian looked as though he wanted to argue, but Evelyn shot him a glare and he fell silent.

“Right, enough of that. We’re cooking. I’ll take the ingredients out, and you wash the vegetables.”

Adrian set Pip down and obediently fetched water to wash the greens. By the time Evelyn had started the rice and put the ribs on to stew, Eddie arrived with Calvin and the others.

Only a few months had passed, yet every one of them had changed. Calvin looked much older. Wendy’s hair had grown long. Ray and Evan had both shot up and now looked every bit like proper young men.

“Evelyn, I missed you so much.”

Ray and Evan both rushed forward and threw their arms round her. Wendy ran over too and wrapped herself round Evelyn’s leg.

“All right, all right. I missed you too. No crying now.”

She crouched and looked at Wendy. “Did you miss me too?”

“Yes.”

The answer startled Evelyn so much she stared.

“Wendy started talking after the earthquake,” Calvin explained softly from the side. “Then when you disappeared, she cried for days and kept saying she wanted to go and find you.”

His eyes were red too, and one look at all of them was enough for Evelyn to know they must have suffered terribly before making it to the base.

Eddie was wiping at his eyes in the back as well. Evelyn then saw that they had even brought gifts for her. Wendy had brought her a ribbon hairband with a butterfly on it.

Her nose stung suddenly. She quickly beckoned everyone inside to sit down.

“Let me introduce you properly. This is Adrian. Adrian, these are my people. Calvin, his daughter Wendy, Ray, Evan, and Eddie, my brother in all but blood.”

“Hello,” Adrian said.

“Hello, hello. Just call me Calvin,” he replied warmly. “Since you’re Evelyn’s boyfriend, that makes you family. Wendy, call him brother-in-law.”

Evelyn nearly sprayed out the mouthful of water she had just taken. This was getting more ridiculous by the minute.

“No, no. Wendy, just call him Adrian. Ray, Evan, same for you, just call him Adrian. He’s in the hunting crews too, so you lot can get to know each other.”

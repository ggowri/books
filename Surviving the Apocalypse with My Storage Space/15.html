Chapter 15 Rainstorm, Venom Moths: Part 11

"Then I guess I'll have to cut off your filthy hand first."

The moment Evelyn opened the door, her knife flashed.

With one clean swing, Tyler's right hand—still gripping a blade—was severed clean at the wrist and dropped to the floor.

A bloodcurdling scream tore through the hallway. Doors flew open as curious neighbors, holding torches, came out to see what was going on. But the second they caught sight of the long, gleaming military knife in Evelyn's hand, still dripping with blood, they gasped and nearly fell back in shock.

No one dared linger.

One by one, they all slammed their doors shut again—except for Calvin, who stood still, and the rest of the Wren family, who had just arrived at the scene.

"Aaaaagh, it hurts! My hand! My hand!" Tyler howled, writhing on the floor. "Evelyn, you crazy b— I'm gonna kill you! You'll pay for this!"

Evelyn raised the tip of her knife and aimed it at his face.

He froze, trembling violently, and shut his mouth on the spot.

"Should I take the left one too?" she asked coldly.

"No, no, please don't!" Maya cried, throwing herself between them. "Evelyn, please, don't hurt my brother! I'm begging you! We only came to ask for shelter—why would you chop off his hand?! That's a crime! You'll go to jail for this!"

She turned desperately to Calvin, tears streaming down her face.

"Calvin! You're a cop, aren't you? Help him! Arrest her! She's lost her mind! She used a knife—she cut someone's hand off!"

Calvin's lips pressed into a thin line. His eyes met Evelyn's expressionless face. At that moment, he remembered what his wife had told him.

"Evie is smart. But she's got a temper. As long as you don't provoke her, she's fine. But if you do... she's not the kind of person you want to mess with."

"I'm not a cop anymore," he said simply.

And just like that, he made it clear—he wasn't getting involved.

Maya's face twisted in hatred as she turned away from him. She knelt to retrieve her brother's hand, only to realize that it was too late. The venomous moths had already devoured it completely.

The sight made her stomach turn. She screamed, gagging in horror.

Tyler's parents lunged toward Evelyn, their faces red with fury. But the moment she raised her bloodied knife again, they froze in place, fear halting them mid-step.

Evelyn listened to their curses and sobbing, then slowly stepped forward until she stood directly across from Tyler.

He was pale, sweat soaking his clothes, his face twisted in pain. Seeing her approach, he whimpered and hid behind his parents.

"I don't take people in," Evelyn said, her voice low and cold. "If you can't understand that, the next thing I slice off will be your ears."

With one last glance, she turned around, stepped back into her apartment, and slammed the door shut.

The cries and shouting continued outside.

Evelyn calmly wiped her knife clean. After a short rest, she pulled out her inflatable boat.

The rain had stopped. It was time to head out.

She planned to scavenge for supplies. If she waited any longer, there'd be nothing left.

She stuck more than ten heating patches all over her thermals, including the soles of her feet, then put on her fleece-lined waterproof coat. Over that, she wore two full-body protective suits.

Goggles. Mask. Everything sealed tight.

She tied a rope around her waist to cinch the suit down for better movement, then strapped the military knife to the back of her belt.

Grabbing the folded inflatable boat in her arms, Evelyn left her home.

After locking the door, she sprayed a special blend of poison she had mixed herself across the entrance.

When she reached the stairwell on the fourth floor, she set the boat down on the water, climbed in, and started the motor.

Under cover of darkness, she left Willow Heights.

The hum of moth wings filled her ears.

Whirr… Whirr… Whirr…

The floodwaters were littered with floating debris—everything from trash thrown out of upper floors, to dead cats, dogs, rats... and human corpses.

In the wake of a disaster like this, death didn't just mean the loss of life. It meant losing the last scrap of dignity, too.

Evelyn steered aimlessly through the flooded city.

Yes, she was here to collect supplies.

But part of her also just wanted to see what the world had become.

After half an hour of drifting through the flooded city, Evelyn still hadn't seen a single other person.

Eventually, she stopped in front of one of Elaris's landmark buildings. Towering high above the drowned city, it stood as a hollow monument to what was. Now, with the streets swallowed by floodwater, its only remaining value was its height.

Evelyn wasn't interested in that building, though. What she had her eye on was the pharmaceutical company tower right next to it.

Finding a small window she could climb through, she stashed the inflatable boat back into her storage space, then scaled up to the third floor. She slipped through a narrow ledge and dropped inside.

Only after she landed did she realize that she was in the men's restroom.

This company had 58 floors and was one of the most reputable pharmaceutical firms in Elaris. Evelyn had even bought shares in it once and made a couple thousand dollars from it.

She stepped quietly out of the restroom, holding her military knife at the ready.

The building was swarming with venomous moths—inside the offices, the break rooms, even on the expensive computers. Their green, bulbous eyes followed her movements. Maybe it was the lingering scent of insecticide on her clothes, but they didn't dare come too close. They hovered near her ears instead, buzzing incessantly.

Evelyn didn't overlook anything. Clean desks, water dispensers, coffee machines, sofas, boxes of tissues, bottles of hand soap—if it was usable, she stored it away. Once the water level rose again, all of it would be ruined by sewage.

The lower levels were all business departments. The labs and production areas were located on the higher floors, but she wasn't in a rush. She had plenty of time and planned to work her way up slowly.

Her eyes lit up when she spotted a full bookshelf in one of the offices.

Internal medical texts from a pharmaceutical company? That was a treasure trove.

She cleared off the moths, then took the entire bookshelf, books and all, into her storage.

The floors were blanketed with moths. Every step she took crushed them with a wet squelch.

On the eighth floor, she found an office decorated with a jade cabbage and a golden horse statue.

Tacky.

She rolled her eyes at the gaudy taste of what was clearly a nouveau riche boss, but her hands moved fast, storing everything regardless.

In the back room, there was a small bedroom, a bathroom, and two wardrobes. One held men's suits. The other was filled with women's dresses and high heels. Judging by the styles, Evelyn guessed they belonged to two different people.

Under the men's wardrobe, she found a safe.

It required a fingerprint to open, which immediately killed her interest. Still, she stored it away. Who knew? Maybe it held a stash of gold and jewelry.

The office on the thirteenth floor belonged to someone seriously superstitious. Maybe they hated the number thirteen. The place was covered in good-luck charms and coin strings tied with red cord.

The windowsills were lined with nine dying fortune trees. All dead now.

She found nothing useful here.

In the next office, the decorations were stylish and personalized, clearly curated by a woman in her mid-thirties—likely someone deeply in love.

Sure enough, in the private room, Evelyn stumbled upon three entire boxes of adult items. Designer sets. Full collections. Along with all kinds of lingerie and... accessories.

Evelyn let out a low whistle and casually pocketed the power bank sitting on the nightstand.

On the outer desk, she noticed a full purple clay tea set. She couldn't tell whether it was valuable, but in the nearby cabinet, she found a wide selection of teas—rows and rows of them, all well preserved.

She took those.

Drinking tea? That was something she could relate to.

Time slipped by as Evelyn slowly and methodically scavenged her way upward, collecting supplies with quiet efficiency.

What she didn't know was that, far above her, hidden inside one of the offices at the top of the building, a group of people were still desperately hiding inside.

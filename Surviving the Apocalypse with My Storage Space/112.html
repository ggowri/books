Chapter 112 Adrian Never Gets Full

“Clara, do they sell the vegetables grown in the base to ordinary residents?”

“They do. There’s a villa behind the Settlement Market that specialises in selling vegetables, fruit, rice, flour, oil, salt, alcohol, soft drinks, the lot. But most of the people who shop there are researchers and the leadership class.”

She lowered her voice slightly, as if passing on valuable insider knowledge.

“But listen to me, it’s outrageously expensive. I really wouldn’t recommend buying anything there. A single cabbage can cost thirty points.”

“Is it officially run?”

Clara nodded, and Evelyn understood at once. No wonder the Settlement Market itself did not stock vegetables or grain. The authorities had kept that business firmly in their own hands.

She was planning to invite Calvin and the others round for a meal. If she used shopping as an excuse, she could take things out of her storage space without raising suspicion. She was no fool, and certainly not about to waste points like that.

When she returned to the apartment building, she spotted Adrian straightaway. Even wrapped up from head to toe, he still stood out in a crowd.

“Evelyn, your boyfriend must be very handsome, mustn’t he?”

Evelyn deeply regretted ever pretending to be a couple with Adrian. She was hardly thin-skinned, but being reminded of it every five minutes still made her feel awkward.

“He’s all right, I suppose.”

“I’m telling you now, there was this really handsome boy before, and Miss Hawthorne took a fancy to him and had him brought straight to her. You’d better keep an eye on yours.”

Miss Hawthorne?

Evelyn gave a dry little laugh. Who exactly was going to take Adrian anywhere? With that savage temperament of his, anyone trying something improper would probably end up with a snapped neck.

“Adrian.”

The moment she reached him, he looked at her with quiet reproach. Evelyn understood instantly. Either he was hungry, or he was sulking over the base’s food again.

“How was your day?”

“Fine enough. Yours?”

“I’m exhausted. Too many patients. We couldn’t keep up.”

“You should eat more later,” he said with complete seriousness.

Evelyn burst out laughing. “Is that meant to be comforting?”

“Yes.”

After parting ways with Clara, the two of them went straight back to 601. Evelyn checked on Pip and Lark first. Thankfully, both little creatures were lively enough and had not destroyed the place.

She washed her hands, took off her coat and hat, then brought out a grill plate, along with her miraculous little smoke extractor.

“We’re having grilled meat tonight.”

Adrian’s eyes lit up at once. He sat down immediately, waiting to be fed.

“Evelyn, I brought down a wild boar by myself today. Seven hundred points, all mine. I’ll give them to you.”

Evelyn let out a little huff. “What are you showing off for? I made nearly a hundred today myself. I did two operations.”

Adrian carried on as if he had not heard that at all. “If I give you all my points every day, can I have grilled meat every day too?”

Evelyn shot him a sidelong glance. “Done.”

Points could be used to buy supplies as well, and Evelyn decided she would do one large shop every week and spend them promptly. No point hoarding a pile of points only to be forced to leave one day with nothing useful bought. That would be a complete loss.

“Oh, by the way, I’m planning to invite a few friends over for dinner. Do you want to join us?”

Adrian looked at her darkly. “Were you planning not to feed me?”

That was not what she meant at all. Yesterday, when they had met Eddie, Adrian had not said a single word. He had barely even acknowledged him. Anyone seeing it would have assumed he was painfully shy.

“I just thought you might feel awkward.”

Adrian shook his head. “I want to eat.”

Food was the only thing in his mind. Why would he ever feel awkward? Evelyn decided she had simply overthought it.

“Fine. Once Eddie comes to let me know, I’ll tell you.”

She laid out several plates of meat, beef, lamb, and black bear, without sparing a thought for whether eating them all together might lead to nosebleeds or some other nonsense. Satisfaction came first.

“How many people are in your team?”

“Eight.”

Evelyn looked surprised. “I thought the prey was supposed to be divided equally among the whole team.”

Adrian looked perfectly calm. “They were hopeless. Not nearly as capable as you. I had to work alone.”

Thank you so much for the compliment, Evelyn thought, rolling her eyes.

Once they had eaten their fill, Adrian washed up while Evelyn bathed Pip and Lark. The two little things had become quite grimy during the journey, and with everything she had been busy with yesterday, she had forgotten all about it.

Since entering the base, Pip and Lark had been unexpectedly well-behaved. Perhaps they could sense the sheer number of humans around them, and it made them uneasy.

After washing the pots and dishes, Adrian went back to 602. Evelyn freshened up and got into bed. She noticed the temperature had risen to minus thirty. It seemed the worst of the cold would soon be over.

The next morning, the moment Evelyn arrived at the Medical Division, she saw Eddie. He had finally changed into a clean set of clothes, and he looked in decent spirits too.

“Where were you yesterday? I didn’t see you at all.”

Eddie sighed. “I got sent out with one of the hunting crews again. Their jobs are dangerous, and people get bitten by prey all the time. Without a doctor there, some of them would die on the spot. So the base assigns medics to go out with them.”

“I thought you said you were with the fishing team.”

Eddie grinned. “I wear several hats. When the fishing team’s resting, I go with the other hunting crews. When the fishing team goes out, they’re away for days, crossing mountains and all sorts. It’s not as if they can head out every single day.”

That was true enough. Once the vehicles could go no farther, the rest had to be done on foot.

“Did you tell Calvin and the others?”

Eddie nodded. “I did. They’re all free tomorrow.”

“Good. Tell them to come to mine for dinner tomorrow evening.”

“How are you even going to cook? You haven’t got a kitchen, or proper cookware, and you’ll have to buy things too. It’s too much trouble.” Eddie looked thoroughly unconvinced. “Honestly, don’t bother. Work at the base is exhausting enough. It’s enough just to see each other.”

“I’ve got my ways. Stop worrying. Tell them all to come round after work tomorrow.”

Eddie could not persuade her otherwise, so he gave up and let her have her way. Evelyn changed into her protective gear and began another day’s work.

After finishing in the afternoon, she took a detour to the villa behind the Settlement Market. A very long queue had already formed outside. People stood there shivering with cold, clutching vegetable baskets as they waited to buy food.

The selection was surprisingly broad. There were all kinds of meat, fish, prawns and crab. The vegetables included greens, aubergines, tomatoes, cucumbers, potatoes, tofu, chillies and more, mostly crops that were easier to grow. There were greenhouses in the Underground Quarter, and evidently the harvest had been excellent.

Evelyn now had a rough sense of the market. Most of what she had in storage could be brought out without seeming suspicious. Still, there was no need to make the meal too lavish. Otherwise, Calvin and the others might feel burdened by it.

When Evelyn returned to the sixth floor, she found Adrian standing outside her door.

“What are you doing here? Why didn’t you go back to your own flat?”

Adrian pressed his lips together. “I saw you coming back. I only waited two minutes.”

Evelyn handed him a spare key to her flat. There was nothing particularly valuable inside anyway. Adrian had given her all his points, and she trusted him enough.

“Sometimes I genuinely question your intelligence.”

She opened the door and shot him an exasperated look.

The moment Adrian stepped inside, he hooked a finger under one of his coloured contact lenses and flicked it out.

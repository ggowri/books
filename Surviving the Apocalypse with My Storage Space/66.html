Chapter 66 Departure

After three or four days of rest, the refugee groups began leaving in waves.

But the larger convoy still showed no sign of moving. Calvin and Eddie went out again to investigate, yet they came back empty handed.

Evelyn had already stored everything worth taking into her space, including the bedroom and bathroom doors.

In early October, the temperature suddenly dropped overnight. It fell from a brutal fifty five degrees Celsius to around forty.

For a moment, it almost felt like the apocalypse might be ending.

But Evelyn knew better.

When something was abnormal, it usually meant something worse was coming.

After the temperature drop, more refugees began setting out for Drakepool Base. Their luggage was already packed and loaded into their vehicles. Most of it was tied to the roofs.

Their vehicle was a nine seater. Without luggage, six people inside would still feel spacious. Now, with supplies crammed in, two people had to squeeze into a single seat. Three spare tires were strapped to the rear.

Evelyn could not help worrying about whether the vehicle could handle the weight.

"Such a shame," Eddie murmured on the rooftop, staring at the distant blaze. "Decades of work. Generations of effort. Gone just like that."

Every building touched by the fire had been reduced to a blackened shell.

Flames towered into the sky. Half of Elaris was stained red. It was the first time they had seen the scale of the inferno so clearly.

It felt like finally seeing the true face of this catastrophe.

"When I was little, Elaris was full of low houses," Calvin said quietly, leaning against the railing. "There were bicycles everywhere. Hardly any trees. Dust filled the air. My parents earned two hundred dollars a month combined."

He stared into the distance.

"Thirty years later, it became a first tier city. Beautiful landscaping. Skyscrapers rising everywhere. Their retirement pensions added up to twenty thousand a month. Tens of millions of people worked for thirty years. One disaster, and it's all gone. The people are gone. The towers are gone."

"The old nine to five life wasn't so bad," Eddie said with a sigh. "Even that annoying boss doesn't seem so annoying anymore. If I had another chance, I wouldn't turn down a rich woman's affection."

Everyone turned to look at him.

He shrugged. "Living off your looks is a skill too. Guess I realized it too late."

He glanced at Evelyn, curiosity in his eyes. "What about you? Any regrets? Not falling in love? Not doing something wild and rebellious?"

"No," she replied simply.

Eddie clicked his tongue. "What a boring life."

"Let's each make a wish," he said suddenly. "I'll start. I wish this damn nightmare would end soon."

"I hope Wendy lives a healthy, peaceful life," Calvin said softly.

"I hope we can find my mom and dad."

"I hope we won't run into any more danger."

"Will we ever come back?"

"Yes," someone answered firmly. "As long as we're alive, we'll come back."

*****

On the third day of cooling temperatures, after careful discussion, they decided to leave on October 10th.

The vehicle had undergone multiple inspections and maintenance checks. It was in perfect condition.

Several survivors from Block C had already left. Only the people in Block E showed no movement.

Evelyn packed a few sets of clothes into her backpack and fastened the straps tightly across her abdomen. She stored her crossbow and military knife inside her space to avoid drawing unwanted attention.

They gathered on the first floor.

Calvin took the driver's seat. The rest squeezed tightly into the vehicle. When he said, "Let's go," the engine rumbled to life, and the car slowly rolled out of Willow Heights.

The people from Block E saw them leaving and hurriedly packed up to follow.

"Where's the convoy now?" Calvin asked.

"By Mercer City," Eddie replied. He had snuck over there several times to scout. "The better vehicles are parked in front. Motorcycles and three wheelers are at the back."

"We'll blend in when we get there. If anyone asks, we stick to the same story. We're refugees from Stonehaven City. Not locals from Elaris."

"Right. And smear more dirt on your faces. Especially you, Evelyn."

Evelyn pulled out a small mirror and glanced at herself. Her face was already filthy. Her neck and ears were darkened with grime. Her hair was greasy and tangled.

She looked worse than a beggar who had lived on the streets for a decade.

"Remember," Calvin muttered as he drove, nerves showing through his constant reminders, "all six of us are men. That includes Wendy and Evelyn."

An hour later, they reached Mercer City.

One entire street was packed with vehicles and ragged refugees. Evelyn lifted the binoculars and studied the expensive cars lined up at the very front.

A few men approached their vehicle. The leader knocked on the window, signaling for them to get out.

Evelyn wrapped her filthy, foul smelling scarf tightly around her head and hunched her shoulders, making herself look malnourished and exhausted.

"How many?" the man asked.

"Six."

He held a notebook, writing down the license plate number and the headcount. His gaze swept across them before settling on Calvin.

"If you want to follow our convoy, you pay protection. Not much. Half a kilo of grain per person. Cigarettes and liquor work too. One pack or one bottle."

Calvin had anticipated this.

But instead of handing it over immediately, he hesitated, playing along. Only when the man's patience wore thin did he start bargaining.

The man was not the bargaining type. Several others stood behind him, gripping steel pipes, ready to swing.

"My friend, we don't have much grain. Can you cut us some slack?"

"That's the rule here. Everyone pays. You think you're special?" the man snapped. "Six of you is only three kilos. Not much. One minute. If you don't pay, get lost."

Reluctantly, Calvin dragged a bag out of the vehicle.

The man leaned over to check. Inside were torn, dirty clothes, with a small sack of grain hidden underneath. He grabbed it, weighed it in his hand, then tossed it to someone behind him.

"What's on the roof?"

"Just hoes and shovels."

The man opened one of the roof bags. Seeing nothing but tools inside, he did not continue searching.

"All men?"

"All men."

He stopped in front of Evelyn. The tip of his pen poked her shoulder. She quietly stepped back and lowered her head further.

"Open your backpack."

She unzipped it. Inside were nothing but grimy clothes. The man snorted and moved on to the next vehicle.

They climbed back into their car in silence.

At noon, the convoy finally stirred. The vehicles at the front began starting their engines.

"Following a bunch of bandits like this… are we really safe?" Eddie asked, worry written all over his face.

"Three kilos of grain to have them clear the way for us," Calvin replied calmly. "Worth it."

Cars rolled forward one after another. A crowd of people on foot trailed behind.

What awaited them on this journey was unknown.

But at that moment, they all felt the same thing.

Confusion. Fear. Uncertainty.

Chapter 43 Heatwave, Snake Infestation: Part 7

The extreme heat was clearly making the giant python miserable. It thrashed its tail restlessly, sending snake carcasses in the floodwater flying. Evelyn held her breath and observed it carefully. When she saw it open its mouth and flick out a black tongue, a thought formed in her mind.

It was overheating.

Under the relentless heat, even the floodwater had begun to warm. That was why it had broken the surface, trying to find a new place to coil. But the scorching walls made it impossible to move far. Watching it swim back and forth, occasionally slapping the water with its tail, Evelyn came up with a way to deal with it.

She still had some of the poison she had brewed earlier. Evelyn took out two freshly killed chickens and smeared the potent toxin all over their surfaces and inside their cavities. Her plan was simple. She would use the smell of meat to draw the python's attention, then toss the chickens into its mouth the moment it opened wide.

It was risky.

But she estimated her chances at seventy percent.

She could have used open flames to heat a gas canister and thrown it into the python's mouth, blowing it apart from the inside. But a gas explosion was uncontrollable. If it damaged the building, the consequences would be catastrophic.

The python continued wreaking havoc. Its tail swept across the building opposite. The railings and windows on the fifth floor shattered instantly. The halo around the sun had turned blindingly white, impossible to look at directly. Evelyn put on gloves, positioned the crossbow to her right, and pushed the window open about twenty centimeters.

She picked up one of the chickens and shook it lightly. The scent of blood drifted outward.

The python reacted immediately.

At this moment, the chicken was only bait. Evelyn herself was the most dangerous living target.

She took out a whistle and blew hard. The piercing sound made the python sway its head slightly as it tried to pinpoint her position.

As it crawled closer, Evelyn could clearly see its coarse scales and patches of half shed skin. She swiftly threw the first chicken straight toward its head. The python instantly opened its mouth. At the same time, Evelyn threw the second chicken.

It moved fast. Violently.

She even caught a glimpse of the four rows of teeth inside its mouth.

Calmly, Evelyn took out a lighter and an alcohol block, ignited it, and threw it out. Her aim was not perfect the first time. It struck the python's head and dropped into the water, bursting into flames immediately.

The fire enraged the python.

After swallowing the two poisoned chickens, it began whipping its tail wildly.

Evelyn did not hesitate. She lit another alcohol block and threw it again. Just as the python opened its mouth, the burning block fell straight inside.

A faint smile curved Evelyn's lips.

She picked up the crossbow.

This time, she aimed for the seven inch point.

A snake's seven inch point lay where the neck met the body. That was where its heart was. One clean hit there, combined with the poison and the burning alcohol, and it would be dead within ten minutes.

The python thrashed violently. The burning alcohol block inside its body would not explode, but it would continue to burn. It was already being tortured by the searing pain within.

Evelyn pressed her advantage and fired.

Today, luck was not on her side.

The first bolt went wide, scraping across the surface of its scales and leaving only a shallow mark.

Don't rush. Stay steady.

Cold sweat broke out across Evelyn's palms. The blazing sun made her dizzy. Gritting her teeth, she fired a second bolt.

This time, it hit.

The arrow sank deep into the seven inch point. The giant python let out a terrifying hiss, its mouth flying open as it thrashed its tail with all its remaining strength. In the end, it collapsed amid violent convulsions, crashing into the floodwater and piles of snake corpses. Its massive body sent smaller snakes and water splashing everywhere.

Evelyn's hands trembled as she held the crossbow. She stood by the window, silently watching the python sink to the bottom.

Worried that more giant pythons might appear, Evelyn decided to leave the brick wall in place for now. Three hours later, large patches of the python's skin began to rot. Evelyn kept watching through the binoculars. When the stench of decay spread, red headed blowflies appeared above the corpse.

The next day, almost all the snakes in the water were dead. When the water level dropped to the third floor, mud and silt were exposed. Where water still remained, it had turned into muddy swamps.

The swamps were filled with snake corpses, including the giant python now swarmed by flies.

After two months, Evelyn opened her front door for the first time.

She wore protective gear and carried a shovel.

The moment she saw piles of snake corpses stacked in the hallway, her stomach churned. The stench of rot nearly made her pass out.

Once she started, she did not stop.

Beginning right outside her door, Evelyn cleared the snake corpses from the hallway. Mixed in among them were two human skeletons. She shoveled those up as well, opened the stairwell's protective window, and tossed everything straight down.

It took her three hours just to clear the tenth floor. She hurried back home, drank a bottle of glucose to replenish her strength, and rested for half an hour before she felt somewhat recovered.

She opened the door to the Rivers family's apartment next door. One look at the dense piles of snake corpses inside and she immediately backed out.

From the tenth floor up to the twelfth, Evelyn spent the entire day clearing. She checked every apartment. Not a single survivor.

When she reached Calvin's door, Evelyn had no hope left. She pushed the door. It did not budge. Locked from the inside.

She was about to turn away when she heard a faint sound from within.

Evelyn froze, then pounded hard on the door.

"Calvin? Jessica? Is anyone there?"

There was no response, as if the sound had only been her imagination.

Evelyn stepped back, then kicked the door. On the fifth kick, it finally burst open. Standing in the doorway, she was hit by a wave of foul stench and quickly retreated several steps, holding her breath.

The apartment was filthy and chaotic. Thankfully, there were no snake corpses. The balcony windows were intact.

She checked room by room. When she saw three severely decomposed bodies in one bedroom, she gently closed the door.

In the end, she found Wendy and Calvin barely alive in the bathroom.

Calvin lay on the floor, a fruit knife clutched in his hand. His emaciated arms were covered in countless cuts. The shock of the sight left Evelyn speechless.

She checked Wendy first. Aside from extreme weakness, she was otherwise fine.

Calvin's condition was terrible. He was still alive, but severely dehydrated, his wounds already festering.

Evelyn carried Wendy to the living room, then dragged Calvin out. She had just fed them half a bottle of glucose when Wendy woke up.

Wendy stared blankly at Evelyn, not crying, not making a sound.

Evelyn gave her a bit more glucose. When she saw the piles of empty drink bottles in the living room, she understood. They must have survived by drinking beverages salvaged from the floodwater. When food finally ran out, Calvin had fed Wendy with his own blood.

Calvin's condition did not improve. Even after acupuncture and medication, he still showed no signs of waking.

Throughout the entire process, Wendy sat quietly to the side without a single sound.

Looking at her small frame and swollen face, Evelyn reached out and gently rubbed her dry, yellowed hair.

Chapter 7 Rainstorm: Part 3

"Mr. and Mrs. Jones, you should head over to Mrs. Rivers' place. She's so kindhearted, she won't turn you away."

With that, Evelyn turned and walked back into the kitchen. As for the angry voices shouting outside her door, she let out a cold laugh and acted like she didn't hear a thing.

In the kitchen, she brewed a pot of ginger tea. The temperature had dropped too fast lately—catching a cold right now would be dangerous.

An hour later, the thunder returned. Someone had been struck by lightning on their way back to the complex.

Several power poles were leaking electricity. Three people in the community had already died from electric shocks. The group chat was flooded with outrage—some people shouting, others cursing. The only bright spot was that at 6:30 p.m., power was miraculously restored.

Evelyn took the opportunity to simmer a big pot of lamb soup. After drinking a full bowl, warmth finally returned to her limbs.

She packed the rest of the soup into plastic containers and stored them in her space, then picked up her phone to check the group chat.

To her surprise, someone from Block A was in the middle of a heated argument. Evelyn scrolled up and quickly figured out what had happened.

There was a small supermarket inside the community, but it had shut down on the very first day of the rainstorm. Just earlier, a few masked men had broken in and looted it. The heavy fog made it impossible to tell who they were, but everyone knew—they had to be residents of the complex.

One person was cursing them nonstop, calling them bandits and saying they deserved to die. Evelyn didn't bother reading the rest.

At 10 p.m., the power went out again. The complex echoed with angry shouts and frustrated groans.

Evelyn placed a battery-powered emergency lamp by her bedside, then sat down with an acupuncture book in her lap. But the cold made it hard to focus. Her eyelids were growing heavy, and just as she was drifting off, someone started pounding on her door.

Bang! Bang! Bang!

Evelyn rubbed her eyes and sat up.

"Evelyn, please! It's my daughter Emily—please save her! You studied medicine, didn't you? I'm begging you, open the door!"

The voice belonged to Laura from 901. Evelyn got out of bed, put on her clothes and shoes, and picked up the emergency lamp before walking to the door.

Through the peephole, she saw Laura Evans and her husband Jason Evans standing anxiously outside. Jason was holding a little girl, around three years old. The couple had married in their forties. Emily was their miracle child—their entire world.

"Evelyn, please! She's unconscious! I'll get down on my knees if I have to—please, save her!"

Evelyn frowned, then unlocked the door and opened it.

Laura's eyes lit up with desperation and hope. Evelyn held the lamp close to Emily's face to check her condition.

"She's probably been scared by the thunder," Evelyn said calmly. "Fright like that can trigger a high fever. If she's sweating and her pores are open, cold air can get into the body."

She glanced at Laura. "Check her temperature. If it's under 38.5, use physical cooling. If it's higher, she'll need medication or a hospital visit."

Laura's hands were shaking badly, and tears were streaming down her face. "We don't have a thermometer…"

Evelyn paused for a moment, then reached out and touched Emily's forehead. "Go home and give her a cold compress on the forehead. Wipe her down with alcohol. When she improves, give her a warm bath. Don't let her catch any more chill. Keep her calm and comfortable."

"Okay, okay! Thank you, Evelyn. Thank you so much! So… no medicine needed?"

Evelyn nodded. "She should be fine."

Jason thanked her, cradled Emily in his arms, and quickly headed back. Laura bowed several times, nearly tripping over herself with gratitude.

Evelyn waved her off and told her to hurry home to care for the child.

Once the door closed again, Evelyn no longer felt sleepy.

In her past life, during the heatwave months, Jason had gone out searching for food and was beaten to death during a robbery. Laura and Emily had both died later—alone, locked inside their home.

Evelyn walked to her balcony and pushed aside the insulation panel. Through the thick glass, she looked out at the street below.

Rain pounded the world outside. Thunder cracked like the sky was tearing apart. But what truly unsettled her was the corpse floating in the rising water.

The trees that hadn't yet been ripped out by tornadoes swayed violently in the storm winds. Their branches screeched against one another in a sound that was eerily like a blade being sharpened.

Evelyn pushed the insulation panel back into place and let out a deep breath.

The next morning's news feed was a nightmare. A mountain landslide had wiped out an entire village. A mysterious virus had been detected in parts of Africa. Wild animals were flooding into urban areas in one country after another.

Evelyn had just finished her breakfast when someone pounded on her door again.

"Evie, my grandma has a fever. Can you come take a look?"

It was Claire Brooks from 902. They weren't exactly close, but they had interacted before—just recently, in fact, at Mr Richard Grant's memorial. Coincidentally, Claire also studied at Elaris University, majoring in law.

In Evelyn's last life, Claire had once shared half a bun with her. That small act had saved her life.

It was a debt Evelyn had never forgotten.

Now, she could finally repay it.

She went inside, put on a knit cap and a mask, then opened the door and followed Claire downstairs.

"Thank god you live in Block D," Claire said as they walked. "If we didn't have a medical student like you around, I really wouldn't know what to do. Emergency hotlines aren't working, and the floodwater downstairs is too deep to go out."

She looked exhausted. Her family situation wasn't easy. Her grandparents were elderly, her grandfather already suffering from dementia, and now her grandmother was running a high fever. Her parents had both reached retirement age. As their only child, Claire carried the entire burden on her shoulders.

"I'm not a real doctor," Evelyn said. "Just a half-trained one."

"Don't be modest. You're a genius, Evelyn. I mean that."

Claire hesitated, then added, "Last night… I heard some shouting outside your door. Are you okay?"

"I'm fine." Evelyn's expression turned grim at the memory of the previous night.

When they arrived at 902, Evelyn could already hear the faint, wheezing cough from inside. The moment the door opened, it hit her—warm, stale air and a heavy sense of worry.

Claire's grandmother was lying in bed, buried under three thick quilts. Her complexion was pale with a yellowish tinge. Across the room, her grandfather was staring blankly at the ceiling, dazed but calm.

Evelyn knelt beside the bed and took the old woman's temperature. It was 40°C. Her brow furrowed.

She stepped out of the room and motioned for Claire to join her in the hallway.

In her past life, Evelyn remembered hearing that Claire's grandmother had passed away. But at the time, Evelyn had been in such a bad state herself, she hadn't paid much attention. Claire had never come to her for help. They eventually lost contact.

When Evelyn saw her again, she'd been the only one left in her family.

"Claire," she said quietly. "Your grandma has a 40-degree fever. This isn't just a high temperature. I think she's also dealing with a lung infection… maybe even central nervous system involvement."

Claire's face went white. Tears streamed down her cheeks.

"Evie, is there anything you can do?"

Claire's father stepped out of the kitchen, his hands dripping from bailing water, and looked at her with desperation.

"Darling... is it that serious?" he asked.

Her mother stopped what she was doing and stared at Evelyn anxiously.

Evelyn hesitated. This could get her into trouble. The old woman's life was at risk—and she wasn't a licensed doctor. But this was Claire.

And she owed her.

"I have two fever reducers and some antibiotics at home," Evelyn said slowly. "But honestly, the safest option would be to get her to a hospital."

Hope lit up in Claire's eyes. She grabbed Evelyn's hand.

"Can I buy them from you? I'll pay triple! Or I can trade—rice, water, whatever you want. But I really don't think she can handle the trip to the hospital. It's freezing outside, the water's too deep, and if she catches a chill, it might get worse."

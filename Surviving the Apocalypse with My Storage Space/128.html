Chapter 128 The Avenger Returns

The base had descended into chaos. After a quick discussion, Evelyn and Adrian brought Calvin, Wendy, and the others to stay in 602. Adrian slept on the floor in 601, a curtain stretched between them, while Evelyn and Wendy took the bed.

Having everyone together brought a sense of security they hadn’t had in days.

“Every day, someone’s causing trouble in the Administration Hall. Yesterday I heard people talking about setting the base on fire. I think this time the base won’t be able to contain it,” Calvin said, sharpening his knife as he shared the news.

“But the base is huge. There are so many people living here. If they really set fire, even more people will die. Would they really do that? Out of revenge, just to take more lives?”

“Evelyn, what do you think the base will do about this?” Eddie asked, curious to hear her assessment.

“First, the leadership is divided. Everyone wants to be in charge, yet no one can. When something happens, they start passing the buck. No one wants responsibility. Second, with so many dead, the base will need to push one person forward as a scapegoat, or the anger of the residents won’t subside. Third, after purifying the water, the management will drink it first and get rid of all the livestock. The pig farm’s quiet—probably they don’t plan to deal with the mad pigs. Fourth, the dead will need to be buried somewhere else, and the air, soil, and water must be sanitized. Fifth, the base will distribute supplies to calm people’s anger.”

Those were the only points Evelyn could think of. How the leadership would actually handle it, she had no clue. Those in power were used to being at the top; empathy probably wasn’t part of their skill set.

“The base is still selling everything except meat. I’ve got hundreds of points left on my resident card. I need to redeem them. What do you think I should get?”

“Compressed biscuits and instant noodles are probably the safest,” Evelyn said, and couldn’t help but laugh. The others joined in.

“Let’s wait and see. Within three days, the base should respond. Then we’ll know what they plan to do.”

—

That night, Adrian got up and went out. Evelyn opened her eyes at the closed door, puzzled at what he was up to.

The next morning, when Eddie knocked on the door, Evelyn and Wendy were still in bed. Adrian had already boiled water and was engrossed in a book.

“Evelyn, big news.”

Evelyn climbed out of bed, hair a mess, giving him a pleading look.

“Has the base announced their solution?”

Eddie shook his head. “This morning, several bodies were hung in front of the Administration Hall. The heirs of the Hawthorne and Burton families were among them, along with other managers from two different factions.”

Evelyn glanced at Adrian, who didn’t even lift an eyelid, still engrossed in 1000 Everyday Recipes.

“Tell me everything,” she said.

Eddie sat down and began narrating carefully.

“This morning, I went out to exchange supplies and noticed a lot of people running toward the Administration Hall. You know me—I can’t resist a crowd, so I went too. When I got there...”

“Stop. Get to the point. Don’t waste my time with details.”

“How can it be a waste? To tell the whole story, you have to start from the beginning. Don’t miss any detail. Are you listening? If not, I’m leaving.” He puffed out his chest arrogantly.

“Adrian, knock him down.”

Adrian grabbed an empty cup nearby and hurled it at Eddie, hitting the back of his head. A bump immediately swelled.

“Ouch! Alright, alright, I’m wrong, I give up! You violent lunatic, Adrian, I’ll remember you,” Eddie said, giving Adrian a sarcastic laugh.

“You’re obedient, huh? You do whatever Evelyn tells you,” he added.

“Can you get to the point now? And be serious,” Evelyn said. Eddie coughed and straightened up.

“Fine. Here’s the main point. When I arrived at the Administration Hall, I saw four bodies hanging. One had an apology placard pinned to it. It said that if the base couldn’t produce regulations within three days, all management personnel wouldn’t survive.”

Eddie finished and began clapping.

“Not bad. In this world, there are still avengers for justice.”

Evelyn glanced at Adrian, who had caught her looking, and they both subtly looked away.

“And then? How did the base react?”

Eddie smirked. “The crowd took the bodies. The patrol went to the leadership. Seven or eight men arrived, said a lot of soothing words to calm the people, and promised that within three days they’d give a satisfactory reply. Only then were the bodies returned.”

“Do you think this avenger is the same one as before?”

Eddie meant the previous case, when several women were assaulted by three scoundrels, and the avenger found and hanged those three at the Administration Hall—exactly the same method.

Evelyn’s lips curved slightly. “Definitely the same person.”

“What a good person,” Eddie muttered, impressed.

Evelyn glanced at Adrian, who twitched his ears—clearly eavesdropping yet pretending nothing had happened.

Since the leadership fiasco, the residents had vented some of their anger. The number of disturbances had fallen. Everyone was now waiting for the base to announce their solution. If the base couldn’t act within three days, the avenger might strike again, and the leadership could face a complete reshuffle.

All the management personnel had bodyguards, and their residences were under surveillance. Yet someone had managed to abduct and hang the previous victims in the Administration Hall without being recorded.

Who was this avenger? One person, or a group?

The residents were curious, and satisfied.

The management were furious, fearful, uneasy.

These past days, meals had been shared communally. Wendy had recovered from her serious illness and, somehow, displayed incredible strength. A four-year-old lifting a bucket of water.

Everyone praised her, and Wendy grinned foolishly, her innocence restored.

Elliot and Clara had stayed inside for the last few days, occasionally coming by to ask for news, which Evelyn shared with them.

That afternoon, the base’s loudspeakers blared from below.

From the rain, heat, earthquake, and snow to the upheaval in the farmland, the announcements portrayed the management as exhausted heroes serving the people without reward.

The residents weren’t having it. They started throwing things and shouting abuse. Eventually, the avenger was invoked.

“The Avenger will kill you.”

The announcer was struck in the head by a stone, blood gushing, and fled in disgrace.

There were no winners in this war. With the wheat harvest approaching, if this issue wasn’t resolved, dozens of acres would go unharvested, creating another huge loss.

Right now, the priority wasn’t salvaging public opinion.

It was solving the problem.

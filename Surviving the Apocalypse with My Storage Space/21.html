Chapter 21 Rainstorm, Venom Moths: Part 17

That night felt especially long.

Evelyn gave Calvin basic stitches and emergency treatment. Whether he would survive now depended on his own will to live.

The crying in Block D, B, and E never stopped through the night. At nine in the morning, Evelyn carried her medical kit to Claire's apartment. Her grandparents were preparing the bodies of Claire's parents. Evelyn stitched the wound on Claire's back, then went home.

Back in her own place, Evelyn slid down to the floor. Her hands would not stop shaking. After a long while, she forced herself up and peeled off the protective suit, revealing eyes red and bloodshot.

She took a long, overdue hot shower. After eating, she collapsed onto the bed. She thought she would not be able to sleep, but instead she slept straight through until eight in the evening.

She had just gotten up and splashed water on her face when she heard Jessica calling from outside.

"Evie, Calvin is doing very badly. Please come take a look. I'm begging you."

"Don't panic. I'll grab my medical kit and come right up. You go back upstairs first."

Evelyn rushed to the twelfth floor with her kit. Calvin was in terrible condition. His injuries were too severe, and he had developed a fever and infection. Evelyn treated the wounds again. When she saw his young, clueless daughter standing nearby, her chest tightened. Without a father to protect her, how was a girl this little supposed to survive?

"I still have some traditional medicine at home. Boil it and feed it to him."

Jessica looked overjoyed. "Evie, that's wonderful. But if we take it, will you still have some left? We can't take something you need to save lives."

Evelyn nodded. "His condition is urgent. Watch him carefully. If anything seems wrong, go out to the balcony and ring this bell."

She had noticed a small bell meant for children's toys. It would be easier than Jessica running back and forth.

Jessica thanked her again. Just as Evelyn was about to leave, she brought out a box from the bedroom and handed it to her. Evelyn opened it and saw a heavy gold bar inside.

"Like you said, things should go both ways. Evie, this isn't worth much anymore, but you saved Calvin's life. Our family doesn't even know how to repay you."

Evelyn accepted the box, then pulled a lollipop from her pocket and handed it to Wenwen.

"He'll get better."

The neighbors who had died were also sent to 601. From that day on, the number of venom moths seemed to decrease. The number of people did too.

Claire's grandmother could not withstand the blow and passed away the next day. Her household was left with only her and her grandfather, who suffered from dementia. On the third day, Calvin finally pulled through and regained consciousness.

Next door, the Rivers family continued to erupt into daily arguments. On the fourth day, the torrential rain suddenly stopped. People in the complex began venturing out to search for supplies. Claire and Evelyn teamed up and headed for the food factory on Millstone Avenue.

Claire had grown quiet, but there was a new toughness in her that had not been there before.

Many people were out scavenging along the way. Inflatable boats collided from time to time. Everyone's temper was growing shorter, and the slightest friction could turn into drawn blades.

The standing water was filthy. Anyone who fell in would retch for a long time after being pulled out.

There were also collapsed buildings and steel bars hidden beneath the water. One wrong move could mean being skewered through the body.

When they passed a hotel, people were fighting over blankets. Some had their boats stolen and stood there screaming curses into the air.

"Everyone's gone mad," Claire muttered, watching the chaos with cold eyes. "Evelyn, this whole world has gone mad."

They had only taken out one inflatable boat. Claire steered at the front while Evelyn sat in the back, crossbow and knife in hand. A few people tried to target their boat, but after witnessing how ruthless Evelyn could be, no one dared come close again.

Whether the food factory still had supplies was anyone's guess. When they reached their destination, they did not see anyone else. Worried the boat might be stolen, they talked it over. Evelyn would go in first to scout. If she found food, she would bring it out in batches. Claire would stay behind to guard the boat.

With a waterproof bag slung over her shoulder, Evelyn climbed in through a glass window. It was a canned food factory. Canned goods were sealed, which meant the venom moths could not destroy them.

The production floor and warehouse downstairs were already flooded. Fortunately, the factory was built differently from residential buildings. The water only reached the third floor. After entering, Evelyn strapped a high-powered flashlight to her head and began searching. Someone had clearly been here before. Everything was in disarray. Still, she found dozens of boxes of canned meat hidden under oilcloth in a corner.

She took thirty boxes, then carried the remaining six outside. First, the inflatable boat was small and could not carry much more. Second, hauling too many supplies made you an easy target. Trouble was best avoided whenever possible.

Claire had not expected them to find so much. A faint smile finally appeared on her usually indifferent face. Evelyn wanted to check upstairs as well, so she left the crossbow with Claire.

This was not a place to linger. She swept through the upper floors. Besides ten more boxes of canned food, she found two hidden cases of bottled water in an office. There was also a bag of biscuits in a drawer, two cups of instant noodles, and a pack of beef jerky. Evelyn left one box of canned food where the supplies had been hidden and took everything else.

"Water?" Claire's eyes widened.

Evelyn handed it over, then jumped down from the windowsill and returned to the boat.

"Yeah. One case each, and we split the snacks."

Claire shook her head. "I'll take one case of water. You take an extra box of canned food. You did more than I did. The snacks are yours."

Evelyn took back the crossbow and laughed softly. "Enough. Don't be so polite. Surviving comes first."

Claire turned away and wiped her eyes. Evelyn pretended not to notice. She tied everything down and covered it with torn curtains as camouflage. On the way back, Claire steered again, with Evelyn guarding the rear.

They had barely left the factory grounds when they ran into a group of people. The others sized up the two women and their boat. When they noticed the knife and crossbow in Evelyn's hands, they backed off.

Evelyn's gaze was icy, fingers tight around the crossbow, ready to fire at any moment. Only after they had put some distance between themselves did she relax slightly.

"I really thought they were going to rush us," Claire said.

Worried about her grandfather with dementia, Claire was anxious to get home. Evelyn did not delay them. They pushed forward at top speed. They still ran into trouble along the way, but managed to deal with it without incident.

When they returned to Block D, Evelyn heard Fiona wailing loudly. Seeing Evelyn and Claire come back loaded with supplies only made it worse. Fiona snapped and charged at them. Evelyn kicked her away without expression.

"You wanna die?"

"What's wrong with you, Evelyn?" Pauline screamed at the top of her lungs. "If you hadn't provoked my mom, would she be like this?"

Evelyn lifted her foot, ready to kick her as well, but the Rivers family's son-in-law, Henry, hurriedly dragged Pauline back.

"Evie, you're back?" he said quickly. "Don't argue with Pauline. Sigh, my brother-in-law went out looking for supplies and still hasn't come back. They're just worried. Seeing you return, they got a bit worked up."

Henry stared at the supplies in Evelyn's hands, his other hand gripping Pauline tightly.

Chapter 30 Rainstorm, Toads, Cold Wave: Part 5

"You guys have a base?" Evelyn asked, expression calm. "How many people are in it?"

"You join us, I'll tell you everything," said the young man, flashing a grin. "Let's at least get acquainted first. I'm Ryan. What's your name?"

This Ryan was the same guy who'd pointed a knife at her moments ago. Now, the knife was tucked away.

Evelyn narrowed her eyes slightly, sizing them up.

All three men were energetic and well-fed, their motor raft and protective gear in near-pristine condition. No visible signs of wear or panic. In this world, that said a lot.

She spoke again, testing. "I have several people at home. Would you be able to take all of us in?"

Ryan turned to exchange a glance with the others. "Your family? Any elderly or kids?"

"Yes."

He shook his head. "Can't do. Old people and kids are dead weight. We don't take them."

So that was how it was. A base made up entirely of young survivors. From Ryan's tone, they weren't total thugs—but they weren't saints either.

"I'll be going then," Evelyn said. "Mind moving? You're blocking the path."

Their raft sat across the narrow alleyway like a barricade.

"Wait, what's your name? And that crossbow of yours… got another one? I've got food to trade."

Evelyn glanced at the supplies stacked on their boat. Her gaze sharpened.

"No," she replied simply.

Ryan let out a disappointed "ah," but signaled his companion to pull the raft back a few meters.

Evelyn nodded slightly, restarted her engine, and sped off toward the nearest street corner.

"Hey, at least tell me your name!" Ryan shouted after her.

Evelyn raised a hand, waved it twice behind her, and disappeared down the flooded alley.

"I really wanted that crossbow," Ryan muttered. "Shame she didn't have extras."

"You could've just killed her and taken it," said the man beside him with a cold chuckle.

Ryan rolled his eyes. "What, you think a knife's faster than a crossbow? You trying to get shot in the throat?

"Besides, what kind of man goes around murdering girls for gear?"

"Tch. Sentimental."

"Oh yeah?" Ryan snapped. "Then why'd you pull the raft back so she could pass?"

"…Fine, whatever. Let's go. Ryan was right—there's no need to mess with her. But Gavin's not wrong either. Kindness doesn't get you far these days."

"So what do you suggest?" Ryan and Gavin both turned to look at the third man.

"I don't know," he muttered. "Let's just head back."

*****

Evelyn had barely left Ryan's group when she turned a corner—and walked right into an ambush.

A net exploded outward from both sides, cast from the rooftops above. In a flash, it dropped over her motor raft, pinning her in place.

A half-dozen men surged forward, shouting over each other.

"Damn it, no supplies again? We waited an hour for some broke-ass loner?"

"Who cares? She's got a raft! Grab it and deal with her—fast!"

Evelyn's expression didn't flicker. She dropped into a crouch on the raft, fingers brushing the net. Standard nylon. A quick burn or slash would break it.

Her mind worked fast. There were six men, spread out, coming from both sides. Their posture betrayed overconfidence. They thought she was weak.

The moment the nearest attacker lunged, Evelyn drew a razor-sharp blade to rip the net open.

Next, she pulled her crossbow and fired. Two of the front men collapsed instantly, arrows embedded deep in their chests.

"She's armed! Be careful!" someone shouted behind her.

However, before the people behind him could dodge, Evelyn reflexively kicked them into the water. A crossbow bolt shot out swiftly, taking down three people in just ten seconds. Evelyn then tore off the fishing net from his body and aimed his crossbow at the remaining four.

"Wait, wait, let's talk this out!" one of them shouted in panic. "We'll give you everything! All of it! Just let us go! We had no choice, we were desperate. Please, spare us!"

Evelyn's gaze shifted to their two motor rafts.

They were loaded with supplies.

Then she noticed something else.

One of the burlap sacks at the back was moving.

…There was someone inside?

She froze for barely two seconds.

That was all it took.

One of the men suddenly raised his knife and lunged, aiming straight for her raft.

Evelyn fired.

The bolt pierced his throat.

He dropped stiffly onto the deck, clutching his neck, making hoarse choking sounds. After spitting out a few mouthfuls of blood, he went still.

The remaining three exchanged one look and instantly started their engines, trying to flee.

As if she would let them go.

Evelyn fired three times in rapid succession.

She got all three bodies.

They collapsed almost simultaneously.

She steered closer, retrieved her arrows, then kicked the corpses into the water.

Splash!

A whirlpool formed, and the bodies were dragged under.

Then she approached the raft with the moving sack.

She stared at it for a few seconds.

It twitched again.

Muffled "mmph" sounds leaked out.

She cut through the ropes with her blade.

Three sacks split open.

Inside were two young women and a teenage boy, maybe fifteen or sixteen.

Their hands and feet were bound and their mouths were sealed with tape.

No one knew what they'd been through.

Their clothes were thin and torn. Their eyes were full of terror. Bruises and wounds covered their exposed skin.

The moment they saw Evelyn, they started trembling, scrambling backward in fear.

She cut the ropes and ripped off the black tape without ceremony.

"Don't be scared," she said calmly. "They're all dead."

She pointed at the water.

One of the corpses had already floated back up.

The three shrank together, staring wide-eyed at the surface.

Evelyn ignored their reaction and opened the remaining sacks of supplies.

There were clothes, shoes, bottled water, instant noodles, and bread.

The clothes were filthy and reeked of mildew.

She left most of it behind, only taking a few buckets of instant noodles.

"These two rafts and the supplies are yours," she said. "Take them and go."

She returned to her own raft.

The three stood there in shock for a moment, then rushed to the food, tearing open packages and stuffing their mouths.

The boy grabbed a jacket and wrapped himself tightly.

Then he noticed the knives left behind.

He picked one up.

Without hesitation, he slashed at the floating corpse.

Evelyn raised an eyebrow.

The smell of blood drew in the toads.

They swarmed onto the body in seconds.

The sight was revolting.

She turned away.

As she started her engine, the boy suddenly looked up at her and whispered, "Thank you."

Evelyn nodded once and drove off.

The boy jumped onto the other raft and grabbed one bag of supplies. The two girls looked at him, confused.

"I'm going home," he said quietly.

"Home?" one girl sobbed. "I… I don't even know where mine is anymore."

The two girls clung to each other and broke down, unable to hold back their tears.

The boy clenched his fists, staring at the endless gray water.

A shadow fell over his eyes.

He couldn't find his home either.

*****

When Evelyn returned to the apartment complex, she ran into Claire, who was out collecting toads.

When she saw that Claire had pulled herself together again, she felt relieved.

Too many people couldn't bear the loss of their families. They chose to end their own lives. It happened often now.

For most people, just losing electricity, water, and internet was enough to shatter their will to live.

They exchanged a few words and went their separate ways.

Over the next few days, Evelyn decided to stay inside.

She lit several heaters. Only after the room warmed up did she peel off her heavy padded jacket.

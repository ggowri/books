Chapter 19 Rainstorm, Venom Moths: Part 15

"You ran into raiders last night?"

Evelyn stayed silent for a few seconds before nodding.

"I'm not accusing you of anything," Calvin said. "If you fought back against raiders, that's self-defense. And besides, the world isn't what it used to be. I just came to warn you. Be careful. There was a home invasion in Block C. A lot of people didn't make it. Those raiders were brutal. You shouldn't go out for a while."

Evelyn looked at Calvin. At a time like this, he still came just to warn her. She hadn't expected that.

Evelyn didn't like owing anyone. She turned and grabbed a bag from inside, then handed it to him. The plastic bag on the outside still had dried blood on it. They were leftovers from the loot she'd taken from the raiders' boat the night before.

Calvin took the bag and opened it. Inside were two large heads of cabbage.

"You didn't need to give me anything. I didn't come here for this."

"I know. Just take it. Think of it as a fair trade."

Calvin almost laughed. Fair trade? Was that what this was?

"Alright, I'll take it. So, how's the situation out there?"

"There are bodies floating in the streets and alleyways. Venom moths everywhere. The water's rising fast. Also…" Evelyn paused, then added, "Stock up while you still can. The rain's not stopping. If the reservoir breaks too, then what?"

She left it at that.

Calvin's expression changed. The thought of the reservoir bursting left his eyes wide with fear.

He gave Evelyn a few more warnings, then lifted his torch and hurried upstairs. Watching him disappear, Evelyn let out a quiet sigh.

In the face of nature, humans really were small.

But people were resilient. Disasters kept coming, but someone always managed to survive, stubbornly clinging to life.

After he left, Evelyn sorted through the spoils she'd brought back the night before.
Only then did she realize just how much she'd managed to gather. Once everything was organized, she kept it all in her spatial storage. It had been a productive trip. She was more than satisfied.

After lunch, she drew the curtains and scanned the world outside. The bodies floating in the water made her frown.

Were those attackers really just ordinary raiders?

That afternoon, Calvin and a few neighbors installed a new security door on the fourth floor. While they worked, Evelyn stepped out to clean up around her own door. As she did, she caught snippets of conversation coming from downstairs.

"She's just a girl, but she was gone for a day and a half and came back with supplies. That's not normal."

"Who knows? But she's got guts. I saw her last night. Her protective suit was covered in blood. I'm telling you, she probably went out and robbed someone too."

"I've said from the start she's not a good person. Everyone who's ever stayed with her ends up worse off. Didn't Tyler Wren lose a hand? She's a savage."

"Would've been better if she died out there. The Wren family even took an axe to her door yesterday morning and couldn't break through. That thing's solid. Her place looks safer than anywhere else."

"If she dies, who's gonna treat us when we get sick? Don't say that. Someone might hear."

*****

Later that afternoon, Claire came by. She was shivering with a cold, and it was getting serious. Luckily, she'd stocked up on medicine at Everwell Plaza earlier.

"Evelyn, thank goodness you made it back safely. Why were you gone so long? Everyone thought you weren't coming back. The Wren family was so angry, they took knives and axes to your door. Thank god it held."

Evelyn took one look at her pale face and weak posture and told her to go home and rest.

"I'm fine. I mainly came to tell you that two nights ago, raiders came to the complex. They said they'd be back tonight. Don't go out. Actually, don't go out at all for the next few days. I'm serious, so many people died in the building next to ours. The men were killed, and the women..."

Claire's voice cracked. Before she could finish, she broke down completely.

"How did it end up like this, Evelyn? Do we still have any hope? I'm so scared. I don't want to die. Do you think the people in charge have abandoned us?"

Who wasn't afraid of dying?

"Claire, if those people come back, then fight back. Don't be afraid. Think about your family. Only if you stay alive can you protect them."

As for the authorities, it wasn't that they didn't care. They were either overwhelmed, or dealing with things even more critical like relocating supplies, building shelters, or setting up bases.

Claire stared at Evelyn blankly. "I… I don't dare."

"That's normal," Evelyn said quietly. "We grew up in a civilized world. We had proper education. Most of us are scared to even kill a fish or a chicken, let alone fight back against monsters like them.

"But if you're afraid, they won't show you mercy. And if you die, do you think your grandparents and parents can survive on their own?"

Claire's expression slowly hardened.

She studied law. She believed in rules and order. But she wasn't stupid.

She knew that surviving came first.

"Evelyn… I understand now."

Evelyn nodded. "Good. Go back and get some sleep. Save your strength. Be ready."

After Claire left, Evelyn stared at her own door for a long moment. Then she went inside.

She took out more than ten kinds of medicinal herbs and brewed them together into a pot of poison.

After three hours of boiling, she delivered some to Calvin's place and to Claire's. The rest, she poured into a spray bottle.

Night fell.

An eerie silence settled over the entire complex.

Fully geared up, Evelyn stood by the window, binoculars in hand, watching the area below.

At eleven, everything was still quiet. People finally relaxed a little.

The storm that night was mixed with hail. The sharp cracks against the glass made everyone uneasy.

Evelyn, however, stayed alert.

At midnight, a splash of water struck the window.

The sound made her heart sink.

They were here. And there were many of them.

A scream pierced the night.

The balcony windows of the building across from hers were smashed. The attackers laughed wildly, completely unrestrained.

Evelyn's hands trembled. The veins in her wrists stood out sharply.

She heard the security door downstairs being shaken.

They weren't just carrying knives. They had axes and saws too.

A long time ago, Evelyn's dream had been to become a doctor like her parents. To hold a blade only for saving lives.

But today, the knife in her hand was meant for killing. For killing those who had lost all humanity.

If she didn't kill them, she wouldn't survive.

Evelyn told herself again and again that this was to rid the people of evil. She shouldn't be afraid or hesitate.

Bang!

Suddenly, a deafening crash echoed through the building.

The security door on the fourth floor was torn down.

Their cruel laughter spread everywhere.

Axes slammed against doors like death warrants, tormenting every resident inside.

"Please… please let us go. We have nothing at home. Don't kill us. Please, don't kill us…"

"Nothing? Isn't this bag of rice something?" "You lying old fool. Tonight, your family will be our appetizer. Everyone, kill the men. As for the women… hehe, take them all."

"No… please, let us go…"

"Let you go? Hahaha. Fine. I'll grant your wish."

The blade fell.

The old man died with his eyes wide open.

A burly thug dragged a young woman into the bedroom. The others snickered obscenely as they began ransacking the room for supplies.

Chapter 39 Heatwave, Snake Infestation:Part 3

Once the wall was finished, the apartment grew dark, barely different from a basement. Evelyn had no choice but to place an emergency lamp in the living room and keep it on twenty four hours a day.

The next morning, when she woke up and reached out into pitch black darkness, she remembered that she had sealed off all the balcony glass. She turned on the desk lamp in the bedroom. Once it was connected to the battery pack, light flooded the room.

Evelyn fixed herself breakfast, turned on the radio, and slid in a cassette. Cheerful music filled the air. She shut herself off from everything outside. She did not look, did not listen, did not respond.

Deep down, she knew the snake population had already spiraled out of control. Elaris was on its way to becoming hell.

All she could do now was wait. Wait for that terrifying acid rain. Once it fell, there might still be hope of wiping out the snakes.

The entire day slipped by as Evelyn read, practiced acupuncture, jumped rope, trained her arms, and watched TV. In the afternoon, she took out a bowl and soaked two handfuls of soybeans. They needed to soak in clean water for seven or eight hours, then be transferred to a small bucket to sprout. In three or four days, they would be ready. Perfect for hot pot.

The smell of cement had faded a little. Evelyn laid down plenty of moisture proof mats in the bedroom. She took out the yarn and crochet hooks she had stockpiled and decided to knit a sweater to pass the time.

For Evelyn, thinking had always been easier than working with her hands. She followed the steps in the book, failing again and again. She poked her fingers full of tiny wounds. By the hundredth time she thought about giving up, she finally managed to crochet a clean edge.

Time passed quickly in this self entertaining isolation. On the third day, Evelyn stared at the freshly finished product and nearly choked.

Was this rag that looked like something a beggar would wear really what she had spent three whole days making?

Forget decorative edging. It was not even symmetrical. One sleeve was long, the other short. One wide, one narrow. Probably only a mutated creature could wear it. Thinking about the dozen boxes of yarn she had stored in her space, Evelyn felt deep regret. If she had known she had zero talent for this, she would have stockpiled more rice instead. At least food that went into your stomach truly belonged to you.

The bean sprouts were ready. Two handfuls of soybeans had produced a whole bucket of sprouts. She had planned to make hot pot, but looking at the completely sealed apartment, Evelyn realized a serious problem. The smell would have nowhere to go.

Looks like it would have to be plain boiled bean sprouts instead.

Once she decided, she acted. Evelyn took out a head of lettuce and prepared to make blanched lettuce as well. With the temperature rising, eating more vegetables would help cool the body.

She spread a rug across the living room floor, set up a low table, took out a frozen bottle of Sprite, positioned the projector, and picked an arthouse film to play.

For a moment, Evelyn felt as if she had returned to the days before the apocalypse. The apartment felt like Truman's world. As if one day, all she had to do was open the door, and she would step back into a normal world again.

Sometimes, Evelyn talked to herself.

People who lived alone, if they went too long without speaking or communicating with others, could lose the function of their vocal cords and even become mute. Their minds would also grow sluggish. Evelyn did not want to end up like that. She took out a notebook and pen and recorded her daily life, day by day.

The soundproofing in the old building was poor. Evelyn could still hear noises from outside. Many times, people begged for help in the hallway. Some went so far as to kick and pound on doors like they had lost their minds. Because Evelyn never responded, the neighbors probably assumed she was already dead. Someone even tried to chop her door with an axe, attempting to break in. But the reinforced security door she had installed was extremely solid. Not a scratch on it. They only wasted their strength.

By mid July, Evelyn checked the thermometer. The temperature had climbed to thirty five degrees. Even the walls felt hot to the touch. Outside the window, the sound of snakes crawling could be heard clearly.

She took out electrical wires and a breaker, then brought out the air conditioner and installed it in the living room. The outdoor unit was placed on the balcony. For now, she did not turn it on. Running two fans together consumed far less power than a single air conditioner. She had plenty of gasoline and diesel stored in her space, but she had no intention of wasting them.

The fans ran all night. When Evelyn woke up the next morning, her face felt dry and tight, her lips already cracking. She quickly took out lip balm and moisturizer and slathered them on her face like they were free. Looking at her disheveled reflection in the mirror, Evelyn touched her face and felt too disgusted to look any longer.

"Painful to the eyes," she muttered.

Since she was alone, Evelyn did not bother holding back. She changed into a light camisole and immediately felt cooler. After being cooped up for so long, she could not resist pulling back the curtain. Through the only window that had not been sealed with bricks, she locked eyes with a green banded sea snake coiled outside, nearly two meters long.

Three seconds later, Evelyn calmly pulled the curtain shut, took a deep breath, and nearly collapsed to the floor.

If she had not seen wrong, the black and white sea snake clinging to the window was about as thick as her thigh. Its dark purple black tongue flicked out, as if confirming her position.

Evelyn was just about to drink some ice water to steady herself when a sharp slapping sound suddenly came from outside the window. She swallowed hard and carefully edged closer, lifting a corner of the curtain.

The sea snake had raised its head high, its tongue poised to strike.

Fighting the urge to drop to her knees, Evelyn pulled two bottles of medicated oil from her space and placed them near the window seam. But the gap was sealed too tightly. The smell did not seem to leak out at all. Her actions probably looked ridiculous to the snake. Even though snakes had poor eyesight, Evelyn could feel that dangerous gaze locked onto her. The sea snake slammed its tail against the glass, its vertical pupils widening slightly. Evelyn felt her hair stand on end. She dropped the curtain and fled back to the living room as fast as she could.

At that moment, Evelyn desperately wished someone would swoop in and wipe out all these snakes. She didn't care who—St. Patrick, Indiana Jones, even Samuel L. Jackson on a plane would do.

Because of that menacing sea snake outside the window, Evelyn completely lost her appetite. That evening, she crept back to the window and peeked through the curtain. The snake was gone. A flicker of joy flashed through her eyes.

"Finally gone. That wasn't easy."

That night, Evelyn had another nightmare.

In the dream, the green banded sea snake came back. It brought many other snakes with it. They clung to the window, flicking their tongues as they stared at her.

Terrified, Evelyn broke down crying, fell to her knees, and begged for mercy. Then, suddenly, the sea snake spoke.

"Damn human. You tried to smoke me out with that stench. I'm going to eat you."

Evelyn rolled off her folding bed and lay on the floor, dazed for a long time. Then she heard the sound of a tail slapping the glass again. She could not hold it in anymore. She dry heaved and threw up a stomachful of sour liquid.

The terror of snakes was not just because they were venomous or powerful.

It was because they were disgusting.

Evelyn told herself that unless she planned to hide in this room for the rest of her life, she had to overcome her fear of snakes.

If she wanted to survive, she could not afford any weaknesses.

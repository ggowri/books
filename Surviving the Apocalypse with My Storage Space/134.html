Chapter 134 The Wasteland Apocalypse

After the acid rain began, the base was plunged into total darkness and cut off from water. The top floor started leaking as the corrosive rain ate through the ceiling. A thick, acrid stench filled the air. From the windows, the base looked like a poisoned wasteland, shrouded in white fog that made it hard to see.

Evelyn put on goggles and a mask, watching the chaos outside.

The fog from the acid rain was toxic. Inhaling it caused dizziness, nausea, and abdominal pain.

Tenants in Building 10 were many. The first-floor residents had moved up, while those on the top floor were relocating downward. Occasional stifled cries echoed in the hallways. Evelyn stepped outside to check and found a few children huddled on the stairs, shivering.

She handed Eddie two pieces of ginger, instructing him to chop them and distribute them outside. Chewing ginger would help relieve nausea.

After a full day and night of acid rain, many residents had inhaled the toxic fog. Illness began to spread. Vomiting echoed from outside, and stepping out brought the stench directly to the nose.

Word spread that Evelyn was a doctor, and people knocked desperately on doors for help. She wrapped herself tightly, then opened the door.

“Doctor, please save my wife.”
“Save my son first.”
“Please, my daughter! She’s already unconscious.”

“Calm down, one at a time,” Evelyn said. “All I have are silver needles. I can only relieve dizziness and nausea. If you’re not sick, go back inside and seal every crack with paper or cloth. Do not open doors or windows frequently. The toxic fog is severe. I’m only coming out once—protect yourselves.”

Those who were still able quickly retreated indoors. Evelyn began examining the unconscious.

Adrian stayed behind her. If anyone tried to push past, he blocked them.

Eddie, fully geared, joined to help. Adrian protected Evelyn while Eddie checked the patients. Working together, they stabilized the residents of Building 10 efficiently.

“Do not swallow the ginger. Just hold it in your mouth.”
“Doctor, my wife is vomiting again. Please check her.”
“Let her vomit. Afterwards, give her ginger to settle her stomach. Then I’ll use the needle.”
“Doctor, my stomach hurts.”
“You inhaled too much toxic fog. Induce vomiting, and drink any clean water you have at home.”

These were crude methods, but they worked. Luckily, Evelyn and Adrian had harvested a large amount of honeysuckle at Stag’s Hollow. She pulled out a small bag and asked Officer Shaw to distribute it, one handful per person. With the power out, they had no way to prepare medicine, so it had to be eaten raw.

Shaw knew the herbs were from Evelyn’s expedition, but he didn’t tell the residents. He claimed they were collected from the wheat fields. Some recognised the honeysuckle and its cooling, detoxifying properties, and the explanation was accepted.

Unexpectedly, there was also a pregnant woman in Building 10. Evelyn frowned at the sight of her swollen belly.

“Doctor, is my wife okay? Is the baby alright?” a young man asked.

Evelyn wanted to slap him. Of course the baby wasn’t fine—anyone inhaling the toxic fog, let alone a pregnant woman, would be severely affected.

In the corner, a six- or seven-year-old boy sat huddled. He was thin, pale, and malnourished, with a large head and dry hair.

“How many months?” Evelyn asked.
“About five,” the man whispered, eyes downcast.

“I’ll try the silver needle first. She needs to wake up quickly.”

Evelyn noted the woman’s swollen limbs, wondering why anyone would choose this time to have a child.

The woman stirred, her face pale to the point of frightening.

“Doctor, is my baby okay?”
“For now, yes.”

The woman managed a weak smile. The man exhaled in relief. Evelyn looked at the small boy and turned away, helpless.

By the end of the morning, most of the residents in Building 10 were stabilised.

Evelyn wasn’t a saint. Once back inside, she locked the door. Anyone going out into the toxic fog now and dying would have no one to blame but themselves.

The base was shrouded in smoke and fog. Occasionally, cries for help from neighbouring buildings could be heard. But outside, the acid rain pooled deeply, leaving management powerless.

“This is the end. Will the soil even support crops again? The trees outside must be corroded,” Eddie said, worry etched on his face.

“The Underground Quarter’s planting area should be unaffected,” Evelyn replied.

“Luckily, all the wheat outside was harvested. Otherwise, the losses would be catastrophic.”

“We don’t even know when the rain will stop. If it were just heavy rain, we could still gather firewood or food. But now, it’s so corrosive it’s worse than sulfuric acid. How can something like this fall from the sky? Is it volcanic in origin?”

Everyone sat on the floor. Officer Shaw began inventorying their food, water, medicine, and survival plans.

“There should still be some intact rooms on the first floor. If we can dig up some clean soil, we can plant crops. I have vegetable seeds, raw beans, and three pounds of wheat from the base. Eddie, Ray, and Evan each have three pounds too. That’s twelve pounds altogether.”

“Good thing I exchanged all my points for compressed biscuits. We also collected a lot of clean water. Rationed, it could last months. But if this acid rain continues for over a week, trouble will come even inside the buildings,” Eddie muttered, frustrated.

“I exchanged plenty of compressed biscuits too. They’re not tasty, but filling. Soaked in water, they last longer,” Officer Shaw added, relieved.

Wendy raised her hand. “Dad, I can’t swallow the biscuits.”

“Once I grow some vegetables, you’ll have fresh food. Hang in there a bit longer,” Shaw reassured her.

“I’ll help plant,” Wendy agreed.

Evelyn handed out a few candles. The sky had darkened with the acid rain, and the rooms were powerless, leaving only candlelight.

Once everyone had planned, they returned to their rooms. Adrian stayed in his chair, refusing to move.

“What do you want to eat?” she asked.

He shook his head.

It was the first time Evelyn had seen him refuse food.

“If you’re not eating, why are you staying here?”

“I’m making my own bedding.”

He went back to Unit 602, carrying his mattress and blankets.

“Sleep. I’m here. You don’t need to be afraid.”

Evelyn laughed through her frustration. “Afraid? Me? I’m not afraid.”

“You looked very pale a moment ago. Were you not scared?”

She sighed. “No, I am worried. From today, we’ve officially entered the wasteland apocalypse.”

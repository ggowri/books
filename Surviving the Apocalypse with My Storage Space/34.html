Chapter 34 Rain Stops, Human Nature

Evelyn walked over to Dylan and crouched down. She put on gloves and pulled open his clothes. The moment she saw the large patches of rotting flesh, her heart sank.

This was a classic case of pemphigus. It was an autoimmune skin disease that often appeared in middle aged and elderly patients. Once the immune system went haywire, blisters and large bullae formed on the skin, followed by erosion, oozing, infection, pain, and itching. Many patients also developed ulcers and erosion in the oral mucosa.

The ulcerated areas were excruciating to the touch. Protein loss happened rapidly, and body weight dropped drastically in a short time.

Evelyn pressed her lips together and said nothing. Without medical equipment, any diagnosis she made with the naked eye felt painfully inadequate.

Natalie said her father only started showing symptoms after the torrential rain. In just fifty days, aside from his face, almost no part of Dylan's body still had intact skin.

Evelyn's silence shattered the fragile hope Natalie and Margaret had been clinging to. They wiped their tears quietly at the side. Evelyn first handed Natalie a tube of ointment. She had been kicked in the abdomen and kept gasping in pain whenever she spoke.

Evelyn explained Dylan's condition to them. The unspoken meaning was clear. He could not be saved. Not only were there no conditions for treatment, there was no longer any point in treatment at all.

Margaret could not accept it. She collapsed onto Dylan and cried uncontrollably. Evelyn quickly grabbed her and pulled her back.

"Even breathing causes pain where his skin has ulcerated," Evelyn snapped. "Throwing yourself on him like that only makes it worse. Do you want him to suffer more?"

Margaret was stunned by Evelyn's blunt outburst. She froze, then crumpled to the floor, covering her face as she sobbed.

Natalie was much calmer. She stared at her father in a daze, not saying a word for a long time.

Evelyn had seen what she needed to see and said what needed to be said. As for what came next, she had no intention of getting involved. She picked up her medical kit and had just reached the door when Natalie ran over and stopped her, her face twisted with pain.

"Is there any way to let him leave without suffering?" Natalie asked hoarsely. "He's in so much pain. He keeps crying out. Dr. Foster, my dad is a real man. He took two knife wounds saving a child who was being held hostage and never cried once. I've never seen him shed a tear. I've never seen him kneel and beg anyone. But just now, the way he panicked, the way he knelt and begged them not to drive us out. I can't forget it. I'll never forget it."

Natalie was only fifteen years old, still in her third year of middle school. She had not yet learned how to hide her hatred. When she spoke of those people, the vicious light in her eyes made Evelyn see a reflection of herself.

"There's no such medicine," Evelyn said quietly. "Your father won't make it through the night. Stay with him and talk to him while you can. This ointment is for you."

That kick had hastened Dylan's death.

When Evelyn returned home, sleep was no longer an option. A light drizzle still fell outside. Even through the walls, the stench of floodwater lingered in the air. With the emergency lamp on, Evelyn lay awake with her eyes open until dawn.

The chilblains on her hands and feet had just scabbed over before new sores formed. She had gone too long without sunlight, and her skin looked frighteningly pale.

At six in the morning, Dylan stopped breathing.

His body was carried up to the rooftop by Natalie and Margaret. When the people from 1101 learned of his death, they made a scene for the entire day, terrified that Dylan had left behind some kind of virus to infect them. They shouted about driving Natalie and Margaret out as well. It was only when Calvin and William from 801 stepped in that the chaos was finally brought under control.

Natalie and Margaret temporarily moved in with Claire.

Quinn from next door came again. This time, she wanted to buy miscarriage prevention medicine from Evelyn. Evelyn did not know why she had suddenly decided to keep the child, but her answer remained the same. There was no medicine.

She did not want to have any involvement with anyone from the Rivers family. Quinn did not get angry. Instead, she gently stroked her belly and smiled brightly at Evelyn.

"John has become an idiot. My father-in-law says that once I give birth to this child, I'll be the Rivers family's greatest contributor. My husband will come back to take me home soon. When he sees that I gave him a son, he won't fight with me anymore. He will definitely come back. I have to let him know I'm pregnant. I can give birth. I'm not some hen that can't lay eggs."

Evelyn frowned at her. It was obvious that Quinn's mental state was deteriorating.

She seemed to have come just to show off. After rambling to herself for a while, she left.

Back home, Evelyn pressed her fingers to her temples, speechless.

Even with Calvin as a warning example, many people still went out to salvage supplies. Everyone clung to a sense of luck, believing that Calvin had only been bitten because he was unlucky.

That afternoon, several people in the building were bitten by nematodes. They were even less fortunate than Calvin. They had multiple wounds.

The unluckiest of all was Lucas from 1101, the man who had kicked Natalie and Dylan earlier. Everyone else had been bitten on the calf. Only he had been bitten somewhere unspeakable.

That was something Evelyn could not treat.

The rules remained the same. Treatment in exchange for supplies. These people cursed Evelyn in their hearts while flattering her out loud, delivering salvaged wood to her door and politely begging her to treat them.

As for Lucas, Evelyn's suggestion was simple. Cut it off. If it was removed, he might survive. His family was devastated. It was cruel, but compared to his life, that thing no longer seemed important.

Lucas completely broke down, screaming and crying, begging them not to cut off his manhood. But his wife acted fast. One clean cut. His dignity was gone, but his life was saved.

Later, Claire told Evelyn that Lucas had cheated multiple times. Even after being caught, he never changed. His wife had been in the middle of divorcing him when the disaster struck, forcing the issue to be put on hold.

No wonder Lucas's wife had been so decisive. Evelyn almost thought she worked at a slaughterhouse.

Natalie was very young, but she was incredibly tough. Every time Evelyn pulled back the curtains, she could see Natalie downstairs salvaging supplies. After Dylan died, Margaret fell ill. Losing her father and having to rely on others forced Natalie to grow up fast.

On May twenty seventh, the rain finally stopped.

The gray sky slowly faded, revealing faint traces of light. But because of the deadly solar radiation before, no one dared to step outside.

To celebrate the end of the rain, Evelyn decided to make dry pot chicken for dinner.

She took out the alcohol stove, placed an alcohol block inside, and lit it. A wok was set on top. Once the pan heated up, she added all the base ingredients.

Music played on her phone. With earphones on, Evelyn hummed along while stir frying.

She loved spicy food, so she added plenty of chili peppers. When the chicken and potatoes were almost done, she tossed in cabbage and onions, then finished with sesame seeds and cilantro. Perfect.

The torrential rains that had lasted for two months were finally coming to an end. Everyone prayed for the floodwaters to recede as soon as possible.

On the eleventh floor, someone had dragged out a table and set up candles, framed photos, and a Bible flipped open to Revelation.

The churchgoers were long gone. Now it was the ghost-hunters and doomsday cults taking their turn.

Chapter 142 Toxic Haze 4

Pip kept stopping and starting as he ran ahead, with the others following behind.

“Evelyn, are you sure Pip can do this?” Eddie asked.

She shot him a look. “You’re allowed to be useless. Pip isn’t.”

This was the little mountain spirit she had brought back from Ashen Ridge. Of course he could do it. Besides, Pip had drunk Adrian’s blood. He was noticeably cleverer now.

“You can say Pip’s brilliant without putting me down.”

Eddie tossed back his greasy long hair, clutching a stick in one hand. He looked exactly like some ragged old gang leader.

“You shouldn’t have doubted him, then. He’s very capable.”

The two of them kept sniping at each other until they entered a single room building in the West Wing, where everyone fell silent at once.

Hardly anyone lived in this block now. They had searched the area before, yet somehow they had still missed this place.

Pip began squeaking sharply and scampered upstairs. On the seventh floor, he stopped outside one of the doors and scratched at it with his paws, then looked up at Evelyn, tail swishing furiously.

She bent and picked him up.

Calvin and Eddie kicked at the door with their weapons in hand. Behind them, the missing child’s parents could no longer hold back. They threw themselves against the door too.

Even the frailest people could summon terrifying strength in a moment like that.

The door gave way quickly.

A gaunt man stood inside, holding a knife to a child’s throat. He glared at them all with savage eyes.

“Get out. If you don’t get out now, I’ll kill him.”

The child’s parents broke into desperate sobs. The boy was barely conscious, and there was blood all over him.

“Benny. My son. It really is Benny.”

His mother was so overwhelmed she fainted on the spot.

Evelyn swept the room with a glance. It was tiny. Apart from this child, there was no sign of anyone else.

“Back off,” the man shouted. “Or I kill him now.”

Evelyn took a few stones from her pocket and handed them to Adrian.

He flicked them out in a blur.

The knife flew from the man’s hand. He screamed. A second stone smashed into his jaw, and blood poured down at once.

Calvin and Eddie rushed forward and pinned him down. The child was pulled free and carried out.

“Where are the others?”

The man kept howling. Calvin wrenched both his arms out of joint. Eddie kicked him twice. At last the man broke.

“I’ll talk. Stop hitting me. I’ll tell you everything.”

“They’re in the storage room on the second floor of the laboratory building.”

The laboratory building stood at the farthest end of the base. It took nearly an hour to walk there from the West Wing.

“Then why is this child here?”

“I was going to take him over there, but he kept fighting me. I had to lock him here first. Everyone in the base was out looking today. I was going to dump him somewhere, but he woke up, so I hit him. That’s why he lost so much blood.”

“You animal.”

As soon as he finished speaking, the boy’s father lunged forward and kicked him twice.

“We go to the laboratory building first,” Calvin said.

Everyone followed him.

Only when they reached the building did Evelyn understand why nobody had managed to find the children before.

There was a hidden room inside.

But when they entered it, they found only two children tied up and two girls chained with iron shackles. The other three younger children were gone. Beside them lay some half-burnt firewood and a battered iron pot.

“Where are the other three?”

The parents rushed at the man and started beating him savagely. Before long he was barely conscious.

“They were gone ages ago,” he said, then burst into hysterical laughter. “Gone a long time ago.”

A foul stench rolled off him in waves.

Evelyn and Eddie took off their coats and wrapped them round the two girls. They looked dreadful.

Then Eddie drew a knife and handed it over.

“Whether you want revenge or just to vent your hatred, go on.”

One of the girls took the knife, walked up to the man, and drove it straight into his chest. Then she twisted it.

He stared at her with bulging eyes, struggled twice, and went still.

The three couples who had lost their children surged forward too, venting their grief and rage on his body.

Evelyn stepped back from the crowd and pressed a hand to her chest, feeling an ache there she could not ignore.

“What’s wrong?” Adrian asked.

She turned to him, her voice hoarse. “My heart hurts.”

“Go back. Drink blood.”

Evelyn blinked hard, forcing the sting back from her eyes.

“It’s all right. It’ll pass.”

From inside the room came waves of crying.

The parents who had found their children wept with relief.

The parents who had lost theirs broke down completely.

Only the dead man lay in silence, and even tearing him to pieces would not have been enough.

The sky above remained black and utterly lightless. Under that endless darkness, there would only be more killing.

Adrian took Pip from her arms, then crouched so she could climb onto his back. The two of them left first.

When they returned to the villa, Whitmore came over at once to ask what had happened. Evelyn told him everything, leaving nothing out.

“Beast,” he said through clenched teeth. “Lower than an animal.”

His hands shook the entire time. His eyes were red with fury.

“Hang his corpse at the Administration Hall. Let everyone see what became of him.”

Nathan and Secretary Bennett both nodded.

When Calvin came back, he no longer dared leave Wendy alone in the house. Wherever he went, he carried her on his back. The survivors with children at home no longer dared be careless either.

The toxic haze had become a perfect cover for crime. As long as it remained, the fear hanging over everyone’s heads would remain as well.

Nathan and Secretary Bennett dragged the corpse to the front of the Administration Hall and hung it upside down from a pillar with thick rope.

Some people came to whip it and vent their anger.

Others had different thoughts.

The next day, the body was gone.

Evelyn’s maize had fully ripened. When she picked it and weighed it, there turned out to be more than thirty kilos.

Some could be kept for seed. After turning over the soil, she planted sweet potatoes and potatoes in its place.

After that incident, the base grew noticeably calmer.

Calvin’s name spread everywhere. People kept coming to him, asking him to organise a defence force for the base. In return, they were willing to pay a little tax from whatever food they managed to grow.

Calvin asked everyone for their opinion.

Evelyn said yes, though she admitted she was not suited to that kind of work.

He asked Whitmore and the other two as well, and they strongly supported the idea. Since leaving was not an option for now, the base needed order and stability. Most importantly, every child had to be protected.

In the end, Calvin selected thirty people to work in three shifts and set up a temporary security team. He also hired two extra people to cook for them.

That way, everyone’s safety was protected, and the security team had a means of earning a living too.

The amount each survivor had to hand over was small. There were not many guards after all. Every two or three months, one cabbage or half a bowl of wheat per person was enough.

Calvin only handled the recruitment. The rest he left to Nathan and Secretary Bennett.

They were the ones who truly knew how to run things.

The base also introduced a new set of rules. No fighting. No bullying the weak.

The elderly were gathered together and given seeds as well, so they could support themselves.

After one of the regular light rains, Whitmore organised people to build a number of water reservoirs out of wet mud. Rainwater was collected there, then filtered with plant ash.

“It almost feels,” someone said, “as though things are getting better again.”

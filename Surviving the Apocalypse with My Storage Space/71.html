Chapter 71 On the Road of Escape: Part 2

"When we first left Elaris, why didn't they tell everyone this?" Ray asked. "Why wait until we're halfway there to announce it with half temptation, half threat?"

"Because by the time we've made it this far," Eddie replied, "everyone is exhausted. Whether they have cars or are walking, they're spent. Now we're stuck. We can't move forward easily, and we can't turn back."

He glanced out the window.

"Going back to Elaris isn't even an option. It's probably burned to the ground by now. By revealing it at this point, they know we'll compromise. And for the remaining half of the journey, they can squeeze us even harder."

"That's psychological abuse," Evan muttered. "Like manipulation. You want to break free, but you have no choice except to cling to the very thing controlling you."

You might think he was just some spoiled rich kid with no real ability.

But in truth, people like him had already arranged the fates of thousands.

As expected, over the next two days, the convoy's management became far stricter.

Hayden clearly treated the several thousand refugees as his personal labor force. One wave of his hand and everyone was clearing roadblocks. Another wave and they were pushing or lifting vehicles.

The gun at his waist almost seemed unnecessary.

Those who had tried walking ahead on their own were eventually overtaken by the convoy. Their faces were numb, but their eyes followed the vehicles with naked hunger.

Along the road, the only animals they saw were giant rats, aside from the single gray rabbit Evelyn had caught.

The sky was empty. Not even a bird's shadow.

As temperatures dropped, daylight shortened. Night stretched to fifteen hours.

"In a few more hours, we'll reach Shattered Ridge," Eddie said, spreading the map across his knees. He circled the area with his finger.

"Before the apocalypse, it was a wildlife reserve. Eighteen mountains linked together. There was a livestream explorer who went deep inside for a broadcast. Once he entered the inner mountains, he vanished. They sent out a lot of police to search for days, but never found him."

He folded the map.

"Online, people said he was eaten by a black bear."

"Pretty sure the bears are all dead by now," Ray chimed in.

Eddie snorted. "Those things are tougher than humans. Humans aren't extinct yet. Why would the bears be?"

He stared at the landscape sliding past the window, worry creeping into his expression.

"Does anyone else feel cold? A few days ago it was scorching. Now it's freezing. This crazy weather is going to kill us."

He dug out a black jacket from his bag and pulled it on.

Noticing that Evelyn remained tightly wrapped from head to toe, he could not resist teasing.

"Evelyn, you can take that scarf off your head. Aren't you afraid of getting lice?"

Evelyn paused.

Her scalp had been itching a little.

"No."

"You'd better check. If it's bad, I can shave your head for you."

"I said no."

Eddie raised his hands in surrender. "Fine, fine. My mistake. It's my head that has lice. So, Dr. Foster, could you please shave me bald?"

"I'm busy," she replied coldly.

Calvin watched them with amusement. He pulled a dagger from his pocket and flicked it open in front of Eddie.

"I'll do it for you."

Eddie hurriedly leaned back. "My bad. I was joking. I need my hair to stay warm."

Wendy covered her mouth and giggled in Calvin's arms.

After half an hour of rest, the convoy moved again. The man relaying orders shouted that they had to reach Shattered Ridge before nightfall.

A bad feeling crept into Evelyn's chest.

At six in the evening, darkness fell.

The convoy stopped at the foot of the first peak of Shattered Ridge. Evelyn lifted her binoculars and stared at the dense, shadowy forest for a long time.

"This is the first peak," the man with the whip announced loudly. "Used to be an ecological botanical reserve. Rich vegetation. Plenty of animals. Mr. Hayden says you're free to move around. Go into the mountains, hunt if you want. Just one rule. We depart at five sharp tomorrow morning."

"What is this Hayden playing at?" Eddie muttered.

Evelyn's brow lifted slightly. "He's tossing out bait."

"Bait?"

"If there really are bears in there, the first group going in becomes their food. He gets to sit back and reap the benefit."

She continued calmly, "And if there are capable fighters among the refugees, he can recruit them."

"Twisted," Eddie said angrily. "Is this how rich people entertain themselves?"

"Wouldn't know," Evelyn replied with a shrug. "I've never been rich."

"Me neither," Calvin sighed. "My monthly salary was only a few thousand."

"I've never even started earning yet," Ray grumbled.

"I only got ten dollars a day in allowance," Evan added solemnly.

Even Wendy shook her head sadly.

Eddie snorted. "As if I'm some rich guy. I didn't even own a house or a decent car."

By then, many people were already getting out of their vehicles, eager to try their luck in the mountains. Maybe not a black bear or a deer. At least a rabbit.

"Should we get out too?" someone asked.

Evelyn nodded. "Everyone else is. We should too. Never be the one who stands out."

She glanced at them. "You build a fire next to the car. I'll take a look inside the mountain."

Eddie stared at her. "Didn't you just say not to stand out?"

"I can make sure I come back alive. Can you?" she asked quietly.

"I'll go with you."

"You'll slow me down."

"Then let Calvin go."

"Calvin has to watch Wendy and guard the car. Me leaving alone won't draw attention. Just wait for roasted rabbit."

She tightened her backpack and gripped her dagger.

"Be careful," Eddie said finally. "Don't stay too long."

"Two hours max."

Dressed in black, Evelyn slipped into the forest and melted into the night.

With her night vision goggles on, figures a hundred meters away were clear as day.

In the mountains, dead trees were sprouting again at their roots. Animal tracks marked the ground. Deeper inside, she spotted droppings left behind.

She crouched, studying the dark pellets carefully.

Truthfully, she could not tell whether they belonged to a goat, a rabbit, or a muntjac. She only cared about how hard they were, estimating how long ago the animal had passed through.

Voices drifted from somewhere nearby.

She dropped the now hardened pellets, wiped her hands in the dirt, and slipped away through the brush.

Based on the dryness, the droppings were at least four or five days old.

Which meant one thing.

There were still wild animals in these mountains.

Hopefully, just small ones like rabbits.

If it was a black bear, someone was going to get hurt.

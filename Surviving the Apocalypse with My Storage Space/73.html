Chapter 73 On the Road of Escape: Part 4

Evelyn lay flat for nearly twenty minutes. The elk she had been hoping for never appeared. Instead, a herd of wild buffalo came crashing into view.

They were massive, jet black, with long, curved horns that caught the light. These were nothing like ordinary water buffalo or cattle. Their bodies were taller, thicker, built like moving walls of muscle. Evelyn counted seven of them. After a brief pause, she decided to create a distraction and lure one or two into the nearby forest.

"Stay here. Don't make a sound." She gently patted the little squirrel's head and pointed toward the forest.

"I'm heading over there. I'll be back soon. If anything goes wrong, just run. Thanks, little guy."

With her crossbow in hand, Evelyn sprinted toward the trees. The lead buffalo immediately caught the noise and charged after her.

The moment she confirmed it was following, she slipped her scarf into her spatial storage and ducked behind a massive boulder. Three buffalo entered the forest. They were alert, each one scanning a different direction. She had to kill fast and clean. Wild buffalo were notoriously aggressive, huge enough that even lions or tigers might hesitate to challenge them.

Cold sweat dampened her palms. She began counting down in her head.

"Ten, nine, eight, seven…"

The lead buffalo snorted, thick breaths blasting from its nostrils as it searched for her scent.

Her shoe brushed against a fallen leaf. The faint rustle sliced through the silence. The buffalo locked onto her position and began advancing slowly.

Each one weighed close to a ton. They could sprint at terrifying speed. One sweep of those horns could end a life instantly.

She could not afford a frontal clash.

The lead buffalo stopped about twenty meters away. Its tail lashed impatiently. Evelyn raised her crossbow and steadied her aim.

The instant it shifted as if to turn, she fired. The bolt shot forward and pierced straight through its skull. Without hesitation, she released a second arrow.

The leader crashed to the ground. The other two panicked, but she gave them no chance to escape. One shot each, clean and lethal. Three enormous bodies collapsed among the trees.

She waited three full minutes, making sure they were truly dead before approaching. She retrieved her bolts and examined the carcasses. All three were young adult males. Prime condition. The meat would be excellent.

There was no time to skin or butcher them today. She stored the bodies in her space, wiped the blood from her hands with dirt, and returned to the edge of the swamp.

The remaining four buffalo finished drinking and moved toward the forest, searching for the ones that had disappeared. Evelyn had already erased the signs inside. When they failed to find their missing companions, they would not linger long.

She washed her hands thoroughly and spotted the little squirrel still crouched in the cattails. Smiling faintly, she walked over and scooped it up.

"You're still here? Tough little guy."

She took out a packet of sunflower seeds and opened it, holding it out for the squirrel.

"I'm heading out. Stay safe here. Maybe we'll meet again."

Evelyn reached out to pat the squirrel's head and was about to set it down when it suddenly leapt onto her back, all four paws clinging tightly to her.

"You want to come with me?"

The little squirrel opened its mouth and nipped her finger, then bounced against her back again.

Evelyn sucked in a breath, baring her teeth from the sting, and quickly pulled it off.

"You can't come with me. Where I'm going might be even more dangerous than this."

The squirrel shoved its head against her coat, trying to burrow inside as if it already knew that was the safest place to hide.

Her brow lifted. "You've gotten clever, huh? You even know to hide inside my jacket? I'll give you another bag of sunflower seeds. Stay here and live a good life, okay?"

The squirrel refused to budge. It was determined to stick with her. Evelyn let out a quiet sigh and finally gave in.

Even though its tail was long and fluffy, its body was only about the size of a four-month-old kitten. Tucking it inside her coat would not be much trouble.

She pulled out the canvas bag she had used earlier for wild vegetables, placed the squirrel inside, and hung it in front of her chest. After checking the time, she nodded to herself. It was about time to head back.

She moved quickly on the return trip. As she descended the mountain, she heard distant shouting. Someone must have taken down a large animal. She avoided them, circling around and heading straight down the slope.

When she neared the convoy, she saw Calvin and Eddie sitting beside a small fire near the vehicles.

"I'm back."

The two men immediately stood.

"Did you run into any danger?"

Evelyn shook her head. "No. By the way, I found water. We'll split into two groups. I'll take you up to collect it. Put the buckets inside your bags. Don't let anyone notice."

She kept her voice low so only Calvin and Eddie could hear.

They stared at her in shock for several seconds.

"Hurry."

She had originally planned to mark the route and let them find it on their own. But the deep mountain paths were complicated, and she worried they might get lost. When she had left earlier, she had not carried anything obvious with her. Now she grabbed another bucket. In her luggage, she still had a five-kilogram flat plastic water container. She stuffed it into her backpack and headed back into the mountains with Eddie and Ray.

The little squirrel still hung from her chest. She had tried to leave it in the vehicle, but the moment it was away from her, it grew anxious and curled tightly against her. She could not bring herself to abandon it.

"What are those people yelling about?" As soon as they entered the mountains, the distant shouts reached them again. Ray could not help asking.

"They probably ran into something big. We'll go this way. Ignore them."

Evelyn led from the front. Her stamina was solid. After running all night, she had not complained once. Eddie and Ray, on the other hand, were already red faced and gasping after climbing just one stretch of slope.

"Evelyn, you've got the makings of special forces," Eddie called out, envy thick in his voice as he stared at her back.

"Are you saying you don't?"

Eddie choked.

"How is that possible? If I want water, I have to be capable. Even if I die, I'll die after I get a drink."

With two burdens trailing behind her, their pace slowed considerably. Along the way, they ran into a rabbit. Ray and Eddie insisted on showing off their skills. The rabbit escaped without difficulty.

Evelyn chose not to comment and kept moving.

When they reached the swamp and saw the clear water rippling gently among the rushes, Eddie suddenly dropped to his knees with a soft thud.

"I could die happy right now."

Evelyn ignored him. After filling the water container, she scanned the surroundings carefully. There were fresh buffalo tracks near the grass. It looked like the remaining animals had returned once.

The two men filled every bucket, bottle, and cup they had. At the far downstream edge, Eddie even washed his feet. Ray followed his example.

Evelyn checked the time and urged them on.

"Move. It's time to go."

"Why don't we just stay here?" Eddie said reluctantly. "There's mountains and water. We wouldn't starve."

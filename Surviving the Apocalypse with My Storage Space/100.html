Chapter 100 Whirlpool and the Python

A fireball plunged straight into the underground stream.

The calm surface exploded instantly. Water shot into the air. The tail of the inflatable kayak was splashed hard.

Rowan rowed fast, even in this chaos. In the same instant, he twisted around and shoved Evelyn down flat against the kayak, shielding her from the force of the blast.

Evelyn hooked one hand around the edge of the kayak and clutched Sprig and Fallon tightly against her chest with the other.

Boom!

Another explosion tore through the water.

This time, the surface actually caught fire. Flames flickered across it, streaked with faint blue.

A wave of heat rolled toward them. At that exact moment, the kayak shot through a narrow passage and into another branch of the cave system.

"Rowan, something's moving under the water."

He had clearly sensed it too. But his expression did not change. His strokes remained steady.

"There's something below us."

Something?

Before she could process what he meant, the disturbance beneath them intensified. The current began twisting, forming a whirlpool that tried to drag both kayak and passengers downward.

She reached instinctively to pull off the black cloth over her eyes, but the pain was still unbearable. She could not open them.

"Hold on to me," Rowan said calmly.

He shifted direction and began rowing against the pull of the whirlpool.

Evelyn grabbed the hem of his clothes with one hand. With the other, she pulled out her gun, ready to fire at any second.

She could not see, but her hearing was sharp. She could judge danger by sound alone.

She pressed her body low against the kayak to reduce resistance as they fought upstream. Water slapped against them in heavy bursts. A thick, fishy stench rose from the mist, enough to make her nauseous.

The suction below felt immense, like a black hole beneath the surface.

Then suddenly, bang!

The kayak launched upward, thrown more than thirty feet forward.

In the next second, it ruptured.

The craft burst apart. Evelyn and Rowan were flung into the water in different directions.

In mid fall, Evelyn cinched the cloth bag tight and looped it around her neck. The moment she hit the water, she swam forward with everything she had.

She heard Rowan calling her name.

She shouted back, locking onto his position by sound.

Behind her, the suction intensified.

She cursed herself for never buying a grappling hook.

The cave walls on both sides were slick and vertical. There was no way to climb. They could only swim forward.

Pulling out another kayak now would only make the suction worse.

Her clothes were soaked. Without hesitation, she stripped off her outer coat mid swim to lighten herself.

She was fast in the water. Within seconds, Rowan reached her side and dragged her ahead of him.

There was no time for words. They swam hard.

After about thirty meters, they finally broke free of the whirlpool's pull.

But before they could breathe in relief, another disturbance rippled beneath them.

Evelyn heard a sound that did not belong.

Rowan had once mentioned a hibernating giant python in the mountains.

"There's a snake," they said at the same time.

She held her breath, straining to pinpoint its position.

Rowan did not hesitate. He kept pulling her forward.

At some point, he had taken the cloth bag from her and was holding it above the water, keeping Sprig and Fallon safe.

Even now, he had not abandoned her.

He had not abandoned the two little ones either.

A strange feeling rose in her chest.

She had wanted to kill him more than once.

Maybe she had been narrow minded.

"I'm hungry," Rowan suddenly said out of nowhere.

Evelyn almost laughed.

"I'm hungry too."

She gulped down a mouthful of filthy water.

It burned all the way down.

"Cough, cough, cough… ugh—"

Her face went pale. The black cloth over her eyes had been washed away. Her eyes were swollen, bloodshot, vision blurred and aching. She coughed and retched, looking utterly wrecked.

Suddenly Rowan's hand pressed hard against her stomach.

She guessed he was trying to help and didn't fight him.

He pushed twice, firm and precise. She gagged and spat up the dirty water she had swallowed.

"Thank you." Her voice was hoarse. From the cold, her skin had already turned bluish purple.

"Don't let go of me."

He swung her onto his back again. She clung tightly to him, her hand brushing the cloth bag tied above his head.

The two little ones were still alive.

A violent surge rammed into them from below.

Evelyn's expression shifted. "The snake's on the right. It's coming up!"

She raised her gun. After yanking on her night vision goggles, she blinked hard. Everything was blurry, but she could make out a massive disturbance in the water.

"There's a small rock platform ahead!"

Rowan abruptly changed direction.

At that exact moment, a massive force exploded upward from beneath them. A giant tail lashed out, coiling around both of them and lifting them from the water.

Evelyn pulled the trigger.

Bang!

She missed.

The bullet scraped across black scales and ricocheted into the stone wall.

"Give it to me."

Rowan held out his hand. She shoved the gun into it.

The tail tightened and flung them sideways. Her body slammed into the rock wall, then crashed back into the water. The impact knocked the air from her lungs. The pain made her lose her grip.

She sank.

Rowan had already broken the surface and was firing at the python.

She swallowed another mouthful of foul water before fighting her way back up, pressing herself against the slick rock wall.

Forget the gun.

She pulled out her crossbow instead. That was what she handled best.

The python surged from the water.

It was twice the size of the one she had killed back in the residential complex. Black scales glistened under the faint light. When it opened its mouth, four rows of jagged teeth gleamed inside.

The tail whipped toward her again.

She barely dodged. Even so, it clipped her back. Pain flared hot and sharp, snapping her fully awake.

"Rowan! I know how to kill it!"

She dragged out a case of compressed gas canisters. Twelve in total.

When he reached her side, she shoved the crossbow, the canisters, and a lighter into his hands.

"Light them and throw them into its mouth!"

He took them.

Then with his free hand, he grabbed her and hurled her away.

"You go first."

She nearly swore out loud.

I can barely see. How am I supposed to go anywhere?

But she didn't argue.

She swam without hesitation.

After several desperate strokes, she spotted a larger rock ledge ahead and clawed her way onto it.

Behind her—

Boom. Boom. Boom.

Explosions thundered through the cavern.

She dropped flat and covered her ears.

After what felt like forever, the noise finally faded.

Slowly, she pushed herself upright.

"Rowan! Rowan!"

She called his name over and over.

No answer.

A cold weight settled in her chest.

A terrible premonition.

She slumped onto the rock platform, staring blankly at the water.

Her stomach churned violently. She vomited up sour water.

Somewhere in the chaos, her boots had been torn away. She was left in soaked thermal layers that clung to her body, dripping.

Her limbs were going numb. A hammering pain pounded inside her skull.

"Are you hurt?"

The voice came from her left.

She snapped her head toward the sound.

"Rowan?"

"It's me."

"You're alive?"

"Yeah."

Chapter 90 He Has Heterochromia

Evelyn gritted her teeth and forced her dislocated arm back into place.

The pain was so intense her eyes watered instantly. She crouched there for a long moment, breathing through it, before slowly approaching the unconscious man again.

She did not dare relax.

One hand pressed a dagger firmly against his throat. With the other, she shoved aside his long, filthy hair. Mud caked his face. Without hesitation, she pried his eyelids open.

One eye was blue and the other was red.

Heterochromia.

She stared at him, her emotions tangled.

In her previous life, she had heard countless rumors about people with different colored eyes. They were said to be anything but ordinary. She did not know if the heterochromia bearer captured by the base back then was this same man, but judging from what she had just witnessed, he was clearly not normal.

She stood and searched the forest until she found a massive pit.

Returning to him, she grabbed him under the arms and began dragging.

It took her over a hundred meters to reach the edge.

From her space, she took out the last of her poison. She forced his mouth open and poured it down his throat.

Then she shoved him into the pit.

To cover her tracks, she erased her footprints in the snow. She went back to retrieve her crossbow, gun, and dagger, then ran straight toward her cabin.

Once inside, she braced her hand against her chest, gasping for air.

Sprig and Fallon approached her, but she had no energy to comfort them. She locked every door and window, then pulled out medicinal herbs from her space and began preparing more poison.

Her arm throbbed where it had been dislocated. She soaked a towel in hot water and pressed it against the swelling. Though she had forced the joint back into place, the area was already inflamed.

He had been brutal, so had she.

But this was the first time she had encountered someone that terrifying.

It unsettled her deeply.

She had saltpeter stored in her space. She decided to make gunpowder. Mixed with sulfur and charcoal in proper proportions, it would not be as powerful as modern explosives, but it would be more than enough to kill a person.

She was convinced the heterochromia bearer had come from the cavern.

That day, when she entered the cave, had she accidentally released him?

Regret crept in.

If she had known her curiosity would bring this kind of trouble, she would have stayed away.

Sprig and Fallon watched her restless movements with wide eyes. Sensing her tension, they wisely kept their distance.

She had not expected the first day of the new year to unfold like this.

That night, she slept poorly.

Just before dawn, she finally drifted off, only to fall into a nightmare.

In the dream, the man she had thrown into the pit crawled out, covered in blood. He dragged himself to her cabin, strangled Sprig and Fallon, then reached for her with filthy hands.

She jolted awake.

The familiar room steadied her racing heart.

He was dead.

He had to be.

The gunpowder was not ready yet, but she had brewed a fresh batch of poison. Even if he somehow survived, if he dared to return, she would make sure he never left again.

She hung binoculars around her neck. At the slightest movement outside, she would be ready.

One day passed.

Then two.

Then three.

Everything remained quiet.

She became certain the poison had done its job. Though part of her felt cold for what she had done, she was overwhelmingly relieved he was gone.

"Sprig, Fallon, I've been too busy these past few days. Let's watch something together today."

She took out her tablet and played Sprig's favorite cartoon.

Outside, heavy snow drifted down.

Inside, the cabin was warm and peaceful.

Far away, at the bottom of the forest pit, the man opened his eyes.

Confused, he stared at the sky above him.

*****

On the fifth day of the new year, the snow finally stopped.

Evelyn decided it was time to process the cowhide. Leather was warm and durable. Perfect for making shoes.

She hung the hide up and pulled out a blowtorch, carefully burning away the remaining hair.

As usual, Sprig hovered nearby, curious about everything she did. She pushed it away. It came right back.

"If you keep bothering me, I'll burn your tail off and turn you into a bald squirrel."

Sprig seemed to understand the look in her eyes and reluctantly backed off.

Satisfied, Evelyn hummed and continued torching the hide.

"Chi chi… chi chi…"

"What now? Can you be quiet for a minute? I'm busy. I'll play with you later."

"Chi chi… chi chi…"

The sound grew sharp and urgent.

Evelyn looked over and saw Sprig's fur standing on end.

It was a warning.

She turned toward the fence, and froze.

Five days ago, she had poisoned him and thrown him into a pit.

Now he was standing outside her fence, alive.

She only froze for a second.

The next moment, she grabbed the crossbow hanging outside the cabin and fired.

He caught the bolt again.

Then he leapt.

Cleared the fence in a single bound, at least four or five meters high, landing inside her yard.

And started walking toward her.

"Stop. Don't come any closer."

Her voice rang sharp across the snow.

She had seen it clearly. That jump was not human.

She raised the blowtorch and ignited it. Flames roared to life between them.

"Who are you?"

He tilted his head slightly.

Said nothing.

She pushed the flame higher. He took a small step back.

"Get out. Get out!"

"H… hungry."

The word came slowly, like he had not spoken in years.

For a second she thought she had misheard.

She blasted the flame toward him again.

"Get out!"

"Hungry."

He moved closer.

That familiar motion.

Like he was about to start sniffing her again.

Was he planning to eat her?

She waved the torch wildly.

And then he vanished from her front.

A blur of movement swept past her shoulder.

Before she could react, he had grabbed Sprig from the corner.

It was so fast it felt like a gust of wind.

"Chi chi!"

Sprig shrieked, tail stiff, fur puffed up.

Fallon flattened itself against the ground in terror.

Evelyn's heart dropped into her stomach.

She rushed forward.

"Say that again. What do you want?"

"Hungry."

He turned his mismatched blue and red eyes toward her. They were calm and empty.

She swallowed her fury and forced her voice softer.

"I have food. I'll give you food. Give me back my pet."

She could not beat him.

Blades did not kill him. Bullets did not stop him.

She was just flesh and bone.

Sometimes survival meant bending.

He ignored her words.

Instead, he sat down on the cabin steps, Sprig still in his grasp, roughly petting it with heavy hands.

Evelyn ran inside and grabbed a plate of leftover braised pork.

She brought it out carefully.

"Food. Here."

He leaned forward and sniffed it.

Then, casually, he tossed Sprig through the air.

The little creature landed in Evelyn's arms.

She exhaled in relief.

Scooping up Fallon from the ground, she rushed both of them into the bedroom and shut the door behind her.

Chapter 145 Toxic Haze 7

A year slipped by in no time, and still there was no sign of the toxic haze lifting.

Most of the wood beyond the Safe Zone had already been picked clean. The temperature continued to hover between thirteen and sixteen degrees.

The rats had long since been wiped out by relentless hunting. Even so, some people had bred quite a few indoors, and before long several shops in the Safe Zone were selling rat meat.

Evelyn and Adrian still kept a measured distance from everyone else. Life in the Safe Zone was still hard, but as its rules and routines gradually settled into place, the survivors had, for once, managed to hold on to a sliver of peace.

It still rained lightly once every three days. The stored water was enough to drink, but only just. Washing your face was a luxury. A proper bath was out of the question.

Over the course of that year, many elderly people with no family quietly passed away. The younger children, under Secretary Bennett and Nathan’s guidance, grew up quickly.

They could now go farther afield to gather firewood. These days firewood could be sold inside the Safe Zone, and it fetched a higher price than vegetables.

Evelyn remained utterly absorbed in growing medicinal herbs. She rarely went out, and there was even a Do Not Disturb sign hanging on her door. Still, whenever someone needed treatment, she never turned them away.

Her price had not changed either: one cabbage or half a bowl of wheat.

Adrian went hunting from time to time. Evelyn had no idea how far he travelled. All she knew was that he always came back with something.

After more than a year without sunlight, Evelyn found that the skin hidden beneath her clothes had turned frighteningly pale, as though she were suffering from some incurable illness.

Whenever her hair grew long, she cut it short again. She had never once let it grow out.

Mayor Whitmore and the other two had managed to put together several more handcarts. In the nearby hills they had discovered a coal seam and planned to dig it out to use as fuel in place of wood.

The mine was large enough for everyone to work it. Calvin and Eddie had both spent several days there, each returning with a small cart full of coal.

Wendy, however, had all the luck. While digging coal with Calvin, she stumbled across a nest of wild eggs. She brought them back, and two days later an entire brood of chicks hatched, thirty in all. Evelyn traded her for two and kept them on the ground floor. She already had plenty of fertilised chicken, duck and goose eggs in her space, but until now she had never had a suitable excuse to bring them out. Thanks to this little opportunity, she could finally raise chicks in plain sight.

“Adrian, look. What do you think these are?”

He glanced at them. “Birds?”

“Wrong. Chicks. I’m handing them over to you. You’re responsible for feeding them from now on.”

Adrian looked at Evelyn with complete seriousness. “When they’re grown, can we eat them?”

“Of course. Though we could keep them for eggs as well.”

At last, his otherwise dull life had found a bit of novelty. Adrian threw himself into raising chicks.

Animals also grew quickly in the toxic haze. In about a month, the two hens Evelyn had picked were fully grown. But after waiting ages, they still showed no sign of laying. In the end, Evelyn took out some fertilised eggs from her space and decided to hatch another brood herself.

The two grown hens naturally ended up in Adrian’s stomach.

Eggs that should have taken more than twenty days to hatch broke open in just seven, and not a single one was lost.

And so Evelyn’s chicken farm was officially established. Adrian, naturally, became its manager. With forty or fifty birds in total, every single one was fed and cared for by him.

Once people in the Safe Zone heard that Evelyn was raising so many chicks, they began turning up with wheat and corn to barter. She refused to trade any from the first batch, but by the second, each household was allowed two.

Wendy was utterly baffled. Why was it that the chicks she had raised neither laid eggs nor hatched any? To cheer her up, Evelyn swapped two with her. Sure enough, the very night they arrived, the birds started laying.

Clara Bennett also traded Evelyn for two chicks. Before long, her place had turned into a chicken farm as well.

Then one day, Toby came shouting at the top of his lungs.

“Mr Whitmore, this is bad, really bad!”

Everyone assumed something awful had happened and rushed outside.

“Toby, what is it?” someone asked.

“People. A lot of them. They say they want to come into our Safe Zone. I told the others to stop them, then ran back to find Mr Whitmore.”

A lot of people?

There were still people coming to Blackwater Safe Zone?

Quite a few were curious, but Mayor Whitmore became wary at once. He took two teams of security guards with him. Armed with sticks, hoes and whatever else they could grab, the group headed out in force.

At the entrance to the Safe Zone, several children stood there gripping sticks, blocking a group of ragged strangers in tattered clothes.

“Where are you from? What are your names? Why have you come here? How many of you are there?”

One of the children fired off the questions in a rapid stream. The group facing them looked grey with exhaustion, gaunt to the point of being little more than skin and bone. They answered honestly enough.

“We came from Langford. After the acid rain, the houses collapsed, all the trees died, and even the water ran out. We had no choice but to leave. We couldn’t see where we were going, so we just kept moving in the dark. We walked for half a year before we reached Elaris, but Elaris was in ruins as well. There was nothing to eat there. Then we walked another half year before finally ending up here. My son came ahead to scout and said he’d seen some houses. We thought the place was empty, so we tried to come in.”

The middle-aged man spoke plainly and without evasion. Behind him stood a young man and a young woman, his son and daughter. The rest were fellow refugees who had escaped alongside them.

By the time Whitmore and the others arrived, the children were already chattering over one another, recounting everything that had just happened.

The moment the man saw Whitmore, his face filled with shock.

“You’re Mayor Whitmore?”

Whitmore frowned. “And you are?”

The man’s voice thickened with emotion. “I used to work in a factory in Langford. You came to inspect it once. I remembered you.”

Langford was a small county town under Seabourne’s jurisdiction. The man named the factory and even remembered the year and month Whitmore had visited. That was enough for Whitmore to believe most of it.

“How many of you are there?”

“Twenty-five. Three elderly people and two children. When we first left, there were more than a hundred of us. Now only twenty-five remain. We can work, Mr Whitmore. Please, could you take us in? We truly can’t go any farther. Everyone’s feet are swollen beyond recognition, and we don’t know where else to go. You can search our bags. We aren’t carrying weapons, only a few hoes and sickles.”

Whitmore and the other two did not answer at once. After a brief discussion, they went to ask the others what they thought.

Perhaps it was the sight of the old people and the children, but many found themselves softening.

“Let them stay. Put them up near the Settlement Market. Those children are in a pitiful state.”

The children were clearly malnourished, with patchy hair faded yellow and white.

The elderly looked as though they barely had any life left in them. Still, there were plenty of people in the Safe Zone now, along with security guards, so no one was too worried that the newcomers might harm anyone.

Once their belongings had been checked, Whitmore let them in.

Evelyn only learned about it when Clara came to tell her. The next day, several of the newcomers came by carrying things to barter for supplies.

One glance was enough to tell they had suffered terribly on the road. Every face was drawn and bitter, every pair of eyes empty of light.

One of them offered their only unbroken bowl and spoon in exchange for a few corn seeds. Clara’s heart softened at once, and she made the trade on the spot.

After watching them for a while, it became clear that they meant no harm. Having finally found a place to rest, they too began trying to live properly again.

In the Safe Zone, some people died, while others crossed mountains and wilderness to reach it alive.

Chapter 138 Life Saving Supplies

Evelyn had been waiting for this moment for days. She wanted to see for herself just how much of a backup plan Nathan had really kept hidden.

At midnight, Evelyn and Adrian followed Nathan and Secretary Bennett out into the dark. All four of them had come prepared, carrying coils of rope and several sacks.

When Nathan led them into the Underground Quarter, Evelyn was genuinely surprised. She had not expected the supplies to be hidden there.

The Underground Quarter had been wrecked almost beyond recognition. Every floor tile had been prised up so people could dig at the soil underneath. Some of the pits were already more than ten metres deep. All the equipment had been stripped away, including the street lamps and even the rubbish bins.

Secretary Bennett had clearly taken a heavy blow from everything that had happened. Since waking, he had barely spoken. His hair seemed to grow whiter by the day. Mayor Whitmore was much the same.

After weaving through one turn after another, the four of them arrived at a laboratory. That place too had been emptied out. Watching Nathan’s back, Evelyn narrowed her eyes. He had hidden it well.

There was a hidden room inside the laboratory, but the supplies were not there. They were in a cellar beneath it.

Three layers of security later, they finally reached it.

Inside were sack after sack of rice and wheat, along with crate after crate of water.

Nathan looked exhausted. He turned to Evelyn and spoke with complete seriousness. “Take some for yourselves. The rest I want to distribute to the survivors who stayed behind. This is the last emergency grain and water reserve. The three of us can do no more than this.”

Evelyn had guessed as much, yet hearing him say it aloud still moved her.

The parasite incident had once left her with plenty of doubts about the way the base had been run. But seeing this now, she could not help thinking that official authority still carried weight after all.

“How are you planning to hand it out?” she asked. “If you carry it outside, the crowd will tear you apart.”

Nathan rubbed at his temples. “We’ll leave it here. Put up signs outside and make sure everyone knows. If we try to distribute it ourselves, there’ll be trouble. Besides, there’s too much to move.”

Evelyn nodded. She thought the plan was rather flimsy, but since they had already made up their minds, she did not object.

She and Adrian took away two sacks of rice, two sacks of wheat, and four crates of water.

Nathan and Secretary Bennett each carried two sacks on their backs, two more in their hands, and a crate of water balanced on one shoulder.

They made five trips in total, and there was still a great deal left inside. Evelyn wrote dozens of signs and had Adrian hang them up across the base, making sure everyone would see them.

When they returned to the villa, everyone was stunned by the sight of the food and water they had brought back.

“This belongs to all of us,” Evelyn said. “There isn’t enough to waste, so we’ll have to be careful with it. The road ahead is still going to be hard. I hope everyone keeps their spirits up, and that we get through it together.”

As soon as she finished, the others crowded round, touching the sacks and crates as if they hardly dared believe they were real.

“It really is grain.”

“The wheat smells incredible.”

“Evelyn, keep it with you,” Calvin said. “You can look after it.”

At that moment Nathan suddenly raised a hand.

“In that case, ours can stay with you as well.”

“No,” Evelyn said at once.

“The supplies for the three of you should stay in your own hands. If they’re kept with me, they may not be safe.”

Nathan looked as though he wanted to argue, but Whitmore stopped him.

“Listen to her.”

Evelyn smiled faintly, then went on. “Thanks to the three of you, we now have supplies. We’re grateful. This is life-saving food.”

“There’s still quite a lot left, but the others need to survive too. What remains belongs to the rest of the survivors.”

Everyone nodded in agreement. Evelyn had Adrian carry the supplies into the room, and after that the group dispersed.

By the next morning, word had spread to every corner of the base that food had been found.

Everyone rushed towards the Underground Quarter.

The first people to find the cache were five middle-aged men. To keep order, they armed themselves and organised the distribution. No one was allowed to take extra. Each person received five kilos of wheat, five kilos of rice, and six bottles of water.

Two days later, everything had been handed out.

There had been plenty of disputes along the way, but they were all settled in the end. The remaining three sacks of rice and five crates of water were divided equally between the five men.

After all, keeping order had not been easy.

Every survivor received food and water.

And with that, hope flickered back to life.

Over the following days, survivors started searching the base for hidden rooms, cellars, and secret passages, all in the hope of finding more supplies. But after that one cache, they found nothing. They dug and searched everywhere, yet never uncovered so much as another scrap.

Some people left one after another. Those who remained began the hard business of surviving in the ruined base.

The villa next door became vacant, and Nathan, Whitmore, and Secretary Bennett moved in.

The vegetables Calvin had planted in the sitting room began to push up tender green shoots. Pip and Lark were finally allowed out to roam a bit. Most of the time Wendy played with them, while everyone else kept going out to search for useful things. Even a single seed was worth keeping.

There was one piece of good news.

Half a month after the acid rain ended, a light shower fell without warning.

The rainwater was a bit dirty, but it was not poisonous. Survivors rushed out with buckets and basins to catch as much as they could. It only lasted a few hours, yet that rain gave everyone another sliver of hope.

People also found piles of tattered old clothes in all sorts of places. Once the dirt was beaten off, they could still be worn. At a time like this, no one cared whether clothes were clean. Warmth and basic cover were enough.

Nathan, Whitmore, and Secretary Bennett found the bodies of some of their dead subordinates. They dug graves outside the base and buried them as simply as they could.

Betrayed by their own people, reduced to this, and with countless others dead because of it.

Whitmore could not accept it.

He wanted to find the Hawthornes and the Burtons and take everything back. But they had a huge head start and were probably already thousands of miles away.

What was he supposed to do on two legs alone?

How was he meant to catch them?

How was he meant to take anything back?

“So what you’re saying,” Evelyn said, “is that if we leave, you want to travel with us?”

She had not expected Whitmore to come to her about this.

“Yes,” he said. “I know I’d be a burden. But Secretary Bennett and Nathan are both capable fighters. They know how to handle themselves. And I can see that among your group of nine, you’re the one everyone follows. If you agree to take us, the others won’t object.”

Evelyn smiled. “What makes you think I’d agree?”

“I can’t be certain that you will. But I can offer one piece of advice. The Northwest has unusual terrain, and more importantly, it is vast. There are no nuclear power stations there either.”

On that point, he and Evelyn happened to think alike.

But while Whitmore wanted to head northwest to hunt down the Hawthornes and the Burtons for revenge, Evelyn wanted only to survive and find somewhere fit to live.

“We can travel together,” she said, “but whatever it is you plan to do, I won’t be involved. None of us will.”

Whitmore looked puzzled. “Not even if there’s a great deal to be gained?”

“I don’t need it. I only want to stay alive. I didn’t save the three of you out of kindness. I did it because of what you had. Clear boundaries make life easier for everyone.”

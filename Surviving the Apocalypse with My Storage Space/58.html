Chapter 58 Heatwave, The Deal: Part 2

Right. She could also make more mung bean soup and iced glutinous rice balls.

There were still plenty of ingredients in her space, barely touched. Evelyn decided she might as well prepare enough ready-made food to last a year or two.

That night, just as she finished cleaning the kitchen, someone knocked.

Thankfully, she had moved the air conditioner into the bedroom. The living room was still sweltering. If someone knocked and she opened the door to a blast of cold air, she would have no way to explain it.

Eddie stood in the hallway. His face was crudely bandaged, making him look almost ridiculous.

"So this is what you look like?" His eyes widened in disbelief when he saw her clearly for the first time.

"Judging by your voice, I thought you'd be ugly. Sorry, that's not what I meant. I mean, your voice is so low and rough, deeper than mine. I figured you'd look plain. I didn't expect you to be this good-looking. Are you some kind of celebrity? An influencer?"

"What do you want?" Evelyn crossed her arms, expression icy.

"I wanted to ask. When are we going to get the stuff?"

She paused for a few seconds. "Give me the address. I'll go alone."

Eddie blinked. "You're not taking me?"

"Your foot's injured. Planning to slow me down again?"

"But what if you take everything and don't honor the deal?"

She let out a cold laugh. "I don't stoop to that. But if you lied to me, you'd better start picking out your burial plot."

"I never lie." He sounded both offended and helpless.

In the end, he gave her the address.

He knew he would only be a burden. Her going alone was the best option.

Back inside, Evelyn began preparing.

Living nocturnally had become second nature. She was practically a bat at this point.

The address Eddie gave was far. A round trip on foot would take at least three to four hours.

She switched into heavy Martin boots, thick and stiff. Beneath the insoles, she slipped in a thin metal plate to protect against nails or exposed rebar.

Fully covered from head to toe, she grabbed her keys and backpack and headed out.

She left at nine thirty. By the time she reached the high-rise residential building, it was already eleven thirty.

She wiped sweat from her face with a tissue and looked up.

Twenty-two floors.

This was going to kill her.

The area had already been searched by a Drakepool Base supply team. It had been stripped clean. Anything portable was gone. Only large furniture remained.

She headed straight for the twenty-second floor.

Her legs trembled. Her head felt light. Sweat kept dripping down her forehead no matter how often she wiped it away. Her clothes clung to her skin, suffocating.

The apartment door had been removed entirely. The floor was filthy, cluttered beyond belief.

She rested at the doorway for two minutes before stepping inside, carefully avoiding the worst of the debris.

In the kitchen, she pushed aside two large refrigerators.

Running her hand along the wall, she pulled out a knife and sliced through the wallpaper. Peeling it back revealed a small hidden door.

Three combination locks.

And a thick steel slab for a door.

Evelyn sighed and pulled tools from her space.

High-end locks often fell to the simplest, most direct methods.

Twenty minutes later, the door swung open.

Calling it a hidden room was generous. It was barely five square meters.

There were not many items inside.

But as Eddie had said, every single one was a weapon.

This trip had been worth it.

Evelyn decided she would give Eddie a slightly bigger share.

After sweeping every weapon into her space, she left immediately.

On the way back, she ran into two vultures.

Dawn was approaching. The vultures were out hunting.

They had probably not seen prey in a long time. They flapped excitedly, circling overhead. Their shrill cries cut through the wind.

Without changing her expression, Evelyn pulled her crossbow from her space.

Animals sensed danger sharply.

The instant she raised the weapon, both vultures dove at her.

She did not panic.

After several real fights, her accuracy had reached near-perfect precision.

She fired.

The bolt pierced one vulture's abdomen. It shrieked, a high, ragged sound of pain.

But she did not escape unscathed.

One of the vultures raked its talons across her shoulder and arm. The claws tore deep, leaving gashes that cut nearly to the bone.

Evelyn rolled into the sand and ducked behind a large rock.

Both vultures had taken a bolt, but one arrow would not kill them.

She had to finish it.

Blood streamed from her wounds. Gritting her teeth, she waited for the split second she needed.

When one of them swooped low again, she fired.

The bird crashed into the sand.

She lunged forward, drew her combat knife, and severed its head in one swift motion.

A fierce gust of wind swept over her.

She lay in the sand, staring up at the dark sky.

Both vultures were dead beside her.

Her vision blurred.

Sweat soaked into the sand beneath her. Blood still flowed steadily from her wounds. The burning pain faded into numbness.

She had to thank Calvin. Without months of speed and reaction training, she would not have survived tonight.

In the end, she was just an ordinary person.

She felt fear every time danger struck. She hesitated. She wanted to retreat.

But she had no choice except to push forward again and again, breaking through her own limits. Surviving one crisis after another.

Her hands trembled violently.

A dull ache spread through her chest. When the vulture had clawed her, its wing had slammed into her torso as well. Just once, but it felt like her organs had shifted out of place.

She pulled painkillers from her space and swallowed a handful.

A suppressed cough escaped her lips.

When the pain dulled enough, she forced herself upright and continued walking with her trekking pole.

The fight had cost her time.

Thankfully, the sky was not fully bright by the time she reached home.

"You're finally back."

Eddie was pacing the tenth-floor hallway. He jumped when she appeared.

"What are you doing here?"

He snorted lightly. "Worried about you. Wait. You're hurt?"

She waved him off before he could grab her arm.

"Minor injury. Why don't you ask about the weapons?"

He shook his head.

"If you got them, that's because you're capable. Right now, I just want you alive. We've crossed paths twice already. Your life matters more than that stuff."

For a moment, she did not know what to say.

"I got them," she said finally. "But I need to treat my wounds first. I'll bring your share later."

She glanced at the sweat soaking through his clothes and waved him off.

"Go rest. Don't stand here."

He nodded. "Alright. Take care of yourself first. I'm not in a hurry. Oh, and Calvin and the others are back. He told me once I'm healed, I can go out with him to look for supplies."

She nodded and went inside.

Once the door shut, she quickly cleaned her wounds.

Standing before the mirror, staring at the deep claw marks across her skin, Evelyn felt that familiar surge of relief.

She was still alive.

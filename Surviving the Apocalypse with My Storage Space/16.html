Chapter 16 Rainstorm, Venom Moths: Part 12

The sixteenth floor was a cafeteria.

The moment Evelyn stepped in, she froze.

Seven or eight skeletons lay collapsed on the floor, scattered like fallen mannequins. Tattered scraps of clothing still clung to the bones, though some had bits of rotted flesh and tendons still attached. Venomous moths swarmed over them, gnawing away in clusters. The air reeked of blood, rot, and a suffocating metallic stench.

One of the bodies at the far end still had a swollen arm attached.

Holding her breath, Evelyn carefully stepped around the corpses and made her way toward the kitchen. She wanted to check the pantry. If it hadn't been breached, she might be able to collect some supplies.

The floor was sticky with moth guts—like a layer of melted candy that clung to her boots and made every step heavy and slow.

The moment she entered the kitchen, a wave of decay hit her.

Inside a steamer, mold-covered buns sat in piles. A nearby pot of porridge was full of dead moths.

Thankfully, the dishes in the sterilizer cabinet were still clean. Evelyn inspected them, then stored them away without hesitation.

She moved to the pantry door—it was locked.

But it was only a wooden door.

Evelyn pulled an axe from her storage space and swung hard, splitting the door open with a loud crack. She reached through the gap and unlocked it from the inside. Moths swarmed toward the open entrance, but Evelyn quickly lit a pine torch and stuck it in the broken gap. The thick smoke blocked the doorway long enough for her to slip inside and shut the door.

The pantry was untouched.

More importantly, it was fully stocked.

Evelyn scanned the space quickly. Rice, flour, oil—everything was here. It was like walking into a compact supermarket.

There were dozens of bags of premium rice alone, each weighing a hundred pounds. In the back corner, she found a brand-new pressure cooker, still in its box.

She stored everything.

There was no rush. No need to leave right away.

She paused to spray more insecticide on her protective suit, changed into a fresh pair of hiking boots, doubled up her masks, and only then cracked the pantry door open again.

The pine torch had burned down, filling the hallway with thick, acrid smoke. The moths hovered three meters away, afraid to come any closer.

Grabbing a fresh torch, Evelyn stepped out of the cafeteria and continued upward.

On the seventeenth floor, she stopped in her tracks.

It was a medical herb storage.

Her eyes practically lit up.

She rushed to open the door, but the moment she stepped inside, her heart sank again.

More skeletons. A thick cloud of moths.

Still, she didn't waste time.

She headed straight for the shelves.

To her relief, the herbs were all sealed in airtight containers. Nothing had been contaminated.

Her first instinct was to take everything. But a thought struck her—what if, after the disaster worsened, the government sent out teams to collect medical supplies for survivors?

If they showed up and found the storage completely empty, what then?

But what if it wasn't officials who came? What if it was one of those post-disaster gangs? Criminals posing as survivors?

If the supplies fell into their hands, they'd only use them to exploit others.

Evelyn hesitated for a long time.

In the end, she decided to take most of the inventory, but not all of it. The remainder she hid carefully—out of the moths' reach, deep behind reinforced shelves.

When she finished storing everything, exhaustion hit her like a wave.

It was already midnight.

She'd been scavenging for five hours straight.

Still, she only let herself rest for a couple of minutes before moving again.

Next stop: the eighteenth floor.

This level held the pharmaceutical company's final product storage—a vault of ready-to-distribute medications.

Fortunately, like the herbal supplies, these had been sealed properly.

The damage here was minimal.

The finished medications had been shipped from the factory. There weren't that many, only a few dozen crates, but each one was valuable.

Hours passed.

By the time Evelyn reached the twenty-third floor, exhaustion had set in completely. More than the fatigue, though, was the biting cold.

She checked her thermometer—minus ten degrees Celsius.

Thunder rumbled in the distance. She walked to the window just in time to see a violet lightning bolt tear through the sky. Then came the storm.

CRACK!

The sharp sound of shattering glass echoed through the building. Evelyn knew it—lightning had struck a nearby window.

She finished raiding the department manager's office and slipped into a small break room to rest.

Peeling off her filthy protective suit, she gagged at the stench clinging to the fabric. Her stomach twisted. She washed her hands, then hooked up her electric heater to a backup battery. Warmth filled the room instantly.

She laid a mat on the ground and sat down cross-legged, staring at the glowing coils in the heater. But her mind wasn't on the warmth.

She was thinking—Could there still be survivors in this building?

After all, every floor since the cafeteria had held bodies.

Once the blood had returned to her fingers and toes, she pulled out a boxed meal and a lamb broth. Eating warmed her from the inside out and gave her a new wave of energy.

But Evelyn didn't continue looting right away.

She shut her eyes.

With fifty-eight floors to search, even at top speed, it would take her two or three more days to finish. No need to rush.

She woke up three hours later. Though it was already 8 a.m., the sky outside remained gray and dull—no different from night.

Packing away the heater and battery, she applied new heating patches, suited up again, and began a new day of scavenging.

The twenty-fifth floor was a gym. The once-luxurious equipment had been claimed by venomous moths, now riddled with holes and filth.

The twenty-sixth held a library and a coffee lounge.

Seeing the torn and ruined books made her sigh out loud.

On the twenty-seventh, she found a supply room untouched by moths. It was filled with office supplies—reams of paper, pens, ink.

Evelyn stared at the neatly packed boxes of blank paper and felt oddly comforted. She packed everything into her storage space, even the pencils and markers.

Coming out of the supply room, she glanced at the moths on the wall—and somehow, they didn't seem quite so revolting anymore.

By 7 p.m., her legs were starting to give out from exhaustion.

She looked at the floor plaque: 42F.

Kicking open the safety door, she staggered inside and picked a clean office to crash in.

She immediately pulled out the portable heater. The heating patches stuck to her body had turned stiff from the cold. Her hands were red, and her toes itched like crazy—early signs of frostbite.

Worried, she peeled off her shoes and socks and stuck her feet near the heater, then applied frostbite cream.

The warmth made her drowsy.

She dozed off almost immediately.

Half an hour later, she jerked awake, needing to use the restroom.

But then she thought of the moths in the bathroom.

Frustrated, she ran her fingers through her hair and sat there, contemplating her options.

By the time she returned to her makeshift room, she looked dead on her feet.

After dinner and a quick face rinse, she pulled out her sleeping bag and got ready to sleep again.

But just as she was about to crawl in, she froze.

Footsteps.

Someone was here.

She turned off the heater and held her breath, listening intently.

The steps echoed from down the hall. And it wasn't just one person.

As they drew closer, Evelyn could make out the voices.

"I'm cold and starving… I'm going insane," grumbled a young man, his voice tense and irritable.

"If only I hadn't stayed for overtime on April 1st," a woman sobbed. "If I'd believed the warning online and stayed home, none of this would've happened…"

Evelyn's eyes narrowed.

She swiftly packed up her belongings and took out a dagger, gripping it tightly in her hand.

Chapter 118 The Hand of Justice

Miss Hawthorne’s death swept through the base like a gust of wind, then vanished just as quickly.

Compared with gossip, people cared far more about having enough to eat, enough to wear, and some assurance that they would live to see the next day.

When warmth finally returned to the earth, Evelyn washed all the dirty clothes and hung them out to dry.

Adrian said the nearby prey had already been hunted out, so they would have to go farther afield now. The base had also tightened its hunting rules. Live capture was preferred wherever possible, and a live animal was now worth nine hundred points.

Once Adrian began going farther out, he was often gone three or four days at a time, sometimes five or six.

The teams turning the soil grew by the day, and stretches of wasteland beyond the base had already been reclaimed.

Evelyn brought Pip and Lark back, and while she was at it, she gave Eddie some medicine that might save his life one day.

Work in the Medical Division had now fallen completely into rhythm. Evelyn was beginning to count as one of the division’s key people.

Clara had taken her down to the Underground Quarter before. It was not much different from the surface. Everything there had been meticulously planned, growing areas, livestock areas, production areas, and so on.

Once the medicinal herbs were harvested from the growing plots, they were sent to the base factory and turned into medicine. Treatment at the base was expensive. Unless someone had a broken arm or leg, most people would rather endure their illness than come in to be seen.

What surprised Evelyn was that she spotted Sophie and Iris in the medicinal herb section. She had no intention of making herself known. Wrapped up as she was, she passed right by them, and neither of them even looked up.

Adrian had been out for three days on this trip. Evelyn watched the temperature jump from zero to eight degrees, and her unease only deepened.

She had the distinct sense that Heaven was plotting something cruel. Let desperate people see hope again, let them begin to look towards life and the future with expectation, then drop the fatal blow and send them plunging into despair once more.

The newly turned fields had already been sown with the first round of wheat. A husband and wife passed by Evelyn, chatting about how many points they had earned that day and how next month they would be able to buy their child a packet of sweets.

There were also residents from the dormitories dreaming of moving into a single room, or perhaps even into one of the better apartment buildings.

The next day, when Evelyn arrived at the Surgical Unit, Dr. Hargreaves told her that Daisy had been murdered.

Daisy was from Elaris too. She had been separated from her parents and younger sister, and ever since arriving at the base she had gone to the communal housing area every day to look for them.

She had always been frugal, saving every point she could bear not to spend, all in the hope that if she found her family again, she would be able to give them a better life.

“Yesterday she was telling me this month was her birthday. Once it came, she’d be twenty-six. I spent half the night thinking about what present to get her.” Clara broke down, folded over the desk and wept. “I can’t believe this.”

“Where did it happen? Have they found the killer? Where’s her body?”

“In the lavatory on the ground floor of the Settlement Market. They haven’t found the killer. Her body was taken away and cremated. The base has a crematorium. Once people die, they’re burned and buried in the cemetery out back. They call it a cemetery, but really they just throw the urns into one enormous pit.”

Dr. Hargreaves let out a long sigh. Daisy had worked here for four months. By now everyone knew her well.

“The women who were killed before, was it the same toilet on the ground floor of the Settlement Market? Men’s or women’s?”

“The women’s. That lavatory’s already been sealed off. No one expected another murder last night. It was only this morning that a member of staff spotted blood on the floor. They followed the trail and found the cubicle door open. Daisy was lying inside.”

Evelyn had not known Daisy particularly well, but from what she had seen, she had been quiet, gentle, and kind to everyone. She had no enemies. Which meant the person who had killed her was the same one who had murdered the earlier women.

“The base has issued every woman with a bottle of self-defence spray. Sign here and put your thumbprint there.”

Dr. Hargreaves took out two bottles and handed one each to Evelyn and Clara.

On the walk back after work, Clara had still not recovered from the shock of Daisy’s death. Every time a man passed them, she seemed to think he looked like the killer. Seeing how jumpy she was, Evelyn suggested she take a couple of days off, but Clara could not bear the thought of losing the points.

“Evelyn, let’s go to work together and come back together from now on. I’m really frightened. The base doesn’t seem to have any intention of opening a proper investigation. A living, breathing woman has been killed, and the murderer’s facing no punishment at all.”

Evelyn had no objection. Clara was a decent person, and sending her to and from work alone felt genuinely dangerous. She was far too vulnerable.

What Evelyn had not expected was to find Adrian already back the moment she reached the sixth floor. This time, after being away, the murderous air about him seemed even heavier. As always, he was standing outside 601 waiting for her. When Clara saw him, she could not resist teasing Evelyn.

“Honestly, why do you and your boyfriend rent two separate flats? What a waste. Every time he comes back he waits for you at the door. He’s like the most loyal little puppy.”

Loyal little puppy?

Evelyn rolled her eyes inwardly. He was not waiting for her, he was waiting for food.

She did not know how to answer, so she merely smiled. Clara skipped back to 603, while Evelyn walked over, unlocked her door, and let Adrian follow her inside before shutting it behind him.

“This trip went well. We can buy quite a lot. Let’s go tomorrow. I’m off tomorrow.”

As he spoke, Adrian hooked out his contact lenses one by one.

“Sounds as though you went a very long way this time.”

He nodded. “I haven’t eaten properly for days. Evelyn, let’s cook.”

Evelyn took out the ingredients, and Adrian set to work.

“The temperature’s rising. I’m worried something worse is coming next. Think about it for yourself. Do you want to change jobs?”

“I doubt the hunting crews would let me leave,” Adrian said. “I bring in half their prey on my own.”

Evelyn glanced at him sideways. “Show-off.”

“A nurse in our department was murdered. She was only twenty-five, young and hardworking, and she was assaulted and killed.”

There was a heaviness in Evelyn’s voice.

“Every place has its filthy corners. It’s revolting.”

“Where was she killed?”

“In the women’s lavatory on the ground floor of the Settlement Market.”

Adrian gave a slow, thoughtful nod.

After supper, he went back to his own flat. Though he had the next day off, Evelyn still had work in the morning. She fed Pip and Lark and went to bed early.

When she woke the next day, her complexion was pale. She glanced at the calendar and realised her period was due again.

“Evelyn, Clara, this is big.”

The moment they reached the Surgical Unit, Dr. Hargreaves rushed over and stopped them.

“Dr. Hargreaves, what’s happened?” Clara looked stricken. Terrified that another girl had been killed, her eyes were already red with panic.

“This morning, three bodies turned up in front of the Administration Hall. There were words carved into their faces. Their clothes had been stripped off, and that part of them had been cut off too. Each one had a placard hanging from his neck, written in red ink. It said these three were the men who assaulted those girls. A champion of justice found them out, killed them, and dumped them at the Administration Hall. It’s a direct slap in the base’s face. I’d wager every man here with filthy thoughts is shaking in his boots now.”

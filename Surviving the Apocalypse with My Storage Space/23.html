Chapter 23 Rainstorm, Venom Moths: Part 19

When Evelyn stepped into the Rivers home, the first thing she noticed was that there wasn't even a clean place to set her foot.

The living room was filthy and chaotic, the air thick with a disgusting stench. She adjusted her mask and followed Quinn into what used to be her and Zack's bedroom.

Evelyn checked her pulse. It was smooth and full, like pearls gliding on a string.

It was definitely a pregnancy pulse.

"From the look on your face… I really am pregnant, huh?" Quinn gave a hollow laugh. "Two years of trying, and nothing. Now, in the middle of a natural disaster, I finally get pregnant. Isn't that ridiculous?"

She gently touched her belly and stared blankly at the door. Her eyes were dull, lifeless. Something in Evelyn's gut twisted.

"You don't want it?"

Quinn looked up at her, a faint, almost dreamy smile on her lips.

"Zack gave me an ultimatum before. If I couldn't give him a child this year, he'd divorce me. Funny, right? Now he's missing, probably dead, and here I am, pregnant. His mom and sister used to mock me all the time, calling me useless for not getting pregnant. And now that I finally have good news… there's no one left to hear it."

Evelyn said nothing. Her eyes scanned the bedroom.

It was dirty and damp. The floor was covered in sand and muddy footprints. The walls were peeling and moldy from the moisture. Junk was piled in every corner. Even the bedding reeked of mildew.

"Evie… do you have any medicine?"

"No."

Quinn's voice turned desperate. "The rain never stops. I can't feed another mouth. If I give birth, it'll just bring more suffering. Please… help me get rid of it."

She opened a drawer and pulled out a small box, then reluctantly held it out to Evelyn.

"I'm begging you."

"I can't help with that." Evelyn's tone was flat. "I've seen your pulse. That's all. I'm leaving now."

Quinn's expression changed. "Then what am I supposed to do? How do I get rid of it?"

Evelyn didn't respond. She opened the door and stepped out, just in time to see David trying to calm a crying John.

"Evie, how did it go?" he asked.

Evelyn didn't answer right away. He handed her a compressed biscuit. Only then did she nod.

"She's pregnant."

"You can't give her our food!" John yelled, running over to snatch the biscuit from Evelyn's hand. "That's mine! Grandpa, we barely have enough to eat already. Why are you giving it to her?"

Evelyn's gaze turned cold.

Just then, Henry returned, soaked from going out to collect firewood. When he saw what was happening, he stepped in, pulling John behind him.

"Kids don't know any better. Evie, don't mind him. You came to check on my sister-in-law, right? How is she? Is she alright?"

John opened his mouth to protest again, but one sharp look from Henry made him shrink back in silence.

"She's fine. I'm heading back."

"Wait, Evie," Henry called after her. "Can I ask something? When you went out scavenging before… where exactly did you go?"

"The thing is," he continued without waiting for an answer, "the buildings around here are packed. All the supermarkets and malls nearby have been picked clean. My sister-in-law's pregnant now, Dad's getting old, and John's still young. I want to go out and gather more supplies. This rain feels like it's never going to stop."

"The factory," Evelyn replied.

"Are you and Claire going out again?" he pressed.

"No. It's about time. I need to get back."

She left without another word.

Back in her apartment, Evelyn locked the door behind her, then stripped off her protective suit.

The venom moths had thinned out recently, but going outside still required full gear. The rain came and went in bursts, and the temperature kept dropping. It had already fallen below minus thirteen degrees Celsius.

Evelyn folded her dirty clothes and placed them into the laundry bin. She washed her protective suit and hung it to air-dry on the balcony. In just this short stretch of time, she had already gone through two pairs of ruined rain boots.

She pulled back the curtains and looked outside. There were still plenty of people out scavenging for firewood. After the raider attack, many in Willow Heights had died. Even when neighbors passed each other now, no one had the heart to speak. Everyone kept their heads down, working fast, then hurrying home.

That afternoon, a young man on the eighth-floor balcony across from her shouted something into the air, then leapt to his death.

Scenes like that had become almost routine.

Since the disaster, the city had lost power, water, internet, and food supply. Rescue teams had only shown up twice, and hadn't been seen again since. Supplies were distributed once, and never again. In the endless cycle of hope and disappointment, many people's minds had begun to break.

Finally finding a quiet moment, Evelyn mopped the living room and disinfected everything. Then she lit a stick of mugwort incense.

In the bathroom sat a large trash bin. Evelyn made a habit of sealing each day's garbage into small bags before tossing them in. The next time she went out, she'd load the trash into her storage space and dispose of it far from home.

She still had plenty of pre-packed meals stocked in her spatial storage. She'd been rationing carefully. Since the power and water had gone out, she'd stopped cooking altogether. The smell of food could attract attention—and desperate people would do anything once pushed to the edge.

She pulled out a small plastic bucket and decided to sprout some mung beans. It wasn't much, but in times like these, even a little comfort mattered.

Later that afternoon, Calvin and his wife appeared at her door. Evelyn was surprised to see them both fully geared up. She hadn't expected Calvin to bring his wife along while scavenging.

"She didn't feel safe letting me go alone," he said with a faint smile. "And honestly, I want her to start adapting to the outside world too."

"You're not in good shape to be going out yet," Evelyn said, her tone kind but firm. "You should really stay in bed a few more days."

"I can't wait that long." Calvin's voice lowered. "Evie, what you guessed… it might be true. We need to prepare now."

He was referring to the risk of a dam collapse. There were several reservoirs around Elaris. If one broke, it would be catastrophic.

But even if they left… where could they go? And who could guarantee there was a safe place left out there?

"Be careful," Evelyn said sincerely.

"Of course. But before we go, we'd like to buy something from you."

Evelyn understood right away. "I've only got half a bottle left. I'll grab it for you."

The poison wasn't meant for monsters. It was for raiders.

She'd added venom moth fluid to it too. That made it far more effective.

Evelyn handed over the half-bottle. Calvin gave her a grateful smile.

"We'll pay you later. When we come back fully loaded."

Evelyn nodded. "Safe travels."

She watched them go downstairs. As she turned to head home, the door next to hers creaked open. Quinn stepped out, fully geared and carrying a burlap sack. Henry followed close behind.

Looked like they were going out to scavenge too.

Life in the apocalypse was brutal. For pregnant women and newborns, it was nearly impossible. Evelyn could understand why Quinn wanted to end the pregnancy, and she didn't judge her for it.

But there were ways other than medicine to end it. Even so, Evelyn had no intention of getting involved. Something deep inside her told her to stay far away from that kind of trouble.

A small radio sat on the coffee table in the living room.

It had never made a sound.

In some corner of the city, where no one was looking, darkness and crime were rising side by side.

In her last life, by the fifth year of the apocalypse, all of Elaris had become a ghost town. A wasteland.

The few survivors left behind lived like wild dogs, barely clinging to life.

Evelyn had eaten mud cakes, tree bark, rat meat, spiders, and cockroaches.

No one knew better than she did what hunger truly felt like. What kind of despair it could carve into your soul.

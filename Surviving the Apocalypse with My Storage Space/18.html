Chapter 18 Rainstorm, Venom Moths: Part 14

Evelyn's expression didn't change. Calm and methodical, she finished packing the bag and tied it to her back. It wasn't until Eddie urged her for the third time that she finally followed him out of the lab.

"This way. There's a secure door here. We can go down through it."

Eddie held a torch in one hand and clutched a curtain wrapped around himself with the other. Without hesitation, Evelyn followed him through the door.

The only sounds in the stairwell were their footsteps and the crunch of venom moths being crushed underfoot.

Eddie was young, but long-term hunger had worn him down. He could barely keep up, gasping for air every few steps.

They had to stop and rest several times so he could catch his breath. Luckily, no one had followed them. When they reached the fourth floor, Evelyn stepped into an office to check the situation outside.

The storm hadn't let up. The floodwaters were nearly level with the windows.

She opened the glass window, pulled a coil of rope from her waterproof bag, and secured it to the window frame. Climbing up onto the sill, she grabbed the rope and prepared to jump.

"Wait! I don't see your inflatable boat. Are you sure it's down there?"

Evelyn nodded. "Of course. Sort out your gear so it doesn't catch on the window frame. I'm going down first."

Without another word, she started to lower herself. Just before reaching the surface of the rising water, she glanced up. Eddie wasn't in sight.

She released the inflatable boat, stepped onto it steadily, then unstrapped the waterproof bag from her back.

"Come on. Get down here."

"I'm kinda scared of heights. What if I fall? Will you save me? I don't even know how to swim."

Evelyn was speechless.

"You won't die. Stop dragging this out. Five seconds. If you're not down by then, I'm leaving."

Panicked, Eddie scrambled onto the window sill. "Don't go! I'm jumping, I'm jumping!"

Clutching the rope like a man about to face a firing squad, he took one look down. His relief at spotting the boat vanished the moment he saw how dark and filthy the water was. He immediately shrank back.

"Five. Four. Three..." Evelyn began counting down.

Seeing how ruthless she was, Eddie had no choice. Eyes squeezed shut, he slid down the rope. Just as he was about to hit the water, Evelyn caught him by the ankle and flung him onto the boat.

She started the engine, and the inflatable boat shot forward like an arrow released from a bow.

Meanwhile, back in the building, Nina had led a group of people to the lab. But it was empty.

"Mr. West, I swear it's all true. A female thief broke into the company to steal meds. Eddie helped her get into the lab and they took all the special medicine from the safe."

"There was no one helping her?"

Nina shook her head. "I didn't see anyone else. She said she came alone, said she had an inflatable boat."

Cody West stared at her coldly. "Then why didn't you go with them? Why are you here telling us this? Are they sending someone else? Are you the one they left behind as a mole?"

He narrowed his eyes. "Nina, I've told you before. This building is the safest place left. We have food. The water won't reach the top floors. Going out there is suicide. You expect me to believe Eddie just up and followed some strange woman? That guy's known for being impossible to deal with. You sure this isn't a setup? Maybe there's no thief at all."

Nina looked both wronged and furious. "I'm telling the truth! I didn't go with them because I don't trust that woman. And what they're doing is illegal. Why would I help them? Mr. West, I swear I'm telling the truth. Once the rescue team arrives, we can report this to the police. Let them arrest Eddie and that woman."

Call the cops? The world outside had probably already descended into chaos. Who was there left to arrest anyone?

By now, Evelyn and Eddie were already nearing Kensington Heights. Evelyn glanced back at him, watching him wave a shaky torch to ward off the venom moths. Silently, she slid the dagger on her sleeve back into place.

She had been given a second chance at life. There was no way she'd actually trust a stranger so easily. She only agreed to take him with her to use him for access to the meds. That was it.

But when Nina disappeared and Eddie's first reaction was to tell her to run, she'd been caught off guard. Then he'd asked her to go back and check on the cat he'd raised. That was the moment she truly decided to bring him with her.

"I still don't know your name," Eddie said. "Judging from your voice, you sound pretty young. Did someone in your family get sick? Is that why you were out looking for meds? Aren't you scared? I mean, look over there… isn't that a corpse floating by?"

Evelyn glanced at it, unfazed.

"We're just two strangers crossing paths. No need for names. And the corpses? I saw plenty of them in your company building. Nothing to be scared of."

Eddie gave a dry laugh. "Fair enough."

When they reached Kensington Heights, Eddie sighed at the sight of the place he once called home.

"I really didn't think I'd ever make it back here alive. Thank you… mystery hero."

Evelyn scoffed a little inside at the word "hero." If anything, she was more like a bandit or a thief.

"I'll give you five boxes of the meds. Might come in handy if you need to trade for supplies."

It was rare for Evelyn to show kindness, but this time, she did. Eddie didn't act all sentimental. He took the medicine and tucked it into his coat, then climbed through a small window by the fourth-floor stairwell. Once she saw he'd landed safely inside, Evelyn turned to head home.

"Stay safe!" His voice was nearly drowned out by the storm, but Evelyn still heard it. She raised her hand and waved half-heartedly before disappearing once again into the night.

She was almost back at Willow Heights when two inflatable boats suddenly shot out from the side. The men on board were armed with long knives, coming at her from both sides for a sneak attack.

Evelyn reacted instantly. Before they even got close, she pulled out two crossbows and fired in both directions. Her aim was fast and brutal. The attackers never even made it to her boat before sinking into the water, lifeless.

She stared at the water. One of them had survived the arrows and tried to lunge at her. Evelyn struck without hesitation, driving a knife straight into his heart.

Blood sprayed across her goggles. Even through her protective gear, she could smell the sharp, metallic stench.

She had killed someone. In both her lives, this was the first time she'd taken a life.

But there was no shock, no guilt. Only a strange sense that something inside her had finally broken through the surface.

She wiped the blood from her goggles and looked around. Nothing seemed out of place.

Evelyn pulled her arrows from the bodies. They weren't cheap, and she wasn't about to waste them. She also emptied the attackers' boats of any useful supplies.

As for the blood-soaked inflatable boats, Evelyn didn't mind the mess. She stored them both in her inventory space.

Back at Block D, she carried everything upstairs. There were still patches of blood on her protective suit that the rain hadn't washed away.

Unit 701's door opened as she passed. The woman inside stared at her in shock.

"Evie, you made it back?" The tone and look in her eyes made it clear that she'd assumed Evelyn had died out there.

Several neighbors had seen her leave the night before. It had only been a day and a half, but apparently that was long enough for everyone to write her off.

Evelyn ignored her and continued upstairs. Reaching her front door, she looked at the axe marks on the lock and smiled coldly.

So she'd disappointed some people by making it back alive.

It took four full trips to haul everything upstairs. By the end of the night, everyone in Block D knew one thing for sure—Evelyn had returned, bloodstained and carrying more supplies than they could've imagined.

The next day, just past noon, a knock at the door woke her.

She opened it and saw Calvin standing there.

Evelyn's gaze darkened.

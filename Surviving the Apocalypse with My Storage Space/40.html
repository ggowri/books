Chapter 40 Heatwave, Snake Infestation: Part 4

Just as Evelyn had told Calvin before, snakes were extremely vengeful creatures. They not only liked claiming territory, they also tended to stay active within fixed areas. Most snakes lived in groups, like king cobras, though they were also known to cannibalize their own kind, with larger snakes devouring smaller ones.

So when Evelyn pulled back the curtain again and saw the same green banded sea snake from yesterday, its head raised high as it coiled outside her window, something inside her shifted.

What surfaced was not fear.

It was a strange, dark urge. An impulse to conquer it. To hunt it.

Snakes were cold blooded animals. Their blood changed with temperature. Books said snake blood was fishy and bitter. Folklore claimed it was highly nourishing, said to strengthen the body and vitality.

As Evelyn planned how to kill it quickly, the snake was also watching her with equal wariness.

At that moment, human and snake stared at each other through the glass, instinctively assessing one another.

Evelyn wanted to protect her home. This was her only safe zone. She had to drive away the intruder. The snake wanted to claim territory, which meant killing the original occupant.

This was a battle for territory.

Under the snake's gaze, Evelyn took a slab of fresh meat from her space. She coated her military knife and crossbow bolts with homemade poison, placed the meat on the windowsill, then carefully slid the window open about five centimeters.

Luring a snake required speed, precision, and ruthlessness.

Evelyn held her breath, knees slightly bent. Hot air and the stench outside rushed into her nose. She shut out all distractions and focused entirely on the green banded sea snake slowly slithering toward her.

Perhaps it sensed danger. It did not rush forward. Instead, it waited, as if competing with her in patience.

That was a contest Evelyn would not lose.

Today, she would show this snake what a true apex hunter looked like.

A snake's strength lay in sudden strikes. One second was all it needed. If she could sever its head the instant it attacked, she would win.

She only needed to wait for the perfect moment.

Evelyn subtly adjusted her stance. A thought crossed her mind. If the snake's blood sprayed onto her face, hot and reeking, and she accidentally tasted it, she might lose her appetite for an entire month.

Details decided success or failure. That was the most basic quality of a hunter.

Clearly, Evelyn had more patience.

The fresh meat lay right there on the sill. Its temptation to a snake was no different from a drug.

The snake could not resist anymore.

Evelyn raised an eyebrow slightly, but she did not gloat. She did not relax her guard. Her life was balanced on the edge of her blade. Even a fraction of a second too slow, and she would be the one dead.

At that moment, she treated the snake as nothing more than a tool to train her reaction speed.

She did not realize how unhinged she already was.

"Hiss…"

As the snake opened its mouth to bite the meat, its body arched. Scales gleamed under the sunlight.

Evelyn's blade fell.

One second.

Head and body separated.

Blood sprayed across the windowsill and the curtain. Just as she had predicted, it chose a sudden strike.

Only after the blade landed did Evelyn finally release the breath she had been holding.

She had won.

Half of the snake's body dangled from the windowsill, swaying slightly. Its head had already rolled off into the filthy, scalding floodwater below. Evelyn even heard the splash when it hit the water.

She shut the window, carried the snake's body into the kitchen, and tossed it into a stainless steel basin. She looked down at it from above, the posture of a victor.

So fear could be overcome. It could even be erased.

At the moment she killed it, the snake became no different in her eyes from the venomous moths or toads she had dealt with before.

She went back to the window and wiped away every trace of snake blood. As for the curtain, she took it down and replaced it with a clean one. To mask the lingering smell, she lit several sticks of mugwort incense and added a drop of medicated oil.

Processing a snake was no simple task. First, the skin had to be removed. Then she slit open the abdomen and took out all the organs. A snake's organs consisted of the gallbladder, heart, lungs, liver, and intestines. She kept the gallbladder and tossed the rest into the trash.

For Evelyn, eating the snake meat was the real challenge.

She cooked one portion braised and stored the rest in her space. The green banded sea snake was nearly two meters long and weighed a considerable amount. Still, snake meat contained large amounts of bacteria and parasites. It could only be eaten occasionally.

She took a bite.

Surprisingly, it tasted pretty good. The texture was delicate, not tough or bitter.

That night, Evelyn washed her hands with moonshine until they turned red. Only then did she stop.

From that day on, every snake that dared crawl up and challenge her authority, big or small, venomous or not, was killed on sight.

No exceptions.

One day, Evelyn worked up the courage to lean her head out the window. When she saw that the floodwater had dropped to the seventh floor, she barely had time to feel relieved before the sight below robbed her of speech.

Snakes were everywhere.

On the walls. In the water. On floating planks. On abandoned inflatable boats. Wrapped around the skeletons of unknown creatures. On the balconies and railings of the building across the way.

There was not a single empty space.

Evelyn slammed the window shut. Even that did not feel safe enough. Gasping for air, she pressed both hands against the thick bulletproof glass, as if the moment she let go, the snakes outside would swarm in and overrun her home.

She downed a full glass of ice water before the horrifying images in her mind finally receded a little.

Rapid footsteps sounded upstairs. Evelyn's eyes sharpened.

So there were still people alive in the building.

A long time ago, Evelyn had read a post online. The title was: If the world were reset, could humanity reclaim its position as the dominant species?

The comments were all over the place, but one stood out. It had the most likes, and she remembered it clearly.

[If the world is reset, humanity will first face a brutal process of survival of the fittest. The 'fit' will be those with intelligence, physical strength, and resources. Lacking any one of these means elimination. The 'unfit' are the disabled, the elderly, the weak, the reckless, the impulsive, and the arrogant. Possessing even one of these traits leads to rapid extinction. A reset also means humans must once again fight nature and compete with other species. Humanity's greatest strength has never been power, but resilience. Unless a miracle occurs, humans may well become the next extinct dinosaurs.]

Under the double assault of unstoppable natural disasters and other species, did humanity still have a place to stand?

Or rather… how long could humans really last?

The fan and humidifier hummed together in the living room. Amid the low, droning noise, Evelyn lay on her folding bed. Exhausted in both body and mind, she sank into deep sleep.

In the water below, a massive python drifted lazily. Its crimson tongue flicked out as it swiftly coiled around a smaller snake and swallowed it whole.

The weak had no power to resist.

Even among their own kind, they could not escape the fate of being slaughtered.

Chapter 89 Unknown Creature

"Why are there so many more footprints?"

The first thing Evelyn did every morning was circle her yard, checking for signs of animals getting too close.

Today, there were even more tracks outside the fence. The prints were small. She guessed it might be roe deer or maybe a wild boar.

The cup of hot tea in her hands had been steaming one second and already started cooling the next.

She checked the temperature. It had stabilized at negative ten degrees over the past two days, no sign of dropping further. The sky was a dull gray, not a sliver of light breaking through.

She inspected the pond. A thick sheet of ice had formed across the surface.

It was New Year's Day.

For once, she did not sleep in. She got up early and cleaned the cabin inside and out. A new year had begun.

The snowman she had built earlier in the yard was now completely buried. The snowfall had reached nearly a meter deep. She climbed onto the roof and shoveled off the accumulated snow.

Around noon, a light rain began to fall.

As long as it was just snow, the cold felt manageable. But once rain mixed in, even a drizzle carried a bone piercing chill.

She changed into the red sweater and matching scarf she had crocheted. Her hair had grown long enough to cover her ears.

Soup simmered on the stove in the kitchen. Rubbing her red nose, she hurried back inside.

She had already put up the lanterns earlier, so there was nothing special to prepare for the new year. She lazed around indoors all day and ate dinner early at five.

Afterward, she put on her coat to burn the trash. The iron barrel was full. She refused to carry garbage into the new year.

The smell of burning waste was unpleasant. She stepped back to avoid the smoke.

Then she heard a disturbance in the forest behind the house.

She took out her crossbow and moved toward the sound. Maybe she could finally catch the uninvited guest that had been wandering outside her fence lately.

As long as it was not wolves, she could handle it. Wolves were pack animals and notoriously vengeful. She had no interest in making enemies of them.

Step by steady step, she moved forward. The snow was thick and packed hard now, no longer swallowing her boots.

Deeper in the forest, she heard it again.

It was a heavy thud, like something large collapsing.

She quickened her pace.

About twenty meters ahead, she spotted a dark mass crumpled in the snow.

Her body went rigid. She raised the crossbow and approached slowly.

The shape moved.

She fired immediately.

But as the figure fully came into view, she froze.

It was a person curled up in the snow. It was filthy, ragged, and barefoot.

Blood spread across the white ground beneath them.

Her heart jolted. She rushed forward to check the injury.

And in that instant, a pair of hands shot up and clamped around her throat.

She gasped.

Before the stranger could twist and break her neck, the dagger in her hand plunged straight into their chest.

The grip on her throat did not release completely, but it weakened.

Without hesitation, she pressed her palm against the hilt and drove the blade in deeper.

As she struggled with him, she caught a glimpse of his face beneath the tangled, filthy hair. Mud caked his skin. His hands were huge. She saw the outline of an Adam's apple.

It was a man.

His grip slowly slackened.

She thought he was dead.

Collapsing onto the snow beside him, she dragged in huge gulps of air.

"Pfft…"

She turned her head.

The man she thought was dead rose stiffly to his feet.

He pulled the dagger from his chest. Then he yanked out the crossbow bolt that had pierced through his ribs.

Blood sprayed across the snow, staining it red.

Evelyn stared at him, heart pounding, and raised her crossbow again.

She fired.

He dodged.

Not only dodged. He caught the bolt midair.

Her mind went blank.

The speed of a crossbow bolt was nearly comparable to a bullet. And he had grabbed it with his bare hand.

That was not even the worst part.

She had ambushed him twice. Stabbed him. Shot him.

He was still standing.

He was tall, nearly six foot three. His hair hung over his face, hiding his eyes.

Then he pulled open his tattered coat.

The wound in his chest was no longer bleeding.

It was healing, right in front of her eyes.

Evelyn bit the tip of her tongue, forcing herself to stay calm.

A thought flashed through her mind.

Had she brought something out of that cavern? Something corrupted?

He stepped toward her slowly, twirling the captured bolt between his fingers until the tip pointed back at her.

She did not retreat.

Whether he was a human or demon, she would end this today.

Without drawing attention, she pulled a handgun from her space. It was already loaded.

She narrowed her eyes.

The moment he moved closer again, she pulled the trigger.

It was her first time firing a gun. The recoil felt similar to the crossbow.

But in the next second, the gun was no longer in her hand.

The man, who had been three meters away, was suddenly right in front of her.

Ten centimeters.

She could smell him. Blood. Cold air. Something wild.

Her heart hammered against her ribs.

This was the first time she had felt such overwhelming danger.

"Who are you?" she demanded, her voice low.

His gaze shifted from the stolen gun to her.

Then he bent down and began sniffing her like a dog.

He said nothing.

She tried to step back, but his hand clamped down on her shoulder.

With her free hand, she pulled out a blade from her space. It was the sharpest blade she owned. It could slice through iron like mud.

When his nose trailed from her face down to her neck and lower, she drove the blade straight into his abdomen.

He straightened abruptly.

One hand shot out and grabbed her arm.

Crack!

Her arm dislocated with a sickening snap.

She cried out in pain as he pulled the blade from his body. Blood sprayed across her coat.

"Did you come from the cavern?" she demanded through clenched teeth. "Did I disturb you a few days ago?"

In her mind, this thing was not human.

What human could teleport? Catch a bolt midair? Heal from fatal wounds in seconds?

She almost regretted ever entering that cave.

If she could be reborn, then why could something like a zombie not exist too?

"I didn't damage anything inside. I only took some porcelain. I'll give it back. I'll return it all. Will you leave after that?"

She gripped her crossbow, preparing to fire bolt after bolt until he looked like a pincushion.

Then he stepped closer again.

She moved back instinctively.

A gust of wind lifted the tangled hair from his face.

She saw his eyes.

They were different colors.

She froze.

What…

And in that exact moment, he collapsed.

His full weight slammed into her as he lost consciousness.

She shoved him off and kicked him away.

Minutes passed, but he did not wake.

Clutching her dislocated arm, furious and shaking, she kicked him again.

Chapter 106 Reunion with an Old Friend

“Hand them over?”

“That’s right. I can vouch for you and get you into Blackwater Base.”

Evelyn narrowed her eyes slightly. “In that case, forget it. We’ll make our own way there.”

The woman laughed. “Make your own way there? Since the earthquake, anyone entering Blackwater Base has to hand over ten kilos of grain. Looking at the two of you, you’ve barely got a penny to your name and still think you can get in. Of course, if someone vouches for you, you can enter by doing three months of unpaid labour, then start drawing rations from the fourth month. That’s your way in.”

Vanessa Cole looked Evelyn and Adrian over with open scorn. From her voice and the pair of eyes visible above her scarf, Evelyn guessed she was somewhere in her thirties.

That overbearing, superior manner of hers was almost laughable.

“That’s true,” the man beside Vanessa added, stepping forward to explain. “The base regulations changed after the earthquake. Before, you only needed an identity card. Now you have to pay in grain or hand over something else of value. If you pay in grain, you only need to do one month of unpaid labour. If you can’t pay in grain, then it’s three months. If no one vouches for you, it becomes six.”

“Then I suppose we’ll keep wandering,” Evelyn said quietly. “Stay alive for as long as we can.”

“You can stay outside if you like, but you cannot stay at Silvermere River.”

Vanessa spoke again, her eyes fixed greedily on the little squirrel. If she could take the squirrel and that brightly coloured little bird back and present them to Miss Hawthorne, she would never have to come out fishing with these stinking men again.

The calculation in her eyes did not escape Evelyn. Evelyn lightly pressed Adrian’s hand, then asked, “Why not?”

“Because the fishing rights to Silvermere River belong to Blackwater Base. If you refuse to leave, we have the right to deal with you.”

When she had finished, she turned to Captain Mercer, who had said nothing so far, and stepped closer with a fawning smile.

“Captain Mercer, these two are highly suspicious. I suggest we execute them on the spot.”

Evelyn was speechless.

“Let me see your identity cards,” the Captain said at last.

He looked to be about thirty-five, stood around six feet tall, and had a calm, level gaze. The sort of man who was clearly not simple.

There was no chance Evelyn was about to produce an identity card. She shook her head anxiously.

“We lost everything after the earthquake. The cards disappeared long ago.”

“How old are you?”

“Twenty.”

“And you?” he asked, turning to Adrian.

“Twenty.”

Mercer gave them both a long look. Frankly, neither of them looked twenty. More like forty.

Then he glanced at the tent beside them. There were no dangerous items in sight. On the contrary, the place looked pitifully shabby. Their clothes were in rags, their expressions timid and cowed, their whole bearing listless and worn down.

“The base has rules,” he said. “Everyone is treated equally. If we find survivors outside, we are meant to bring them back and settle them there. I can take you with us, provided you are willing to do six months of compulsory labour.”

“Captain Mercer, I…” Vanessa tried to protest, but he cut her off with a glare.

“If anyone tries pulling tricks under my nose, they’ll be the first I deal with on the spot.”

His gaze swept over the group before settling on Vanessa again. She shrank back into the crowd at once, lowering her head and not daring to speak again.

“If you agree, you can join our fishing team right now.” His eyes dropped to the shovel in Adrian’s hand, the landing net, and the bucket beside him. “If you refuse, then take your things and leave Silvermere River.”

Robbery, plain and simple.

Evelyn had just opened her mouth to speak when someone suddenly pushed through the crowd behind them. Their eyes met at once.

“Evelyn?”

“Eddie?”

She stared at him in disbelief. Good grief, he looked wretched. The clothes on his back were in even worse condition than hers.

Eddie looked at Evelyn with much the same complicated expression. They had not seen each other in months, and now not only was she clearly living rough, she had somehow acquired a strange man at her side. As her honorary older brother, this was not something he found easy to accept.

“This is my cousin,” he said at once. “Captain Mercer, I can vouch for them.”

“Dr Thompson, she really is your cousin?” the Captain studied him, though he did not press the matter. After all, Eddie was the doctor assigned to the fishing team.

“Yes. I told you before, I’m from Elaris too.”

Then Eddie turned to Evelyn, all exaggerated emotion. “Evelyn, I’ve finally found you. Your cousin has missed you so much.”

He flung himself towards her as if to embrace her, but Adrian blocked him before he could get close.

“Cousin, I’ve missed you too,” Evelyn replied sweetly.

Eddie shot Adrian a sidelong look, clearly asking who on earth this man was. Evelyn gave him a glance that said not now.

“Since they’re relatives of Dr Thompson, they can come back to the base with us,” Mercer said at last, settling the matter.

“My cousin is a doctor too,” Eddie added quickly. “And a better one than I am.”

Mercer looked pleasantly surprised. Skilled professionals were what the base lacked most, and doctors counted among the most valuable of all.

“Very well. Dr Thompson, see to your cousin and her husband.”

Eddie nodded at once. “Of course, Captain Mercer. They’re decent people. They’ll behave themselves.”

Captain Mercer gave Adrian one final look, then took the others off to continue fishing.

That left only Evelyn, Adrian, and Eddie on the bank.

“What is going on?” Eddie hissed the moment the others were out of earshot. “Evelyn, who is he?”

She signalled for him to lower his voice. “His name’s Adrian. We met after the earthquake. We’ve been relying on each other to survive ever since. What about the others? They’re all right, aren’t they?”

The subject changed so smoothly that Eddie could hardly object. He glanced at Adrian once more but let it go.

“They’re all at the base. Officer Shaw ended up looking after Wendy, so although he could have joined Security, he finally went to the Growing Grounds to grow vegetables instead. Ray and Evan joined other hunting crews. I’m in the Medical Division, assigned to the fishing team for field missions.”

That told Evelyn most of what she needed to know. From the sound of it, the base had developed reasonably well.

“We only arrived there a bit over a month ago,” Eddie went on. “Before that, we were hiding out in the mountains. Then we ran into a hunting team and went back with them.”

He looked at Adrian again before turning back to Evelyn. “He really is your boyfriend? Evelyn, you’re young and pretty. Don’t lose your head.”

She nearly laughed aloud. In terms of looks, Adrian probably had her beaten.

Then again, dressed like this, he did look even more ragged than a beggar.

“Of course it’s true,” she said.

She still wanted to protect Adrian’s identity. Somewhere deep down, she could not bear the thought of him being persecuted again.

“So yes, he’s one of us. Now hurry up and tell me about the base. How does the housing work?”

“There are loads of people there. Everyone has to work, and you’re assigned to different posts according to your ability. Housing depends on that too. Some people sleep in communal dormitories, fifty or even a hundred packed into one room. Others get single rooms, about four square metres. But you have to pay fifty kilos of grain to move into one of those.”

“And what about you? What sort of arrangement have you got?”

Eddie lifted a brow. “Single room, obviously. I handed over something valuable.”

Evelyn’s mind immediately went to the two guns.

“Oh, and one more thing. There are a lot of criminals in the base. This week alone, three women were assaulted. Once you’re there, make sure you protect yourself.”

“Is there electricity? Water? How do people eat?”

“There’s electricity, but it’s strictly rationed. There are wells, though the water quality is poor. Meals come from the canteen. Two a day. Enough to keep you full, but the food is dreadful. Once a week, on Mondays, there’s an extra serving of meat.”

Chapter 64 Scorching Heat, The Refugees: Part 2

When Evelyn returned home, the scene from earlier kept replaying in her mind.

She knew the real reason she had walked away. It was not because she had killed that man. It was not even because she had watched the others tear into him.

It was because the bolt she fired had been coated in a lethal toxin. Within three hours, every person who had eaten that flesh would rot alive.

Evelyn rushed into the bathroom and retched. She stared at her hands. They still looked clean.

For a split second, she swore she could see them drenched in blood.

Knock. Knock. Knock.

A soft knock came from the door.

She rinsed her mouth and stood up. By the time she opened the door, her face had returned to its usual calm.

Eddie stood outside, holding her crossbow bolt.

"Thank you," she said.

She took it from him. The blood, bits of flesh, and sand had already been cleaned off. Still, a faint metallic scent lingered.

"No problem. Get some rest," Eddie said gently. "They got what they deserved. Don't carry this on your shoulders. We'll handle patrol tonight."

Evelyn forced a small smile, closed the door, and went back inside with the bolt in her hand.

Maybe the flaming shot had frightened everyone. Maybe they had eaten enough to quiet their hunger for the moment. Either way, the troublemakers downstairs were gone. All that remained were a few extinguished torches and scattered bones.

That night, Calvin and Eddie stood guard downstairs. Ray and Evan borrowed a pair of binoculars from Evelyn and kept watch from the rooftop. No one dared to sleep. They were all afraid the refugees would return.

At five in the morning, Evelyn heard several agonized screams from the building next door.

Someone stumbled out into the open.

She stepped onto the balcony and looked down.

They ran while vomiting. Dark red liquid mixed with decaying flesh spilled from their mouths. Behind them, others cried, cursed, and moaned in unbearable pain.

They tried to rush toward Block D for help, but collapsed halfway there. None of them got back up.

Under the brutal heat, the bodies decayed even faster. Not a single person who had eaten the meat the night before survived.

*****

The air soon filled again with the stench of rot.

There were still survivors in the neighboring building. When Ray and the others returned the binoculars, they told Evelyn that about twenty people were hiding on the sixth floor of Block C. Most of them were women and children.

"Where the corpses are, wild greens are sprouting everywhere," Evan said. "They grow huge in just a few hours. Are they feeding off the rotting flesh?"

His words made Eddie and Ray go pale.

Evelyn remained silent, standing off to the side. She glanced at the dull gray sky outside. No one could tell what she was thinking.

"So… are we still going to dig for wild greens?" Evan asked quietly, looking at Calvin, the only one who seemed steady.

"We dig," Calvin said firmly. "How many people have been buried in this land since ancient times? The dead become soil. Crops grow from soil. What's there to fear? Tonight, we keep digging. That's the food that keeps us alive."

At ten that night, just as the people of Block D stepped outside with their tools, two frail, skeletal women darted out from the side.

When Evelyn caught sight of their exposed calves and arms, she noticed whip marks crisscrossing their skin.

She glanced at Eddie and Calvin.

In that instant, she understood why the two women had come.

"Sir… could we speak with you privately?"

One of the women stepped forward and blocked Calvin's path. She kept her eyes lowered, too afraid to meet anyone's gaze. Her trembling hands betrayed the fear and struggle churning inside her.

"What do you want?" Calvin frowned and took two quiet steps back.

The woman hesitantly reached out, as if to grab his sleeve. The moment he drew his knife, she nearly stumbled to the ground in fright.

"S-Sir, I… I…"

She stammered for a long time without managing to say anything. The woman behind her suddenly pushed her toward Eddie, then grabbed the front of her clothes and yanked them open.

"Sir, my younger sister just turned twenty. She's very pretty. Interested?"

The younger woman tried to pull her clothes back together, but the one beside her held her hands in place.

Eddie's eyes widened in shock. He reflexively pulled out a knife and pointed it at them.

"D-Don't come any closer."

At that moment, the woman was shoved forward and stumbled straight into him.

The other one walked toward Calvin, calmly unfastening her own coat.

Ray and Evan were so startled by the scene that they hurriedly hid behind Evelyn.

"Sir, we don't mean anything by it," the woman said. "We just want someone to rely on. My sister and I have no family left. It wasn't easy making it to Elaris. We don't eat much. We can work. Just give us a place to sleep."

"Put your clothes back on and leave."

Calvin's expression darkened. Wendy was still clinging to his back, her wide eyes taking in everything happening before her.

"Sir, that's your daughter, right? I can take care of her. I might look dirty, but I'm only twenty five. Please don't drive us away. We really have nowhere else to go."

As she spoke, she dropped to her knees and wrapped her arms around Calvin's leg, sobbing.

Nearby, Eddie kicked the other woman aside and quickly retreated behind Evelyn.

As if she could not feel the pain, the woman scrambled back to her feet. When she spotted Evelyn behind the mask, she reached out to grab her hand.

"What is wrong with you?" Eddie snapped. "I told you to get lost. You couldn't latch onto me, now you're trying her?"

He lifted his foot again, ready to kick.

Evelyn glanced at the woman, then picked up her shovel and bucket and walked away.

"Wait, don't go!"

The woman tried to chase after her, but Eddie stepped in front of her.

"I said get lost."

She shrank back slightly, but still looked unwilling to give up as she turned toward Calvin again.

"Sir, can my sister and I stay?"

"Stay my ass. Get out," Eddie snapped before Calvin could speak. He grabbed the woman by the arm and dragged her aside.

"Take one more step closer and don't blame my knife. Your life is hard. You think ours is easy?"

The two women were dragged away without mercy. They could not understand why their usual trick failed here. Faced with two living, breathing women, these men had remained completely unmoved.

The security door was locked behind them.

Ignoring the two sobbing women outside, the group began searching for wild greens and giant rats.

The wind had weakened recently. Calvin did not need to blow his whistle at two in the morning to call for a retreat. This time, Evelyn had not come out to dig for greens. She wanted to see just how many outsiders had arrived.

Like them, people filled the streets and alleys, digging for wild greens. Startled by Evelyn's sudden appearance, they scattered like frightened birds.

She did not go far. After circling the area once, she headed back.

When she returned, the two women were still crouched downstairs at Block D. The moment they saw her, they ran toward her with their clothes hanging open.

"Sir, please. Have mercy. Take us in. We're good at taking care of people. Just give us a place to stay. We can find our own food."

"You just had a baby?" Evelyn asked, her gaze settling on the chest of the woman who claimed to be the older sister for a few seconds.

Chapter 60 Heatwave, Wiped Out

"I guess you could say that."

Eddie looked surprised when she admitted it. "You're this capable. You'd adapt anywhere. And you've got weapons. What are you afraid of?"

Evelyn gave him a look as if he were hopeless.

"You think they'd let you carry weapons into the base?" she said flatly. "And in a harsh collective environment, standing out is the stupidest thing you can do."

She studied him with a faint, unreadable smile.

"You've got decent looks. You know that, right? In times like this, whether you're a man or a woman, if you've got looks but no power to protect yourself, the outcome is worse."

Eddie visibly shivered.

Evelyn said nothing more. She unlocked her door and went inside.

It was still dark. She could squeeze in a little more sleep.

She had not been trying to scare him. In the apocalypse, young, good-looking men could be just as vulnerable as women.

*****

In the days that followed, Eddie and the others continued going out for supplies.

He really did seem blessed with absurd luck. Almost every trip yielded something worthwhile. Once, they even found several barrels of gasoline at an abandoned gas station.

With fuel secured, everyone felt more stable.

If the day came when they had to leave, just like Eddie said, they could hit the gas and go.

Meanwhile, Evelyn stayed home, stockpiling cooked food.

When the temperature climbed to 54 degrees Celcius, the winds grew stronger. The fires in Northside had already swallowed several industrial parks and commercial districts.

There were still many people in the city.

Eddie's group ran into ambushes frequently.

The worst time, they were completely wiped out.

All their supplies were taken. Each of them returned bruised and bloodied.

Evelyn no longer asked for supplies in exchange for medical treatment.

In this building, they were tied together now. Bound by the same rope. She was willing to give for them.

The iodine and gauze from Elaris University finally came in handy.

Eddie stared at the medical supplies with blatant envy.

He had been hurt the worst with one cracked rib, a dislocated wrist, and a fractured lower leg.

Evelyn silently took back her earlier thought about him being lucky.

He was more like cursed.

"Where did they ambush you?" she asked calmly.

"Are you going to avenge us?" Eddie's eyes lit up. "Evelyn, I knew I didn't misjudge you. You're amazing. From today on, we're siblings by fate. If you ever need me, I'll charge into fire and water for you—"

Evelyn pressed firmly on his injured area.

He howled in pain.

"Okay, okay, I was wrong. I'll call you big sister. I'll be your little brother. Deal?"

"Stop talking nonsense. Can you answer properly?"

"I have been answering properly," he muttered.

When he met her cold, shadowed gaze, he shrank slightly.

"It was at the Harrison Street farmers' market," he said more seriously. "Eight or nine big guys. They had weapons."

Evelyn frowned. "You had weapons too. What happened? Don't tell me you don't know how to shoot."

Eddie avoided her eyes.

"I left the gun at home."

She stared at him.

"I think you left your brain at home."

Evelyn worked quickly.

She treated Eddie first, wrapping his ribs tight and stabilizing his wrist and leg. Ray and Evan had the lightest injuries, so she left them for last.

Calvin was in worse shape.

His nose was fractured, and an arrow from a crossbow had lodged in his back.

That one worried her.

"Evie, don't go after them," Ray said, his speech slightly slurred from a missing tooth. "We were careless. We'll accept it. But they had crossbows. Bigger than yours. If Mr. Calvin hadn't thrown one of his homemade bombs, we would've been completely wiped out."

He spoke through the gap in his teeth.

It was a miserable sight.

Wendy stood beside her father, fanning him desperately with a small handheld fan.

Evelyn walked over and gently touched her hair.

"Don't be scared. Your dad will be fine. I'm going to treat him now. You cheer us on, okay?"

Wendy hugged her tightly and nodded with all her strength.

Evelyn opened the medical kit and began.

The attacker's aim had been poor. The bolt had missed any vital organs.

After she removed it and stitched the wound, Calvin regained consciousness from the pain.

He was alert. His eyes were clear.

Only then did she truly relax.

"Evie… thank you," he murmured.

She shook her head. "Don't talk. The injury's serious. You won't be going out for at least half a month."

He gave a bitter smile. "We finally found some biscuits and sausage last night. All gone."

The room fell quiet.

The loss hurt.

"Calvin, don't lose heart," Eddie said, sitting up stiffly. "Once we're healed, we'll bring Evelyn and wipe them out. A gentleman never forgets a grudge."

Evelyn expected Calvin to object.

Instead, he nodded.

"Alright. Give me two days. I'll sharpen my blade."

She blinked.

Well. Settling scores was a virtue too.

After finishing everyone's wounds, Evelyn returned home.

She ladled the bone broth she had simmered into plastic containers. One massive pot filled two hundred boxes.

She sealed them, packed them into crates, and stored everything in her space.

Days of nonstop cooking had paid off. The amount of prepared food in her space had visibly increased.

The steamed buns she had made earlier were gone.

Over the next few days, she planned to steam several thousand more.

Elaris was in the south, but Evelyn loved buns.

When she was little, she had grown taller than most kids her age. Other parents had asked her mother if she fed her special supplements.

Her mother would laugh and say, "She just loves wheat. And she's not picky."

The memory made Evelyn smile.

After a week of recovery, Eddie and the others resumed light rehabilitation exercises on the eleventh floor.

Evelyn gave them each a dose of herbal medicine for heatstroke and checked their wounds again.

"I've noticed something," Eddie said suddenly. "There haven't been any vultures these past few days."

He pointed out the window.

They all looked.

Only endless yellow sand and howling wind.

"Could the wind blow the fire from Northside over here?" Ray asked nervously.

"We're southeast of Elaris," Eddie said, patting his head lightly. "It won't reach us."

"If the vultures are gone… what if something else shows up?" Ray added hesitantly.

Everyone turned to him.

He scratched his ear, embarrassed. "Just saying."

"He's not wrong," Evelyn said thoughtfully. "For the next half month, stay alert."

She calculated the date in her mind.

It was already mid-September.

In five days, it would be the Mid-Autumn Festival.

"Why don't we pool what we have," Calvin suggested gently, "and have a meal for Mid-Autumn?"

The last time they had done that was here.

Back then, his family had still been whole.

Claire had still been alive.

"Sounds good. I'll contribute two packs of instant noodles and a bottle of water," Eddie said immediately.

Evelyn had no objections.

The proposal passed unanimously.

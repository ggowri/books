Chapter 69 Departure: Part 4

The temperature dropped to twenty eight degrees. The air even felt damp.

At midnight, the convoy stopped again. The same man who relayed orders came around, announcing they would rest in place for five hours and resume at five in the morning sharp.

Evelyn quietly slipped away from the group.

They were now near the border between Elaris and another region. Mountains rose on both sides of the road, offering plenty of cover. She headed into the hills, planning to find a cave where she could change her underclothes and wipe herself down.

She would not wash her face, neck, or hair. The dirtier she looked, the better.

Many trees in the mountains had withered under the relentless heat. Wearing night vision goggles, Evelyn moved silently through the brittle forest. In a small hollow between slopes, she found a cave.

She shone a flashlight inside to check for animals. Finding none, she stepped in.

From her space, she took out a basin of water and quickly washed her body. After changing into clean underclothes, she resisted the urge to linger under the rare full moonlight outside.

She put the basin away and left at once.

On the way back, to avoid suspicion, she dug half a sack of wild greens and caught a gray rabbit.

Just as she was about to descend the mountain, some unmistakable sounds drifted from a nearby patch of woods.

Starving to death and still finding time for that.

Evelyn covered her ears and walked off quickly.

"You were gone a long time," Eddie said when she returned.

"What happened?"

"A few people ran to the front to beg for food. It turned into an argument. The ones begging got beaten to death."

Evelyn's expression hardened. "The people up front aren't simple. They kill without hesitation."

Eddie nodded. "Maybe they really are relatives of someone powerful at Drakepool Base."

Evelyn smiled faintly and dropped the topic. She pulled aside the wild greens in her bag, revealing the rabbit underneath.

The others stared.

"You went hunting rabbits?"

"Just got lucky," she replied. "I see a lot of people lighting fires to cook. Let's make something too."

They got out of the vehicle and dug a shallow pit beside it. Ray and Evan gathered firewood while Evelyn skinned the rabbit and skewered it on a branch.

Around them, others were roasting giant rats. Some were even cooking rice porridge. Blending into the activity made them less conspicuous.

"My friend," a man nearby called out, walking over. "Can we trade? Three bowls of porridge for half your rabbit."

Calvin stood up to negotiate. The sight of white rice porridge in the other man's pot made his eyes flicker.

"Five bowls."

The bowls were ordinary small porcelain ones. The iron pot held a full batch of porridge. It was thin, but at least there were visible grains and rice water.

The man hesitated.

Then he saw Evelyn pull out a plastic bag from her pack and sprinkle some chili powder over the rabbit meat.

He swallowed hard.

"Deal."

After the man left, Calvin crouched down beside Evelyn, looking apologetic.

"Sorry. I decided without asking you."

"We're a team," Evelyn said calmly. "Anyone can make a call. No need to worry about that. And even if you hadn't agreed, I would have. It's a good trade."

"Evelyn's right," Eddie said. "We're a team. What matters is helping each other get to the base alive, not nitpicking over every little thing. Tomorrow I'll try to catch a rabbit too. Let you all taste the fruits of my labor."

Calvin worked the hardest out of all of them, yet he was the one who felt the least secure. He had a three-year-old child with him. Most of the time, the others helped watch Wendy. To avoid becoming a burden and to repay everyone, he always pushed himself the most.

When the rabbit was done roasting, Calvin took it over to complete the trade.

He came back with five bowls of porridge. From his own portion, he quietly poured half into Wendy's bowl.

Evelyn glanced at it. There was a layer of rice grains settled at the bottom. A little white sugar had been sprinkled on top. The taste was faint, but there was still a hint of sweetness.

"My dream's pretty simple now," Eddie said between sips. "Eat my fill every day. Sleep well. No more living on edge."

Evelyn shot him a look. "That's not simple."

"True," he admitted. "Just eating enough is already hard. But now that the temperature's dropped so much… do you think the disasters are finally over?"

Evelyn looked up at the bright full moon.

A strange unease pressed against her chest. It felt like the quiet before a storm.

Back in the car, everyone tried to get some rest. Evelyn could not sleep. She kept count of how many people walked past their vehicle.

The spare tires had been moved to the roof to prevent anyone from slashing them.

At four in the morning, someone crept toward the rear of the vehicle.

Evelyn and Calvin opened their eyes at the same time.

The moment the figures reached out, the two of them threw open the doors, rushed down, clamped hands over the intruders' mouths, and knocked them unconscious.

"Stealing fuel or tires," Calvin muttered, glancing at the two men on the ground.

"You go back," he told Evelyn. "I'll handle it."

She nodded and returned to the car. Eddie and the others stirred awake.

She briefly explained what had happened. Two minutes later, Calvin climbed back in.

"I dumped them in a ditch. They're still alive. Took two fingers from each."

"Looks like we're being watched," he said coldly, wiping blood from his dagger.

At exactly five, the convoy moved again.

The morning went smoothly. No roadblocks.

By four in the afternoon, they stopped in a small town.

Collapsed buildings lined the streets. The road was cracked and broken. As usual, each vehicle sent out two people to clear debris while the rest searched nearby intact houses for supplies.

"How much longer until we reach the base?" a young man's voice complained from inside the RV. "It's only supposed to be thirty-something kilometers. We've been on the road for three days. Why are we detouring this far?"

Others inside murmured gently, trying to soothe him.

"Why didn't the old man send a helicopter to pick me up? Why make me ride in a car with these filthy refugees?"

"Hey. That girl. Bring her over."

A young girl near Evelyn was dragged away. Two large men hauled her to the RV, tossed her a towel, and ordered her to wipe her face clean.

The girl trembled violently but dared not resist. She carefully wiped the grime away.

"Tsk. She's not bad. Clean her up and bring her inside."

A lazy voice drifted from the RV.

No one dared to look. Everyone lowered their heads and kept working.

*****

Clearing debris and filling in the road took three hours.

By the time the convoy set off again, it was already seven in the evening.

In the car, Evelyn used a needle to pierce the blister on her palm. She squeezed out the fluid and tore away the dead skin.

"Wasn't this route supposed to be clear?" Eddie grumbled. "Why are we clearing roadblocks every single day?"

"This is the clear route," someone replied. "All the others were washed out by floods."

"Evelyn, lend me the needle."

She handed it over. When she saw the dense cluster of blisters covering Eddie's palm, her scalp tingled.

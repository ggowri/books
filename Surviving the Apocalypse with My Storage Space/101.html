Chapter 101 The Bat Attack

The stone ledge was roughly five square metres across. Evelyn took out some dry kindling, and Adrian lit it for her. The fire caught quickly and soon burned steadily.

Evelyn settled Pip and Lark in front of the flames to warm them up. They looked weak and miserable, but thankfully they were still alive.

“Are you all right?”

She had already applied ointment to her eyes and wrapped them with a strip of black cloth. Adrian turned his back to her so she could change into dry clothes.

Once she had sorted herself out, Evelyn took out two boxed meals and handed one to him.

“I’m fine.”

“Is the snake dead?”

“Yes.”

Only then did she properly relax. She rinsed her mouth with water before starting on her food. Since she could not see anyway, there was no need for Adrian to turn away when he changed. She simply sat by the fire as she was, calm and unbothered.

Adrian watched her for a moment. Even with her eyes covered, she ate neatly and steadily, without the slightest sign of panic. He could not help being curious.

“Aren’t you scared?”

Evelyn frowned faintly. “Scared of what? We’re still alive, aren’t we?”

He fell silent for a long while before saying in a low voice, “You’ve got a solid attitude.”

In the end of the world, that was what got forged above all else. Your frame of mind. Someone with shaky nerves would never have survived this long.

After eating, Evelyn stretched out her hands towards the fire again.

Pip and Lark managed a little food as well. She stroked their heads, soothing the two small creatures without a word.

“We need to find a way out of here.”

Evelyn did not think it would be that simple. “Are there still fireballs outside? Even if there aren’t, the whole mountain is probably still burning. The moment we go out, we’ll be roast meat.”

When they had entered the caverns, everything the fireballs touched had already been ablaze. Ashen Ridge had turned into a sea of fire.

They both went quiet.

After they had finished eating, Adrian took a torch, and Evelyn took another. They began examining the stone ledge, hoping to find the entrance to a hidden passage, but after a full circuit they found nothing at all.

“There’s no hidden passage here.”

The caverns split into hundreds of branches, and every one of them looked more or less the same. Evelyn had already forgotten the route she took the last time. Now that she could not see, it was even worse.

“Let’s rest a bit longer, then keep moving forward.”

Evelyn nodded. She put away the crossbow, leaving Adrian with one gun while keeping the other in her own hand.

“Have you used up all the gas cylinders?”

“I fed every last one of them to it.”

No wonder the noise earlier had been so violent. She had genuinely thought the whole cavern was about to collapse.

The fact that he had walked away from that without a scratch made his abilities seem all the more unfathomable.

Once the warmth had returned to their bodies, they set off again. Evelyn tucked Pip and Lark into a cloth bag, brought out an inflatable kayak, and the two of them continued onward.

“What do you think those fireballs were?”

“Possibly meteorites. Some of them ignite and explode when they come into contact with oxygen.”

The moment Adrian said it, Evelyn found herself half convinced.

They squeezed through a narrow section of the caverns and entered a new stretch of water. Evelyn took out a brick and threw it in. After waiting half a minute and seeing no reaction, they finally paddled on.

The deeper they went, the lower the temperature dropped, and the thinner the air became. Apart from the dip of the paddle, the cavern was utterly still.

“I wonder whether a compass would even work in here.”

If the magnetic field inside the caverns was unstable, it might be useless. Evelyn took one out and handed it to Adrian, letting him decide which way to go.

“It’s no good. The magnetic field’s a mess.” He glanced at it once and passed it back.

“Let’s find somewhere to hole up. Once the fireballs stop and the fire outside dies down, we can leave.”

Evelyn was tired now. The spot on her back where the snake’s tail had struck still throbbed, and her tailbone was very likely fractured. She could only sit at an angle.

“All right.”

Then the silence of the cavern shattered.

“Chi chi chi… chi chi chi…”

At the sound, Evelyn tightened her grip on the gun and went on alert at once.

“Bats. I remember there are bats in here.”

Adrian swept his gaze around, but could not see them.

“How many?”

“A lot. Thousands. They roost on the ceiling. Look up.”

He lifted his head just as one of the bats dived. It spread its wings wide, almost like a parachute opening in mid-air.

“Get down.”

The words had barely left his mouth when the bat let out another sharp cry and lunged straight at him. Evelyn immediately took out a long military knife and a protective suit and shoved them towards him. Using a crossbow or gun on bats would have been a waste.

Adrian moved with startling speed. The bat had not even come within half a metre before he sliced it cleanly in two.

The first bat was down, but that only agitated the colony. They sent out an attack signal through high-frequency sound, and the moment that first one fell, the rest poured out in a black tide and rushed at them.

Evelyn pulled out the poison mixture she had prepared earlier, along with a gas mask. She tossed two spray bottles to Adrian, slipped into a protective suit, fitted the mask over her face, and began spraying straight at the oncoming bats.

Good thing she had planned ahead.

Though she could not see, she could hear the bats dropping into the water one after another. The poison was working beautifully.

She had mixed a sedative powder into it as well. Anything that inhaled it would lose consciousness almost immediately.

But there were simply too many of them.

They came in waves, and the shrill chorus of their cries was enough to pierce the eardrums.

“How much poison have you got left?”

Evelyn made a quick calculation. She had mixed a whole large barrel and filled thirty bottles. It should be enough.

“Thirty bottles altogether. I’ve got insecticide too.”

She took out several cans and began alternating the sprays. Poison and insecticide together cut down the bats by the swarm. They rushed in wave after wave, and fell in wave after wave, until the water was thick with bodies.

It was just as well Evelyn could not see. If she had, her aversion to dense clusters would have been unbearable.

Adrian started paddling while Evelyn kept spraying. He pushed the kayak forward nearly ten metres, but the bats pursued them relentlessly. There was no shaking them off.

“Looks like we’ll have to wipe them out.”

Bats used sound frequencies beyond the range of human hearing to navigate and hunt. In a simple contest of speed, humans had no chance against them.

Evelyn could feel it now. Adrian had gone cold with murderous focus. Even standing beside him, she could sense the lethal force rolling off him.

Several bats landed on her back and clung there, impossible to shake loose. She could only keep spraying at the swarm charging towards them. Adrian spotted them and slammed them away with one hard sweep of his hand, pulling her out of danger yet again.

The two of them worked together seamlessly. Even against thousands upon thousands of bats, they never once lost the upper hand.

“Adrian, how many are left?”

“Nearly done.”

Two minutes later, Evelyn shook out her aching wrist, stamped to death a bat that had landed on the kayak, and asked wearily, “How many are left now?”

“Nearly done.”

The same answer again.

Evelyn was speechless.

She considered using fire to frighten them back, but Adrian dismissed the idea at once.

“They’ve made up their minds to mob us. Even with torches, they’d still come straight through the flames.”

The unity of animals could be terrifying too. At that moment, Evelyn and Adrian were the enemy, and it was a fight to the death. Just as he had said, they were not going to retreat.

Each time they cleared a wave, Adrian paddled them farther ahead while Evelyn carried on spraying. Behind them, bat corpses layered over one another until they had covered the entire surface of the water.

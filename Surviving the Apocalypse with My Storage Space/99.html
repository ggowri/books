Chapter 99 The Fireballs Fall

Evelyn envied his regenerative ability, but no one knew what kind of side effects it carried.

She would not gamble with her own body. She did not need to conquer the world. Being able to protect herself was enough.

"Can I ask you something?" she said. "What city did you come from?"

Rowan's hand paused briefly, but he did not hide it. "Havenport."

She was surprised. "I'm from Elaris. What happened to Havenport?"

"After the earthquake, it became ruins."

That told her something important. When the earthquake struck, he had still been in Havenport. That meant he had walked all the way here. But why enter the mountains?

"Why did you come into the mountains?" she asked.

He looked at her and countered, "What about you?"

"I saw signs of snowfall after the quake, so I came up here to hide for a while."

"I smelled prey. I came up the mountain, but I passed out from hunger."

What happened after that, neither of them mentioned. No need to risk another fight.

Over the next few days, the temperature dropped again, plummeting to minus thirty three degrees.

Evelyn had a bad feeling. Her instincts were usually right.

Even Rowan went out less. He said the animals in the mountains had started migrating in large numbers, moving northwest. Animals sensed danger better than humans did.

On February first, the sky briefly lost its heavy gray shroud. A rare light broke through.

Evelyn stretched out her hand. Maybe it was her imagination, but the faint light felt slightly warm on her skin.

It lasted only about ten minutes.

Then the sky darkened again.

"Do you think there's going to be another earthquake?" Evelyn stared outside and suddenly pulled on her coat, pushing open the cabin door.

"You think so?"

"I have a bad feeling. I need to store everything outside first." She started dismantling the gate of the fence.

At that exact moment, the world went black.

Not gradually. Instantly.

Evelyn raised both hands in front of her face. She could not see them at all. For a split second, she wondered if she had gone blind.

"Evelyn."

Rowan rushed toward her. He grabbed her roughly and bolted back into the cabin, slamming the door shut.

Her eyes burned sharply. It took about ten seconds for her vision to return, shifting from a blur to clarity.

She looked around at the familiar interior. The fireplace. The low table. Sprig and Fallon.

Outside the window, it was still pitch black.

Not the darkness of night.

Something deeper. More absolute.

There was no light in the sky at all. It was as if the sun had vanished.

Evelyn wiped away the tears caused by the stinging pain and looked at Rowan in silence.

"The sun is gone."

He stood at the window, staring out.

He did not understand why the sky had turned black in an instant. Three minutes had passed, and nothing had changed.

He turned to her, his expression serious.

"Get ready to leave."

Her hands were trembling, but she forced herself to move. The cabin could be taken with her, since the foundation and structure were integrated into her space.

She pulled out two sets of heavy thermal clothing and mountaineering boots for Rowan. She had once considered abandoning him. But in a crisis like this, they needed to fight side by side.

She changed into her own gear as well, choosing the lightest and warmest layers she had.

She had just finished changing when Rowan suddenly threw the door open and rushed in.

"Move."

He dragged her out of the cabin. The sky above was pitch black, and through that darkness, a blazing fireball tore downward.

A deafening explosion followed. In the distance, flames erupted out of nowhere.

Evelyn shoved Sprig and Fallon into a cloth bag and, at top speed, stored the cabin away. There was no time for the smaller items.

A second fireball fell.

Then a third.

"Where do we run?" she shouted.

"The karst cave."

Rowan grabbed the bag and slung it over himself. They had barely sprinted fifty meters from the yard when a fireball slammed into the spot where the cabin had stood.

Boom!

Flames burst outward.

Evelyn had guessed Rowan possessed night vision, so she had not given him the night vision goggles. But when the fireballs exploded, the sudden flare of light was blinding.

Pain stabbed into her eyes again.

Tears streamed down her face, but she made no sound. She ran behind him with everything she had.

Another fireball crashed down ahead, blocking their path. Sparks splattered onto her coat, instantly burning a hole through the fabric.

Rowan had been slowing himself to match her pace. The moment she stumbled and fell, he grabbed her, threw her onto his back, and sprinted toward the cave at full speed.

Fireballs rained down endlessly. The explosions pounded against her ears.

Her eyes would not stop tearing. She yanked out a strip of black cloth from her space and blindfolded herself, then wrapped her arms tightly around Rowan's neck.

Sprig and Fallon shrieked inside the bag. There was no time to comfort them.

Another fireball slammed down just behind them, blasting a crater into the ground. Nearby fir trees ignited instantly.

A forty minute route became ten.

The moment Rowan charged into the cave, another fireball struck outside. By then, the entire Shattered Ridge was engulfed in flames.

He set her down and noticed the black cloth tied over her eyes, soaked through with tears.

"Are you okay?"

She nodded. "I'm fine. What's happening?"

"The fireballs haven't stopped. We may need to stay inside."

She reached to remove the cloth, but he caught her wrist.

"Don't. It's all fire outside. If your eyes get hit again, you'll go blind."

Her hands clenched into fists.

No.

She could not go blind.

She groped along the stone wall and crouched down in a corner.

From her space, she pulled out a trekking pole and gripped it tightly. The cave echoed with the relentless boom of falling fireballs and the roar of burning forest.

Her eyes still burned. Tears kept flowing, as if someone had sprayed them with molten pepper. She bit her lip hard and endured it.

"We need to go deeper," Rowan said.

He lifted her again and slung her onto his back. She did not argue. She was the burden now. She could not afford to slow him down.

He leapt down the stone steps.

She had no idea how he planned to cross the underground stream ahead. No matter how fast he ran or how high he jumped, he did not have wings.

Afraid he would try to swim, she quickly stopped him.

"I have an inflatable kayak."

She released it from her space. Rowan immediately tossed her into it first.

She did not protest. She steadied herself quickly.

A cloth bag was thrown into her arms. Inside, Sprig and Fallon trembled. The moment they caught her scent, they let out soft cries.

"It's okay. Don't be scared."

She stroked their heads, forcing her own voice to remain calm.

Rowan climbed into the front, grabbed two paddles, and told her to sit tight. The kayak began moving forward.

They had barely gone a short distance when a violent blast erupted at the cave entrance.

A wave of fire shot inward.

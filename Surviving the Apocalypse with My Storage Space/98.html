Chapter 98 Ordinary Days

When they returned to the cabin, Rowan climbed onto the roof to shovel snow. Evelyn stayed inside and cleaned the pheasant she had just hunted. Tonight, she would make pheasant soup.

Earlier, she had weighed herself. With clothes and boots on, she was exactly one hundred pounds. At five foot seven, that was perfectly reasonable. Besides, winter clothes added some weight.

Still, she had gained a little this season.

And that was a good thing. Winter was meant for putting on a bit of fat. She cupped her palm against her cheek and admired her reflection. Even if she reached one hundred fifty pounds, she would still be the best looking creature on this mountain.

Except Rowan.

He did not count as human.

The camellia seeds she had planted were already sprouting tiny buds. She watered them with nutrient solution, hoping that when spring arrived, they would break through the soil.

The snow outside the windows and door had been cleared away, restoring her view. Evelyn cleaned out the ash from the fireplace and lit a new fire.

When Rowan came inside, she glanced at his hands and tossed him a few pairs of gloves.

After dinner, they sat on opposite sides of the room. Evelyn crocheted a sweater. Rowan read. A clear boundary between them, neither disturbing the other.

"Do you have more books?" he asked.

She glanced at the one in his hand, Basic House Construction, then brought him over a dozen more. Essays, novels, medical texts, even comics. Enough to keep him busy for a while.

For the next five days, Evelyn did not step outside once. The temperature had dropped to minus twenty five degrees. That level of cold was already uncomfortable for her. She had no desire to leave the cabin.

Rowan, however, continued his routine. Hunting, chopping wood, processing prey, shoveling snow. She noticed that in the past two days he had started wearing a heavy coat and gloves.

So he could feel the cold after all.

Sprig and Fallon began showing signs of illness. When it came to small animals, Evelyn felt helpless. She had no idea how to diagnose them.

The two little ones lay weakly in their nest, refusing food and water. Evelyn moved them closer to the fireplace and placed two small heaters beside them.

Sprig seemed slightly better. It was probably the sudden drop in temperature. Fallon was worse. Overnight, the colorful feathers on its long tail had begun to fall out. Several patches on its body had gone bald.

Evelyn took some powdered medicine from her space, mixed it with water, and tried to feed them. But the moment Fallon smelled it, its eyes rolled back. Evelyn nearly had a heart attack.

"What's wrong?"

Rowan had just returned and saw her flustered expression. The moment she saw him, an idea flashed through her mind.

"Give me a little of your blood. Just two drops."

He looked at the two small animals, then walked over to Sprig and lifted it.

"They're dying."

Evelyn nodded. "I know. That's why I need you to save them. How about roasted suckling pig tonight?"

He set Sprig down and picked up Fallon instead.

"This one won't last an hour."

"Then add a roast chicken and a plate of braised pork."

He looked at her. "And a boiled fish with chili."

Unbelievable. He was negotiating at a time like this.

Evelyn nodded. "Fine. I'll start preparing now."

Rowan sliced open his finger. Holding Fallon steady, he gently pressed beneath its tongue. The moment its beak opened, he squeezed in two drops of blood.

He squeezed two extra drops into Sprig. It was obvious he liked Sprig more.

Relieved, Evelyn turned and went into the kitchen to cook for the so called lifesaver.

"How are they?" she called out.

Rowan's expression was stiff. "They seem… dead."

Evelyn rushed over. Sprig and Fallon were lying flat in their nest, completely motionless.

She reached out to touch them. Their bodies were soft and still warm.

"They're not dead. They probably just need time."

Rowan glanced at the dishes already laid out on the table, then looked at her quietly. "Can I eat now?"

"I never said you couldn't." She was too worried about the two little ones to argue.

"That's good. You didn't look pleased. I thought you might grab a knife and try to kill me again."

With that, he walked to the table and started eating as if nothing had happened.

Watching his carefree expression only made her more exhausted.

Thankfully, by ten that night, both little ones pulled through. Fallon woke up and started drinking water. Sprig ate a full bowl of beans. Evelyn finally let out the breath she had been holding.

She brought Rowan a tube of scar removal ointment. She had noticed something. His wounds healed quickly, but they left raised scars behind.

Probably one of the side effects.

"What's this?" he asked.

"Medicine. Put it on your scars. It'll help fade them."

He shook his head. "No need. It won't work on me."

Evelyn glanced at his fingertip. The cut from earlier had already formed a raised ridge. Looking closer, she realized his arms were covered in similar scars.

Had his blood been taken before?

She pressed the ointment into his hand anyway. "How do you know it won't work if you don't try?"

"By the way, you seem to be feeling the cold now. Do you need another blanket?"

He glanced at the fireplace and shook his head again. "There's fire. I'm not cold."

She did not push it. After a day of anxiety, she was exhausted. The moment she lay down in her bedroom, she fell asleep.

After recovering from their illness, Sprig and Fallon seemed to have been affected by Rowan's blood too. Their vitality improved dramatically.

Even with the blizzard raging outside, they tried to run out and play. The bald patches on Fallon's body were already sprouting new feathers. Evelyn even noticed their eye color had deepened.

A thought struck her.

She quickly grabbed a mirror and examined her own eyes.

Still black. No change at all.

"Afraid you'll turn into me?" Rowan appeared soundlessly behind her.

She jumped, caught off guard. Being called out like that made her expression stiffen.

"I wouldn't mind having your abilities," she admitted honestly.

"You can only dream." He did not soften the blow.

She threw the mirror at him. He caught it easily and looked into it.

He stared for a long time.

Evelyn noticed his expression shift. From calm, to irritated, and finally to something close to disgust.

He took a slow breath, set the mirror down, and walked away.

The door slammed hard enough to make the entire cabin shake.

She did not think much of it. But the next day at breakfast, Rowan suddenly lifted his head and looked at her. His gaze was deep, unsettling.

"Yesterday you said you wanted abilities like mine. I can help you."

Evelyn blinked twice. She had almost forgotten the conversation.

"What condition are you going to add this time?"

"When the snow melts, you're leaving, right? I'm going with you."

She nearly choked on her food.

He wanted to go with her?

A heterochromic anomaly with terrifying abilities. Taking him along would be like carrying a ticking bomb.

"Actually," Evelyn said carefully, "being an ordinary person is pretty great."

She thought he wasn't worthy of such extraordinary abilities.

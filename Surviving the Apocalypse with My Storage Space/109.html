Chapter 109 The Base Market

“Where do people go to buy things?”

“At the Settlement Market out back. It was set up jointly by the Hawthorne family and the Hu family. The officials and the army also control survival supplies and emergency stores, but most of the resources are still in the hands of those two families. The Hawthornes used to be the richest family in Elaris, and the Hus speak for themselves. They’re an old family with generations behind them.”

Evelyn rubbed at her temple. “So how are the residents inside the base divided up? Is everyone under official management, or do the Hawthornes and Hus have their own people too?”

Eddie gave her a mysterious smile. “For example, the Medical Division and the hunting crews we’re in now belong to the officials. But the Hawthornes and the Hus each have their own medical units and their own hunting teams as well. At the moment, several factions are keeping one another in check. No single side fully controls the base.”

“Who has the most say?”

Eddie smiled. “The army, obviously. They’re the ones with the weapons.”

The three of them left the apartment building and headed straight for the Settlement Market.

Inside, goods could be bought either by exchanging supplies or by paying with points.

The market looked exactly like an ordinary shopping centre from before the apocalypse, except there were guards standing downstairs, each carrying electric batons and guns.

Once they scanned their resident cards at the machine by the entrance, they were allowed in. The place sold everything except grain.

Evelyn even spotted an arcade.

“Bedding, clothes, and shoes are all on the second floor. What sort of quilt are you after? A thin two-pound quilt costs between five and ten kilos of grain, depending on its condition. Most of them are old and worn anyway. They’re all second-hand, brought back by supply teams scavenging through the cities. If you use medicine instead, one box of cold tablets will get you a thin quilt.”

Medicine saved lives. Even if someone had it, who would be willing to part with it?

“We’ve actually got quilts in our packs already. You saw them earlier. They’re a bit dirty, but still usable. I’m more interested in getting a heater and some toothpaste and toothbrushes.”

She had plenty of heaters, toothpaste, and toothbrushes in her space, of course. But they still needed to exchange something.

“Fine. Daily necessities are on the third floor, furniture on the fourth. I’ll take you up.”

The three of them wandered through the market in their ragged clothes as if they had all the time in the world.

Evelyn tugged at Adrian’s sleeve and lowered her voice. “Is there anything you want?”

He looked down at her and answered gloomily, “I’m hungry.”

Evelyn was silent for a beat.

“We’ll eat when we get back.”

His eyes lit up at once. “Good.”

“Evelyn, heaters aren’t cheap. Is this one having one too?”

She shook her head. “Adrian doesn’t feel the cold much. He doesn’t need one.”

Eddie gave a snort. “That’s because he’s built like a telegraph pole.”

When they reached the daily necessities shop, Evelyn looked over the crowded shelves and suddenly felt as though she were browsing a cheap discount store from the old world.

“What do you want?” A thin man came over, giving the three of them a cursory look. His tone was careless, almost dismissive.

Evelyn scanned the shelves and noticed a few unopened bottles of facial cleanser, shampoo, and body wash.

“Boss, how much for toothpaste and toothbrushes?”

“One kilo of rice, or ten points.”

“And how much would this get me?”

Evelyn produced two packs of cigarettes. The man’s eyes brightened instantly. She even saw the corner of his mouth twitch.

“Two tubes of toothpaste. Two toothbrushes.”

She smiled, then picked up a bottle of body wash and one of shampoo. “Add these too.”

“What do you think this is, a robbery?”

“I wouldn’t dare. If you don’t want to, forget it.”

The cigarette packs were in perfect condition. As a smoker, the man had already caught the rich scent of tobacco.

“Got any more?”

Evelyn shook her head. “Last two packs. I only dared bring them out because you look like a decent man.”

He snorted, snatched the cigarettes from her hand, and threw two tubes of toothpaste and two toothbrushes onto the counter.

“Know your place,” he muttered, lifting the cigarettes under his nose and inhaling deeply with a look of pure satisfaction.

Evelyn thanked him, put the things into Adrian’s pack, and led the other two out of the shop.

“Come on. Let’s go buy the heater.”

“Evelyn, you’re incredible.”

Eddie gave her a thumbs-up. She patted his arm. “Keep it down.”

He then took them up to the furniture floor. Most of the items there had clearly been scavenged from elsewhere, with mud still clinging to them. Evelyn picked out a small heater.

“Twenty kilos of grain or forty-five points.”

Now that really was robbery.

In the end, she traded another two packs of cigarettes for the heater. On the way back, Eddie kept glancing at the cloth bag hanging in front of her.

“That thing of yours is like Doraemon’s magic pocket.”

Evelyn merely smiled and said nothing.

Eddie had to return to the hunting crews, so after they parted ways, she and Adrian headed back to the apartment building.

As soon as they reached the sixth floor, the door to 603 next door opened and a young couple stepped out.

“Oh, are you the new neighbours who moved in today?”

Evelyn gave a small nod. They were both clean and neatly dressed, which suggested they were doing fairly well for themselves in the base.

“My name’s Clara Bennett, and this is my boyfriend, Elliot Hayes.”

Evelyn nodded again, with no intention of introducing herself. Clara did not seem bothered by the awkwardness. After greeting them, she simply took Elliot’s arm and left. Evelyn used the moment to unlock her own door and carry their things inside.

Only when she saw Pip and Lark sitting obediently in the room did she finally smile.

“Adrian, go to your room first. I’ll bring your quilt, clothes, and washing things over.”

But he did not move. Evelyn looked at him blankly.

“What is it?”

“Hungry.”

She sighed and quickly took out a boxed meal for him. Truthfully, she was starving too. They had neither eaten nor slept properly these past two days, and it showed. She looked noticeably more worn out.

“Pip, Lark, time to eat,” Evelyn said, stroking Pip’s little head and then brushing a hand over Lark’s tail.

She took the cactus and the two camellia plants out of her space and watered them.

“Is the hunting crew really where you want to be?”

Adrian did not answer directly. Instead, he looked at her and asked, “What about you? Is the Medical Division where you want to be?”

Evelyn shook her head. “I don’t want to go anywhere. I just want to find somewhere and sleep for three days straight.”

“Then stay here and rest. This place is ours for three months. I can earn the points by myself.”

She looked at him and smiled. Adrian frowned slightly, not understanding.

“What are you smiling at?”

“I’ve never lived off someone else before.”

“I can earn loads of points on my own. Why should you go out and suffer for it?” He sounded genuinely baffled.

“That doesn’t count as suffering. Wherever you go, you have to adapt. If I’m out in the wild alone, I have to adapt to extreme weather and unknown dangers. Now that we’re in the base, I have to adapt to the rules here instead.”

She paused, then looked at him seriously.

“And one more thing. Don’t show anyone what you can really do. Just act like an ordinary person. Don’t be the worst one in the crowd, but don’t be the best or the most noticeable either.”

Adrian looked as though he thought she was treating him like an idiot. She always felt the need to remind him of these things.

“But my eyes really are uncomfortable.”

“Either keep the contact lenses in, or dig your eyeballs out. Pick one. I don’t have time for your fussing.”

He made his choice at once.

“The contact lenses.”

Chapter 103 Contact Lenses and Departure

Remembering who Adrian was, Evelyn wisely shook her head.

He was quiet for a moment, then went on. “That laboratory has been there for decades. They used human beings to test special medicines and poisons. Many years ago, a magnitude ten earthquake struck and destroyed the place. The escape route collapsed. Everyone inside, test subjects and researchers alike, died there.”

Evelyn was now fairly sure he had not come to Ashen Ridge because he was hungry at all. He had probably come looking for that laboratory. More than likely, he had spotted her in the caverns, then followed her out.

As for why he had passed out in the snow, that was probably down to whatever was wrong with his body.

She had no interest in asking why he wanted the laboratory. Everyone had their secrets.

Three days passed. By then, Evelyn’s eyes had more or less recovered, and Adrian went out once more to check the conditions outside.

“All the trees have burned away. The fire hasn’t fully gone out, but we can leave.”

Evelyn packed up her things, wrapped herself up tightly again, and tucked Pip and Lark back into the cloth bag at her side. She was just about to head out when Adrian stopped her.

“This way.”

He turned a concealed door set into the stone wall of the hidden chamber. Evelyn watched his back, her gaze thoughtful and measuring.

Beyond it lay a long, narrow hidden passage. A strange sense of familiarity pricked at her almost at once. Had she not been here before? Was this not the same tomb she had once stumbled into?

Adrian clearly knew the place well. He led her neatly past several hidden chambers before bringing her to a steep flight of steps leading upwards. At the top was yet another hidden chamber. He opened it, and Evelyn saw row after row of crates inside.

“Go on. Take them.”

She lifted the lid of one and froze. Every crate was filled with gold, silver, and jewels. Ten full chests in all.

“Adrian, you…”

“The day you came into the tomb, I was already here. I followed you the whole time. I thought you’d find this place, but instead you wandered off and took those worthless broken bowls from next door. Then again, gold and silver aren’t worth much now either. If you like them, keep them.”

So she had guessed right yet again.

“That night, when you came back with that piece of gold, was that deliberate? You wanted me to know there was more here?”

Adrian gave the faintest nod. “You asked for rent. This is the rent.”

In that case, she had no reason to be polite about it.

“Is this everything?”

He turned to look at her, his expression rather peculiar. “What are you, some sort of treasure-eating beast?”

Evelyn smiled and nodded. Adrian ignored her and kept walking.

After crossing another long hidden passage, he brought her to a stone door.

“Put on a mask.”

Evelyn did not hesitate. She took one out at once and put it on. Adrian opened the stone door, and the two of them stepped through. The instant Evelyn saw what lay before her, her expression hardened.

Dry corpses lay scattered through the ruins. Broken glass tubes from experiments littered the ground. Medical equipment sat under layers of dust. The machines were unmistakably old, decades old.

So this really had been a human testing facility.

“To think there’s a place like this just the other side of the wall from a tomb.”

Adrian let out a short, cold laugh. Decades ago, people had still hidden themselves away in remote mountains to carry out experiments for fear of being discovered. Now laboratories were built right inside cities. No one knew that beneath the gloss of prosperity and civilisation, human beings still exploited other human beings in the name of science and technology.

Evelyn took out a gas cylinder and handed it to him. “Blow it up.”

“What, are we destroying every place we come across now?”

The fact that he could still joke at a time like this made her roll her eyes. “If you’re too afraid to light it, then stand back. Where’s the exit? I’ll do it once we’re outside.”

Adrian’s lips curved, only slightly. Evelyn did not notice.

“I’d better do it. With those short legs of yours, you’d never make it out in time.”

Short legs?

She most certainly did not have short legs.

Adrian took the gas cylinder from her and set it down. “One won’t be enough. We’ll need at least three.”

The abandoned laboratory was enormous. Everywhere they went, there were more withered corpses. By the time they reached the exit, they had already placed three gas cylinders inside.

“You go first.”

The moment she stepped out, Evelyn barely had time to adjust to the sharp contrast in temperature, or to register that the sun had finally reappeared, before Adrian was already ordering her away.

She ran some distance before looking back at him. He was still standing at the entrance. They were deep in the mountain now, on the opposite side from the cavern entrance.

On impulse, Evelyn shouted his name. Adrian turned.

“You’re not planning to kill yourself, are you?”

Adrian said nothing.

Then came the explosions.

One after another, the blasts cracked through the mountain. Evelyn ran as if the wind itself had taken hold of her feet, leaping over little pockets of fire as she fled. In that moment she felt as though even an Olympic sprinter would have struggled to beat her.

Ashen Ridge had been burned to ruin. The fir trees stood blackened, their branches still flickering with flames. The snow on the ground had not yet melted, but its white surface was now streaked with ash and splinters of charred wood. Her ears began ringing again. Her mind went blank, save for a single urgent command hammering through it.

Run. Faster. Faster.

Evelyn had once seen news footage of exploding fuel tankers and gas cylinders. They were devastating, the sort of thing that could flatten everything for miles around.

Her legs suddenly gave way beneath her and she nearly went down, but a hand clamped hard around her arm and hauled her upright.

She looked up to see Adrian, filthy with soot and dust. She gave him a quick once-over. Good. No missing limbs. No obvious injuries.

Once again, he had proved himself to be utterly ruthless.

“Are you all right?”

Evelyn nodded, then shook her head.

A puzzled look crossed his face.

“My legs have gone weak. I can still walk, though.”

He looked at her legs with open disdain, then without another word hoisted her up and slung her onto his back.

“You’re hopeless.”

He sounded full of scorn, but his feet moved as though they had wheels of fire under them, carrying them away at astonishing speed.

From his back, Evelyn turned to look at the thick smoke billowing in the distance. She had the odd feeling that after the destruction of that laboratory, something about Adrian had changed.

He carried her for ten minutes before she finally made him put her down. Another moment on his back and she thought she might die of the jolting. It felt less like being carried and more like being shaken apart in the back of a lorry. The second her feet touched the ground, dizziness hit her so hard that she toppled straight into the snow.

“Let’s walk from here. We’re safe enough now.”

Evelyn lay there for two minutes before slowly dragging herself upright again.

When she opened the cloth bag, she found that Pip and Lark had been jolted senseless as well. Waiting for them to recover, she looked around at the land surrounding them and let out a quiet sigh.

As far as the eye could see, there was nothing but desolation and ruin.

When she had first arrived at Ashen Ridge, some of the trees had already been dead, but the woods were still thick, and wild greens had begun to push through the earth. Everything had shown the first signs of returning to life.

Then came an earthquake.
Then a blizzard.
Then fireballs raining from the sky.

Every hint of recovery had been destroyed all over again.

“This is for you.”

She pulled a small box from her space and handed it over. Inside was a pair of black contact lenses.

“What’s this?”

Adrian opened the box and stared at the two thin, round pieces inside, looking thoroughly baffled all over again.

Chapter 143 Toxic Haze 5

The base was no longer called a base.

It had been renamed Blackwater Safe Zone.

The security team was organised into groups of ten, with each pair working together as a unit, patrolling every corner of the safe zone.

Thirty people rotated in three shifts. Ten worked at a time for eight hours each. The work itself was fairly straightforward: settling disputes between residents, keeping order, and the like.

People were allowed to barter food with one another, but they were not allowed to trade children for grain, and robbery was strictly forbidden.

Anyone who broke the rules was expelled from the safe zone.

With a security team in place, people felt much safer. Some even dared to venture outside the zone to look for timber.

The tricycles Calvin and the others had rebuilt finally came into their own. They could be used to haul soil, or wood, and there were still quite a few capable people in the safe zone who managed to build simple handcarts out of salvaged timber.

The residents all knew Evelyn was a doctor now. If they had an ache, a fever, or some other minor problem, they came to her for treatment. Payment could be one cabbage, or half a bowl of wheat.

The orphans who had lost their parents were gathered together, and Nathan led them in digging soil, planting vegetables, chopping wood, collecting clothes, and doing whatever work they could.

Heaven had left them one narrow path to survive: the strange rapid growth of plants, and the small rain that came once every three days.

“Mouse. There’s a mouse.”

Excited shouting broke out outside. Everyone rushed out in a frenzy to catch it. Whoever succeeded was delighted. The rest could only watch in disappointment.

A mouse was meat, after all.

Not long after that, mice began appearing more and more often. Evelyn checked several of them and found they were healthy enough to eat.

For the people in the safe zone, catching a mouse felt almost like celebrating Christmas. At least it meant there would be meat on the table.

Wendy was at that wonderfully lively age now. Calvin wanted to carry her on his back, but she flatly refused. She insisted on taking the little net bag Evelyn had made for her and going out to catch mice instead. After drinking Evelyn’s blood, Wendy had become surprisingly strong, but no matter how capable she was, she was still only four and a half.

“Sister Evelyn, I want to go catch mice. Tell Dad for me, and he’ll definitely say yes.”

Evelyn nodded. “I know you’re very capable, but the haze is thick, and you could trip just walking about. You’re only little. What if you get lost?”

Wendy pointed solemnly at Ray. “Ray’s coming with me. We’ll be safe.”

Evelyn had no choice but to bring it up with Calvin. In the end he agreed. Evelyn gave Wendy a bottle of chilli spray for protection, and Ray took her off to catch mice.

Evan, meanwhile, stayed with Nathan to help out.

Clara came over with some maize to exchange for medicine. Adrian was sitting upstairs on the second floor, Pip in his lap, Lark perched on his shoulder, reading a cookbook with grave concentration.

“Evelyn, why does Adrian love that cookbook so much? Last time I came over he was reading it, and now I’m here again and he’s still reading it.”

Evelyn could not help laughing. “It’s about his only hobby.”

“You should send him out to catch mice. Elliot caught three yesterday. Adrian’s so fast, he’d definitely catch more.”

“Adrian says it’s boring.”

Clara clicked her tongue. “You spoil him. We’re short of everything and he’s still sulking.”

Adrian glanced at Clara, his face full of obvious impatience.

Once Clara had left, he came over to Evelyn looking like a put-upon husband and immediately started complaining.

“Don’t let her into our house again.”

“Why not?”

“She was speaking badly about me.”

Evelyn fought back a smile. “That’s rare. You’re actually angry.”

“Then let’s have fried chicken and grilled meat tonight.”

His thoughts changed direction with startling speed, straight from wounded pride to food.

“What does that have to do with anything?”

Adrian looked entirely serious. “You let her in so she could speak badly about me. You should make it up to me.”

So in the end, it all came back to food.

Evelyn frowned as if considering it carefully. Adrian added in a mournful voice, “If you want mice, I can go catch some now.”

Evelyn fell silent.

That really would not be necessary. The mice could stay for everyone else.

“Given how hard you’ve worked lately, I think you deserve a reward,” she said at last. “Along with what you’ve just ordered, I can make a pan of spicy dry-fried prawns as well. What do you think?”

Adrian’s eyes lit up at once. “Evelyn, I’ll do the work. You go and rest.”

Evelyn burst out laughing and handed him the hoe in her hand.

Ever since the first mouse appeared in the safe zone, there had been countless more every day after that. The security team temporarily turned into a rat-catching squad, and pest control became their latest task.

The mice were nearly as quick as cockroaches, but once the meat was dried, it kept well for a long time. People were catching them partly to turn them into preserved rations.

Some time earlier, when they had still been living in the apartment block, Evelyn had treated a pregnant woman there. The next time she saw her was in that same building.

The little boy was still hiding in a corner. He looked painfully thin, and his clothes were tattered almost beyond use.

Evelyn examined the pregnant woman’s belly. She was not yet due, but she had taken a fall and brought on labour early. There was no choice now. The baby would have to be delivered.

The man of the house paced up and down nearby, full of nerves, and the sight of him irritated Evelyn almost immediately.

“Could you go outside? You’re getting in the way.”

“I’m staying here. I want to see my son be born.”

Evelyn glanced at the little boy in the corner, baffled.

He already had a son, did he not? Why was he so desperate for another?

The woman was in poor condition. Her limbs were badly swollen. She looked at the man with pleading eyes.

“If I don’t make it, you must be kind to Toby.”

Toby was the little boy in the corner. Hearing his name, he cautiously lifted his head to look at her, then lowered it again at once.

“Our son is about to be born,” the man snapped. “Just get on with it and stop talking rubbish.”

“Toby is my sister’s only child. You wanted a baby, and I gave you one, but I’m begging you, please be kind to Toby. Don’t cast him aside. I’m begging you.”

The man said nothing. He turned and glanced at the boy, his face full of annoyance.

“They’re all nothing but burdens. You’d better give birth to my son, or I’ll throw that little burden outside.”

The woman was so shaken by his words that she went into a catastrophic haemorrhage.

An hour later, the baby was born.

A tiny, fragile little boy, weak as a newborn kitten.

Evelyn had experience delivering babies, but she had no way of saving someone from a haemorrhage like this. The woman died with her eyes still open. The man picked up the baby and looked ready to start shouting at Evelyn, but Adrian came storming in just then and the man lost his nerve at once.

“What are you doing in here?”

Adrian did not answer. He only looked at the man.

“Come on,” he said to Evelyn.

She shook her head, cleaned the woman’s body as best she could, and only then left with her medical bag.

“You get out too, you little burden. Your aunt’s dead, and I’m not feeding you. I’ve got my own son now.”

He snatched Toby up and threw him out into the corridor.

Just then Evelyn felt the whole building give a sudden lurch.

She had only just pulled the boy to his feet when Adrian scooped her up and ran down the apartment block stairs with her in his arms.

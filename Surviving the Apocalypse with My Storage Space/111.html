Chapter 111 First Day at Work

Clara had far too many questions. Evelyn had no idea which one to answer first. By rights, anyone who had survived the end of the world ought to have a strong sense of self-preservation and suspicion. Clara, however, looked all innocence and openness. Had she simply been sheltered too well, or was it all an act?

“That building ahead is the Medical Division. You go on in. I’ve got to head down to the Underground Quarter to tend the herbs. Bye then. See you tonight.”

Evelyn gave her a small nod. “See you.”

As soon as she stepped into the Medical Division, a receptionist-like attendant stopped her, checked her residence permit, and confirmed her details.

“We’ve received your file. It says here you know medicine. Can you handle external injuries on your own?”

Evelyn nodded. “Yes.”

“Then you’re going to the Surgical Unit. They’re desperately short of doctors there, so you’ll be starting work straightaway.”

“That’s fine.”

The attendant seemed pleased with the answer. She filled in a form for Evelyn, then led her over to surgery.

They had barely reached the door when a chorus of cries came from inside, and the thick metallic smell of blood hit Evelyn at once.

“Dr. Hargreaves, there’s a new arrival. She can operate, do acupuncture, even deliver babies.”

Dr. Hargreaves was treating a man’s arm wound. The flesh had already begun to rot. Judging by the state of it, the injury was at least a week old. There was not enough medicine for proper treatment, so all he could do was cut away the rotten tissue, give the man an anti-inflammatory tablet, smear on a bit of medicine, and send him on his way.

“Have you got experience?” Dr. Hargreaves turned to look at Evelyn. She was a good deal taller than he was and had to lower her gaze to meet his. She nodded.

“I have.”

“Good. Those ten over there are yours.”

Evelyn glanced over. There was a sickly child, a heavily pregnant woman, and a man lying unconscious, among others.

Dr. Hargreaves handed her a set of tools, including a packet of masks, a packet of gloves, and a protective suit.

Evelyn did not waste words. She put on the suit and got to work immediately.

There were nurses in the Surgical Unit too, but very few of them, and they were even busier than the doctors.

Apart from Dr. Hargreaves, there were two other doctors inside, though for the moment Evelyn could not tell whether they were men or women.

What was more, she quickly realised the whole place was a muddle. It was called surgery, but internal medicine and gynaecology were being handled here too.

So that was what the attendant had meant when she said they were short of doctors. One doctor had to do the work of several, and everyone was so busy they scarcely had time to use the loo.

Evelyn examined the sick child. He was clutching his stomach and crying out in pain. The middle-aged man and woman beside him were presumably his parents, and the woman kept wiping away tears.

Evelyn pressed gently on the lower right side of his abdomen. “Does it hurt badly here?”

The boy nodded. He was so thin and bony that he could not have been more than eleven or twelve.

“It’s appendicitis.”

She turned to Dr. Hargreaves. “How are we handling the operation?”

He glanced at her and pointed towards the operating room beside them.

“Can you manage on your own?”

Evelyn was just about to say yes when the boy’s father objected.

“No. Absolutely not. She’s just a young girl, how can she possibly do surgery? Doctor, we’ve been queuing all night. Couldn’t you let my son go first?”

Dr. Hargreaves’ face darkened. “There are dozens of people waiting here and every single one of them is suffering. Why should you jump the queue? She is a doctor. Stay if you want treatment. If not, get out.”

The man did not dare say another word after being shouted at, but the way he looked at Evelyn was full of resentment.

“If anything happens to my son, you’ll pay for it with your life.”

Evelyn’s eyes turned cold. “And if I save him, should you kill yourself to apologise?”

When the man lunged at her, trying to shove her, she seized him and pinned him to the floor in one swift motion.

“Let go of me.”

Evelyn applied the slightest bit more force. His wrist snapped at once, and his scream tore through the room.

Security from the Medical Division came over with guns in hand.

“Who’s causing trouble?”

Before Evelyn could explain, Dr. Hargreaves spoke to them and laid out exactly what had happened. The man’s wife was crying and begging for mercy at the side, while the boy had fainted from fright.

One of the guards looked at Evelyn, then hauled the man to his feet and cast a warning glance at everyone else.

“Anyone causes trouble again and you’ll be thrown straight out of the base. Doctors are busy. Queue properly.”

Evelyn thanked Dr. Hargreaves. Then, quite suddenly, he pulled down his mask, and her eyes widened.

“You are?”

“Evelyn, do you remember me? I’m Julian Hargreaves, your father’s apprentice. I visited your house before.”

How could she not remember? It had simply been years. When her parents died, he had still been overseas for further training. Back then he had been rather stout, but now he was gaunt, with hair gone entirely white. He looked like a completely different man.

“No time for more just now. I wasn’t sure it was you at first. Even after seeing your name, I hesitated for ages. You’ve grown up, and your face has changed a fair bit. But the way you handled that just now was exactly like your father. Same fiery temper.”

Evelyn pressed her lips together in a faint smile. Dr. Hargreaves patted her lightly on the arm.

“Back to work. If you need anything, come to me.”

After that, the rest of the treatments went much more smoothly. Evelyn still performed the operation on the boy. One operation was worth two hundred points, but only forty made it onto her card. Whether it was the end of the world or the old civilised age, medical care was always ruinously expensive.

The pregnant woman had thin arms and legs, but a belly swollen like a basin. She said her husband was in the hunting crews, and that she had come in because she had been feeling uncomfortable today.

Evelyn examined her belly. There was nothing wrong at the moment, but the birth would likely come within the next day or two. The woman was terribly undernourished, so much so that her hair had gone dry and yellow. Evelyn told her that the baby would probably be very weak when it was born.

A pregnancy examination was worth twenty points. Evelyn received four.

As one patient left, another arrived. From morning until afternoon the Medical Division remained packed. At midday there was half an hour for lunch. It was Evelyn’s first time in the canteen there, and the sight of the food made her think at once of Adrian. He would never be able to stomach fare this poor. He was far too fussy.

One spoonful of greens, one spoonful of potatoes, a bowl of thin rice porridge, and one bun.

There were only a few scattered grains of rice floating in the porridge. The greens and potatoes had no oil in them, and barely any salt. The bun was cold and hard enough to chip a tooth, but Evelyn ate everything anyway, leaving not a scrap behind.

Work finished at half past six. Nine hours in all. After clocking off, Evelyn did not bother with the canteen again and went straight back to the apartment building.

“Evelyn, where did they put you?”

On the way back she ran into Clara once more. Clara was still bright with energy, her eyes sparkling exactly as before.

“Surgery.”

Clara looked surprised. “I’ve heard it’s terribly busy there. Still, there’s one advantage. They give you an extra hundred points a month.”

Dr. Hargreaves had already told Evelyn about that. It was a housing allowance.

“Our herb gardens are very quiet by comparison. Not many points in it, though.”

“Are the herbs growing well?”

Clara nodded eagerly. “Very well. I’m in charge of giving them sunlight.”

Chapter 79 Surviving Alone: Part 3

Evelyn fell asleep right there in the chair.

That night, she had another nightmare. Everyone from Block D was there. So were the torrential rains, the floods, the poisonous moths, the toads, the writhing mass of snakes.

Her leg jerked violently, and she snapped awake.

For a moment, she stared blankly at her surroundings.

The same mountain hollow. Her vehicle parked beside her. The oversized umbrella overhead. The fire pit at her feet, though the wood had burned down to nothing and even the last sparks had died.

She took out a pine torch, lit it with her lighter, and stacked fresh dry wood on top. Soon, the flames leapt back to life.

Sprig was nowhere in sight.

Evelyn stood and stretched, then limped into the RV to check inside. The dampness had dried completely. The metallic smell was gone. She pulled fresh bedding and a mattress from her storage space and made up the bed, then took a moment to admire her new home.

While cleaning yesterday, she had discovered several bags of rice. One of the refrigerators had contained chilled steaks, beer, and bottles of red wine.

There were even a few barrels of gasoline in the storage compartment.

Taking this RV had been a smart move. She had scored big.

The weather was growing colder by the day. She was glad she had chosen to head into the mountains after the earthquake. If she had stayed put or continued toward Drakepool Base, she suspected trouble would have followed her nonstop.

After breakfast, she got to work.

The more she thought about it, the more certain she became that building a small wooden cabin would be safer than relying solely on the RV. If a blizzard came, she could burn wood for heat. Living in the RV would mean constantly burning gasoline for electricity. And in extreme cold, there was no guarantee the doors would even open.

Chainsaw in hand, she began cutting down half the fir trees in the hollow. The cabin did not need to be large. Twenty square meters would be enough.

The firs grew densely here, their trunks straight and tall. Perfect building material.

She worked the entire day. After felling the trees, she cut them into measured lengths and stacked them neatly.

Fortunately, she had removed the doors from her old home and stored them away. She already had doors and windows ready. The fir logs were enough for pillars, beams, and even moisture resistant floor planks.

By evening, Sprig returned.

Who knew where it had roamed all day. It looked tired. By the time Evelyn finished working, the little squirrel was already asleep inside the vehicle.

After sitting in the cold wind all night before, Evelyn felt the beginnings of a stuffy nose. Tonight, she would sleep in the RV. It was cleaned and ready now, so the smaller commuter car could go back into storage.

For dinner, she decided to indulge.

Her wounds were mostly healed. A little spice would not hurt.

She took out a packet of hot pot base and set up a small table beside the fire. A cutting board and knife went on top. From her storage space, she pulled out a grass carp and sliced it into thin fillets, seasoning them briefly.

She placed an alcohol block into a portable stove and lit it. Once the wok heated up, she added the hot pot base and poured in water. When it began to boil, she added the fish slices, tofu, and bean sprouts.

Staring at the fire, she made a mental note. She would need to build a proper stove soon. Cooking over a real hearth would be much easier.

Drawn by the rich aroma, Sprig scampered out of the vehicle.

Evelyn picked up a piece of fish and offered it.

The squirrel licked it once.

Then erupted into frantic squeaking from the heat.

Evelyn burst out laughing. "Serves you right for being greedy."

She poured it a cup of clean water. Sprig buried its face in the cup and drank desperately, then looked up at her with pitiful eyes.

The hot pot was intensely spicy. Sweat beaded on Evelyn's forehead and the tip of her nose, but in weather this cold, a little heat was exactly what her body needed.

After a deeply satisfying meal, she was in such a good mood she even enjoyed washing the dishes.

“Spice it up, turn it red, burn a hole right through my head. If my nose ain’t running yet, I’m not done seasoning it…”

Her off key singing echoed through the mountain hollow. Sprig covered its ears with its tail, looking genuinely pained.

Two thick logs sat on the fire beside the RV, heavy enough to keep burning until morning. Evelyn climbed inside, ready to wash up and sleep.

She had already cleaned the bathroom and stocked it with her own things. She carried the battery pack inside and connected it to the power. The interior lit up instantly. Evelyn began heating water. After working all day and sweating through her clothes, she needed a hot shower more than anything.

She set the cactus she had planted earlier on the table, then opened a book on building a wooden cabin and started reading.

When Sprig realized she was being ignored, it waddled over and stared at the pages with her. Evelyn snickered, scooped it up, and carried it into the bathroom.

The little menace needed a bath too.

Sprig was not afraid of water at all. It perched in the basin and played happily, foam piling on its head and tail. Evelyn gave it a few ridiculous hairstyles. Still, she worried it might catch a chill, so after a short while she lifted it out.

She dried it with a hair dryer, and its fluffy tail puffed up beautifully again. Evelyn could not resist. She turned on her phone and snapped a few pictures.

"Sprig, where did you go today?"

The squirrel chattered for a long time, but Evelyn had no idea what it was trying to say.

"Alright, go to sleep. I'm taking my shower."

Her hair had grown out a lot, but she was not planning to cut it. The bathroom mirror was large. She studied the wound on her forehead.

There was no scar or disfigurement. Thank goodness.

The hot shower revived her. After drying her hair, she looked almost like her old self again. The same face from before the apocalypse, except her eyes were colder now, sharper, more dangerous.

Wrapped in a military coat, Evelyn sat on the bed and returned to her book. She had watched plenty of wilderness survival videos. She had never built a house with her own hands, but she was confident she could handle it. Her storage space held everything, from nails to cement and sand. She had stocked up on all of it.

Outside, the wind howled.

She checked the fire one last time through the window. Everything looked fine. She shut the RV door and got ready for bed.

Her leg did not hurt as much anymore. Her arm was sore from using the chainsaw all day.

She slapped a medicated patch on the ache, then slid under the blankets.

Sprig had been curled in its nest, but as soon as Evelyn lay down, it hopped onto the bed and settled at the foot of it.

She did not chase it away.

Nine thirty.

Early to bed, early to rise.

*****

At eight in the morning, Evelyn opened her eyes to find Sprig already awake. She played with it for a while before getting up.

"Morning. Time for a little exercise. Stretch your leg. Bend at the waist. Clap your hands. Shake your head. Okay, now we're fully awake."

She brushed her teeth and washed her face, then smoothed on a layer of moisturizer. Pulling on a knit hat and her tactical jacket, she geared up for the day.

After opening the door, she added more wood to the fire. Then, saw and measuring tape in hand, Evelyn marched toward the neatly stacked fir logs like she was going into battle.

She had already chosen where the foundation would go. If she wanted protection from wind and snow, the base mattered most. The cabin did not need to be tall, but it did need to hold weight.

She had a rough plan in her head.

Now she just needed to build it, step by step.

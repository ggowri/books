Chapter 68 Departure: Part 3

Three hours passed quickly.

The convoy set off again.

Under the bright morning sky, the long line of refugees slowly crept forward.

"Why are we stopping again?"

They had not even been driving for an hour when the convoy halted once more. Start and stop, over and over. When would they ever reach the base?

Someone in the car behind them muttered a complaint, but the moment the men collecting protection fees walked over, he fell silent.

"Two people from each vehicle go up front to clear the road," one of the men barked. "There's been a landslide. Move fast. If you don't send anyone, pay half a kilo of grain."

"I'll go with Eddie. Calvin stays," Evelyn said immediately.

Eddie grabbed a shovel and jumped out without hesitation. Ray and Evan wanted to go too, but Calvin stopped them.

"Evelyn's arrangement makes sense. You two are still kids. If something happens, you might not come back. The supplies in the car matter too. We'll guard the vehicle."

After everything they had been through together, they trusted each other's judgment.

Evelyn and Eddie made their way to the front of the convoy. A mound of dirt and several massive rocks blocked the road.

More people arrived one after another. Complaints filled the air, but no one dared to refuse.

"Hurry up. Two hours. It has to be cleared."

Evelyn glanced at the man urging them on, then casually shifted her gaze to the vehicles behind him.

Leading the convoy was an off road vehicle. Behind it was a large RV. Three more off road vehicles followed.

When she had walked up earlier, she had seen several people inside the RV playing cards. Their clothes were clean. Bodyguards stood outside the door, pistols tucked into their waists. Anyone who so much as glanced inside was met with curses.

"What are you looking at?" Eddie whispered.

"Nothing."

Evelyn lowered her head and started shoveling. Eddie glanced back at the vehicles but said nothing more.

Two hours later, the road was cleared.

A blister had formed on Eddie's palm. The men shouted for everyone to return to their cars. The convoy was moving again.

People jogged back.

Originally, Evelyn was supposed to drive this stretch. But after the heavy labor, Calvin took over instead.

Back in the car, Evelyn and Eddie were too tired to speak. After a few sips of water, she watched the convoy roll forward again, sleep tugging at her eyelids.

"Eddie, is it true?" Ray asked quietly.

"Of course," Eddie replied. "The people in those first five vehicles are wealthy. They've got bodyguards. The guys collecting protection fees are just their lackeys. Stay away from those cars."

Ray and Evan nodded quickly.

"Must be nice to have money and power," Eddie muttered. "Even in the apocalypse, they still live in comfort."

"You regretting turning down that rich woman again?" Calvin teased.

"Calvin, I didn't know you had jokes in you," Eddie laughed. "But you're right. I regret it deeply. Once upon a time, I had a chance to get rich overnight, and I didn't seize it. If God gave me another shot, I'd grab that rich woman's hand and say, I'm yours."

Laughter filled the cramped vehicle.

Even Wendy giggled quietly in her corner.

Evelyn looked at the sun rising higher in the sky and allowed herself a faint smile.

At ten o'clock, the convoy stopped again. Another problem up ahead.

This time, Calvin went alone to help clear the road. Wendy stayed in the car with Evelyn while the others went to dig for wild greens.

Eddie had some luck. He caught a giant rat.

That single rat nearly cost them their lives.

When Eddie, Ray, and Evan returned, all three of them were smeared with blood and sporting fresh wounds. The moment Evelyn saw the giant rat in Eddie's hand, she understood.

"We were ready to let it go and not argue with those bandits," Eddie said angrily. "Then some idiot woman shows up and tries to guilt trip me. Says I should give the rat to the elderly and kids. If I didn't catch one, that's my problem. But I did catch it. It's mine. Why should I hand it over?"

He winced as he spoke.

"I snapped back once and a few men rushed me. Ray and Evan stepped in to help and got hit too. But we didn't lose. Knocked a few of their teeth out."

Wendy hurriedly pulled a few wild greens from her little pouch and handed them over. Eddie grimaced through the pain and ruffled her hair.

"Thanks, Wendy. I can't eat right now."

Evelyn took out three painkillers and handed them over. After settling Wendy to the side, she began cleaning and bandaging their wounds.

"This road is a disaster," Eddie muttered. "We have to stop and clear it several times a day. The people walking behind us are catching up."

He lowered his voice.

"Oh, and there was a guy on a motorcycle. Poor bastard. Went to dig greens, came back, and his bike had been stripped. Tires gone. Fuel gone."

Evelyn nodded. "That explains the screaming I heard earlier."

"We're all suffering. Can't people at least stop doing horrible things to each other?"

Eddie let out a dry laugh. "When everyone's suffering, who has room to care about others? People are selfish. Everyone just wants to survive. The guy who got robbed was just unlucky."

But theft was not an isolated incident. It happened every day.

Food stolen. Clothes stolen. Fuel siphoned. One careless moment and everything you owned could vanish.

The convoy was too long and there were too many people. In times like these, you swallowed your losses. There was no justice to be found.

After days of eating nothing but wild green cakes, Evelyn's stomach twisted in pain. During the next stop, she wandered farther away under the pretense of digging greens and pulled two steamed buns from her space, devouring them quickly.

But the worst part was not the wild green cakes or the outdoor toilets.

It was her period.

The others noticed she was unwell and tried to let her rest more. Evelyn refused to take advantage. She rested for one day. On the second, she insisted on working again.

Two painkillers. Driving. Clearing roadblocks.

By the end of the day, the pain had dulled.

But the sticky discomfort clinging to her body was unbearable. She could smell the faint metallic scent of blood on herself. She was sure others could too.

"Evelyn," Eddie whispered, leaning closer. "I still have two relatively clean shirts."

She blinked. "What do you mean?"

"I mean… they're clean. You could cut them up and use them. It might make you more comfortable."

A flicker of warmth passed through her chest.

"I have sanitary pads," she said calmly. "Don't worry."

At the mention of those words, an awkward look crossed Eddie's dirt streaked face.

"Good. You're my sister from another mother. If there's anything inconvenient, just tell me. I'll handle it."

Evelyn suddenly smacked his shoulder.

Eddie yelped. "You trying to kill me?"

She gave him an embarrassed smile. "Sorry. I just wanted to say thank you."

"You don't have to assault me to say thanks. I may be a man, but I'm delicate."

Evelyn rolled her eyes.

"Get lost."

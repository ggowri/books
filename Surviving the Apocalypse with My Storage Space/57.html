Chapter 57 Heatwave, The Deal

Eddie staggered, nearly losing his balance. "Do you have a mirror?"

"No."

"…Am I really disfigured?"

Evelyn stared at him.

That was what he cared about right now?

"It looks like it," she said flatly. "Not important. You're a grown man. Why does that matter? Move. It'll be daylight soon."

Eddie clutched his chest like he might cough up blood again. Evelyn instinctively stepped farther away. His hands trembled as if his entire world had just collapsed.

She ignored him and continued forward with her trekking pole.

Seeing she had no intention of comforting him, Eddie hobbled after her.

"Evelyn, is where you live far? Are you alone? I can't believe I ran into you again. I thought you were dead."

She closed her eyes briefly.

Was it too late to cancel the deal?

"You're really noisy."

He grinned. "That's not noisy. I just talk a lot. You know, even though you're dressed worse than I am, you sound full of energy. Must be doing pretty well. Ow. I stepped on a nail."

Evelyn stopped.

With visible restraint, she pulled a wrench from her backpack, crouched down, and pried the nail out of his shoe.

"You carry a wrench with you? That's actually a pretty good weapon. Ow, that hurts. It won't get infected, right?"

She decided not to answer.

The entire walk back was filled with Eddie's nonstop chatter.

By the time they reached Willow Heights, it was past four in the morning. The sun was rising. Harsh sunlight poured down, heating their clothes until the fabric felt scorching against their skin.

Evelyn grabbed his arm and forced him into a jog.

When they finally stumbled into Block D, both of them collapsed onto the floor, gasping for air.

Even through layers of clothing, her arms and thighs felt burned from the sun.

She shot Eddie a glare.

If not for him stepping on that nail and slowing them down, she would have been back much earlier.

"Do you blame me?" he asked cautiously.

Dried blood crusted across his face. He looked frightening and pitiful at the same time.

"What do you think?" she snapped.

"I'm already this miserable. Don't hold it against me. Fine, how about this. The stuff in that hidden room, we split it seventy-thirty. You take seventy."

Evelyn gave a cold laugh. "So what were you planning before?"

"Fifty-fifty. Fair, right?"

"I was thinking I take everything. You get nothing."

She stood, brushed sand off her clothes, and started climbing the stairs.

Eddie stared at her in shock.

That was daylight robbery!

But right now, he was in no position to argue. He swallowed his complaints and trailed behind her.

"At least give me something. Eighty-twenty? Ninety-ten? Still no?"

Evelyn exhaled slowly, then stopped and turned to face him.

"How much you get depends on my mood. There are five people living in this building. Pick any empty unit and move in. Later, I'll introduce you to the others. If you live here, you follow the rules."

Her gaze hardened.

"Otherwise… you understand."

Meaning death.

Eddie nodded rapidly.

They reached the tenth floor.

Evelyn pointed at a door.

"This is my place."

"The units next door are empty. The eleventh floor has been turned into a gym. On the twelfth floor, there are two households. Calvin and his daughter Wendy. And Ray and Evan."

Evelyn brought him up to the twelfth floor and knocked on Calvin's door.

It opened quickly.

Relief flashed across Calvin's face when he saw Evelyn safe and sound. Then his eyes shifted to the blood-covered man standing behind her.

"Calvin, this is Eddie Thompson. He'll be staying here for now." Evelyn gave a brief explanation.

Calvin nodded. "Alright. Understood. Eddie, welcome. My name is Calvin Shaw. Just call me Calvin."

The moment Eddie heard he was a police officer, he brightened.

"Hello, hello, Calvin. I'm Eddie. Evelyn and I met during the toxic moth outbreak. It was a stormy night, thunder and rain pouring down…"

"Stop. Shut up." Evelyn shot him a warning look, barely restraining the urge to hit him.

Eddie grinned.

With his bloodied face, the smile nearly made Wendy cry as she hid behind her father.

Right then, Ray and Evan came out of the neighboring unit. Evelyn introduced Eddie again.

After a brief round of explanations, everyone understood the situation.

Eddie would stay in the Rivers family's empty apartment next to Evelyn. Calvin, Ray, and Evan helped him clean it up.

After issuing a few sharp warnings, Evelyn returned to her own place.

She closed the door, dropped her backpack, and gritted her teeth as she peeled off her clothes.

Her face, arms, neck, thighs, calves. All burned to varying degrees.

After a quick rinse, she applied ice packs to the worst areas. When the heat subsided slightly, she spread ointment over the burns. Only then did the pain ease.

Last night's haul had been excellent.

She reorganized everything she had collected and stored it neatly inside her space. The shelving units were finally put to use, especially for food storage.

Then she took out the two safes from the club's top floor.

She broke them open without ceremony.

The first contained several sets of jewelry. She had no idea if they were real, but they sparkled brilliantly. Hard to resist.

The second safe made her heart race.

A gun.

And several boxes of ammunition.

She had found the safes hidden inside a bed, the mattress hollowed out to conceal them. She had expected gold and diamonds. Instead, she found something far more precious.

A weapon.

Her eyes lit up.

The gun she had taken from the bandits could now be fully operational. Just like that, she owned two firearms.

It felt like winning the lottery overnight.

After examining them briefly, she stored everything away.

She had been out all night. She was exhausted, hungry, and her stomach felt uncomfortable.

She glanced at the calendar.

Her period was due any day now.

The boxed meals in her space were running low. She would need to cook and stock up again soon.

She pulled out a stir-fried pork rice bowl, finished it quickly, then sprawled inelegantly across the mat. The air conditioner hummed, cool air brushing over her skin.

She thought about the hidden room Eddie had mentioned.

Two minutes later, she was asleep.

She did not sleep long.

The burns throbbed too much.

Rubber left in direct sunlight would melt and smoke within minutes. That was how brutal the sun had become.

She got up, reapplied ointment, then washed her hair with cold water before her period started. Even though she had plenty of water, she refused to waste it. Used water could still water plants or wash her feet.

In the afternoon, she carried a battery pack into the kitchen.

From her space, she pulled out a compact, high-tech range hood she had dismantled from Maple Grove. It was designed for indoor barbecue smoke. No bigger than a pencil holder, but it absorbed grease and smoke instantly, completely silent.

She had struck gold with this thing.

She had worried about noise drawing attention while cooking. Now she could finally relax.

She opened a recipe book and began experimenting with different cuisine.

Her stomach growled again, loud and impatient.

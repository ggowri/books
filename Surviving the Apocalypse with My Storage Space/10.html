Chapter 10 Rainstorm, Part 6

When Evelyn got home, she found the power was out again. She dropped her bags in the living room and quickly turned on the emergency light. Stripping off her life vest and raincoat, she slapped on a few heat patches, then soaked in a hot bath until the chill finally left her bones.

After sipping some ginger tea, she began sorting through the things she'd bought that day.

But the most important takeaway from her trip wasn't the supplies. It was the creeping realization that the natural disasters in this life seemed even worse than they were in her previous one.

She pulled back the curtains and looked out at the dark, oppressive sky. The weight of it made it hard to breathe.

The power outage had everyone in the group chat complaining again. Still, a few people were showing off pictures of the generators they'd managed to buy.

But generators needed diesel or gasoline to run. Prices were skyrocketing. Without enough fuel, their bragging wouldn't last long. Worse, flashing those photos around might invite trouble.

The apartment complex's garage had flooded. Every car in it was waterlogged. Some people were still paying off their car loans or mortgages. The torrential rain had left them drowning in more than just water. Anxiety was everywhere.

That afternoon, Evelyn planned to bring out her generator and steam some buns and mantou. But first, she needed to sharpen the self-defense knife that had drawn blood earlier.

Knock knock knock!

There was a sudden knock at the door. Evelyn thought it might be Claire, but to her surprise, it was Laura Evans.

"Evie, can you open the door for a second?"

"What do you need?" Evelyn looked through the peephole, her eyes cautious.

"It's nothing big. I heard you bought an inflatable boat. I just want to borrow it for a quick trip to grab a few things. Two hours, tops. I promise."

Laura was a housewife. Back when Evelyn's mother was alive, she'd spoken well of her, said she was gentle and easy to get along with.

"The eighth floor is renting theirs. A hundred dollars an hour," Evelyn said flatly.

Laura gasped, then knocked again.

"Evelyn, come on, just open the door. I'm not trying to come in or anything, but it's hard to talk with the door shut. Didn't you buy one too? Can't we just borrow it?"

Evelyn's gaze turned ice cold. "No."

Laura's face twisted in frustration. She and her husband were the type to put on a polite front but never followed through. They were all talk, never action. Now that rescue boats were rare and valuable, she'd rushed over to take advantage the moment Evelyn got back. It was shameless.

"Evie, we just need it for two hours. We'll be really careful. We won't damage it, I swear."

Evelyn didn't budge. "Eighth floor. They're renting."

"William Blake at 801 is asking 250 an hour. That's daylight robbery. Once the rescue teams come, we're all going to report him. Evie, I know you're worried about damage, but I swear, we'll treat it like our own. Please."

Evelyn's face stayed blank. Her lips curved into a cold, mocking smile. Did she really look that gullible? Why did people always think they could take advantage of her?

"I heard you're a great swimmer, Laura. Why don't you just swim over instead?"

"How rude, Evie! You're being so selfish. I used to think you were kind. You treated Emily once, and I thought you were someone easy to get along with. I never imagined you could be this heartless. No wonder Linda's whole family left. With neighbors like you, who needs enemies?"

Evelyn didn't bother arguing. She slammed the inner security door shut.

Outside, Laura stood fuming for a while. Then she suddenly realized that her daughter still hadn't fully recovered. Her father-in-law had diabetes. Her mother-in-law had high blood pressure. If Evelyn held a grudge and refused to treat them in the future, what then?

Forget it. Better try the Brooks family.

Bundling her down jacket tighter around herself, Laura rubbed her hands together and made her way to unit 901. She knocked for several minutes, but no one answered. Frustrated, she kicked the door and muttered a few curses before storming back home.

Evelyn received a message from Claire. It turned out Laura had gone to their door first, before coming up to the tenth floor. They had almost opened the door, but Claire's grandma had stopped them.

Claire explained that her grandmother once saw Laura slap a little boy downstairs—all because Emily accidentally bumped into him and they both fell.

People like that were best avoided. Getting too close would only invite trouble.

Evelyn chatted with Claire for a bit, then set her phone aside and headed into the kitchen. Whatever kind of person Laura was, it had nothing to do with her. As long as they didn't drop dead on her doorstep, she didn't care.

After a long afternoon of work, Evelyn finally steamed a batch of buns. Making dough from scratch was exhausting. Any motivation to keep going until tomorrow was gone. She ate two buns and patted her belly, content.

It was her first try, but thanks to her natural talent in the kitchen, they turned out surprisingly delicious.

That night, the power was still out. In the group chat, someone mentioned they were so cold they'd started burning wooden chairs and tables to keep warm. Evelyn guessed that by tomorrow, people would be scavenging for firewood in addition to food and supplies.

Sure enough, the next morning she was woken up by a commotion downstairs. Pulling back the curtain, she saw a crowd picking up tree branches. Someone was even trying to drag away a landscaping tree that had been split by lightning and was now floating in the floodwater.

A streak of violet lightning lit up the sky, followed by a deafening crash of thunder. Evelyn watched in horror as the man dragging the tree was struck directly. His body convulsed for a few seconds before collapsing into the filthy water.

A thin stream of blood rippled through the flood before being quickly washed away by the rain.

People scattered instantly, sprinting back to their homes. Evelyn stared as the man slowly sank beneath the surface. Just then, a rescue boat motored over. Three men aboard sprang into action and managed to haul him out.

Thankfully, by midday, the lightning had eased off. People ventured out again to collect firewood. But those who went out to buy supplies brought back bad news, that Everwell Plaza had shut down.

Claire messaged Evelyn, saying that yesterday afternoon, Laura's family had ended up renting the boat from 801 after all. They just barely made it to Everwell before it closed and stocked up on supplies.

By evening, the apartment next door was a full house again. David Rivers' son and daughter had returned with their spouses and child in tow.

His son, Zack Rivers, was twenty-nine and worked as a security guard for a private company in the west of the city. His wife, Quinn Rivera, was a hotel receptionist. They'd been married for two years and still didn't have kids.

Zack's sister, Pauline, was twenty-seven. She worked at a boutique clothing store. Her husband, Henry Summers, was from out of town and worked as a sales manager at a furniture outlet. The two of them had a young son named John Summers.

Now, five extra people were crammed into a three-bedroom apartment. Within hours, the shouting had already started.

When Evelyn found garbage outside her door, she tossed it right back in front of the Rivers' house.

As she turned, she saw Pauline, Quinn, and Fiona coming up the stairs, each carrying a bundle of sticks. Pauline and her mother were bickering again, mocking Quinn for being married two years without bearing a child.

The moment they spotted Evelyn, Fiona's expression shifted. Remembering how embarrassed she'd been the last time they spoke, she let out a fake laugh.

"Well, aren't you lucky, Evie. All by yourself in such a big place. What a pity your poor parents raised you all those years, only to die before they could enjoy a single day of your success."

Chapter 49 Heatwave, Vultures

A sharp, piercing screech tore through the quiet morning.

A massive dark-brown bird circled above Willow Heights. It flew fast, rising and dipping in quick sweeps. Its head was bald, with only a thin layer of short, dark fuzz. The back of its neck was completely bare, not a single feather in sight. Its enormous wings beat in slow, powerful strokes, spanning a little over two meters. When it folded its wings and dove, its hooked beak opened and it let out another scream.

It was a vulture.

They usually lived in high mountain wastelands or deep forests. They were social animals, scavengers by nature, savage and aggressive. They would even attack other animals on purpose. Their talons were razor-sharp. Against creatures as large as wild bulls or lions, they could tear open a belly in an instant.

The vulture dropped toward the ground, snatched up a black snake, and climbed back into the sky. After circling once, it cried out again and flew off.

When Ray and Ian knocked, Evelyn was in the bathroom sharpening her blade. The boys came to thank her for saving their lives earlier, bringing her a snake that hadn't been dead long.

Evelyn looked at the rat snake that had been killed by the heat. She hesitated for a few seconds, then accepted it.

"Ms. Evie," Ray said, still shaken, "when we went out to pick up snakes today, we saw an eagle. It was huge, really huge. If Ian hadn't dragged me and run, it might've grabbed me and carried me off. If you go out, you have to be careful."

Both boys had the same frightened look, the kind that lingered after you barely escaped something.

Evelyn had heard noises during the day too. But she'd been sorting through the messy supplies in her space and hadn't gone to check.

A huge eagle?

"Okay," she said. "I'll be careful. You two be careful too."

Their clothes and shoes were in tatters, worn down to near nothing. Maybe because they spent so much time with Calvin, the fear in their eyes was gone. They looked ragged, but the toughness in them showed through, the kind that made you look twice.

Evelyn liked dealing with people who were smart, quiet, and knew how to repay a debt. She was used to being alone, but when someone offered her genuine kindness, she didn't reject it. And if someone showed her even a trace of malice, she always paid it back tenfold.

The rat snake was nonvenomous. It was safe to eat.

Evelyn carried it into the kitchen, grabbed a cleaver, and chopped off the head. She skinned it, removed the organs, and cut the meat into sections before dropping them into a bowl.

Her appetite had been surging these past two days. She would eat it tomorrow.

Rat snake hotpot. It actually sounded pretty good.

*****

Evelyn saw the "eagle" Ray had described the next afternoon.

She watched through binoculars for a long time before she was sure. It was an adult vulture, the kind of huge raptor people always said lived on carcasses.

Vultures were active during the day. At night, they usually didn't appear, and they didn't hunt.

After nearly half a month stuck indoors, Evelyn decided it was time to go out.

At nine p.m., the night was fully settled in.

Evelyn slipped out of Willow Heights without a sound. Beneath the brim of her baseball cap, her cool eyes swept sharply across the area. Other than her eyes, she was covered from head to toe, not a bit of skin exposed. She'd tested it before. In forty-five-degree heat, even the wind against bare skin could burn and itch.

Forty minutes later, Evelyn arrived at the most expensive residential complex in Elaris. Before the apocalypse, the housing prices here were ridiculous, a single luxury unit could sell for over ten million.

The people who had lived here were either rich or powerful, the kind of class someone like Evelyn could never touch. Now, standing beneath those towers again, staring up at them, she felt a strange, dreamlike absurdity.

The high-end apartments were empty now. The filthy, reeking stairwell couldn't slow her down. She put on her night-vision goggles and picked up her pace. She had to get back to Willow Heights before sunrise, or the heat and blazing sun would trap her here.

Starting on the third floor, Evelyn searched door to door. Any apartment with an open door had already been looted once. All that remained were bulky items no one could carry away. If she saw a body inside, the only thing she could do was cover it with a cloth.

Still, the luxury complex was eye-opening. Robots that could no longer run without power. A dried-up swimming pool. A private theater. A wine cellar.

On the seventh floor, she even found a motorcycle worth hundreds of thousands. It looked cool, but it guzzled fuel. Evelyn hesitated for a few seconds, then stored it anyway. Her space was huge. It would barely take up room.

The door on the ninth floor showed no signs of being forced. Evelyn knocked. After several minutes with no sound, she pried the lock open. The moment the door swung wide, her attention snapped to a massive poster on the wall.

The person on it was one of the most famous actresses from before the end of the world. Evelyn had even watched her dramas. She was stunning, the elegant, commanding type.

The news had said her family was ordinary. Her parents were both working-class.

Evelyn couldn't help thinking, just three years after debuting and she could buy a ten-million-dollar home. Being a celebrity was nice.

Posters and photos were everywhere. In the walk-in closet, the dazzling rows of clothes and handbags made Evelyn, a complete country bumpkin by comparison, feel like she'd learned something just by looking.

None of it tempted her. She didn't find a body in the apartment either. The owner might still be alive. If she came back one day and found her home stripped clean, she would probably lose it.

But everything here was flashy and useless. Before leaving, Evelyn only took a set of kitchen knives.

The door on the tenth floor was shut tight too. The moment she opened it, a wave of rot hit her. She took out a flashlight and followed the smell. Seconds later, she found the source.

A corpse lay on the living room floor, decayed beyond recognition. Beside it was the half-rotted body of a golden retriever.

She searched the apartment and discovered there was still plenty of rice, flour, and bottled water. Judging by the condition of the body, the owner had died less than half a month ago, probably from a sudden illness. The dog bowl in the living room still held kibble and water. The owner had died, and the dog, refusing to eat or drink, had starved itself to death.

Evelyn found a clean cloth and covered both bodies. She took everything usable from the kitchen.

On the desk in the study, there was a framed photo. A tall, lean, handsome man held a golden retriever puppy in his arms. He looked around thirty, mature and refined.

Evelyn slid the frame into a drawer. She glanced around the study, then took only the books from the shelf.

On the eleventh floor, she stepped inside and immediately saw how badly this place had been destroyed by snakes. The expensive leather sofa was stained with the marks left behind after snake corpses rotted away. There was barely a clean spot to put her foot.

The bedroom and study doors were locked. It took effort to force them open. The bedroom was simple and tidy, with no signs of snake infestation. Based on what she saw, Evelyn guessed the resident had been a successful man in his forties. The closet was full of tailored suits and polished leather shoes. Dozens of watches were displayed in a glass case. Evelyn didn't know their brands or prices, but she knew they were the kind of luxury she could work for decades and never afford.

She checked the time. It was three a.m.

She didn't waste another second. After collecting what she wanted, she headed back.

By around four, it would be fully light. She had to get home fast.

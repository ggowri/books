Chapter 13 Rainstorm, Venom Moths: Part 9

"It's not your fault," Calvin said, placing a hand on his wife's shoulder. "I'm the one who didn't take care of everyone. I left you with this whole family. Now that I'm back, let me handle it. Don't blame yourself anymore."

His eyes turned red when he looked at his parents.

"Evie, you might not know this, but the water level outside has risen again. The temperature's still dropping, and there are now gale-force winds over level eight. Going out now is basically suicide. I'd be fine on my own, but if I try to bring my parents... we might not even make it out of the complex."

There was something he didn't say out loud—Elaris's shops had all shut down. And now, there were more and more looters outside.

"Do you have any sugar?" Evelyn asked. "Boil some hot water and make them a bowl of sugar water. If you don't have heating pads or warm packs, fill plastic bottles with hot water. Boil it using rainwater if you have to. Then place the bottles under their arms, by their stomachs, or in the groin area to help warm them up."

Calvin and his wife sprang into action. Meanwhile, Evelyn walked over to check on their daughter, Wendy, who was sleeping on the sofa.

"Evie, is Wendy okay?"

"She's fine. Don't worry."

Evelyn then pulled out her silver needles and gave the elderly couple acupuncture. Bit by bit, color returned to their faces.

An hour later, they were doing much better. Evelyn packed up her things to leave, and Calvin handed her a can of meat, a bottle of water, and a pack of compressed biscuits.

She accepted them without hesitation.

But just as she reached her door, Miranda Blake from 801 suddenly grabbed her.

"Evie, please, help! My husband William has been vomiting and having diarrhea. He just passed out!"

*****

By the time Evelyn finally returned home, it was already 5 p.m. She put away the supplies she'd earned from treating patients—some food, and from two families, bundles of firewood.

She wasn't in a bad mood. Minor illnesses and injuries she didn't mind treating, but serious cases she would refuse. Her goal wasn't to trade her skills for goods but to protect herself.

She turned on her electric stove and stretched out her hands and feet to warm them. Once her body stopped shaking, she washed up quickly and had dinner, then went straight to bed.

But this was not going to be a peaceful night.

The temperature dropped to minus six degrees. Under torrential rain, thunder, and howling winds, the water level surged past the third floor and crept toward the fourth.

The next morning, Evelyn overheard voices outside. Listening carefully, she finally understood what was going on.

Word had spread that there was a police officer living in Block D, and now people were lining up for help. Some wanted Calvin to help transport their frozen-to-death father to the crematorium at Fellridge. Others demanded he hand over any extra food his family had to feed them.

The crowd kept growing, stretching from the twelfth floor all the way down to Evelyn's front door.

She was curious to see how Calvin would handle it. If he chose to sacrifice himself for the greater good, it would fit his job description. But it wouldn't be smart.

Feed the wolves, and you're the first one they eat.

The noise outside grew louder, so Evelyn opened her door and stepped out. Many of the Block D residents had done the same.

"Why should Calvin give you food?" a tough lady from the fifth floor shouted. She stood with her hands on her hips, glaring at the outsiders. "How do you have the nerve to even ask? Since the rain started, Calvin has been working nonstop at the station. He only just made it back yesterday. His family's sick, and you vultures come knocking during a natural disaster like this? Why not go cry to your parents instead? No food? Then go eat tree bark! Now get lost! You're not part of this building. One more step and we'll beat you out of here!"

"But he's a cop!" someone shouted, clearly unwilling to back down. "Aren't police supposed to serve the people? What's the big deal about sharing a little food? I haven't eaten in two days!"

"So what if he's a cop?" another neighbor snapped. "You think that means he's responsible for feeding you? Why don't you ask him to turn water into wine while you're at it?"

A middle-aged man chimed in too, voice sharp and angry.

"Fine," the complainer muttered darkly. "I won't forget this. He refused to help us, and the rest of you are just as bad. When the rain stops, I'm filing a complaint. I'll make sure he loses his job."

"No need to wait," Calvin said coldly, stepping forward. "Let me make it easy for you. I already resigned. I'm no longer a cop. You've got one minute to get out of Block D."

The moment he finished speaking, a hush fell over the crowd. Everyone turned to stare at him in shock, including his wife and parents.

"You resigned?" someone said in disbelief. "You're lying. We heard you got promoted to chief. Why would you quit?"

Calvin's voice was ice-cold. "If you don't believe me, go ask the department."

Not that it mattered—Elaris's police department had been flooded and evacuated days ago.

The people from outside began to hesitate.

"But you were a cop," said a man with narrow, shifty eyes. "That means you still have a responsibility. My dad's corpse is still in our house. You're stronger than us. You have to help me take him to the crematorium."

"Don't waste your breath," Calvin replied. "Trying to guilt-trip me won't work. You're too scared to go out there yourself, so why should I risk my life for you?"

People from Block D quickly chimed in, voices firm with support. In times like this, unity mattered. Infighting could get everyone killed.

The outsiders finally gave up, muttering curses as they left. Once they were gone, Calvin turned to the others and said, "We need to block off the fourth floor. Things are getting worse. If anyone breaks in to steal or rob, it'll only make things harder."

His warning sent a jolt of fear through everyone. The idea of looters showing up now, with resources already so scarce, was terrifying.

Without wasting time, Calvin dismantled the door from the rooftop. Luckily, William from 801 had a generator and a welding machine. In just twenty minutes, the door was reinstalled as a barricade.

"Now that's what I call peace of mind," someone said. "Calvin really knows what he's doing."

"Exactly. Cop or not, he's still looking out for us. I'm just glad we're in Block D."

Voices of praise echoed through the hall. But Calvin's expression stayed calm and unreadable. Once the door was secure, he quietly returned to his apartment.

Splash!

A loud crash echoed from outside—a heavy object falling into the floodwater. Jessica looked toward the balcony, where her husband had just finished digging a drainage hole.

"What was that sound?"

"Nothing," he said without missing a beat. "Just a piece of the wall that got struck by lightning."

He quickly shut the sliding door and walked back into the room.

Across from them, on the balcony of the fifth floor, a man with beady eyes and a nervous face scanned the area. Seeing no one around, he glanced down into the water below. Only when the shadowy figure vanished beneath the surface did he breathe a sigh of relief, then calmly stepped back inside.

As the temperature continued to drop, something new appeared outside.

Moths.

Palm-sized, grotesque things clung to windows, stairwell walls, building exteriors, and railings. They were everywhere.

Evelyn's scalp prickled just looking at them.

She caught one and brought it home to examine. She stared at it for a long time, trying to identify it, but her mind came up blank. No name surfaced.

It was strange-looking—its wings were marked with bold red and black stripes, like the pattern of a python. Two long, thin antennae stretched out about ten centimeters. Its green eyes were bulbous and glassy. The face looked crushed and distorted, like it had been run over by a car. Its swollen belly was covered in dense, fine hairs.

And it had a strange smell—hard to describe. Not exactly foul, but definitely unpleasant. The longer you inhaled it, the dizzier you felt, followed by nausea and an urge to vomit.

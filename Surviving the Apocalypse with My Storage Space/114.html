Chapter 114 Adrian’s Good Fortune

Wendy stared at Adrian with open curiosity, though she refused to say anything. Ray and Evan exchanged a glance, then one of them called out, “Adrian.”

“You’ve made far too much food. What a waste.”

“What do you mean, waste? Just make sure everyone eats properly. I only need to boil one more vegetable dish and then we can start.”

In total, Evelyn made six dishes: ribs stewed with runner beans, lamb stir-fried with spring onions, a big plate of chicken, braised fish, red-cooked pork, and greens with tofu.

Everyone was still a little reserved. Evelyn shot Adrian a look, and he immediately picked up his chopsticks and took a bite. Only then did the others lift their bowls.

“Evelyn, it’s delicious.”

Evelyn ruffled Wendy’s hair. “Then have some more, all right?”

Wendy nodded and said softly, “He’s pretty.”

Evelyn glanced at Adrian. He was busy tackling the meat with complete focus, far too occupied to notice.

“So are you.”

“Evelyn, is work going smoothly?”

“It is. I even ran into one of my father’s old students in the Medical Division.”

Calvin looked relieved. “That’s good. Having someone you know makes things easier. Oh, and Eddie probably told you already, but several girls died in the base not long ago. Don’t go out alone at night. Since the apocalypse began, a lot of people have gone wrong in the head. There are quite a few extremists about.”

“I understand.”

There were still three bodies lying downstairs. Evelyn glanced at the most dangerous person in the room.

By the time Calvin and the others left, night had fallen. Evelyn and Adrian went downstairs, circled round the back by a side path, and looked around. Adrian checked the area first and, finding no one nearby, gave a slight nod.

Evelyn stepped forward and tested the breathing of the three people in the snow. As expected, they were all dead, their bodies frozen stiff.

She put them into her storage space, and the two of them disappeared quickly into the dark.

Three people vanishing without explanation would certainly provoke another response from Miss Hawthorne. Evelyn dearly wanted to drag that woman out of her comfortable corner and slap her. She had picked a fight for absolutely no reason and dumped a mountain of trouble in Evelyn’s lap.

Adrian, on the other hand, remained perfectly calm. He had no real sense of right or wrong. Whether he killed or not seemed to depend entirely on his mood.

Now the two of them were tied together. If he caused trouble, Evelyn would not escape the consequences either.

As for the three bodies, she would find somewhere else to leave them later. If the three of them had simply vanished into thin air, Miss Hawthorne could come looking for trouble all she liked, but she would still need proof.

Work in the Medical Division remained just as busy, though Evelyn’s points rose quickly as well. A week passed in no time. On her one day off, she decided to take Adrian shopping and spend every last point they had earned that week.

At the villa marketplace, they each took a trolley. Evelyn glanced at the prices and found they were even worse than she had imagined.

Tomatoes were six points each. Eggs were four points each. Small cabbage and other leafy greens were ten points a head. Larger cabbages and Chinese greens went for thirty.

Evelyn quickly did the sums. After six days of work, she had earned 470 points. Adrian had earned 2,250.

“You go and get the meat weighed,” she told him. “I’ll do the vegetables.”

The two of them split up and filled both trolleys to the brim with goods bought on points. People all around them stared openly, their faces full of surprise and envy.

She had plenty of flour and rice in storage already, and what was sold here was old grain and coarse husks. Some of it had even gone mouldy.

The vegetables looked all right, though. Judging by the roots, they were hydroponic. Whether they tasted good hardly mattered. The important thing was that they were edible.

Loaded down with bags, they made their way back, attracting more than enough attention on the road. Thankfully, the weather was still bitterly cold, and everyone was so wrapped up that nobody could see their faces clearly.

When they reached the sixth floor, Clara and Elliot happened to be coming out of their flat. Judging by the look of them, they were on their way to shop as well. The moment they saw Evelyn and Adrian returning with several bulging bags, Clara’s eyes widened. Even Elliot, who was usually so quiet, could not help staring.

“Evelyn, did you hit the jackpot?”

Evelyn only smiled.

“It really must be lovely to be capable. Evelyn, are they still taking on nurses where you work? I could learn.”

“They probably are, but I don’t know for certain. You’d have to ask the managers in the Medical Division.”

Clara looked genuinely tempted. “All right. I’ll go and ask tomorrow.”

Then Elliot stepped forward a little and addressed Adrian in a low voice. “Sorry, may I ask, are you in one of the hunting crews?”

Adrian looked cool and distant, giving every sign of having no intention of answering at all. Elliot looked embarrassed. Evelyn gave a small cough, and Adrian lowered his gaze to her.

“Yes,” he said at last, clearly against his will.

“I’d like to transfer into the hunting crews. Could I join your team?”

Good heavens, now even Elliot had started speaking to him like that. If Evelyn remembered correctly, Clara was twenty-three, and Elliot looked about twenty-five or twenty-six.

“Suit yourself.”

Elliot took that as agreement and brightened at once. “Which team are you in? I’ll apply for a transfer tomorrow.”

“Team Seventeen.”

Adrian was already growing impatient. He kept shooting Evelyn pointed looks, clearly urging her to get inside and shut the door.

She took out her key and opened the flat. Sensing the moment was over, Clara pulled Elliot away.

“Are you actually going to team up with Elliot?” Evelyn asked once they were inside. “He looks terribly thin.”

Adrian gave a proud little huff. “I’m not.”

“Then why did you tell him your team number?”

“Because you coughed at me and made me answer him.”

Evelyn pressed a hand to her forehead in resignation. “What a beautiful misunderstanding.”

She put away everything they had exchanged for points, storing it all neatly in her space, then began cooking dinner in high spirits.

Then came a knock at the door.

Evelyn told Adrian to put his coloured contacts back in before she opened it.

Three men stood outside.

At once, Evelyn thought of the two bodyguards Adrian had thrown from the building.

“And you are?”

“You’re Evelyn Foster?”

She nodded. “I am.”

“Vanessa from the fishing team told our young lady that you have a squirrel and a brightly coloured bird. Our young lady is generous. She’s willing to offer you one thousand points in exchange. Hand them over.”

“Sorry,” Evelyn said calmly, “you’re too late.”

The man blinked. “What’s that supposed to mean?”

“The night we arrived at the base, they couldn’t adjust to the environment. The squirrel and the bird both died.”

The man clearly did not believe her and pushed his way in to search the place. Evelyn stepped aside and let him. After looking through the flat and finding neither squirrel nor bird, he finally returned to the doorway.

“A few days ago, Vanessa came here with two men. I checked the surveillance. They entered the lift, and then disappeared. Can you explain that?”

Evelyn looked thoroughly astonished. “Did that really happen? We never saw them.”

The man studied her, then shifted his gaze to Adrian. The moment he saw Adrian’s face, he narrowed his eyes in a way that meant something far from friendly.

“What’s his name?”

“My boyfriend. Adrian Frost.”

Evelyn took hold of Adrian’s hand. The man looked from one to the other, then pulled out a camera and snapped a photograph of Adrian.

“What are you doing?” Evelyn’s expression hardened.

“Adrian Frost, is it?” The man gave a strange little smile. “Your good days are coming.”

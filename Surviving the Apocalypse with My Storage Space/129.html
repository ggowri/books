Chapter 129 The Source, The Truth

By now, the leadership at the base was in complete disarray. Each faction had tried to shift responsibility, but that had failed. Now they had no choice but to gather together and discuss how to respond.

Each of the powerful families had lost a person—taken silently and killed. The bodies had been displayed, beaten, and fought over. Everyone in management was terrified, afraid for their own safety.

“Who is this person? We have to find them. My son did not die in vain. I will crush them to ashes, as tribute to my son!”

Patriarch Burton slammed his fist on the table, his face dark with rage. Unlike the Hawthorne family, his influence was limited. He had only two sons. The one who had died was his heir; the other was a reckless, indulgent playboy.

Victor said nothing. But his position was the same as Burton’s at this moment—they were both pressing the authorities to find the culprit.

“Two days left. If the base can’t produce a solution, the other side already said it: all management would be eliminated. Then you’ll know who that person is, won’t you?”

Director Collins of the authorities, standing nearby, sneered at Patriarch Burton. They had also lost someone. Were they not angry?

These elite families acted arrogant when earning money and equally arrogant when seeking help, expecting unconditional service from the authorities.

“What did you just say?”

Collins smiled coldly, ignoring Burton’s rising fury.

“Director Collins, watch your tone,” Secretary Bennett cautioned.

“I’d like to say less too, but the priority now is solving the problem, not seeking revenge. Patriarch Burton and Victor Hawthorne only care about their own dead, not the residents the base has lost. I just want to remind everyone: the wheat harvest is only a month and a half away. If this isn’t handled properly, you’ll be left with nothing to eat. Of course, Burton and Hawthorne won’t starve—last night I heard you two had steak and red wine.”

Collins’s sarcasm was unmistakable. Secretary Bennett glanced at him but did not intervene. Both had come from Seabourne, while the Hawthornes and Burtons were rooted in Elaris; loyalties were clear.

“You’re insolent, Collins. Even Mayor Whitmore wouldn’t dare speak to me this way!”

Collins stood and walked behind Victor, dumping a stack of documents in front of him.

“Victor, it’s been reported that several of your sons kept dangerous animals. You’ve raised three Tibetan mastiffs. What have they been fed? And the wolves of your eldest son, the Rottweilers of your second… even your illegitimate child who died midway had several black dogs. Oh, and your daughter, who died suddenly some time ago, kept not only dogs but snakes as well.”

Victor’s face remained stony. Collins began throwing photos across the table, each depicting gore and cruelty. Eventually, Victor couldn’t contain himself and ran to the trash can to vomit.

“Seven or eight months ago, I heard that your eldest son’s dog bit someone, and he made the person kneel and crawl as an apology. Later, the person disappeared. Their family says they were dragged away by your son’s men.”

“Collins, don’t slander us. And stop throwing this nonsense around. What does this have to do with the parasitic worms?”

“Everything. I’m just trying to piece together the truth for you.”

“Have you heard of rabies? Or viruses carried by carnivorous animals? Yes, the base is at fault, but there are too many people living here—not a hundred, a million. When people die, the bodies must be handled, but they weren’t. This is the origin of the parasite. But the source of the deaths? The Hawthorne family’s vicious dogs. You allowed them to bite people. When someone died, you didn’t even dispose of the corpses. Viruses converged, and out came an indestructible parasite.”

“Here are over a hundred photos, all from the past few days. Your Tibetan mastiffs are still consuming this ‘meat.’ You expect me to believe you didn’t know? Where did that meat come from? From the Medical Division’s morgue.”

All eyes turned to Victor, including Patriarch Burton, who had previously aligned with him.

“Victor Hawthorne, you are an animal. Your whole family is a pack of animals. Animals feeding on animals. You’ve destroyed so many lives, and my son too. I want your life in return!”

Burton grabbed a cup and hurled it at Victor, striking his nose. Victor’s bodyguards closed in, and the room instantly tensed to the breaking point.

Victor wiped the blood from his face, sitting there in disgrace. After a long pause, he lifted his head.

“My second son raised those vicious dogs. I failed to discipline him. He will take responsibility with his life, to atone for all the base residents who died. The Hawthorne family will donate half its resources to the residents, and all the dangerous dogs will be destroyed.”

He was clearly pushing his least-liked second son forward as the scapegoat. The others looked on with disdain, but it was the best solution possible. The Hawthorne family was too powerful to uproot entirely.

Secretary Bennett also indicated this was enough for now. That was the message from above.

“The Hawthorne family will provide all equipment necessary to purify the air, water, and soil of the base.”

“Director Collins, shouldn’t your responsibility be greater?” Victor’s eldest son spoke for the first time.

“All the livestock the base raised have gone mad. The Underground Quarter is under your management. When the pigs went wrong, you didn’t deal with it. Instead, you canned the meat and sold it to the residents. And the first pig bite incident traces back six months.”

Collins and the eldest son stared each other down. After a long silence, Collins gave a cold smile and looked away first.

“We will never shirk responsibility. All the livestock will be removed and dealt with. But can the eldest son explain what happened to the person he took away?”

The eldest son remained silent, ignoring the question entirely.

“I propose that all the dead be reburied in a single location, with a memorial stone erected. Families who lost loved ones will receive three times the compensation in supplies and psychological support.”

Secretary Bennett finalized the proposal. No one objected. The meeting concluded, though Victor was left behind, knowing his judgment was far from over.

After a while, a man in his fifties entered. Victor rose unsteadily. The man was a former Seabourne official, ordered here after the disasters to establish a shelter at Drakepool Base and oversee the transfer of all reserve grain and weapons.

Drakepool Base could accommodate a million people, and it was entirely his doing.

“Victor, I heard everything you said, but I am not satisfied.”

The man sat calmly, eyes emotionless, yet Victor felt the hair on his arms stand on end.

“Mayor Whitmore, please speak.”

Victor slumped back into his chair. He understood immediately what was meant.

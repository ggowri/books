Chapter 139 Toxic Haze 1

Whitmore had been surprised by Evelyn’s response, though in the end he said he understood.

After the acid rain came the haze.

A thick, poisonous fog spread through the base, and everyone had to keep masks and gauze over their faces at all times. The air and even the wind carried black grit. If it got into your eyes or nose, the pain was unbearable.

All the survivors were suffering from acid-rain aftereffects. The symptoms came and went without warning: dizziness, blurred vision, nausea, vomiting, and in serious cases, fainting and convulsions.

Calvin and Eddie were busy modifying tricycles. They had too much luggage to carry by hand, and if the day came when they had to leave, they would need transport.

Fortunately, over the past few days they had scavenged iron plates, tyres, and tools from all over the base. Piecing together two or three cargo tricycles no longer seemed impossible.

Every day more survivors left the base. They still followed the old tyre tracks out into the distance. No one knew how long the journey would be, or where it would end. For most of them, leaving was simply another word for trying to stay alive.

The twelve kilos of wheat Calvin and the others still had, Evelyn advised them to keep untouched. If they ever found a patch of clean earth, those seeds might become their only chance of survival.

She even mixed in a few other seeds among the wheat: fruit seeds, vegetable seeds, even tree seeds.

As for the rice and wheat they had brought back, Evelyn suggested turning all of it into simple flatbreads and hard biscuits and hard biscuits that would keep longer.

She stood on the roof of the Administration Hall and looked down over Blackwater Base.

Since the acid rain, the whole place had come to resemble an abandoned coalfield. Black sky. Black earth. Black fog.

“You’ve had a lot on your mind lately.”

Adrian came to stand beside her, speaking in his usual quiet voice.

Evelyn turned to look at him. His expression was as calm as ever. It struck her that even if the heavens came crashing down, his face would probably not change.

“Unless human beings mutate and stop needing food,” she said, “we’ll die out very quickly without soil and water.”

Adrian looked at the stream of people leaving below. Their faces were full of struggle and despair. Some women carried children on their backs as they trudged away beneath heavy loads. Then he looked back at Evelyn, and for once there was the faintest trace of uncertainty in his eyes.

“Will we die too?”

Evelyn sighed. “I don’t know. Maybe I’ll last a bit longer than some. As for you, you might outlive me.”

He shook his head. “No.”

“No what?”

He said nothing.

Instead he looked up at the dark, oppressive sky and finished the thought in silence.

You won’t die.

Evelyn had started prising up the floor tiles in the Administration Hall to gather the soil underneath. There was already plenty of soil stored in her hidden space, but she was not the sort who could sit idle.

The survivors leaving the base all carried off at least a sack of clay from the pits in the Underground Quarter. To them, soil was not dead weight. It was hope.

Several of the single-occupancy buildings in the West Wing collapsed one after another. Their corroded foundations looked like insect nests hollowed out by ant swarms.

By now Calvin and the others had finished rebuilding the tricycles.

In the end, they managed three.

That night, Evelyn secretly took out some nails and reinforced them all. She also topped up everyone’s water bottles while no one was looking.

All the grain had been made into flatbreads and hard biscuits. Even the grain Whitmore and the others had kept for themselves had been turned into cakes with everyone’s help.

Over the past two days Calvin had also organised everyone to collect clothing and blankets. He even gathered up every nail he could pry from the base, storing them away with the same care as if they were silver.

Clara had got her period a few days ago. With no sanitary towels left, she could only make do with strips of cloth.

Evelyn did have pads, but she could not bring them out. Every pack in her hidden space was a pre-apocalypse brand, far too suspicious to explain away.

So she cut up a clean garment into cloth strips for Clara, gave her acupuncture, and prepared some herbs as well. It eased the pain at least a little.

“Evelyn, I’m sure I’ve got acid-rain aftereffects,” Clara said weakly. “There’s something wrong with my blood. It’s dark, almost black, and there’s far too much of it. It feels like it won’t stop.”

In a world like this, survival was even harsher for women.

“It probably is an aftereffect,” Evelyn said. “But don’t panic. I’ll do the acupuncture. This might actually help your body clear some of the toxins.”

Clara lay on the bed, her body cramped so badly with pain it kept spasming. Evelyn filled a bottle with hot water and tucked it inside her clothes against her lower abdomen. Once her hands and feet warmed and the pain eased a little, Evelyn began the treatment.

“Is that any better?”

“It doesn’t hurt as much now. But I’m scared to move. The second I do, it feels like everything is pouring out of me.”

Evelyn understood exactly what she meant. “Then lie still and rest. I’ll go and make the medicine.”

“No, don’t. I can chew the herbs raw. Don’t waste water.”

“Raw herbs won’t do much. Elliot’s already saved enough water for you, so stop worrying.”

Water was scarce now, and everyone saved it where they could. But special cases had to be treated properly.

Besides, Evelyn was adding water to their bottles every day anyway.

Perhaps Calvin had been right when he said there was always a way through. Every three days or so, a light rain would fall over the base. People stored as much of it as they could. Once filtered, some of it was good enough to drink. The dirtier remainder could still be used on the vegetables.

Ten days later, Clara was finally able to get out of bed. Evelyn had not expected her aftereffects to be so severe. Clara said she had never had painful periods before the acid rain.

Evelyn herself was doing rather well by comparison. Ever since she had drunk Adrian’s blood, her body had become markedly stronger. Wounds healed faster than they should, and even her periods no longer left her doubled over in pain.

Adrian really was her greatest support and her sharpest weapon.

A good thing, she thought, that she had not killed him when she first found him.

The cabbage Calvin had planted in the sitting room was finally ready to pick. That evening they did something almost extravagant by their current standards and made a whole pot of cabbage boiled in clean water. Even Whitmore and the other two from next door turned up to share it.

In truth, Evelyn’s group was not short of supplies compared with most. During the scramble after the acid rain, they had scavenged plenty. Adrian was especially quick, and even when Evelyn barely got involved, he somehow always managed to come back with useful things.

They had grain enough.

What they lacked was water.

No one could bear to spend precious water on proper cooking, so most of the vegetables they found were simply wiped clean and eaten raw.

Evelyn also had Pip and Lark to feed. The two little creatures ate a surprising amount, but with everyone living together, she could not openly bring out extra food. Only at night, once they were back in the bedroom, could she slip them a better meal.

Adrian joined them every time.

By the time the number of people left in the base had fallen to only a few tens of thousands, the haze thickened again.

It was like night falling in the middle of the day.

No one could go outside any longer.

Evelyn knew at once that the next disaster after the acid rain had arrived.

Coughing broke out everywhere.

Harsh, relentless, tearing coughs.

Everyone was coughing. Evelyn saw Eddie pounding at his own chest as if he meant to knock his lungs loose. Calvin’s face had gone crimson. After coughing for so long, his throat was so dry and raw he could no longer speak.

Wearing a gas mask, Evelyn stepped outside.

A gaunt woman rushed at her from the side, with several children crowding close behind. They swarmed around Evelyn and tried to search her.

“Have you got any food?” the woman begged between coughs. “Please. Just give me something to eat.”

Evelyn shoved lightly, and the woman went sprawling to the ground at once.

Only then did Evelyn see clearly that there seemed to be a child strapped to the woman’s back as well.

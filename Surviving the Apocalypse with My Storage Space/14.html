Chapter 14 Rainstorm, Venom Moths: Part 10

In just a few seconds after Evelyn opened the window, hundreds of moths swarmed inside.

She reacted fast, emptying half a can of insecticide before finally killing them off. But in the process, she made a terrifying discovery.

Once dead, the moths decomposed almost instantly, melting into pools of filthy liquid. The stench was so sharp it felt like it stabbed straight into her skull. Worse, if a living moth bit someone, the wound would swell up immediately and then go numb.

Evelyn stared in frustration at her left hand, now extremely swollen.

Without hesitation, she slit the skin and let the contaminated blood drain out, then smeared on detoxifying ointment.

Half an hour later, some feeling returned to her palm. She took out her surgical tools, disinfected the area with saline, and began stitching the wound.

There was no anesthesia.

The pain was so intense that veins bulged along her neck. Cold sweat poured down her forehead in heavy drops.

Just then, she heard Calvin's voice echoing outside.

"The moths that showed up today are probably venomous. Don't swat them with your hands. The liquid they turn into after death is likely toxic too. Be careful when cleaning it up. If you're bitten, drain the contaminated blood immediately. Most moths are afraid of fire. You can use flames to drive them away."

His voice faded, and soon after, Evelyn heard crying and screams from downstairs.

After hearing his warning, neighbors began lighting torches and burning books and paper to scare off the venomous moths. But before that, many people had already been bitten. Without timely treatment, the swelling spread across their bodies, ballooning them up until they were barely recognizable.

Outside her window, the moths hovered in thick swarms, like flies and mosquitoes over a latrine, endless and suffocating.

Wearing gloves, Evelyn wiped up the toxic residue on the floor. Then she sprayed disinfectant and air freshener throughout the apartment. By the time she finished, her body was completely drained.

Sharp, stabbing pain pulsed through her hand. Dizziness and weakness followed. She pulled out a box of detox capsules from her storage space and swallowed all of them at once.

Her body alternated between chills and fever.

Lying on the bed, half-conscious, she heard frantic knocking and sobbing outside her door. But she couldn't summon the strength to respond. Her throat was burning dry, like it was on fire. The wound throbbed so badly she almost wished she could cut her whole hand off.

Biting her lip, Evelyn kept reminding herself to stay awake.

She ignored the people outside.

When she finally woke up again, it was already five in the afternoon the next day.

Her hand was much better. The swelling had gone down. She reapplied the ointment and wrapped it in fresh gauze.

Thankfully, before losing consciousness, she had connected her electric blanket to the backup battery. Otherwise, she might have frozen to death in her sleep.

She was starving.

She took out a boxed rice meal she'd prepared earlier and stored away. After devouring it, she finally felt alive again.

Pulling back the curtains, she sucked in a sharp breath when she saw the venomous moths plastered against the glass.

They were huge. Their attack power wasn't that strong, but their venom was terrifying. She didn't even dare imagine what condition her neighbors were in now.

From the moment she woke up, the entire building had felt strangely silent.

Then, suddenly—

Knock. Knock. Knock.

The door was being pounded hard.

Evelyn walked into the living room, paused, then turned back to the bedroom. She suited up in her protective gear and grabbed a can of insecticide before unlocking the door.

When she saw Claire standing outside, torch in hand and fully armored with a helmet on her head, she finally opened the door.

"Evie, thank God you're alive!" Claire's voice trembled with relief. "I've knocked on your door so many times since yesterday. I was so scared something happened to you."

She waved the torch around, driving away the moths swarming in the corridor.

"I got bitten," Evelyn said. "I've been unconscious." Her eyes swept over the mass of moths covering the hallway floor. Her skin crawled. In her past life, she had never seen anything so revolting.

"Is your family okay?"

Claire let out a long sigh. "When the moths first started gathering on our balcony, my parents locked my grandparents in the bedroom. We were trying to swat them with bug zappers, but then we heard Calvin yelling upstairs and switched to torches. Thankfully, we still had some rubbing alcohol at home. We burned them all."

Evelyn nodded. "Good."

"Evelyn… people on the fourth, fifth, and sixth floors are dead."

"Killed by the moths?"

"Yeah. Julia in 402. Duncan in 503. And all five in 601 were bitten to death last night."

It was Claire's first time seeing dead bodies up close. Every time she closed her eyes, the image of their swollen, disfigured corpses came back to her. When she looked at Evelyn, she noticed that the girl's face hadn't changed at all—no shock, no sadness. Just calm.

Was this what it meant to study medicine? To be so emotionally steady?

"After being bitten," Claire continued, voice lower, "the corpses develop red-and-black mottled patterns, just like the markings on the moths' wings. Then they swell up. Once they reach a certain point, they split open along those lines. Calvin said the bodies might even explode like the moths, turning into puddles of corpse water. He suggested moving all the bodies to 601 since that whole family's already gone."

Evelyn raised an eyebrow. That was a smart move. Efficient and contained.

"All their stuff in 601's already been cleared out," Claire added.

"Let them take it. Has the rescue team come by these last couple days?"

"No. But the rain stopped around noon today."

That explained the eerie silence she'd noticed since waking up. Evelyn had felt it—like the entire building was holding its breath.

Seeing that Evelyn was alert and recovering, Claire finally relaxed.

"If we hadn't saved some leftover cement and sand from when we renovated the study, we wouldn't have been able to block the toilet drain in time. Our place would've flooded."

After chatting for a bit, Claire said she had to get back. Evelyn handed her the rest of her insecticide can.

Once alone, Evelyn pulled open the curtains, grabbed a flashlight, and studied the moths outside through the glass. Something clicked in her mind.

She quickly opened the door, caught one of the moths, and sealed it in a glass jar. She wanted to run an experiment.

She needed to know how long they could survive.

At 6:30 p.m., someone knocked and called out her name.

Evelyn didn't open the door immediately. "Who is it?" she asked cautiously.

"It's Maya from 403," said a young woman's voice.

Evelyn remembered her. Maya Wren was in her last year of high school. She had a younger brother, Tyler Wren, who was a freshman. Their family was infamous in Block D for favoring the son and neglecting the daughter.

"The water outside is almost flooding our place," Maya said. "Can we stay at your place for a few days? I can pay you a thousand dollars."

"I've already said it," Evelyn replied coldly. "I don't take anyone in."

"Please, Evelyn! You're all alone in there. I'm begging you. We can't hold out anymore. The moths are everywhere. Just open the door, please—"

"Open the damn door or I'll break it down!" a boy's angry voice interrupted. Evelyn recognized it immediately. It was Tyler.

Then came the thud of something striking the door.

But all three of her doors were reinforced metal, not wood. She wasn't worried.

Her expression turned cold, and a glint of steel flashed in her eyes.

From her storage space, she pulled out a military knife—twelve inches long, razor-sharp.

She hadn't expected to use it so soon.

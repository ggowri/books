Chapter 105 The Fishing Team

Since they had decided to clear out every fish they could, it meant staying by the riverbank for quite some time.

Whenever she had a spare moment, Evelyn chopped down the trees along the bank. Oddly enough, although Ashen Ridge had been struck by fireballs, there was no sign of them anywhere near Silvermere River. The whole place was still wrapped in snow and frost, with no scorched craters and no burnt-out patches of ground.

Fortunately, when she had been stockpiling supplies, she had bought plenty of foam boxes and water tanks. The empty tanks now turned out to be perfect for storing fish.

That afternoon, they carried on fishing.

As they hacked more and more holes into the ice, one stretch of it gave way completely. Evelyn immediately brought out a fishing net. Once it went into the water, they could haul up a hundred or even two hundred pounds of fish in one go.

Even the tiny ones went into storage. Their current approach was simple. Leave nothing behind and strip a place clean.

Evelyn took out an inflatable boat and went down onto the water to check where the shoals were gathering.

“Adrian, loads over here. Come and net this side.”

“There are loads on this side too. Silver carp, all of them. Seven or eight kilos each.”

Most of the time, though, Evelyn stayed on the bank gutting fish. She was bundled up in so many layers that moving about was clumsy work, and she was forever slipping or nearly falling into the water.

On top of that, her period was due any day. In the end, she reluctantly accepted that staying on shore and dealing with the catch was the safer option.

She had already devastated most of the trees along the bank as well. Lately she had been so full of energy that she constantly felt as though she had strength to burn. If she sat still for too long, her whole body became restless.

Adrian said it was a genetic mutation. Evelyn, in response, had taken to checking her reflection every single day to see whether she was turning into something grotesque. Thankfully, apart from her pupils growing paler, she had found nothing wrong.

“We’ll have to move camp tomorrow. Three kilometres further downstream.”

Adrian took a sip of fish soup and nodded in a perfunctory sort of way.

Another day of excellent returns came to an end.

Once they moved downstream, Evelyn discovered that the fish there were even larger, and many of them were species she had never seen before.

“This one must be fifty or sixty pounds.”

“That’s one of the smaller ones. There are bigger ones in there.”

Wearing a thick winter hat, Evelyn stared in astonishment at the giant fish Adrian hauled up.

“Is that an arapaima?” It looked a bit like one, though she could not be sure.

Adrian did not care what sort of fish it was. In his world, there were only two categories. Edible and not edible. Delicious and not delicious.

“Does it taste good?”

Evelyn nodded. “Apparently it does. I’ve never had it.”

“Were you poor before?”

Evelyn felt that one land squarely in her chest.

“Just an ordinary family.”

Adrian had no real concept of what an ordinary family meant. All he took from it was that Evelyn had never eaten this fish before, which meant he ought to catch plenty and let her have her fill.

Evelyn was thrilled. Watching basket after basket of fish disappear into her space gave her the absurd feeling that she had risen from small landowner to richest person alive.

That evening, she deliberately kept one of the giant fish aside. She had never eaten it before, but at the end of the day it was still fish, and fish could not differ that much in preparation.

The arapaima was so large that one fish could be steamed and braised, with the remaining sections sliced up for a spicy poached fish dish.

“Let’s have hotpot tomorrow.”

“Fine.”

Sadly, by the following day, her period had arrived right on schedule, so she had to start watching what she ate.

They stayed by the river for twenty whole days, until every container in her space was filled to the brim.

That day, Evelyn had gone into the woods behind them to cut more timber when she heard movement. She scrambled up a tree, pulled out her binoculars, and looked. A large group of people had appeared in the hills about two kilometres away, and they seemed to be heading straight for Silvermere River.

Evelyn hurriedly put the wood away and ran back.

“Adrian, people are coming. Put the contacts in, quickly. I’ll hide the fish. I think they’re a fishing team from Blackwater Base. Four or five hundred of them, at least.”

Adrian looked perfectly calm. The only issue was that he had absolutely no idea how to put in contact lenses. In the end, Evelyn had to help him before he managed it.

“They’re uncomfortable.”

“Put up with it.”

Evelyn cleared away everything valuable from the tent, leaving only one tent behind, along with two ragged quilts, a bucket, an iron shovel, a kettle, and an iron pot.

“Your face still isn’t dirty enough.”

Adrian took the mirror she handed him, glanced into it, pinched up some mud from the ground, and casually smeared it across his face.

“Put this tattered padded coat on. Pull your hat down and cover your face. And stop standing so straight. In this weather you ought to hunch a bit.”

“Why aren’t we just avoiding them?”

Evelyn shot him a look. “Because I want to pick up a bit of information.”

She gave him another once-over and nodded with satisfaction. Excellent. He now looked exactly as he had the first time she met him.

She herself was no less convincing. Out came the dirtiest, most threadbare coat she owned. Add her excellent acting, plus a shrinking, timid posture, and she looked every inch a refugee on the verge of starving to death.

Adrian went back to hacking holes in the ice and catching fish, while Evelyn stayed on the bank gathering firewood. A few minutes later, a dense crowd of people came into view.

“Good God, there are people here. Captain, come quickly. There are people fishing here.”

“There’s another one on the bank, and a tent too. Captain, there are survivors living here.”

“Quick, let’s have a look.”

Evelyn’s sight and hearing were sharp enough now that she could make out the leading men from quite a distance.

“Oi, you two. Come here.”

She exchanged a glance with Adrian. The two of them froze for a moment, then Evelyn turned as if to run, only for someone to hurry over and block her path.

“What are you running for? We’re not bad people. We’re hunting crews from Blackwater Base. Where have you two come from? What’s your relationship? How long have you been staying here?”

The questions hit her one after another. Evelyn looked at them in frightened silence and said nothing. By then, two men had already brought Adrian over as well.

He was holding a shovel in one hand and half a bucket of small fish in the other.

“You really aren’t bad people? You really are from Blackwater Base and came here to fish?”

“Yes, that’s right. We’re all survivors from Blackwater Base. This is the chief captain of the hunting crews, Captain Mercer. Going by your voice, you’re a woman, aren’t you? Are you two husband and wife?”

Adrian was far too tall for anyone to mistake his sex, even though he had obediently stooped a little, as Evelyn told him to. He still towered over everyone else.

Evelyn nearly choked. Before she could answer, Adrian gave a muffled grunt of assent.

“Where have you come from?” the Captain asked again.

Afraid Adrian might say the wrong thing, Evelyn quickly clutched his arm and put on an expression of fear and unease.

“Elaris. We’re trying to get to the base, but we got lost. Since you’re from Blackwater Base, could we go back with you?”

After saying that, she gave several coughs for effect. Mercer looked the two of them over, then sent someone to inspect the tent. When they found a grimy little squirrel and a small bird by the fire, everyone reacted with amazement.

“Are these your pets? You’ve hardly got any food left in the pot and you’re still keeping pets?” A young woman picked up Pip, fascinated. Evelyn stepped forward and snatched Pip back at once.

“We found them in the mountains. They keep us company.”

“We can take you to Blackwater Base,” the woman said. “But those two little things will have to be handed over.”

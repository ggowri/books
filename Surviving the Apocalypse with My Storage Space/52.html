Chapter 52 Heatwave, The Cellar

Vultures were carrion eaters. Massive birds that could soar thousands of feet into the sky. Their meat was tough, dense, and likely riddled with parasites and bacteria. On top of that, processing one was no simple task.

Evelyn laid out the pros and cons for the two of them. But judging by Ray's expression, he had no intention of giving up those birds.

"If we dry the meat under the sun, there won't be any parasites left. Our immune systems are strong. We're not scared of bacteria. Evie, people are digging up tree roots to eat now."

Evelyn sighed. "Then be careful when you handle them. Don't cut your hands. Salt it properly. Air-dry it. Smoke it. Leave it under direct sun long enough."

She went back inside and brought out a sharp kitchen knife for them to borrow.

There were rules for life in civilized times. There were different rules now.

If she had not been reborn with that spatial storage ability, Evelyn knew her situation would be far worse.

The next day, when Ray returned the knife, he brought her half a pound of vulture meat. It had been sliced into strips and rubbed with chili powder. The chili had been scavenged on one of their earlier supply runs.

The meat had been handled surprisingly well. Evelyn had to admit, she was impressed.

Two days later, Calvin took the other two out again to search for supplies. He was not the type who could sit still. Maybe it was the habits of his former profession, but he was meticulous and always alert to danger.

The daytime temperatures climbed higher with each passing day. When it hit 52 degrees, even the vultures outside could not endure the relentless sun. Their sharp cries pierced the air.

The ground downstairs had already cracked open. In the middle of the night, Evelyn went down to take a look. Some of the fissures were nearly two inches wide. The soil had turned to sand. Even the slightest breeze would kick up clouds of yellow dust.

The next day, when Calvin and the others returned, they knocked on her door with unexpected good news.

They had found supplies at Everpeace Monastery on West Ridge. A lot of supplies. They wanted Evelyn to join them that night to help haul everything back.

"Calvin, Ray, Evan, you should divide what you found among yourselves. The things we brought back from Maple Grove last time will last me a long while."

"Don't turn us down," Calvin insisted, eyes bright with excitement. He even gestured with his hands to show the size. "There's really a lot. This was all thanks to Ray. He said that when his mom used to take him to Everpeace Monastery to burn incense, he once saw the monks carrying sacks of rice down into a cellar. We went there last night just to try our luck. The cellar is still intact. We got unbelievably lucky."

"Yeah, Evie," Ray chimed in. "I want all five of us to survive. We have to stick together. Help each other. No dividing things up like strangers. If you refuse, then next time we get sick, we won't dare ask you for help."

Evelyn looked at him and could not help but smile.

"Fine. I'll go. What time?"

"Nine thirty. Bring rope and sacks." Calvin checked his watch and made the call. Then everyone went back to prepare.

At nine that evening, Evelyn began assembling her gear. Two burlap sacks. A coil of rope. Her crossbow and sharp blade strapped to her arm. She pulled on a fully sealed sun-protection suit.

After a moment's thought, she took out a large water container, filled it to the brim, and secured it to her back.

Wendy was once again locked safely inside Calvin's apartment.

At nine thirty sharp, the four of them left Willow Heights and headed toward Everpeace Monastery on West Ridge. On foot, it would take about fifty minutes.

No one spoke along the way. Each of them kept their head down and pushed forward.

Calvin led at the front. Evelyn took the rear.

Ray and Evan were not as strong physically, but they gritted their teeth and kept up without slowing the group even a little.

At ten twenty, the four of them reached the foot of West Mountain.

The floods had destroyed the steps and pathways. Now the slope was a chaotic mess of jagged rocks and loose sand, making the climb brutally difficult.

West Mountain had once been nothing more than a forest park. Now every tree had been cut down, leaving behind only scattered stumps. They grabbed fallen branches to use as makeshift walking sticks and practically crawled up the mountain on all fours.

Everpeace Monastery had suffered heavy damage from the flooding. The massive statue at the entrance was half buried in sand, yet its compassionate gaze still seemed to overlook the ruined city below.

"The cellar is in the back," Calvin said quietly. "This stretch is tough. Be careful."

To avoid drawing attention, they moved in complete darkness. Evelyn did not switch on her flashlight. Thankfully, she wore night vision goggles. Compared to the other three, she had a clear advantage.

Ray and Evan kept slipping and falling. Evelyn could see the skin torn open on their knees, blood soaking through, but neither of them made a sound.

After stumbling and scrambling their way to a small detached courtyard behind the temple, Ray explained that this had once been the kitchen. The cellar was beneath it.

They rolled aside a massive stone slab. Calvin took out a small shovel he carried with him and began digging. After scraping away the top layer of dirt, a thick iron plate was revealed.

That was when Evelyn understood how the cellar had survived the torrential rains and flooding intact.

It was reinforced like an iron cage.

Once all four of them climbed inside, Evelyn realized it held more than just grain. There were stacks of books, scriptures, and numerous valuable artifacts.

"When we came in last night," Calvin said quietly, "there were several bodies near the entrance. They must have hidden down here to protect these things. The iron plate can only be opened from the outside. Whoever was outside probably died. The ones inside were trapped."

The bodies had been moved to a corner. Calvin had covered them with a black cloth.

Evelyn scanned the cellar. It was not large, around sixteen hundred square feet. Historical records said Everpeace Monastery had a history of over a thousand years. This cellar was likely built by people from that distant era. It was airtight, the oxygen thin, the temperature low. Exquisite murals still adorned the walls.

"What a shame," Calvin murmured, then began hauling supplies.

There was rice, flour, dried noodles. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, cabbage.

Evelyn loaded a fifty-pound sack of rice, five large cabbages, three bundles of noodles, and roughly twenty pounds of potatoes.

"We should make another trip," Calvin suggested.

Ray and Evan agreed immediately. Evelyn did not object.

They each packed what they could carry and climbed out one by one. Calvin replaced the iron plate, shoveled sand back over it, then the four of them worked together to push the heavy stone slab back into place.

The descent was even harder. The sand was loose and soft. One wrong step and you went down. Evelyn fell several times herself.

Sweat soaked her forehead, dripping into her mouth. It was salty and bitter. Her socks were drenched, squelching inside her shoes as if she were walking through water.

With the added weight on their backs, their return was painfully slow. Evelyn shared some of her water. After a few precious swallows, they regained enough strength to keep moving.

The trip back took an hour and a half. Thankfully, they ran into no one.

After unloading and resting for half an hour, they set out again for a second run.

There was enough grain in that cellar to feed them for a year.

Now the only real problem was the lack of water.

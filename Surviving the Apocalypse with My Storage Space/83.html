Chapter 83 Surviving Alone: Part 7

Staring up at the ceiling, Evelyn suddenly remembered.

The lightning rods.

She had forgotten to install them.

Dragging out the ladder she had built earlier, she positioned it firmly against the cabin. With a lightning rod in hand, she climbed onto the roof.

She chose a solid spot, secured the rod tightly, and climbed back down.

From the rooftop, she had taken a look around. The fog was so thick she could barely see anything beyond a few meters.

Today she still had to deal with the buffalo heads and organs.

Better to finish it quickly.

She carried the heads, hooves, and basins of organs from the cabin and placed them under the large umbrella. Before starting, she checked on the rendered tallow.

It had fully solidified into pale blocks.

Perfect.

She sealed the metal buckets and stored them away.

Sitting beside the fire, she began skinning the buffalo head. Once the hide was removed, she carefully extracted the brain and set it on a plate.

After processing the tongue and hooves, she finally paused to rest.

The tastier the ingredient, the more troublesome it was to prepare.

She peeled off her gloves and washed her hands thoroughly with soap, then returned to the fire with the plate.

She wrapped the brain in lettuce leaves and placed it over the charcoal to roast. When a rich aroma began to rise, it would be ready.

Beef brain was said to nourish the body and replenish energy. Most people could not accept it, but animal brains were surprisingly nutritious.

The afternoon would be devoted to the organs.

That part gave her a headache.

The buffalo stomach was enormous, and there were four of them. The rumen required the most effort. The other three could be managed more easily. The intestines were just as troublesome.

Fortunately, these wild buffalo had fed on grass and leaves. When she opened the stomach, the smell was not overwhelming. There was even a faint scent of greenery.

She rinsed the intestines with warm water, turning them inside out. Then she scrubbed them with wood ash four or five times, finishing with a final wash using flour.

By the end, they were spotless.

As for the hides, she would deal with them later.

Inside, the wooden cabin was now completely dry. Outside, the snow had piled up to her knees. One step into it and she sank deep.

The water pipe had been installed. The water heater was in place.

It was time to move in.

In the bedroom, Sprig's nest sat near the heated brick bed. Now there was also the small injured bird. Evelyn had decided to keep it for the time being.

She took out two flowerpots and planted camellia seeds in each. Camellias were cold resistant. With luck, they would sprout this winter.

She placed the cactus on the bedroom table as well.

The little house was finally starting to feel like a home.

Winter was here.

After winter would come the New Year.

"Sprig, can you not cause trouble? I'm making two lanterns. Stop getting in the way."

She was cutting some paper in front of the fireplace when the squirrel smacked her arm with its tail. Finally losing patience, she picked it up and set it farther away.

She had given the little bird the name Fallon.

Because it had fallen into her home.

She hung the lanterns outside the cabin door.

Instantly, the place felt like a home.

Staring at the thick blanket of snow, she suddenly felt like building a snowman.

Sprig stood at the doorway watching her. She waved.

"Sprig, come see. I'm making you out of snow."

The squirrel stretched its neck out, then quickly retreated. Too cold.

Evelyn burst out laughing and kept rolling snowballs.

She built a father, a mother, herself, and Sprig. On top of snow Sprig's head, she placed a tiny snow bird for Fallon.

Beside them, she shaped Calvin, Wendy, Eddie, Ray, and Evan.

"Still a month to go, but happy New Year in advance, everyone."

She scooped up a handful of snow and tossed it into the sky.

She already had a mild cold. After playing in the snow, it worsened.

Back inside the cabin, she shivered uncontrollably and quickly boiled ginger tea, gulping it down. Sneezes and sniffles followed one after another.

For the next few days, she decided not to go outside.

She would rest completely.

"Ah-choo!"

Sprig was startled by her violent sneezes and leapt to the floor. Fallon's injuries had mostly healed by now and it began hopping around the cabin with Sprig.

After taking cold medicine and sweating it out under the blankets, Evelyn felt slightly better. Lying in bed made her crave something spicy. Suddenly she wanted chopped chili fish head.

Fine.

Dinner would be chopped chili fish head.

Maybe sick people were always greedy. She also wanted popsicles. Watermelon. Frozen pears.

Stop it. You do not.

She rolled her eyes at herself and headed into the kitchen.

*****

The next day, she was back to full energy.

She even admired her own recovery speed. When she caught a cold, she caught it hard. When she recovered, she bounced right back.

Staying cooped up inside all day felt pointless. Before the snow grew any deeper, she decided to cut more fir logs and store them.

She would not touch the remaining trees inside the hollow. Some needed to stay to block the wind.

Instead, she chose a more distant slope.

For this logging trip, she prepared thoroughly. From head to toe, she wrapped herself in layers, even putting on protective goggles.

After snowfall, snow blindness was a real risk.

"Sprig, you and Fallon stay home. I'll be on the slope to the right. I'll be back soon. Be good. Don't tear the place apart."

Sprig circled her boots. She patted its head, checked that its food bowl was still full, and stepped outside.

The snow was slightly above her knees now.

She wore thick deerskin snow boots. Walking through the snow was clumsy but not cold at all.

The fir trees on the slope were dense and straight. Once she reached her chosen spot, she pulled out the chainsaw and began cutting.

It took her the entire afternoon to fell dozens of trees. She stored the logs in her space and prepared to head back.

On the way, she spotted a pheasant buried in the snow.

Her eyes lit up.

She hurried over and dug it out.

Dinner would be pheasant stewed with mushrooms. Ingredients acquired.

The pheasant was still alive, staring at her with a look of utter despair.

She slipped and fell several times on the way back, but her mood remained bright.

When she reached the yard, she saw no signs of large animals. Relieved, she opened the gate, stepped inside, and latched it securely.

"Chirp, chirp!"

Sprig bounced wildly at the window when it saw her return.

She shook the snow from her coat and tossed the pheasant aside.

Staying home was comfortable as there was no wind or freezing cold.

But going out to work had its perks too since it was exercise, fresh air, and the occasional free pheasant.

Perfect.

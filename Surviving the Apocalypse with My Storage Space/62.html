Chapter 62 Heatwave, Wild Greens

Evelyn had indeed stockpiled a lot of night vision goggles, buying several boxes at wholesale price.

"Sure," she said.

Eddie lit up. "Perfect. Come by later and pick something you like from my place."

They split up after that.

After several hours of digging, Evelyn had nearly half a bucket of wild greens.

A short whistle cut through the wind.

It was Calvin.

Everyone immediately pulled back.

Once home, Evelyn handed Eddie a pair of night vision goggles. In exchange, she took two painkillers from him.

The appearance of wild greens quickly eased their food anxiety.

Evelyn made steamed buns and dumplings stuffed with the greens. They tasted decent.

But she preferred eating them raw.

There was a subtle sweetness to them, and they seemed to reduce internal heat. After eating them, her body felt lighter, clearer.

In this extreme heat, people were prone to inflammation and nosebleeds.

Even though she had ice water in her space and air conditioning at home, she had suffered several nosebleeds lately.

After two days of eating wild greens, the symptoms faded.

She felt more energized.

That night, everyone gathered in the hallway on time. When the wind weakened, they headed out again.

Even Wendy insisted on coming.

The greens inside the residential complex had already been picked clean.

Evelyn suggested they move out to the main streets. Pairs only. Stay within sight of each other. Weapons on hand.

If they ran into that same gang again, even better.

A sudden hissing sound came from an overturned car nearby.

Evelyn drew her combat knife and approached carefully.

It sounded like a snake.

But when she lifted a broken plank and a massive rat shot out, she froze for half a second.

She had never seen a rat that large.

Out of pure reflex, she threw her knife.

It pinned the creature to the ground.

The rat looked almost like a porcupine, minus the quills. Its body was enormous. Gray-brown fur. It did not resemble any common local species.

More like an African giant rat.

Technically, rat meat was edible.

But plague was another matter entirely.

Evelyn lifted it and examined it carefully.

The fur was glossy. Eyes clear. No discharge. Tail intact. No lesions. No abnormal swelling.

It was healthy.

She hooked it onto her bucket and continued digging.

Soon, Eddie and Calvin encountered similar giant rats.

Meat was meat.

But plague lingered in everyone's minds.

They brought their catches to Evelyn.

"You're half a doctor," Eddie said. "Can't you tell if it's safe?"

She gave him a look and flipped his rat over for inspection.

"You're a researcher. Can't you tell?"

"I don't study rats," he replied defensively.

"This one's fine," she said at last. "The hide is thick. Keep it. Might be useful later."

She handed it back and checked Calvin's.

"I'll teach you what to look for," she added. "Next time, you won't need me."

All three rats passed inspection.

It had been a productive night.

When two a.m. approached, everyone except Evelyn hesitated to leave.

No one wanted to waste the opportunity.

"Don't lose the big picture over small gains. If the wind picks up and sweeps you away, no one will be able to save you."

After saying that, Evelyn headed back first.

The others didn't dare linger. Buckets in one hand, giant rats in the other, they hurried home.

When Evelyn processed her rat, she deliberately took out a boning knife.

She wanted the hide intact.

As for the meat, she planned to make jerky.

She rubbed it with chili powder, ground pepper, and salt, sliced the flesh into long strips, and hung them out on the balcony to dry. Then she went back inside to work on the hide.

Defat. Soften. Dry. Soak. Defat again. Dry again.

Several days later, a nearly perfect sheet of giant rat fur was finished.

With wild greens and giant rats now part of their food supply, everyone in the building had grown busy.

Calvin organized the group to make several net traps. That way, if they encountered giant rats again, they would not need to fight them head-on. Just trap them.

Evelyn gave each of them some salt. In return, Ray and the others gave her a bag of chili powder.

Rat jerky, if preserved properly, could last indefinitely. If they ever had to leave, it would travel well.

Food meant survival.

But luck did not favor them every night. Some nights, they would not see a single rat.

The wild greens still had to be harvested. They dried them and stored them in plastic bags. When needed, they would boil them briefly to rehydrate.

Sometimes they mixed chopped greens with minced rat meat, pressed the mixture into patties, and roasted them over a small flame.

Calvin's method was even simpler.

Wipe off the sand and eat them raw.

Water was too precious to waste on washing.

Soon, the greens inside the residential complex and along the nearby streets were gone.

They ventured farther.

The wind had weakened these past few days, making it easier to move around.

But the temperature refused to drop.

It was suffocating.

A building up ahead suddenly caught fire. It burned for two days and two nights before the flames finally died down.

The charred smell lingered in the air.

Evelyn sneezed.

With the wind calmer, visibility improved. Standing on her balcony with binoculars, she saw nothing but blackened walls and drifting smoke.

Thankfully, no embers had blown toward Willow Heights.

She went down to the eleventh floor.

Everyone was training there.

When they saw her, they looked surprised.

"Calvin, we should service the van downstairs," she said. "If something happens, we need to be ready to leave immediately. Everyone should start packing essentials now. No scrambling at the last second."

There were six people in one van, plus luggage.

The roof rack could hold some, but space would still be tight.

"Tools, food, critical clothing. Nothing unnecessary," she added.

"Evie, you think the fire in Northside is coming this way?" Calvin asked.

She shook her head.

"It would take a long time to reach us. But several nearby neighborhoods have had spontaneous fires lately. It could happen here too. Better to prepare."

"I have a suggestion," Eddie said. "We should find spare tires. We absolutely need backups. The fuel we found could take us two thousand kilometers. The weak point is the tires."

Evelyn nodded in agreement.

Behind Willow Heights, there was an auto repair shop.

They might get lucky.

"I'll go with you," she said. "If we can find repair tools too, even better."

Calvin had no objections.

"Ten p.m. tonight then. Shame we couldn't find a high-clearance SUV."

"The good ones were all swept away in the floods," Eddie said wistfully. "There's a pit up ahead full of limited-edition supercars."

His face was full of regret.

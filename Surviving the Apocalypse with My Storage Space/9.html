Chapter 9 Rainstorm: Part 5

Along the way, they passed many others out gathering supplies like themselves. The rain poured relentlessly. One family's speedboat capsized right in front of them, throwing everyone into the filthy water. Evelyn skillfully maneuvered their boat close, spotting an elderly person and a child struggling. Several neighbors seated behind them quickly reached out to help.

Sewage soaked their clothes, and the child, shivering and teary-eyed, clung to an adult. The elderly person looked pale and swollen, clearly unwell.

"Thank you all so much," one of the adults gasped, catching their breath. "The wind's too strong—it's impossible to steer these boats properly. Are you heading to Everwell Plaza? We just came from there. It's packed, but at least the security team is maintaining order. Better hurry over."

They bowed gratefully as their family got back into the repaired boat and turned off in the opposite direction.

"What a cursed storm..."

"Where are they even going? Doesn't look like a supply run to me."

"Probably to a shelter. I live on the fourth floor. If the water keeps rising, we'll have to evacuate too."

People murmured among themselves, while Evelyn and Claire remained quiet at the front.

When they finally reached Everwell Plaza, the sight was overwhelming—dozens of speedboats crowded the entrance, and a sea of people surged forward. The mall had five floors, with the bottom two already evacuated. Supplies seemed sufficient for now, but the chaos was palpable.

Cries of frustration, shivers from the cold, and anxious tears filled the air. Armed security patrolled the scene, electric batons in hand, glaring at the crowd.

"No shouting, no pushing! Leave your speedboats here. We'll tag them. You'll need your number to get it back," one guard barked.

Someone had already tried to steal a boat and was promptly electrocuted and dragged off—swift and ruthless punishment that sent a chilling message.

People even crowded onto the overpass in front of the mall, desperate for space.

Evelyn and Claire docked the boat and paid a fifty-dollar parking fee. A number was sprayed onto their wrists before they were let inside.

Inside was even worse. It was so tightly packed that turning around was nearly impossible. Evelyn took one look and saw small bottles of water priced at three dollars, large ones at six dollars. Shouting and swearing filled the air, yet hands still snatched items rapidly off the shelves.

Scuffles broke out often. At the first sign of trouble, security would move in, stun, and drag the offenders away.

Evelyn managed to grab a large jug of water, a five-kilogram bag of rice, a cabbage, three large potatoes, three packs of sanitary pads, and a pack of tissue.

She felt like her internal organs had been squashed from the crowd. Someone tried to snatch her items, so she quickly drew the knife hidden in her sleeve and slashed at the would-be thief's side. He clutched his waist and cursed, but with so many people around and Evelyn's quick, discreet move, no one saw who'd done it.

By the time he reacted, Evelyn had already disappeared into the crowd—and snagged a can of spicy bean paste on her way.

Claire was right behind her, limping slightly from having her foot stepped on repeatedly.

Evelyn glanced into her basket: one bag of flour and three bundles of noodles. Not much.

She dragged Claire toward a group of middle-aged men and snatched three bottles of water from their stash.

"Claire," she said, low but firm, "food is life now."

Claire blushed. At her height and build, she could barely squeeze through the crowd. Without Evelyn leading the way, she wouldn't have even made it inside. Thinking of her elderly, ill grandparents, she nodded with new determination.

After half an hour, they finally reached the cashier. What would've cost twenty dollars before now totaled over a hundred. The five-kilogram bag of rice, once seven dollars, had spiked to sixty.

Evelyn packed everything into her waterproof sacks. Claire hadn't brought any, so she bought two at the mall—ten dollars each. It hurt.

They didn't leave immediately and made their way to the pharmacy next door. Prices hadn't risen too much there, but with a limit of one box per person per medicine, lines were long and shelves half-empty. Most people went for cold and fever meds.

Evelyn bought a box of painkillers, a box of fever reducers, and one roll of gauze.

The two were fairly lucky—they managed to buy a speedboat, though only the smallest model was left, one that could fit just three people. The price was outrageously high.

With only 50 minutes remaining in their three-hour rental slot, and people already lined up for the next turn, Evelyn and Claire had to be punctual. After retrieving their speedboat from the security team and loading up their supplies, they prepared to head back.

"What about them?" Claire glanced toward the direction of the others.

"You said they'd make their own way back," Evelyn replied, not sarcastically but out of genuine curiosity. Even now, Claire still cared enough to worry about others?

"But what if they didn't manage to get a boat? If we leave them here, they'll be stranded."

Evelyn gave her a serious look. "They have families. If they're stuck, someone will come for them."

"But—"

Before Claire could finish, the six others came trudging out, each carrying pitifully small bags of supplies. When they saw Evelyn and Claire still there, the despair in their expressions finally lifted.

"Evelyn, Claire! We thought you left already. That place is a zoo—and the prices are insane."

"You even managed to get a boat? We checked with the vendor, and they said they were out for today. The price's gone up twentyfold too."

"You two really did great—two girls doing better than all us guys," one of them said with a forced laugh.

They looked with envy at the two bagged speedboats and the waterproof sacks of supplies. Evelyn ignored them, and after an awkward pause, the man shut his mouth. The others busied themselves trying to climb aboard.

Just before starting the engine, Evelyn turned around and said flatly, "This boat is from someone on the eighth floor. Three hours, three hundred dollars. There are eight of us, so that's 37.50 each."

Everyone, including Claire, stared at her. Claire quickly caught on and echoed, "Yeah, let's all chip in."

The silence stretched uncomfortably until a middle-aged man from the fifth floor finally spoke up, promising to pay once they got back home.

Only then did Evelyn start the engine. The six others exchanged looks, some rolling their eyes at her, others muttering sarcastic comments under their breath. Evelyn ignored them as she'd bought a speedboat in full view today. From now on, she wouldn't need anyone else's help to move around.

The return trip was much quicker, but the cold had taken its toll—despite gloves, Evelyn's hands were numb. Just as they neared the complex, a thunderclap cracked overhead, causing people behind her to shriek in fright.

Then, the bloated corpse of a large dog floated by, its stench so powerful it made her gag. Evelyn held her breath and steered them swiftly back to the compound.

Once docked, they returned the boat to its owner, and the six passengers begrudgingly handed over their share of the cost. Evelyn carried the supplies in one hand and the deflated boat in the other as she headed upstairs.

"Evelyn," Claire called out when they reached the ninth floor.

"Thanks for today," she said sincerely. "If it weren't for you, things would've gone a lot worse."

Evelyn shook her head. "Go home and rest."

Each returned to their own apartment.

Inside 901, Jason pulled back from the peephole excitedly and turned to Laura.

"Evelyn and Claire got food—and a boat! Go ask if we can borrow it. We need to head out too."

Chapter 26 Rainstorm, Toads

The next morning, a deafening boom shattered the tense silence that hung over the neighborhood.

Evelyn yanked open her curtains. Something white had splattered across the window glass. A terrible thought crossed her mind. She grabbed her binoculars and scanned the water outside for the bloated corpses that had been floating there just yesterday.

They were gone. All of them.

Evelyn had once seen videos online of whale carcasses exploding—sudden, violent bursts of built-up gas. She'd heard that the bloated corpses in this kind of extreme weather, what people called giant-view corpses, could explode with a force rivaling a bomb.

But this was her first time witnessing it, live and up close.

When temperatures dropped to minus sixteen degrees, the venom moths had vanished overnight. All they left behind were pools of sticky corpse-fluid and an overwhelming stench of rot.

When Calvin returned from a supply run, he brought grim news.

An old residential apartment nearby had collapsed during the night. The entire ground beneath it had sunk into a massive pit. No one survived.

Evelyn understood the worry on his face.

The rain had been falling nonstop for a full month. The city's underground drainage systems had failed days in. The ground surface was eroding fast. Once the soil structure gave out, collapses like this were inevitable.

Worse still, the floodwaters were carrying debris from upstream like cars, steel bars, and tree trunks downstream with terrifying momentum. If those got stuck anywhere, the blockage would stop water from flowing properly, leading to massive internal flooding.

Evelyn knew what was coming next.

Major flooding, collapsed roads, dam failures, landslides, and mudslides.

Elaris was built low, in a natural basin. If the reservoirs burst, the entire city would become a drowned island.

At least there was some good news. Wendy's chickenpox had almost cleared up, and by some miracle, Jessica hadn't caught it.

Back in her apartment, though, Evelyn couldn't sit still. An uneasy restlessness took hold of her.

There were too many variables and too many unknowns.

What if Willow Heights was next to collapse? What would she do then?

In her last life, the pattern of natural disasters hadn't been much different—but she was almost certain that neighborhood hadn't collapsed back then. Instead, during a heatwave, a gas tank had exploded in one of the apartments and destroyed the whole building. A few survivors had still been pulled from the rubble.

And more importantly, after the rains in her previous life, the city had quickly frozen over.

But this time? Despite temperatures plunging past minus ten, the water hadn't frozen. Instead, venom moths had appeared.

That afternoon, Evelyn went out.

When she saw the enormous crater with her own eyes, her heart dropped.

Floodwater from all directions poured into it, creating a terrifying waterfall. It was a rare moment—the clouds broke slightly, letting through a narrow shaft of light. Through the mist, Evelyn thought she saw the faint arc of a rainbow.

But instead of beauty, all she felt was dread.

For the first time, she truly felt how small and powerless she was.

It felt like she had been dropped into a giant disaster simulation game. One where some unseen hand kept designing crueler and crueler levels. The sudden sinkhole was just another stage. And all those who died in it were nothing more than background NPCs—scripted casualties.

Evelyn looked up at the sky, imagining a face watching her from above, sneering.

Then that unseen hand conjured a gust of wind and tossed her into the pit, like she was nothing.

She didn't let the thought linger.

Taking a detour, Evelyn made her way to the park where she'd cut trees before. But it was already stripped bare. Even the stumps were gone.

On the way back, she passed a half-finished housing development. It was built on slightly higher ground. Inside, a few trees still remained.

Evelyn steered her motor raft inside, took out her axe, and chopped the greenbelt trees into chunks before loading them onboard.

She flexed her stiff fingers, the cold biting at her skin.

Time to head home.

On the way back, a loud plop behind her made Evelyn turn sharply.

A toad had landed on the back end of her motor raft.

But this wasn't just any toad.

It was bigger than any toad she'd ever seen. Its bulging, unblinking eyes stared straight at her. Its lumpy, black-brown skin reminded her of the now-vanished venom moths.

Toads, also known as bufo, usually avoided the cold. Especially cold like this—minus ten degrees and falling. The ones she'd seen before were barely fist-sized. This one was the size of a soccer ball.

It was grotesque. The kind of thing that would make someone with trypophobia faint on sight.

Toads were poisonous, but their toxins could also be used as medicine. Evelyn stared at it for a couple of seconds, then calmly poked it with a thin stick until it dropped back into the water.

When she returned to Block D, she spotted more toads hopping through the stairwell.

That's when it clicked.

The venom moths were gone. Now, it was the toads' turn.

Sure enough, within two days, they had taken over the stairwells and the water just like the moths had before.

The residents were miserable. They couldn't even step outside their doors. The stairwell floors were covered in toads. And when dozens of them croaked at once, it wasn't like frogs—it was louder, deeper, and echoed in a way that made your scalp crawl.

What made it worse was how far they could jump.

If anyone came out, the toads would immediately leap onto them. Their skin secretions weren't just poisonous, but sticky and impossible to shake off.

Evelyn tried torches and insecticide.

Nothing worked.

Every time a toad was about to croak, the skin around its throat would puff up like a balloon. It looked… just like those bloated corpses before they exploded. The giant-view corpses.

Since the toads appeared, Evelyn had stayed inside. Outside, the floodwaters kept rising.

The fifth floor was barely hanging on.

Then, suddenly, the door next to hers slammed open.

It was John.

He'd opened it without warning, and in seconds, the hallway swarm of toads flooded into their apartment.

Evelyn heard shouts, cursing, furniture being knocked over. She rushed to throw on her protective suit, grabbed a pair of tongs, and opened her door.

"Kill them! Throw them out! Don't let those damn things in the house!"

"Dad, the balcony's full of them! They're in the bedroom too!"

"It's all John's fault! Why'd he open the door? That brainless kid's nothing but dead weight!"

"You're calling someone dead weight? You talk about my son like that and I'll beat you, you crazy woman!"

"You dare touch me? I'm pregnant with Zack's child! When he comes back, I'll make sure he deals with you!"

"If he comes back. I'm telling you, Zack isn't coming back!"

"What did you just say, Henry? What do you mean by that?"

Quinn and Henry began to fight, hurling accusations and fists at each other.

David managed to calm John down, then stormed over and slapped both of them.

"If you two want to keep fighting, get out. John is my grandson. He stays. You two outsiders can leave anytime."

The moment he said that, Henry and Quinn awkwardly dropped their hands, backing off in silence. David glanced at Quinn's belly and muttered, "You better keep that baby safe."

Evelyn, crouched at the door, clamped one of the toads with her tongs, shut her door, and went back inside without a word.

She placed the toad on the counter, pulled on gloves, and grabbed a surgical knife.

First, she severed the head. Then she peeled back the skin and removed the poison glands.

Toads were toxic, yes—but also edible.

Maybe… the best way to deal with them was to eat them.

Still, a flicker of hesitation passed through her.

They'd emerged from the floodwaters. Who knew what kind of filth they carried?

But the moment passed.

She tossed the cleaned toad into hot oil, just like how she used to cook bullfrog—deep-fried, or boiled in spicy broth.

As the meat sizzled, a rich aroma filled the air.

Evelyn took a bite.

Surprisingly, it was good. The flavor was solid.

But these things seemed… mutated. The largest one she'd seen was nearly basketball-sized.

Whether there would be any side effects remained to be seen and required observation for several hours.

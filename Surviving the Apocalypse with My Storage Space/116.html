Chapter 116 Recruitment and Turning the Soil

Because the lumps on her face would take two or three days to disappear on their own, Evelyn wrapped herself up tightly when she went to work at the Medical Division the next day. At lunchtime, she did not even go to the canteen.

“What’s wrong with your face?”

After lunch, Eddie came looking for her. Seeing that she was still covering herself up, he was full of curiosity.

“An allergic reaction. I thought people might be put off if they saw it, so I covered it.”

Eddie frowned. “What did you react to? There should be allergy medicine here. I’ll see if I can find some for you.”

“No need. It’ll be gone in a couple of days. How are Pip and Lark? They haven’t wrecked the place, have they?”

“They’ve been angelic. Don’t worry. I can look after them perfectly well. You can leave them with me without a second thought.”

Then Eddie seemed to remember something. He glanced around, then leaned closer and lowered his voice.

“They took several doctors away from the base. Guess what happened.”

A flicker passed through Evelyn’s eyes, though she only shook her head. “How would I know?”

“Apparently Miss Hawthorne spent all last night being sick at both ends and convulsing. Then this morning her whole body started swelling up. The doctors said her private life had been far too chaotic and that she’d caught something nasty. Told her to stay calm and abstain.”

Beneath her mask, Evelyn’s smile nearly broke through.

“That’s really rather tragic.”

Her hands never stopped working as Eddie went on.

“Tragic, my foot. I’ve never seen such a female bandit in my life. Any good-looking man in the base gets snatched up by her sooner or later. There was a boy before who refused her no matter what. She had her bodyguards tie up his parents and throw them out of the base. In the end, the guards beat him to death.”

“The base did nothing?”

“What could they do? She’s the Hawthorne family’s precious daughter. There are several sons in that family, but only one daughter. I suppose she inherited Mr. Hawthorne’s appetite. They say he’s got seven or eight children. Only the eldest was born to his proper wife. The rest are all illegitimate.”

He lowered his voice even further.

“Do you remember Young Mr. Hawthorne? He’s one of them.”

How could Evelyn possibly forget? Young Mr. Hawthorne’s camper van was still sitting in her storage space.

“Why aren’t you out on a mission today?”

Eddie looked helpless. “I’ve been transferred over to support your Surgical Unit.”

“The temperature’s rising. Whenever things change too strangely, trouble follows. Spend your points while you can. Stock up on supplies.”

Evelyn kept her tone deliberately vague.

By now she had more or less grasped the pattern of these disasters. The moment one ended, the next began. There was never any time to breathe.

“I know,” Eddie said quietly. “Last time, those medicines you slipped to us really saved us. Wendy and Ray were ill for days. Calvin was in despair. He nearly followed them.”

Evelyn could imagine how hard it had been for them.

“At least all of us are still alive.”

That afternoon, a shaft of sunlight appeared. Evelyn took out her thermometer and checked it. The temperature really had climbed to minus twenty.

It was the first time she had heard the base loudspeakers broadcasting an announcement.

“Attention, attention. As the temperature rises, the base is urgently recruiting two thousand workers. In two days’ time, snow clearing and soil turning will begin. All base residents are encouraged to register on the first floor of the Administration Hall. Male workers will receive ninety points per day. Female workers will receive seventy points per day. End of notice.”

Turning the soil?

Evelyn could not work out why the base wanted land turned over at a time like this. Were they planning to build, or to plant?

Workers growing vegetables in the Underground Quarter only earned forty points a day. Soil turning was paying nearly double that. Quite a few people were immediately excited, as though they wanted to run straight to the Administration Hall and sign up on the spot.

The next day, Evelyn heard the gossip from Dr. Hargreaves. Apparently the base experts had predicted that once the cold ended, there would be a stable period lasting one to two years. That was why they were hiring workers to turn the soil. They intended to plant a genetically modified wheat.

This wheat, supposedly, could be harvested once every three months. It was said not to be fussy about soil either, and frozen ground would apparently produce an even better yield.

Evelyn felt her eyelid twitch.

The expert sounded deeply unreliable.

A harvest every three months? Did such wheat really exist? Ordinary wheat usually took 220 to 250 days to mature. And with weather as extreme as this, swinging from bitter cold to sudden warmth, how could anyone possibly guarantee that once the cold was over, no other natural disaster would follow?

Even with a second life behind her, Evelyn still could not predict what was coming next, unless this expert happened to be able to divine the future.

“Evelyn, do you think this sounds believable?”

Evelyn did not believe it for a second, but she could not say so aloud.

When she got home, she asked Adrian what he thought of it.

He gave her just two words.

“We’re finished.”

The base held an enormous population, larger than a small county town, with more than a million survivors living there from all over.

Livelihood was the most difficult and most important issue of all.

The power structure inside the base was already tangled enough as it was. Someone as insignificant as Evelyn could know perfectly well that the plan was unsound, but what exactly could she change?

That evening, Clara knocked on her door. She told Evelyn that from the next day onward, she would be starting work in the Surgical Unit. As for her boyfriend Elliot, the electrical maintenance department refused to release him, so he would have to remain where he was.

On the third day, the temperature rose again, to minus fifteen. Registration for the soil-turning work had already closed, and there were three thousand more applicants than expected.

Five thousand people turning soil.

The base clearly had grand ambitions this time.

Dr. Hargreaves said that with the weather warming, the base had decided to build several pig farms and chicken farms on the surface, moving the animals up from the Underground Quarter.

The grey sky had begun to turn blue. Everything seemed to be moving in a better direction, and even Evelyn started to feel that perhaps she had been too pessimistic.

Clara arrived in the Surgical Unit as a nurse, assigned to Evelyn.

She was hopeless.

After only one day, Evelyn felt completely drained by her.

In the end, Dr. Hargreaves had no choice but to hand Clara over to Dr. Shaw instead. Unfortunately for her, Dr. Shaw was not a man blessed with patience. Less than half an hour after Clara was reassigned, he had already shouted at her until she cried.

Evelyn did not interfere.

Even before the end of the world, that was simply how workplaces were. If someone lacked ability and still failed to work hard or take things seriously, being told off was inevitable.

If Clara wanted more points, then she would have to learn how to endure, study properly, and become more capable.

“Evelyn, I can’t do this. It’s too exhausting. I’m scared of learning injections, it’s horrible, and Dr. Shaw is terrifying.”

Evelyn looked at Clara and said nothing.

A full day’s work was tiring enough already. Nobody wanted to spend the end of it listening to someone else complain.

“Evelyn, if I go back to the herb gardens now, do you think the supervisor will tell me off?”

“You could ask.”

Clara scratched her head awkwardly. “I studied fine art at university. I wanted to be a painter. I just never thought the apocalypse would come. If it hadn’t been for Elliot, I might have starved to death during the rains. I don’t want to be a burden to him. I really do want to try, but I’ve realised I can’t do anything. I’m no good at anything.”

It was easy enough to see what sort of life she had once had. She had probably grown up in comfort, cherished and protected, the kind of girl who had always been kept safe in the palm of someone’s hand.

“When people are pushed to the edge, they find strengths they never knew they had.”

Clara took a breath. “Then I’ll try holding on a bit longer. Maybe tomorrow I’ll finally get the hang of it.”

Chapter 22 Rainstorm, Venom Moths: Part 18

Evelyn had never dealt with Henry directly, but she could still tell what kind of man he was. He was cunning and ruthless. No wonder, in her previous life, everyone in the Rivers family had died. Only Henry and John had survived.

She glanced at Fiona, who was already delirious and acting like a madwoman. It seemed that the needle was finally taking effect.

"So rabies really is terrifying," Evelyn said with a mocking smile as she carried her loot home.

Pauline was so furious she nearly jumped up again. Henry, however, fell into thought after hearing Evelyn's words. He studied Pauline quietly.

"Did you get the rabies vaccine or not?"

"Henry, what do you mean?" Pauline snapped. "You think I'm going to get sick? I already told you, that dog wasn't a stray. And it didn't even draw blood. My mom said I didn't need the shots. Five injections, do you know how much time that takes? I don't have time for that."

Henry stayed silent, head lowered, lost in thought.

Then, without warning, Fiona suddenly lunged forward and bit Pauline hard on the neck.

Henry froze for a second, then kicked Fiona away.

At that moment, David and Quinn, who had gone out to look for Zack, rushed back and saw everything. David's eyes turned red with rage. He stormed forward and slapped Henry twice.

Blood poured down Pauline's neck.

Pauline stared at her mother, who had completely lost control. She assumed Fiona had only gone crazy from worrying about Zack.

Only Henry stood there in shock, staring at Fiona. Then he stumbled back a step, his face filled with terror.

"She bit someone. She's mutated. Just like those venom moths. She's going to turn into a zombie."

Henry had read plenty of novels and watched countless zombie movies. Seeing Fiona like this, his mind immediately jumped to apocalyptic horror scenes.

He glanced at Pauline.

Suddenly, he shoved her down the stairs.

David felt like his son-in-law had lost his mind. His son was missing. His wife had gone insane. His daughter was nothing but trouble. Now even his son-in-law had snapped.

Endless resentment flooded his heart.

He stood in the stairwell and screamed hoarsely.

Quinn watched everything with cold eyes, as if the chaos had nothing to do with her.

Pauline tumbled down the stairs. After struggling to her feet, she looked up and saw her husband staring at her like she was dangerous.

"Henry, are you crazy? You actually pushed me?"

Henry shook his head in pain.

"Pauline, your mom bit you. Look at her. Does she look normal? She's going to turn into a zombie. You were bitten too. You'll turn into one as well."

"Pauline, I'm not going to kill you. Just go. Leave here. Go now."

Pauline was stunned.

She looked at Fiona.

Her once sharp and capable mother was lying on the floor, clutching her head and making strange noises. Her face was twisted in pain, her eyes rolling back.

She really didn't look normal.

Pauline touched her neck. The bite was numb and aching.

Her legs gave out. Her mind went blank.

She dropped to her knees.

"No… we're human. We're not monsters. Mom was just too stressed. I'll be fine once my wound is bandaged.

"Henry, don't do this. I'm scared.

"Dad, believe me! I'm normal!"

But Henry didn't look at her. He turned to David with a tortured expression.

"Dad, Mom and Pauline are really going to turn into zombies. Like in the movies. Cannibal monsters.

"John is still young. I can't let him get hurt.

"Dad, he's your only grandson."

Only then did David understand what his son-in-law was implying.

He didn't want to believe that normal people could turn into monsters. But Fiona was rolling on the ground, foaming at the mouth. Nothing about her looked normal.

"Dad, don't listen to him," Pauline cried. "Mom and I are fine. You can't abandon us.

"John, save me! John, please!"

She sobbed as she crawled up the stairs, only to be kicked down again by Henry.

"Pauline, don't hurt John. He's your son. As his mother, make one last sacrifice for him."

Pauline shook her head wildly, screaming at the top of her lungs. Neighbors upstairs and downstairs could all hear her cries for help. But Henry's words hung in the air like an executioner's blade over everyone's heads. No one wanted to take the risk.

If anything, they just wanted Pauline and Fiona gone as fast as possible.

David's eyes were bloodshot. He was over sixty years old, and he knew people too well.

If he didn't make a decision, their family might not survive in this building at all.

He looked at Pauline, eyes red with emotion, and made a promise. "Pauline, I'll take care of John. But you need to take your mom and leave. If you don't go, I'll have no choice but to lock you both in 601. Go find somewhere to hide outside. When you're better… I'll come get you."

Pauline sobbed and screamed, refusing to go downstairs.

That was when Fiona suddenly lunged at David, jaws wide. He reacted quickly and kicked her away.

Staring at her writhing on the floor, his face twisted between terror and relief.

Henry stepped in to help. The two of them forced Pauline down, then he turned to Quinn.

"Go grab Fiona."

Quinn froze. After a second of hesitation, and under Henry's urging, she slowly began walking toward Fiona.

Pauline's fear turned to despair. She begged over and over. David looked at his daughter. His hands loosened for a moment—until Henry grabbed him, faster than she could run.

"Dad, John is still a kid. He can't lose us. Pauline's already lost it. She's going to turn."

Just then, Quinn suddenly looked up at David and spoke slowly.

"Dad, I… I think I might be pregnant. This could be Zack's only child."

Henry snapped his head toward her. Quinn locked eyes with David.

He was hit with a wave of emotion—grief, hope, guilt. And just like that, his wavering heart hardened.

Pauline's screams echoed through every corner of Block D.

In the end, it was her own family who pushed her and Fiona out of the building.

The Rivers family had once had seven members. Now, only four remained.

Back in her apartment, Evelyn was sorting through the day's spoils. The noise outside slowly faded. She glanced at her right index finger. The pinprick had already disappeared, just like the problem she had needed to solve.

Perfectly handled.

As for those annoying loudmouths, Evelyn smirked.

Useless people would get what was coming to them. She wasn't going to waste her time.

Whether Pauline and Fiona lived or died, no one really cared. The residents of Block D only sighed, vaguely disturbed. For David—a man known for being kind—to kick out his own wife and daughter… he clearly wasn't ordinary after all.

The next morning, Quinn and David appeared at Evelyn's door.

His hair had turned half-white overnight. He looked ten years older. Quinn stood beside him, pale and empty-eyed like a ghost.

"Evie," he said quietly, "about what happened before… I'm ashamed, and I'm sorry."

"Pauline and her mother are… well, there's no point talking about it now. I came today because we need your help. Quinn says she might be pregnant. Would you… could you take a look for us?"

Evelyn's eyes flicked to Quinn's belly. Instinctively, Quinn took a step back and clutched it.

"I only treat people in exchange for supplies."

David nodded. "We understand. We won't shortchange you."

"Evie, would you come over and check my pulse?" Quinn asked, her voice soft with a trace of pleading in her eyes.

Evelyn narrowed her gaze, trying to figure out what exactly Quinn was planning.

Chapter 78 Surviving Alone: Part 2

She endured the night like that.

Evelyn sat by the fire until dawn. Even with painkillers, her injuries throbbed. Lying inside the tent did nothing but make her more restless, so she gave up trying to sleep and simply watched the flames until morning.

At six the next day, thick fog swallowed the entire mountain.

She limped out to survey the area. Within three hundred meters, there was no flat ground, no pond, nothing suitable for long term shelter. Finding a proper place to settle would not be easy.

She still liked the swamp where she had collected water before. But that was on another peak. Getting back there would not be simple either.

Forget it. Better to find another wetland nearby.

Sprig had developed a full blown imprinting instinct. The moment it woke, it tried to leap onto her. Wherever she went, it followed. The little thing was harder to manage than a zoo full of monkeys. Evelyn had long since given up reasoning with it and could only indulge it.

Though the rain had stopped, the fog hung heavy, and the mountain air remained cold. She pulled out a military coat and slipped it on. Warmth spread through her body instantly.

Sprig's tail had fluffed back to its former glory. It trailed right behind her boots. More than once, she accidentally stepped on its tail. It would puff up like an offended cat, squeal, and dart into a corner, staring at her with wounded eyes.

She coaxed it for a long time before it forgave her. Then, forgetting the pain, it followed her again and promptly got stepped on once more.

Breakfast was simple, boiled eggs and warmed milk. To avoid aggravating her injuries, she stayed away from spicy or acidic food.

By afternoon, the pain in her wounds had eased considerably, but the fog showed no sign of lifting.

The moment she stepped out of the cave, moisture clung to her lashes and hair. She held her breath and listened carefully to the forest.

Occasionally, birds fluttered off. Fallen leaves drifted down from the trees. She reached out and caught one midair.

The earth still carried a damp, slightly metallic scent from the rain, but it was not unpleasant.

Wiping the mist from her face, she limped back inside.

The trees in these mountains were thick and sturdy. Many had died during the extreme heat earlier in the year. Once she found a proper place to settle, she planned to cut down some of the deadwood and store it in her space.

After the apocalypse, extreme weather came without warning. If a brutal cold snap hit, she would need plenty of firewood prepared.

Two days passed.

She saw no signs of other survivors in the mountains.

Her heart gradually steadied.

Alone, she had complete control over food and supplies. Still, whenever she thought of Calvin and the others, a thread of worry tightened in her chest.

On the third day, the temperature dropped sharply.

A cold wind swept across her skin, raising goosebumps.

A bad feeling crept in.

She feared a sudden blizzard.

Quickly, she took out the thermometer.

Eleven degrees.

Her brows knit together as she glanced around the cramped cave.

If snow really came, she might have to relocate immediately.

Fortunately, her wounds barely hurt anymore. Her body seemed to be healing faster than before.

The fog grew thicker by the day.

On the fourth morning, after finishing breakfast, Evelyn did not hesitate any longer.

It was time to set out and find a more suitable place to stay.

She packed everything back into her storage space, pulled on her hat and gloves, grabbed her trekking pole, and set off again with Sprig in her arms.

After being carried for a while, the squirrel insisted on climbing down to walk on its own. Sometimes it leapt into the trees, digging through old pinecones. Most of the kernels were long gone, and it chattered angrily at the empty shells.

The forest floor beneath the leaves was slick with moss. The moment Evelyn stepped on it, her balance gave way and she crashed down. Her knee slammed into the ground. Thankfully, she reacted fast and lifted her fractured leg before it struck.

Seeing her fall, Sprig sprang down from the tree at once.

Evelyn braced herself with the trekking pole and pushed back to her feet, spitting out dirt and leaves. She nearly swore out loud.

Seriously?

Not even twenty minutes in, and she had already fallen.

She looked up at the fog drenched forest. Even her breath came out in white mist. Gritting her teeth, she pressed on.

After seven or eight grueling hours, she finally found a mountain hollow.

It lay between two peaks, wide and relatively flat. Fir trees filled the valley, and beside it stretched a patch of marshland.

Evelyn was immediately satisfied.

After resting for half an hour, she pulled out a chainsaw from her storage space, filled it with fuel, and began cutting down several fir trees to clear enough room for the RV. If she could park it here, she would have a proper place to live.

The roar of the chainsaw shattered the quiet. Birds burst from the treetops in alarm.

Sprig darted between the ground and branches, restless but curious. Evelyn paid it no mind and got to work.

For a moment, she felt like a budget version of a cartoon lumberjack, chainsaw roaring, snow flying everywhere. The only difference was she still had her hair. Hopefully she would not share his cursed luck. The last thing she wanted was to run into another bear.

After more than an hour, she had felled over a dozen fir trees. She used a hoe to clear stones and level the uneven ground.

Then she released Hayden's luxury RV from her storage space.

As for her own new RV, she was not about to risk it yet.

This Mercedes off road RV was high end, no doubt about that. Unfortunately, it was soaked in blood. The moment she opened the door, a thick metallic smell poured out.

Pulling on a mask, Evelyn decided to clean it thoroughly.

She stored the refrigerators and their contents away first, then took out a high pressure washer and sprayed down every surface inside.

She had no superstitions about it. Yes, people had died in there. But once it was clean, it was just a vehicle.

She washed the exterior too, even dismantling the large rooftop tent. As expected, it was far superior to hers. Wealth had its perks. With zero guilt, she claimed it for herself.

The RV would not be ready to sleep in tonight.

So she also brought out her small commuter car from storage and parked it nearby. She could squeeze into that for the night.

No more tents. Setting up and tearing down tents was exhausting.

Honestly, her luck had held. She had assumed it might take one or two days to find a suitable shelter. Instead, she found one in a single day.

Mountains at her back. Water at her side.

It was a good place.

Her biggest worry now was the coming cold.

If it snowed, how long would it last?

Two or three months?

Or two or three years?

Would she ever get out again?

Would she ever see Calvin and the others?

She cut off the thoughts.

No point dwelling on things she could not control.

After finishing the wash, she built a fire beside the RV and propped up an oversized umbrella above it. The temporary settlement was starting to take shape.

As soon as she sat down, she noticed fresh blood seeping through the bandage on her arm. Her fractured leg throbbed fiercely. She must have aggravated it while using the chainsaw.

With a quiet sigh, she reopened the medical kit and rebandaged everything.

At 7.30, it was fully dark.

She gave Sprig water and some beans. The little squirrel burrowed into her chest, ready to sleep.

Leaning back in her chair, Evelyn stroked its fur gently.

The fire burned bright.

Sleep crept over her.

Could the RV withstand a blizzard?

Could she, alone, build a shelter strong enough to block wind and snow?

She would need a fireplace and a heated brick bed.

Her thoughts blurred.

Before she realized it, she had fallen asleep.

Chapter 95 Want to Drink Blood?

The next morning, when Evelyn walked out of her room, she once again saw Rowan sitting by the fireplace.

He looked perfectly alert, with none of the exhaustion someone should have after staying up all night.

"Going hunting?" he asked.

Evelyn gave him a flat look. Absolutely not. She was on her period and in no shape to trek through snow and mountains.

"Not today."

"The injury hasn't healed yet?"

She nodded.

"When will it heal?"

"Two or three more days."

Rowan studied her thoughtfully. A chill crept up Evelyn's spine. Sure enough, the next second, he pulled out a knife.

"Want to drink blood?"

Evelyn almost dropped to her knees. This man was terrifyingly intense.

"No."

"You're really weak." His tone was calm, almost objective.

She glanced at his face and had the sudden urge to smack him a few hundred times.

"I took another look around last night," he continued. "There are still plenty of animals in this mountain range. Oh, and I found a hibernating giant python."

Wonderful. Just wonderful. "And why exactly did you go looking? Bored out of your mind?"

"They're food. Enough to keep me full."

Evelyn stared at him like he had lost it. "One mountain range. Eighteen peaks. All those animals are only enough for you? What are you, some kind of bottomless beast?"

"You can store a lot of things, right?" He had learned to dodge questions and change the subject.

She snorted. "What are you planning?"

"I store everything with you. We split it fifty fifty. Fair deal?"

It did sound reasonable. Still, there was no need for him to hunt this aggressively. The animals on Shattered Ridge had been hibernating. He had probably scared half of them into fleeing.

"Winter is the best time to hunt. Didn't you know that?"

After saying that, he pointed outside. "I already processed last night's catch. Go collect it."

Evelyn opened the door and stepped outside. Two sheep were buried in the snow. With a resigned sigh, she stored them away.

"Want to eat roasted whole lamb?" she asked casually.

He came out immediately. "Is it good?"

"Start a fire. We'll roast it in the yard."

The snow had stopped. Roasting outside meant they did not have to worry about smoke filling the cabin. Evelyn set up a rack, secured one of the lambs flat, and positioned it over the fire.

She moved the table and chairs outside. Sprig and Fallon ran out to join the commotion. Sitting by the fire, she slowly turned the lamb, waiting until it was nearly done before brushing on salt and chili powder.

"When can we eat?" Rowan asked.

"Right now," she snapped.

He rubbed his nose and fell silent.

He sat across from her, lifting Sprig onto his lap. Maybe it was the lingering scent of bloodshed on him, but Sprig seemed uneasy, staring at Evelyn with wide, pitiful eyes.

"Do you know what date it is?" he asked.

"January sixteenth."

He nodded. "It's been that long."

The roasted lamb began releasing a rich, mouthwatering aroma. Evelyn sprinkled on salt and brushed a layer of honey over the surface. The meat sizzled loudly over the flames, a sound that lifted anyone's spirits.

She set small dishes of seasoning on the table. When eating, they could slice off pieces of meat and roll them in the spices.

Once the lamb was perfectly roasted, she carried it to the table.

They could finally eat.

"Can we have this again tomorrow?" Rowan's eyes lit up after his first bite.

"You trying to work me to death?" Evelyn shot back.

"You can't die yet. All my food is stored with you." There was not a hint of humor in his voice.

He looked serious and composed, but underneath that calm exterior, he was completely unhinged. The day she watched him slaughter that pack of wolves, she had seen it clearly.

When he saw fresh blood, there had been excitement in his eyes. She had not imagined it.

"What if I take everything and run?" she asked.

He lifted his gaze to hers and gave a small shake of his head. "I'm not afraid. I can find you."

That made her pause.

"Do you know how I found you that day?" he continued. "I remember your scent. Everywhere you go, you leave it behind."

The piece of lamb in her hand nearly slipped from her fingers.

"Are you scared?" His eyes were calm, empty of joy, anger, or mercy.

"No."

"Then why did you throw me into that pit?"

She cleared her throat. "I was afraid you'd freeze to death. It was warmer down there."

He seemed to smile. Or maybe she imagined it.

"There's a mountain nearby covered in bones. The bodies have probably been there over a month."

Survivors from the earthquake, most likely. They had fled into the mountains and then died after the blizzard hit. Or maybe wild animals had gotten to them first.

"They didn't have your scent."

Evelyn said nothing and kept eating.

He consumed four fifths of the entire roasted lamb. He was thin, yet even after eating that much, his stomach remained flat.

Had genetic modification improved digestion too?

"The wind's picking up."

A freezing gust tore through the yard, lifting loose snow into the air. Evelyn scooped up Sprig and Fallon and hurried back into the cabin, leaving Rowan alone outside.

After that day, the blizzard intensified. Snow piled thick on the roof. Evelyn ordered Rowan to clear it. He bent his knees and leapt lightly onto the roof in a single jump.

Standing in the yard, she looked up at him and suddenly asked, "Does anyone else know you're this capable?"

He looked down at her, one brow lifting. "Everyone who knew is dead."

"What about me?"

"You?" He gave her a faint look. "Can you beat me? You're so weak."

Evelyn smiled. He was not wrong. She did not bother arguing.

"Will you ever leave the mountains?"

"What about you?" he countered.

"Maybe. If you leave, you'd better cover your eyes." It was genuine advice. She did not want him captured and executed again.

"Did I scare you?" After shoveling the last of the snow, he jumped down.

"No."

"You're warning me about people outside?"

"Something like that."

They were standing very close now. She could even see the fine hairs along his face.

"I think you should worry about yourself," he said, then walked off.

Serves him right if he gets captured and executed.

He deserves it.

"I mean I can protect myself. You, not so much."

He threw that in as a final jab.

Evelyn smiled sweetly. "You seem to have forgotten. Your food is still with me."

That finally shut him up.

The blizzard lasted three days. Another three feet of snow piled up. The pond had completely frozen over. For now, they had to rely on the water stored in her space.

Rowan seemed deeply concerned about running out of food. He went into the mountains every day to hunt. Whatever else could be said about him, since meeting him, the amount of meat in Evelyn's storage had multiplied several times over.

From pheasants to roe deer, wild boars to bison, there was nothing he could not kill.

Meanwhile, Evelyn's life had become almost leisurely. She stayed home crocheting sweaters, cooking, drinking tea, watching television, and playing with Sprig and Fallon.

Rowan even cleaned and processed all the prey he brought back. Aside from his sharp tongue and unstable tendencies, he was actually a decent tenant.

"Are there still animals out there?" she asked.

He nodded. "A lot. And this winter, many will freeze to death."

"Tomorrow, I'll go hunting with you."

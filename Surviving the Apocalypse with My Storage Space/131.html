Chapter 131 In the Wild, Cooked Food

Evelyn didn’t believe his nonsense. He ate more than anyone else, with a stomach that seemed bottomless, never satisfied.

Being away from the base, her mood instantly lightened.

She let Pip and Lark run freely, enjoying the rare taste of freedom.

Evelyn set up a large parasol and arranged a table and chairs. She brought out the fruit from the storage space and instructed Adrian to wash it at the stream, while she sat back to rest.

“Adrian, build a fire.”
“Adrian, boil some water.”
“Adrian, make some tea.”
“Adrian…”

She commanded with ease, and Adrian rushed around attending to her every request.

After enough rest, Evelyn set about working. She explored the surrounding hills, gathering a large bag of wild vegetables and catching a rabbit along the way.

“Tonight, we’ll have spicy rabbit.”

“There are fish in the stream,” Adrian said, using small stones to catch four or five quickly.

“The mountains are wonderful. You want something, you have it.”

They cooked dinner together and spent the night in the RV. The vehicle was large, with four beds inside. Evelyn folded up two, giving them much more space.

Adrian didn’t seem inclined to rest. Bow in hand, he returned to the hills. Evelyn ignored him; after the tense days at the base, she was determined to catch up on sleep this time.

A tablet connected to a small speaker played music while she applied a face mask.

Pip and Lark had followed Adrian, leaving the stream silent except for the gentle wind, her footsteps, and the music.

By midnight, Evelyn was asleep. Adrian returned with the hunted prey, with Pip and Lark trailing behind.

At the RV, Adrian cleaned the animals at the stream. It had been a fruitful night. Only then did he return to wash up and sleep.

The next morning, Evelyn woke to find the prey hanging outside. She quickly stored them to prevent the sun from spoiling the meat.

Today she planned to make cooked dishes. The storage still had many lunchboxes, and she intended to fill them all.

Adrian had slept only two hours after returning last night. At dawn, he was already back in the hills, overflowing with energy in a way that made Evelyn envious.

Although she had argued with him yesterday, she remembered the dishes he had mentioned. Following his tastes, she brought a basket of fish caught in Silvermere River to the stream and prepared to make fish balls.

Just as she carried the basket over, Adrian returned, this time with a string of rabbits. Evelyn stared in surprise.

“You raided their burrow?”

“No, only ten.”

“That’s enough. You won’t need to hunt for the next few days. Help me instead.”

Adrian set down the rabbits and helped her clean the fish.

Evelyn returned to the RV and brought out a large pot and a steamer to cook rice. At the base, she hadn’t been able to cook freely. Here, with space and time, she could.

She pulled out vegetables—potatoes, eggplants, peppers, onions—from the storage. Cooking was a pleasure, both for the food and for calming her mind.

Once the dishes were ready, Adrian packed them into boxes, and Evelyn stored them in the space. They had developed a silent understanding; they knew what the other was doing without speaking.

The sun shone brightly. Evelyn pulled out all the black bear meat from storage, planning to make jerky. Luckily, she had plenty of chili powder and salt—she could never run out.

She also pickled the white radishes and cleaned a sack of small hot peppers, chopping them finely.

“I’m going to make chili paste, then prepare fish head with chopped chili,” she said.

Adrian immediately got to work.

They spent seven days at the stream. Adrian had hunted most of the local game, and they were ready to head further south.

The storage now held enough cooked food to last them two years. Evelyn drove while keeping an eye out for obstacles; occasionally, Adrian had to clear stones from the path.

When they arrived at Stag’s Hollow, Evelyn’s face lit up.

“There are so many rare herbs here. Adrian, let’s stay a few days. I want to dig up everything.”

With a hoe in hand, she ran into the hills, while Adrian stayed behind to set up camp.

“There’s Gastrodia. Jackpot.”

Evelyn hadn’t expected such a windfall. She now truly believed Officer Shaw’s saying that fortune always finds a way.

“And honeysuckle too.”

She spent five or six hours in the hills, not intending to stop. As night fell, she strapped a headlamp and continued digging.

“You’re not hungry?” Adrian asked, finding her kneeling in the dirt, carefully tending to a herb.

“I’m fine. Make yourself something if you’re hungry. I won’t sleep tonight—I have to get them all.”

“The herbs aren’t going anywhere. Why the rush?”

“I won’t sleep anyway when I get back,” she said, excitement written all over her face—a look Adrian had never seen.

He watched her for a while, then knelt beside her, helping dig.

Later, Adrian removed his coloured contact lenses. In the dark, the headlamp illuminated his face, and his heterochromatic eyes gleamed like a cat’s.

“I’ll help you,” he said.

He took the hoe, and she directed him where to dig. Like mischievous thieves, they worked through the night.

“The variety is incredible. And we dug hundreds of kilograms in a single night. Amazing.”

Evelyn processed the herbs while Adrian washed them by the stream.

“You really love this, don’t you?” he asked.

“Absolutely.”

Adrian nodded. “I understand.”

A few hours later, he returned with a sack, placing it before her. She opened it to find Dendrobium, Coptis, and small ginseng roots still in soil.

“You’re like Doraemon,” she said.

Adrian raised an eyebrow. “So, can you rest now?”

Stretching with satisfaction, Evelyn sighed.

“Yes. I’m going to sleep now.”

After storing the herbs, she lightly patted Adrian on the shoulder.

“You’re incredible,” she said and walked away, not noticing his flushed ears and neck.

They stayed at Stag’s Hollow for five more days, digging up all the herbs. Only then did Evelyn decide to leave.

But as they reached an abandoned town, a sudden downpour forced them to set up camp temporarily.

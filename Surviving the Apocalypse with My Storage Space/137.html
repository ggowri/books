Chapter 137 Turning the Tables

That very night, one of the neighbouring buildings suddenly collapsed.

The base had been so badly corroded that everything below the ground floor had hollowed out. Hundreds of people had been living inside. By morning, every last one of them was dead. Not a single person was pulled out alive.

The residents in the high-rises were terrified. They started moving to lower ground at once. Evelyn decided to relocate to the Manor District. Once she mentioned it to Calvin and the others, they all agreed. Elliot and Clara moved with them as well.

There were plenty of houses in the Manor District, but by the time they arrived, most had already been taken. Calvin found an empty villa right at the edge. All the doors and windows had been stripped out, though being on the end suited them well enough. It was quieter, and safer too.

There were nine of them in total, but only four bedrooms. Calvin shared one with Wendy. Elliot and Clara took another. Ray and Evan shared the third. That left one room for Evelyn and Adrian. As before, Evelyn slept on the bed, while Adrian took the floor. Eddie had no choice but to squeeze in with Ray and Evan.

Calvin took Eddie and the others off to search the various buildings in the base for usable doors and windows. Evelyn, Adrian, Elliot, and Clara went to the Underground Quarter to dig up soil. They planned to grow vegetables inside the villa.

Once the doors and windows were in place, they would still need firewood.

A week later, two thirds of the survivors had already left the base. Those who remained were still fighting over soil and scrap wood.

Calvin had considered leaving too, but Evelyn’s instincts told her this was not the right moment. Since she was staying, the others stayed as well.

That night, Evelyn and Adrian went to the Administration Hall again. They had only meant to look for wooden boards to burn, but on the top floor they stumbled across something astonishing.

Three middle-aged men had been tied up and dumped inside one of the offices. The moment Evelyn saw the locked door, she knew something was wrong. Before going in, she checked the plaque outside.

It read Mayor Whitmore.

The three men were barely clinging to life. By the time Evelyn and Adrian found them, one of them looked as though he was moments from death.

Their mouths had been sealed with black tape. Thick rope bound their bodies in hard knots.

Evelyn stepped forward and tore the tape from one man’s mouth. He gulped down air in ragged breaths and looked at her with desperate, pleading eyes.

“Please,” he rasped. “You have to save Mayor Whitmore and Secretary Bennett. I’m Nathan Collins, from the base leadership. The Hawthornes and the Burtons joined forces and turned on us. There was a traitor among us. They took all the supplies. Our people were all killed by them.”

Evelyn glanced at Adrian, then bent to untie the ropes binding the three men.

Nathan’s wrists and ankles were rubbed raw, blistered and bleeding. He was filthy. After days without food or water, his clothes were stained with dirt and grime. Evelyn frowned as she looked at him.

“You’re saying the Hawthornes and the Burtons took everything. If that’s true, why didn’t they kill you too? Why leave you here instead?”

A flash of bitterness crossed Nathan’s face. “They abducted Professor Whitaker. Patriarch Burton forced the professor’s son to kill us, but the lad couldn’t do it. In the end, they just locked us in here and left us to die.”

At the mention of the name, Nathan’s hands began to shake.

“What’s happening outside?” he asked.

“Everyone thinks the authorities ran off with the supplies,” Evelyn said. “At first people were furious. Devastated too. A lot of them killed themselves. Others followed the lorry tracks and left. A few days later, about two thirds of the survivors were gone. The only ones still here are those too old, too weak, or too ill to travel.”

By the time she finished, Nathan’s eyes had turned red.

Mayor Whitmore and Secretary Bennett were still unconscious. Evelyn pulled a cup from her pocket. It held some medicinal brew she had prepared earlier. After giving both men a drink, she and Adrian made to leave, but Nathan stopped them.

“Miss, since you’ve already helped us this far, help us all the way. Save our lives, and I can give you something in return.”

Evelyn studied him coolly. “I thought you said they took everything.”

Nathan gave a dry laugh. “A clever rabbit always keeps more than one burrow. If we hadn’t left ourselves a way out, we’d never have lived this long.”

Evelyn rather liked the answer. No arrogance, no airs, and at least he was honest.

“What do you have?”

“Anything but medicine.”

One brow lifted. “Any planes? Lorries?”

Nathan clutched his chest and broke into a cough. When it passed, he shook his head with difficulty. “No. Just some water. And some grain.”

Evelyn snapped her fingers. “Fine. We’ll save you. But if you go back on your word, I won’t be polite about it.”

She drew out a knife and turned it idly in her hand. Nathan gave her a weary, bitter smile and nodded.

“I won’t break my promise. Save those two, and the supplies are yours.”

Evelyn looked at Adrian. He understood immediately. He hoisted one man over each shoulder, while Evelyn helped Nathan downstairs.

Leader or not, if you wanted to live, you needed something worth bargaining with.

Once they got the three men back to the villa, they laid them out in the sitting room. Evelyn went upstairs and fetched some herbs she had dug up at Stag’s Hollow. After Calvin and Eddie heard the whole story, they helped treat the men’s wounds and find them clean clothes.

The three of them had clearly been beaten before they were tied up. Their injuries were serious.

Evelyn looked them over and found herself genuinely impressed by their stamina. They were all men in their forties or fifties, yet after so many days without food or water, they were somehow still alive.

“So you really were the leaders of the base?” Eddie asked again, still wanting to be certain. “You really didn’t abandon everyone and run?”

Gritting his teeth against the pain, Nathan explained it all once more.

Eddie was so furious he looked ready to explode. “Typical old-money families. Scheming, vicious, not a shred of humanity.”

Nathan could only smile bitterly. At least Mayor Whitmore and Secretary Bennett were still alive.

Evelyn forced more medicine into them and tended their wounds. The two men woke once, only to slip back into unconsciousness soon after.

After Elliot and Clara joined the group, they took charge of gathering firewood with everyone else. Both of them were diligent, constantly coming and going with armfuls of timber.

At six in the morning, Nathan finally gave in to exhaustion and fell asleep.

Not long after, Mayor Whitmore woke.

Evelyn had always assumed she would never come into contact with someone at his level. Instead, not only had she met him, she had seen him at his lowest.

“How many days has it been since the acid rain ended?” he asked.

“The ninth day.”

He nodded slowly. “Then we won’t catch them now.”

Evelyn cleared her throat. “Now that you’re awake, I should explain something. Nathan made a deal with me. If we saved you, we’d get water and grain in return. You’re the top man here, so you’re not going to back out, are you?”

Whitmore shook his head. “No. And thank you. I never thought we would still be alive to be rescued. I had already given up hope. Miss, don’t call me a leader. At this point, I’m nothing at all.”

Evelyn smiled, but said nothing.

There were no spare rooms left in the villa, and all the surrounding houses had already been taken by other survivors, so Nathan and the other two had no choice but to sleep on makeshift bedding in the sitting room.

Three days later, their injuries had improved enough that they could walk about again.

“Mr. Collins,” Evelyn said, “where are the supplies you promised?”

By now Nathan had grown familiar enough with her that, seeing how bluntly she chased him for the goods, he laughed despite himself.

“I haven’t forgotten. I’ll take you tonight.”

Chapter 149 Ant Swarms

Once she had finished with everything at home, Evelyn went to see Calvin and passed on Nathaniel’s words exactly as he had said them.

Calvin was left speechless, though he had no intention of pursuing the matter any further.

For a whole month, not a drop of rain fell. Before long, the Safe Zone was in the grip of a water shortage. Evelyn looked up at the darkened sky and could not shake the feeling that something else was coming.

The chickens in the shed clucked without pause, setting everyone’s teeth on edge. She noticed several people heading towards the villa where Mayor Whitmore and the others lived. No doubt they were going to ask for help.

Fortunately, Whitmore had always been the sort to think ahead. Whenever it rained, he made sure everyone stored as much water as possible. Once filtered, rainwater kept well enough if it was sealed in earthen jars.

Half a year earlier, the Safe Zone had built a small kiln and fired a batch of clay jars specifically for storing filtered rainwater.

Evelyn was in the middle of a game of five in a row with Adrian when Clara called to her from outside. When she stepped out, she saw Clara standing in the haze with Archie strapped to her back.

Archie was now fourteen months old and had already begun saying a few words. Evelyn reached out and pinched his little cheek.

“Evelyn, I found a lot of yellow ants in the vegetable patch just now.”

“Yellow ants?”

“Yes. They’re down by the roots. They’ve chewed them right through.”

“I’ll come and have a look.”

The two of them went to Clara’s place. As soon as Evelyn arrived, she saw piles of uprooted vegetables by the door. Their roots had been hollowed out, the leaves had already gone yellow, and the ground was crawling with tiny yellow ants.

“Did they only appear today?”

“I’m not sure. I haven’t been in the vegetable patch much these last two days. I came out just now to pick some corn and found the stalks were hollowed out at the base as well.”

“We should be able to get rid of them. I’ll go back and mix up something for you. Spray it over the soil.”

“Right. Thanks.”

On the way home, Evelyn spotted dense trails of black ants as well. They seemed to be moving house, filing out of one pit in an orderly line and disappearing into cracks at the base of the villa walls.

And it was not just a one-off. In the space of a hundred metres, she saw four or five separate columns on the move.

Ordinarily, ants moved like that before rain.

Back at home, Evelyn checked her own vegetable patch and, sure enough, found more than a dozen nests of yellow ants.

She pulled up every herb and vegetable in the soil, then hurriedly fetched some insecticide and sprayed it over the patch. But after nearly half a bottle, the yellow ants showed no reaction at all. She rushed upstairs, took out several medicinal ingredients, and began making an insecticidal powder by hand.

“What are you doing?”

Hearing the noise, Adrian came out of the room. When he saw her sorting through herbs, he frowned.

“There are ant swarms in the vegetable patch. I tried insecticide, but it won’t kill them, so I’m making poison powder instead. Also, there are black ants all over the road outside, moving in columns.”

Adrian went downstairs, scooped up a shovelful of soil, and stared at the yellow ants wriggling through it. After a few seconds, he went straight outside again.

“There may be a downpour coming.”

Evelyn sighed. “I was thinking the same. We need water, but if a storm like that hits, a lot of these half-ruined houses will collapse the moment floodwater rushes through.”

Adrian, however, was thinking along a different line entirely. “We need to kill the chickens in the shed now, then clear the soil off the ground floor and lay down stone or planks. If water gets into the house, the whole ground floor will turn into mud.”

Evelyn nodded. “All right. But I need to finish the poison powder first and take some to Clara and the others. If we don’t kill these things now, they could breed out of control and turn the whole Safe Zone into one giant ant colony.”

She already had a horror of dense swarms. The thought alone made the hairs rise on her arms.

Evelyn mixed up a whole bucket of poison powder. After pulling on gloves and a mask, she went out to treat the patch. The powder was strong enough that within minutes of scattering it, every yellow ant still moving in the soil had died.

She kept some back at home and carried the rest out to distribute.

After giving portions to Clara, Eddie, Calvin and the others, she took what remained to Whitmore’s villa and told them everything at once, both about the yellow ants and the black ants moving house.

“Adrian and I both think there’s going to be a downpour. Once you’ve spread the powder, you’d better warn everyone to prepare.”

Whitmore did not dare take it lightly. He went straight to inspect the vegetable plots and found the ground exactly as Evelyn had said, full of yellow ants. They were tiny, barely two millimetres long, and very easy to miss unless you were looking for them. When he saw the roots that had been chewed through, his face darkened at once.

“Nathan, hand out the rest of the powder. Secretary Bennett, go and warn everyone. Tell them to get ready. A storm may be coming.”

Once she had delivered the warning, Evelyn went home and began clearing the soil from the ground floor. Then she and Adrian hauled in stones from outside to level the floor.

By then Adrian had already slaughtered every chicken in the shed. The two of them worked through the night without stopping until everything was in order. Evelyn soaked the feathers in water, then spread them out on the balcony to dry once they were clean.

To save water, most of the others skipped the washing altogether. Once the feathers were dry, they kept the down and stuffed it into the lining of their clothes for warmth.

When word spread through the Safe Zone that a downpour might be coming, people reacted as though the sky itself were falling in. Every disaster had begun with heavy rain. No one wanted to remember those days.

Now they were being told another storm might be on the way. Some people could not take it and broke down crying on the spot.

Whitmore personally went round inspecting everyone’s houses. Some looked as though they might collapse and needed to be evacuated in advance. A few residents refused to move no matter how much he tried to persuade them, and in the end he could only leave with a helpless shake of the head.

Eddie and Calvin went out and dug up several more cartloads of coal. When Whitmore said the poison powder was not enough, Evelyn made another half bucket.

Meanwhile, the black ant swarms outside multiplied by the hour. They covered walls, filled cracks, and poured into every hollow left behind by the acid rain. Dense, black, ceaseless, they seemed to be everywhere.

Calvin and the others followed Evelyn and Adrian’s example and filled in their vegetable plots as well. But no one could bear to slaughter all the chickens they had worked so hard to raise.

The moment Evelyn stepped outside with her medical case, the haze caught in her throat and made her sneeze.

She wiped at her face and found not only moisture from the fog, but a fine layer of dust and grit as well.

Somewhere not far off, she could hear voices complaining and swearing. Since the start of the disasters, that muttering resentment had never really gone away.

They had only just managed to scrape together a few days of stability, enough food to fill their stomachs and somewhere to sleep. And now another calamity loomed.

For everyone in the Safe Zone, it was not only physical torment. It wore at the mind as well.

They lived in constant dread, day and night, always afraid that at any moment something else might happen.

Evelyn covered her mouth and coughed a few times, trying to keep it quiet. The young girl walking ahead of her turned back with obvious guilt in her eyes.

“Doctor Evelyn, I’m sorry to trouble you.”

Evelyn shook her head. Though she was wearing a mask, she still did not dare speak. The moment she opened her mouth, it felt as though the toxic haze would pour straight in.

When they reached the Settlement Market, she saw the patients she had come to treat. They were among the group who had fled from Langford, and now they were all crowded together on the third floor of the market building.

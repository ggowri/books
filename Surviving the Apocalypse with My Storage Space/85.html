Chapter 85 Surviving Alone: Part 9

Evelyn hummed to herself as she cooked.

Entertaining herself like this was not so bad. She did not feel lonely at all. If anything, she felt completely free, completely at ease.

"Whew. Let me taste it. It's hot!" She quickly gulped down a mouthful of cold water to cool her burning tongue. Serves me right for being impatient.

"It's delicious. Seriously amazing. Chef Evelyn just mastered another famous dish. I'm incredible."

After a careful taste test confirmed the flavor was spot on, she proudly carried the pot over to the fireplace. Time to continue the show she had started yesterday.

"No idea how long this snow is going to last. Let's check today's temperature."

She grabbed her thermometer and stepped outside. The red line had dropped to negative eight degrees.

"It was only negative five last night. Now it's already negative eight this afternoon. What is wrong with this weather?"

Shivering, she hurried back into the cabin with the thermometer in hand. Food came first. No matter what happened, eating was the top priority.

After dinner, she went back to crocheting her sweater. Technically, she had more than enough clothes stored in her space to last a lifetime. Even so, she liked staying busy. Especially in the cold. Low temperatures made her mind sluggish and her body slow.

That was exactly when she needed to move more, to keep her hands and brain active.

When you lived alone, you had to talk to yourself sometimes. It kept your mind sharp. Dementia was not just something old people got. Young people were not immune either.

She even had a Rubik's Cube stored away. Honestly, it was one of the best tools for training logic and adaptability.

After two more hours of crocheting that evening, she had finished shaping the shoulders. Evelyn stood up and stretched her arms and legs, then checked the time. Only seven thirty.

The snow outside had piled up thick and solid. It had compacted under its own weight. When she stepped on it now, she barely sank. In a while, she could probably glide across it on a board.

Every day, Evelyn practiced slow martial arts forms outdoors. Sprig and Fallon had no idea what she was doing, but whenever she exercised, they always stayed nearby to watch.

She still had not explored the surrounding mountains. Thinking about the possibility of finding more pheasants in the snow, she decided to head out the next day for a proper look around.

The following morning, she slung her crossbow over her shoulder, settled Sprig and Fallon inside, locked the door, and set off.

Left peak, then.

Staring at the dense fir forest ahead, she had a feeling today would be a good day.

The snow crunched beneath her boots. It was pristine. When she glanced back, the entire forest was untouched except for her single line of footprints.

After walking for about twenty minutes, she stopped to rest. Pulling down her mask, she exhaled clouds of white mist. From her coat pocket, she took out a small bottle filled with vodka. One sip sent warmth spreading from her chest to her fingertips.

She twisted off the cap and took a drink. A faint flush bloomed across her cheeks.

After a few minutes' rest, she continued deeper into the mountain. Not long after entering the peak, she spotted animal tracks and droppings scattered across the snow. Plenty of creatures had not gone into hibernation.

Just a few minutes in, she found a pheasant lying beneath a tree.

She crouched down and gently patted its head. Its eyelids twitched. It was still alive. Looks like it froze stiff again.

Evelyn pulled out a foam cooler. After knocking the pheasant dead, she placed it inside. Only lifeless things could be stored in her space, so she had no choice but to finish it off first.

Gripping her trekking pole, she continued searching the area.

If only she had a hunting dog. With a sharp nose, it could have helped her track down pheasants in no time.

The fir trees on this mountain were thick and sturdy. She could just barely wrap her arms around their trunks.

"Oh wow, there's another one."

She rushed over and scooped up a brightly colored pheasant from the snow.

This one seemed to have already died. There was still a faint trace of warmth in its body, which meant it had not been long.

The fallen branches nearby were crusted with ice. Evelyn picked up a shard and examined it for a moment. Clear and glittering, it was almost beautiful.

"Oh ho, this feels amazing."

Alone in the forest, she ran wild for a bit, laughing to herself as she stomped across the endless white snow, leaving footprints everywhere.

"Let's make a rabbit."

She began hopping through the snow, deliberately shaping her footprints to look like rabbit tracks.

After bouncing along for dozens of meters and creating a whole trail of fake rabbit prints, she pulled out her phone. It took nearly two minutes just to power on.

She snapped a photo of the snowy tracks, then switched to the front camera and took several selfies.

Once she finished, she powered the phone off again and continued forward with her trekking pole, scanning for more pheasants.

Winter was not an easy time for hunting. She had no intention of actively tracking prey anyway. Picking up the occasional frozen straggler was more than enough to keep her fed.

As she ventured deeper into the mountain, she spotted a massive cave halfway up the slope. Calling it a cave did not feel quite right. It looked more like a karst cavern. Even at the entrance, she caught a sharp, unpleasant smell. The ground was slick and damp. One wrong step and she could slip straight inside. She did not dare rush in.

"Hey!"

She shouted into the darkness. It took a long time before an echo rolled back, thick and muffled.

"Looks like this cavern is huge."

She had seen news reports before about adventure streamers who went missing in Shattered Ridge. Staring at the cavern entrance now, Evelyn lowered herself to the ground and began inching forward on her stomach. She pulled out a flashlight to see how deep it went.

The beam was powerful. In the distance, she spotted pale stone pillars. Stalactites.

Those were typically found in karst landscapes. How could Shattered Ridge have something like this?

She did not dwell on it. The stretch just inside the entrance was slippery but relatively flat and wide. As long as she crouched and moved slowly, she would not fall.

She switched to a headlamp she could strap to her forehead and stored her crossbow in her space. She was going in for a closer look.

Just in case, she swallowed a few antibiotic pills.

"Could this be an ancient tomb? What if there's gold and treasure inside? Haha…"

The more she thought about it, the more excited she became. Maybe she would trigger some kind of golden cheat ability. That was how it always worked in TV dramas, right?

A lone hero stumbles into a hidden paradise, finds a secret martial arts manual, and becomes an unbeatable master.

The ground felt sticky beneath her gloves. White crystalline residue clung to the fabric, looking almost like coarse salt. She crouched and slowly edged forward. If those formations really were stalactites, she might have struck it rich. Stalactites were even used in traditional medicine.

Drops of water fell from above. Evelyn tilted her head up.

Rows of milky stone hung down from the ceiling.

Her breath caught in her throat.

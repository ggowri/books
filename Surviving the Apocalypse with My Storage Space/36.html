Chapter 36 Flooding, Heatwave: Part 2

Evelyn's words stunned all three of them into silence.

Judging by the expressions on Margaret and Natalie's faces, they clearly had no idea about this either.

"Dr. Foster, are you sure?" Margaret asked in disbelief. "I'm really pregnant? Four months already? But I never felt anything. My belly didn't even grow."

"I wouldn't misdiagnose this," Evelyn replied calmly. "As for why your belly didn't grow, it could be due to your constitution, or it could be severe malnutrition."

Margaret touched her stomach, her expression dazed. "No wonder. I haven't had my period for so long. I thought it was just because I'm getting older."

"Mom, let it be removed," Natalie said urgently, gripping her mother's hand. "It's already dead. Once it's taken out, it won't hurt anymore."

Margaret looked at Evelyn, grief and pleading mixed in her eyes. "Evie, help me get it out. It hurts so much. I'm begging you."

"Your condition is very dangerous," Evelyn said flatly. "Delivering a stillbirth is complicated. If something happens to you, it has nothing to do with me."

"What do you mean by that?" Natalie snapped, anger and panic colliding as she confronted Evelyn. "At a time like this, how can you say something like that? You're a doctor."

Evelyn smiled faintly, her attitude unchanged. "I'm a doctor, not a saint. And definitely not a god. I can save people, but the rules still apply. Treatment in exchange for supplies."

"You're cold blooded."

"I'm only cold blooded," Evelyn replied coolly. "You're sick in the head."

She turned to look at Claire, her expression indifferent.

"I'm going home. If there's nothing important, don't bother me again."

Claire was startled by Evelyn's sudden coldness. Seeing that Evelyn was really about to leave, she hurried forward to stop her.

"Evelyn, don't be angry. Natalie is still a kid. With Margaret in this condition, she was just too anxious. Don't take it personally. How about this? You treat Margaret, and I'll pay the supplies for her."

Evelyn raised an eyebrow, her smile unreadable. "And how much are you planning to give me? What's inside her stomach has to be taken out bit by bit."

Claire hesitated. She glanced at Natalie and Margaret, but neither of them looked at her or said a word. A trace of discomfort rose in her chest. She wasn't asking for gratitude, but their attitude still made her feel hurt and chilled.

"I'll give you five cans."

Evelyn nodded without hesitation. "Deal."

If someone was willing to play the fool, she wasn't going to turn down five cans for free. Evelyn opened her case and took out the silver needles inside.

She inserted the needles into Margaret's abdomen. Almost instantly, the pain eased, and Margaret's tense expression relaxed.

Claire pulled Natalie aside and whispered something to her. Natalie looked at Evelyn's busy back, her expression conflicted.

"Natalie," Claire said softly, "Evelyn is a good person. She just keeps personal matters separate from business, and when she saves people, she never hesitates. No matter how anxious you were just now, you shouldn't have spoken to her like that."

Natalie kept picking at her fingers and said nothing.

"Natalie," Claire continued, "I still have a camping tent at home. I can lend it to you. Once your mom feels a little better, you two can move up to the rooftop."

Natalie's head snapped up. Shock filled her eyes.

"You want to drive us out too?" she cried. "Ms. Brooks, my mom and I are both weak women. How are we supposed to live on the rooftop? There are still burn marks from the bodies up there. And what if it rains again? What are we supposed to do then?

"Is it because of what I said to Dr. Foster earlier? Did that make you angry, so now you want to kick us out? I can apologize. I can kneel and apologize to her. Please don't make us leave. I'm begging you."

Natalie dropped straight to her knees.

Claire jumped in fright and hurried to pull her up. "Don't do that. Alright, alright. Go apologize to Evelyn, and I'll let you stay a little longer."

Natalie immediately burst into tears of relief and smiled. "Thank you, Ms. Brooks. You're such a good person. Dr. Foster is a good person too. Don't worry, I won't talk nonsense ever again."

Claire patted her on the shoulder. When Natalie lowered her head, the smile vanished from her face.

A few minutes after the silver needles were removed, Margaret's stomach began to cramp violently again. Sweat poured down her face as the veins in her neck bulged.

"When you feel a strong downward pressure, push hard and expel everything," Evelyn said calmly. "That's all. I'm going home now."

She had no intention of staying to endure the smell. Claire went back to her room and brought out five cans. Evelyn accepted them without hesitation.

Natalie walked over to Evelyn, bowed deeply, then apologized solemnly. Evelyn gave Claire a look that was hard to read and did not pursue the matter further.

Let go of the urge to save everyone. Respect other people's fate.

Back home, Evelyn took off her mask and gloves and washed her hands over and over again.

The cans were braised pork from the food factory she had looted with Claire before. Evelyn had eaten them twice already. They tasted good, perfect over rice or with steamed buns.

She took out two buns, opened a can, and that was her breakfast.

It took Margaret an entire day to expel everything from her body. That evening, Claire and Natalie came upstairs together to ask Evelyn to check on her again. What Evelyn did not expect was that they had actually kept what had been expelled.

She swallowed the urge to curse. After checking Margaret and finding nothing wrong, she inserted a few more needles.

"Dr. Foster," Natalie asked cautiously, "can we throw these things away now?"

Evelyn looked at Natalie for a long moment, truly studying the girl for the first time.

Claire said she was still a child. Fifteen years old, only four years younger than Evelyn. Was she really still a child?

There was nothing visibly wrong on Natalie's face, but Evelyn's instincts told her this was deliberate. She had kept those things on purpose, just to disgust her.

"If you want to keep them as a souvenir, that's fine," Evelyn said lightly. "After all, that was your little brother or sister."

With that, she picked up her needle case and left Claire's apartment.

Once home, Evelyn did not hesitate. She boiled hot water and scrubbed herself clean from head to toe.

Late at night, Evelyn lay on her bed, wearing earphones and watching a downloaded movie on her tablet.

It was a romance film. The male and female leads were childhood sweethearts who went through countless hardships and misunderstandings. In the end, they married other people. Decades later, after both their spouses died, they reunited in their seventies or eighties, cleared up the misunderstandings, and finally got back together. The movie ended with two white haired elders holding a wedding, confessing their love, declaring each other their true love, while everyone present cried their hearts out.

Evelyn shut off the tablet with a blank expression. Somehow, her mood felt even worse.

Did people not have mouths when they were young? Could they not just talk when misunderstandings happened? If it was true love, why drag innocent people into it? The ones who got hurt died early, while the two shameless idiots lived long lives.

What a brain-dead plot. Absolutely stupid.

Evelyn tossed and turned, unable to sleep. Instead, she worked herself into hunger. She took out a box of chopped chili fish head, a box of spicy crayfish, opened a bottle of cola, and switched to an animated show.

After one episode, full and satisfied, her mood finally recovered.

Chapter 110 Reporting In

After they had eaten and drunk their fill, Evelyn went with Adrian to Flat 602 first and helped him bring out all his things. She even gave him an electric blanket.

“Keep a barrel of water here. The water in the bathroom is only good for flushing the loo. You can’t drink it.”

Adrian had no objections. Evelyn stood to one side with her arms folded, watching him sort through his things, make the bed, and fold his clothes. To her surprise, he was remarkably tidy. Even the quilt ended up folded into a neat square.

“Give me a few more books.”

Seeing how much he liked reading, Evelyn handed over several more.

Back in 601, she began putting her own room in order. She laid down a thick sleeping mat and an electric blanket, then placed Pip and Lark’s bed at the foot of hers.

She brought out a desk, set her three pots of flowers on top of it, and added a lamp.

Then she took out a water filtration unit. She meant to filter the bathroom water and see whether, once treated, it might at least be safe enough for washing clothes and bathing.

There was still plenty of water in her storage space, but saving what she could was simply common sense.

The lock would need changing too. She had only glanced at it earlier, but that sort of lock would take a skilled hand all of three seconds to pick. It offered no real security at all.

Evelyn stared at the filtered water. It looked perfectly clear, and the smell of bleach had faded to something faint, but she had no testing kit and did not dare drink it just on trust. Eddie had said that plenty of people in the base had already developed dental problems after drinking groundwater.

She decided to use the filtered water on the cactus instead. If, after three days, the plant showed no sign of damage, then the water was probably clean enough.

Once the room was sorted, she replaced the lock and decided to wash up herself.

It was six in the evening now. Before long, the residents of the base would be finishing work and coming back.

Evelyn had no intention of installing a water heater. There were too many variables in this place, too many things beyond her control. One careless move could cause endless trouble.

She took out a heater, boiled the filtered water from the bath barrel, and made do with that for a quick wash.

Her hair had grown quite long. Standing before the mirror with a pair of scissors, she trimmed it down again until her old clean, sharp short style returned.

A knock came at the door. Adrian had come over. When he saw her new haircut, he actually paused.

“Don’t tell me you can’t recognise me.”

“I can.”

A smile tugged at Evelyn’s lips. “So what do you want?”

“I’m taking the rubbish out. I can do yours as well.”

She glanced at the bag in his hand. It was full of worn-out clothes and shoes he had changed out of.

Then she noticed he had put on a clean set of clothes and clicked her tongue.

“You do scrub up well enough. But you’d better cover that face of yours. Pretty boys don’t have an easy time of it.”

Adrian quietly took out a mask and put it on. Evelyn thought he was missing the point, so she fetched a black winter cap and tossed it at him.

“When I say cover yourself, I mean properly. Do you call that covered?”

Only when he had the hat on as well, with most of his face hidden, did she hand over her own rubbish bag.

“Oh, and later I’ll give you a filtration unit and a heater. You can’t bathe in that water as it is.”

After Adrian went off to throw the rubbish away, Evelyn checked the window. It was small and flimsy. She was the sort of person who never truly felt safe, and anywhere she lived had to be reinforced until there was not a single weakness left, not one blind spot.

She had glass in storage, but nothing for cutting it.

Wait.

Diamond could score glass. She remembered gathering quite a lot of jewellery when she had been collecting supplies before.

By the time Adrian came back, he found her lying on the floor, struggling to cut a sheet of glass with a diamond.

He watched for a few seconds, then walked over without a word and took the diamond from her hand.

Evelyn watched him draw it lightly across the pane. The glass split cleanly in two.

“Do you want me to praise you?”

Adrian nodded. “Shouldn’t you?”

Evelyn gave a little snort. “Very impressive.”

Once the glass had been replaced, she finally felt at ease.

She handed him the filtration unit and the heater, then locked her own door from the inside and got ready for bed.

Before sleeping, she took out the paper she had brought back from the Administration Hall and looked over it. The rules for earning points were stricter than she had expected.

In the hunting crews, killing a large animal such as a bison earned eight hundred points if it was brought back alive, seven hundred if dead, to be divided equally among the team.

Smaller animals, such as pheasants, were worth thirty points alive and twenty-five dead.

The Medical Division was split into drug research, herbal cultivation, surgery, internal medicine, gynaecology, and more.

Doctors who accompanied teams outside the base received an additional fifty to one hundred points per mission, depending on their speciality.

Reporting time tomorrow was ten o’clock. Evelyn studied the route map once and committed it to memory almost at once.

She had hated the idea of the base at first, but life rarely cared for anyone’s plans. Now that she was here, she would have to adapt, rather than spend every hour in fear and wariness.

Of course, caution would always remain her rule for survival.

Knowing that Calvin and the others were all still alive made the trip here worthwhile. She was not especially sentimental by nature, but Calvin, Wendy, Eddie, Ray and Evan were the people who had fought their way out alive with her from Elaris, from Block D of Willow Heights. To Evelyn, they were more than companions. They were the sort of friends forged between life and death.

The alarm rang once, and Evelyn’s eyes opened immediately.

She got up, filled the bowls for the two little ones with food and water, then went into the bathroom to brush her teeth, wash her face and change. When choosing what to wear, she deliberately picked a drab grey padded coat.

Adrian knocked right on time. Evelyn opened the door and let him in. They looked each other over, then said nothing, as if by tacit agreement.

She brought out breakfast, one box each. Adrian ate a great deal, so she added five steamed buns to his share.

“Perhaps I should join the hunting crews too.”

Evelyn rather regretted it now. She ought to have gone there in the first place. The two of them could have worked together and stockpiled more supplies in her storage space.

“Am I eating too much?”

He looked genuinely baffled.

Evelyn had no answer for that.

At half past nine, they left the apartment building. Evelyn saw the couple from next door again. They were rather familiar in manner, and the moment they spotted Evelyn and Adrian, they came over cheerfully to say hello.

“Fancy seeing you again. I forgot to ask your names yesterday. I work in the Growing Grounds, raising medicinal herbs. My boyfriend’s in maintenance, doing electrical repairs.”

“I’m Evelyn. I’m in the Medical Division. He’s Adrian, in the hunting crews.”

Clara looked Adrian over and let out a delighted little gasp. “Blimey, your boyfriend’s tall. Since you’re in the Medical Division as well, we can walk over together in a minute. Shame Elliot and your boyfriend aren’t in the same department, or they’d have company too.”

Evelyn only smiled and said nothing.

At the bottom of the building, the four of them split up and headed towards their respective departments.

“I’m twenty-three this year. If I’m older than you, you can call me Clara.”

“I’m twenty.”

Evelyn silently noted down the surrounding roads and signposts. The people hurrying past them all moved with bent backs and quick steps.

Every face seemed marked by exhaustion and numbness. Only a small number looked anything close to lively, with some light still left in their eyes.

“Why did you only move in yesterday? Did you just arrive at the base? Have you really been living outside all this time?”

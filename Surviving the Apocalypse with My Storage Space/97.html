Chapter 97 The Left Hand of God

Just as Rowan had predicted, the temperature plummeted the next day to minus eighteen degrees. Snow poured from the sky without mercy. In a single night, the drift outside the cabin had buried half the door.

When Evelyn woke up, she glanced out the window as usual, only to find her view blocked by a wall of snow.

She added more wood to the heated brick bed, then opened her bedroom door. Rowan was not inside.

Frowning, she fed more logs into the fireplace and stepped to the door. Outside, a thin layer of snow dusted over a set of footprints. He had not been gone long.

It was bitterly cold. Evelyn shivered. In the distance, there was nothing but endless white and rows of fir trees, their branches hung with chains of ice.

At that moment, Rowan emerged from afar, carrying two thick logs across his shoulders. He was still wearing that thin sweater. Snow clung to his hair. His already pale face was drained of all color from the cold. A log rested on each shoulder, and several pieces of prey hung from them.

"Why do you sleep so much?" he asked as he stepped into the yard. He dropped the logs onto the ground and set the prey on the wooden table nearby.

"So much?" Evelyn shot back. "I slept ten hours."

He looked at her seriously. "Healthy humans don't need that much sleep. You're probably sick."

The snowball in her hand flew straight into his face.

"You're the one who's sick. Did a door slam on your brain?" She kicked the door open and stormed back inside.

Rowan glanced at the cabin, then at the prey, completely unaware he had offended her again. Still unwilling to give up, he followed and asked, "Are we eating?"

"Eat dirt."

"I can't do that," he replied solemnly.

He wiped the snow off his face, removed the knife at his waist, and began processing the animals he had brought back.

Inside, Evelyn pulled out a packet of wasabi.

It was time to teach Rowan a small lesson. If he insisted on speaking nonsense, he could suffer the consequences.

At that moment, she felt like a proper villain. Jealous of someone stronger, resorting to petty tricks behind his back.

A wicked smile curved her lips. What could she do? She was just that bad.

She took out two cartons of milk and a tin of tea leaves. Milk tea would do nicely. And someone's cup would come with a special addition.

The drink would be cloudy. No one would notice the extra ingredient. Once he swallowed it, she was certain he would not be able to speak for three days.

She stir fried the tea leaves with white sugar until it caramelized, then added a bowl of water and let it boil into bubbling foam. She poured in the milk, dropped in a small chunk of brown sugar to deepen the color, and let it simmer.

After straining out the tea leaves, she divided the milk tea into two cups. Into one of them, she squeezed half a tube of wasabi and stirred it thoroughly.

Carrying the cups outside, she saw Rowan had already skinned all the prey.

She handed him a cup. "You worked hard."

Rowan paused. For some reason, a chill ran down his back.

"What is this?"

"A drink. You went out hunting again today. I made it just for you."

He took it and, ignoring the heat, drank a mouthful straight away.

Evelyn stared at his face.

No reaction.

Not even a flicker in his expression. His brow did not twitch.

Did he not have taste buds? That could not be right. When eating meat, he could clearly tell how salty it was.

She watched as he finished the entire cup without a change in expression.

She looked down at her own cup.

Had she mixed them up?

"Why aren't you drinking?" he asked.

His gaze was clear and steady, as if every scheme in the world was transparent before him. Evelyn forced a smile and took a small sip.

Sweet. No burn. No spice.

Relieved, she finished it.

"It's sweet. Want another cup?"

Rowan gave her a long, searching look, then lowered his head and went back to work, ignoring her.

Evelyn grabbed the cup he had set on the snowbank and hurried inside. She lifted it to her nose and took a careful sniff.

The wasabi sting was sharp enough to make her eyes water.

And he had shown no reaction at all.

What a monster.

Outside, Rowan waited until she was gone. Then he slapped his stomach twice. Everything he had just swallowed came right back up into the snow.

He scooped up a handful of clean snow and shoved it into his mouth, forcing down the burning heat.

Inside the cabin, Evelyn had no idea.

In the afternoon, Rowan picked up an axe and prepared to head out to cut more trees. Evelyn pulled out a chainsaw and handed it to him. If he loved working so much, she might as well let him work properly.

"I don't know how to use it."

She demonstrated once. He still shook his head.

Left with no choice, she put on her coat, bundled herself up tightly, and followed him outside.

"Why do you love working so much?" she asked.

Rowan turned to look at her, his gaze deep and unreadable.

"Why are you staring at me like that? Did I say something wrong? You just can't sit still."

"You said if I stay here, I can't be idle. I have to work hard. Clean, chop wood, hunt. I've been doing all of it."

Evelyn gave an awkward smile. "That's good. Keep it up."

He said nothing more and continued forward. Evelyn stepped into his footprints, struggling to keep pace.

These days, they switched between butcher and lumberjack. Their routine was simple. Hunt or cut wood.

Ever since Rowan appeared, every tree and animal on Shattered Ridge seemed to be trembling.

"Here is fine." He stopped and looked back at her.

Evelyn was just about to take out the chainsaw when a fierce gust of wind slammed into her, knocking her flat into the snow. Wind and powder whipped over her head, nearly tearing off her hat.

Before she could push herself up, Rowan walked over and lifted her with one hand like he was pulling a carrot from the ground.

She adjusted her hat and was about to thank him when she suddenly sneezed. A tiny bubble formed at her nose.

Humiliation could happen in a single second.

Her face remained calm, but inside she cursed herself for not wearing a mask.

Rowan stared at her for two seconds, then silently stepped farther away.

Her chest tightened.

When he first showed up, he had smelled worse than something dredged out of a cesspool. And now he dared look disgusted?

Evelyn wiped her nose, turned on the chainsaw without a word, and marched to a fir tree.

The whine of the saw tore through the valley.

When the trunk was nearly severed, she dropped the saw and ran, leaving Rowan standing under the tree.

He appeared beside her in an instant, grabbed her without discussion, and lifted her as if to move her farther away.

She kicked at him in protest.

He dropped her straight into the snow.

"Pah!" She spat snow from her mouth and glared at him.

"Why are you angry?" he asked.

She stared at him. "If I threw you into the snow, would you be angry?"

He shook his head.

"You lost your balance. That's not my fault." He walked back, picked up the chainsaw, and cut the fallen fir into smaller sections.

Evelyn snorted but did not argue further. She stored the cut wood into her space. His efficiency was far beyond hers. In a single day, he practically wiped out an entire slope.

They divided the labor clearly. While he chopped trees, she even had time to hunt a pheasant.

On the way back, Rowan finally asked, "What kind of ability is that?"

He was clearly curious about her space. No matter how much she stored inside, it never seemed to fill. Like a bottomless pit.

"Ever heard of the Right Hand of God?" she said lightly. "Mine is the Left Hand of God."

The corner of his mouth twitched ever so slightly.

She almost thought she imagined it.

Clearly, he did not believe her.

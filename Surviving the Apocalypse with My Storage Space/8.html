Chapter 8 Rainstorm: Part 4

"We'll trade for rice, then. I'm out of it." Evelyn returned home, grabbed the medication, and handed it over. In return, Claire gave her half a pound of rice.

That afternoon, Claire messaged to say her grandmother's fever had come down. Even little Emily from upstairs was doing better.

Then came more good news: a rescue team arrived in the neighborhood, moving through flooded streets in rubber boats and speedboats, shouting through megaphones. They weren't just calming panicked residents. They were ordering low-floor occupants to evacuate immediately to emergency shelters.

Priority went to the elderly and disabled. Once registered, teams would come by to pick them up. Of course, there were still a few stubborn seniors who refused to leave, but this wasn't the time for indulging bad habits. Orders were orders, and the situation didn't allow for sentimentality.

The body floating downstairs was finally fished out by the rescue team. One of the streetlamps flickered with a sharp sizzle, sparking for a moment before the downpour extinguished it.

Then, just as Evelyn was cleaning up the kitchen, the knocking started again.

It was Linda Jones from 202. This time, she brought her two children, trying a new tactic. First, she begged and pleaded. Then, she tried guilt. When that failed, she outright threw a tantrum in the hallway, crying and shouting so loudly the walls echoed with her voice.

"Evelyn, have a heart for Lily and Kai! They're only ten. Can you really let them suffer like this? I'll pay you. Five hundred! Okay?"

Evelyn sighed. "Mrs. Jones, those are your kids, not mine. Crying outside my door isn't going to help you. If you really care about them, pack your things and head to the shelter."

"But the shelters are disgusting! So many random people, bad conditions. Kai and Ya can't handle that. I'll give you more—one thousand! Just take in the kids. My husband and I won't come in!"

"Sorry. Not happening."

"You really have no heart at all, do you?"

Evelyn let out a laugh. Sympathy? What was that worth these days? Could you eat it?

Then the kid, Kai Jones, started yelling.

"I'm cold and hungry, Mom!" His voice was sharp and shrill. He wasn't small, either, and he had a temper to match. He began pounding on the door with his fists and feet.

With a loud snap, Evelyn opened the door.

Caught off guard, the pudgy boy toppled forward and landed flat on his face. He wailed, bursting into tears, then immediately started cursing at her.

Evelyn didn't say a word. She simply grabbed him by the collar and tossed him two meters back like he weighed nothing.

"Evelyn! What are you doing to my son? You monster!" Linda screeched.

"Mom! She hit me! Beat her up! Kill her! Then we can live here!" Kai shouted from the ground, his rage mixing with snot and tears.

Evelyn calmly stepped out and picked him up again.

Linda's face turned pale. Her eyes widened with alarm.

"Evelyn, let go of my son! What are you doing? Don't you dare hurt him! I swear, if you lay a finger on him, I'll make you pay!"

Evelyn looked toward the stairwell. "If you don't leave, I'll throw him down from here."

"You wouldn't dare!"

Linda lunged toward her. Evelyn kicked her in the knee, knocking her back.

"Are you leaving or not?"

"You've lost your mind! You think you can get away with this?"

"I'm just cleaning out some trash. Are you leaving, or not?"

"I'll go! I'm leaving! Give me back my son!"

Evelyn dropped the boy on the landing. Linda scooped him up and fled down the stairs without looking back.

Little Lily Jones stood frozen for a moment, staring up at Evelyn's cold expression. When their eyes met, she turned and ran too.

As for Linda's husband, no one had seen him in days. Who knew where he was hiding?

Two hours later, the whole family was forcibly evacuated by rescue workers.

By then, the floodwaters had reached the second floor.

The water was filthy and yellow-brown, full of trash and swollen animal corpses, puffed up like balloons. Sewage was bubbling back up through the drains, and with nowhere else to dump it, people in the building had started throwing their trash right into the flood. Just opening the door into the hallway brought the sharp stench of decay and filth.

The next evening, Claire messaged Evelyn, asking if she wanted to go out the next day to gather supplies. Evelyn thought it over. If she never left home, how would she explain where her food and water came from? If people realized she wasn't short on anything, it would only attract trouble.

Even if she didn't go out tomorrow, she'd have to eventually. Better to use this chance to get a speedboat of her own under a perfectly reasonable excuse.

As soon as she replied yes, Claire informed her that she had already paid a neighbor on the eighth floor, who owned a speedboat, to rent it for three hours—for three hundred dollars. Evelyn glanced at her remaining funds and transferred 150 to split the cost. Claire accepted it without fuss.

In the group chat, plenty of people were begging to borrow power banks. Some households with generators had started charging fees: ten dollars per charge. Opportunistic? Maybe. But people still lined up to pay.

Evelyn didn't plan to pull out her own generator just yet. It was a high-end imported model, nearly silent. And though the post-rain temperatures had dropped fast, it wasn't unbearable. The body needed time to adapt. Toughening up the immune system now would help avoid illness later.

After sipping a steaming bowl of ginger tea, she put on thermal layers, climbed into her well-insulated bed, and slept straight through the night. No insomnia for once.

At six the next morning, she got up, washed, and prepped herself for the outing. Two heat packs went under her thermal wear, topped with a thick padded coat, then a raincoat and rubber boots. By the time she left, she was practically wrapped like a dumpling.

Claire was also ready. They went up to the eighth floor to collect the speedboat, each lifting one end as they carefully brought it downstairs. At the stairwell between the first and second floor, the reeking floodwater had spilled over. The stench made Claire gag, and the wind knifed through their clothes.

Evelyn lowered the speedboat into the water and let Claire get in first.

Just a few meters from the building, several people from the second floor rushed out and began calling their names. Claire glanced at Evelyn. "Should we bring them?"

Evelyn narrowed her eyes at the six people waving from the stairwell. "No. We only have three hours. We're responsible for returning the boat on time and in good condition."

Claire hesitated. Those were all neighbors so it felt wrong to leave them behind.

"But eight people is the boat's capacity," she added. "Might as well take them. Once they buy their own boats, they can figure their way back."

Evelyn raised an eyebrow. Maybe it was living this life again, but she didn't have much empathy left.

But Claire didn't know the truth. To her, it was just heavy rain, not the end of the world.

Evelyn turned the boat around and let the six clamber aboard. It was immediately cramped, and they began chattering, complaining about the awful weather.

"I need to buy one of these boats. It's just inflatable. Shouldn't be too pricey, right? Though with prices the way they are now... it'll probably cost thousands."

"Why not just borrow one when needed? No point buying. Once the rain's done, the water'll go down. Waste of money."

"Like people will keep lending it to you! Come on, it's not the time to mooch."

A squabble broke out at the back.

Claire squeezed closer to Evelyn at the front and sighed. "Evie, when do you think this rain will stop?"

Evelyn glanced at her.

Claire gave a small, nervous smile. "I just... I feel really uneasy. But at least my grandma's better now. Thank you, Evelyn."

Evelyn nodded. "No problem."

As they spoke, the speedboat finally exited the community gates.

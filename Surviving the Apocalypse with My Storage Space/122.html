Chapter 122 No End in Sight

“What’s happening outside?”

“A riot. Don’t go out yet. Stay in here and keep your head down for a bit.”

Evelyn checked Wendy again. Red blotches had already appeared on her face, neck and arms.

“So it really is an allergic reaction. I’ve got some antibiotics. Give her one first. If it’s only an allergy, she should be all right.”

Calvin finally looked relieved. Evelyn introduced everyone properly. Dr. Hargreaves had recovered enough to sit upright, though Clara still looked badly shaken.

“Elliot must be worried sick about me. It’s all my fault. If I’d listened to him and taken two days off, I wouldn’t be trapped in here now.”

Tears started spilling down Clara’s face as she spoke.

No one knew quite how to comfort her, but Evelyn raised a finger to her lips.

“Quiet. Don’t draw anyone over.”

Clara clapped a hand over her mouth and nodded quickly, then whispered, “Sorry.”

The five of them stayed hidden in the utility room for nearly three hours. Once Evelyn decided enough time had passed, she opened the door and went out first to have a look.

She had only taken a few steps when the smell hit her, thick with blood. She put on another mask before she could bear it.

There was nothing downstairs apart from blood.

No people. No bodies. Not a sound anywhere. Just the sticky pools spread across the floor.

Hearing the faintest noise nearby, Evelyn moved towards the operating theatre. Several cabinets had been jammed against the door from the inside. Someone was clearly hiding there.

She knocked, but there was no reply. Then she gave a small cough.

At once, the cabinets began scraping aside.

“Evelyn?”

It was Dr. Mercer.

Once the door opened, Evelyn saw quite a crowd inside. Dr. Mercer was there, along with Dr. Reid, Iris, several patients, and even two security guards.

“What’s it like outside?”

“No one’s there.”

Dr. Mercer cautiously stuck his head out and, after confirming the corridor was empty, finally dared step outside.

“It was horrific. Those people had gone mad. They started attacking each other. Then a team came in. I saw them shooting. They killed every last person involved in the riot.”

“Shooting?” Evelyn had not heard any gunfire at all.

“Silenced guns. Then they dragged all the bodies away.”

Evelyn glanced at the patients in the room. They all shrank back instinctively.

“Evelyn?” Iris called uncertainly. She even reached up, trying to tug down Evelyn’s mask, but Evelyn dodged away at once.

“It is you. It really is you. You recognised me before, didn’t you? Why didn’t you say anything? Evelyn, you actually became a doctor at the base. I thought you were dead.”

Evelyn rolled her eyes inwardly and said nothing.

“Dr. Mercer, I’m going to check outside. If no one’s guarding the place, we might be able to get out.”

“Evelyn...”

“And who exactly are you?” Dr. Mercer finally snapped, glaring at Iris. “Stop bothering her while she’s trying to work.”

Iris was frightened enough by the outburst to retreat straight into a corner.

But when Evelyn reached the main entrance of the Medical Division, she found the doors chained shut. Two security guards stood outside. She knocked, and they turned to look at her.

“What do you want? Stay where you are. Anyone who causes trouble again goes straight to the holding cells.”

That was the first Evelyn had heard of the base even having holding cells.

“And get the inside cleaned up. If it isn’t done, no one eats today.”

Oh, splendid. They were docking rations now. Were they prisoners?

She got nothing useful out of them, so she returned to the operating theatre and told the others what had happened.

“Then we’d better clean the place,” Dr. Mercer said. “We can’t just leave it looking like a slaughterhouse.”

Once he had spoken, everyone set to work.

When Evelyn went back to the utility room, Wendy was awake, though still looked weak and listless.

“The guards said we have to clean the Medical Division. The doors are locked. All the rioters are dead. Only a few people survived by hiding in the operating theatre.”

Dr. Hargreaves took a mop and bucket and got started. Calvin tied Wendy onto his back and joined in as well.

Upstairs, Julian was completely untouched by any of it. When one of his bodyguards came downstairs looking for a doctor, Evelyn reacted instantly and slipped out of sight.

No sooner had they finished cleaning the Medical Division than more patients were brought in. Fortunately, this time extra medicine arrived as well, along with a large batch of herbs.

With Evelyn acting as guarantor, Calvin and Wendy were allowed to leave.

Once Iris realised who Evelyn was, she began hounding her, sticking so close she was practically underfoot.

Within a few hours, the floor had descended into chaos again. Bodies lay scattered across the ground, and some patients started vomiting blood the moment they were brought in.

Oddly enough, Iris seemed much improved. She immediately started making a fuss about wanting to leave. Evelyn did not even hesitate. She said she could be escorted out at once. Instead of being grateful, Iris became even more difficult.

“Evelyn, give me some medicine and I’ll go.”

“I can give you a slap. Would that do?”

“We used to be flatmates. We were classmates too. It’s only been a year. How have you become this cold?”

Evelyn stared at her.

“If you don’t take the hint, I’ll have the guards outside remove you.”

“But I still feel a bit off. What if there are still worms inside me?”

“Then stay here for a few more days and keep being monitored.”

Iris was furious.

“I’m leaving. I just want two packets of medicine. I’ll only feel safe if I’ve got something in hand.”

“The medicine isn’t mine to hand out. Go to the dispensary if you want it.”

“If I could get it myself, would I be begging you?” Iris refused to let it go.

Without warning, Evelyn grabbed her by the wrist and dragged her into the utility room.

“What are you doing?”

Evelyn did not answer. She simply threw Iris to the floor.

“Have you lost your mind, Evelyn? What was that for?”

“I could kill you in here,” Evelyn said flatly. “Then throw you out of the window. So many people have died these last few days that one more would make no difference at all. Iris, do you want to die?”

This time Iris was truly frightened.

She had not expected Evelyn to be this merciless, or to sound as though she really meant it.

“I was wrong. I’ll leave right now. Evelyn, please let me off. I won’t do it again.”

“Who am I?”

“Evelyn.”

Evelyn’s expression turned cold at once.

Iris realised her mistake immediately and shook her head frantically.

“I don’t know you. I don’t know who you are. I’ve never met you.”

“Get out.”

Iris scrambled away from the utility room, half crawling in her panic.

Evelyn pressed two fingers to her temple. She had not slept properly for days. She was exhausted, and her temper was becoming harder to contain. If she had not still possessed the smallest shred of restraint, she might really have hit Iris just then.

More and more patients were being brought in, and the onset period was growing shorter and shorter. Watching people die one after another in front of her, Evelyn felt she had already stepped into hell.

The food sent in from outside was still the same cold, hard steamed buns and tins of meat. Evelyn still did not touch any of it.

Her protective suit was filthy by now, and apart from the herbs, the base had sent no other supplies.

Before long, the Medical Division was surrounded once again.

The temperature climbed to twenty degrees.

For the first time, Evelyn found herself wanting to run.

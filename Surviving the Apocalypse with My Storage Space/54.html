Chapter 54 Heatwave, Human Ties

Relying on memory, Evelyn found several bottles of iodine solution in a cabinet. The others spread out and began searching carefully.

There was a storage room attached to the lab, where students used to change and stash their belongings. The moment Evelyn stepped inside, she moved quickly. From her spatial storage, she took out the water she had prepared earlier and set it quietly in the corner.

"Calvin, Ray, Evan, there's water in here."

The three of them rushed in.

When they saw the five large water containers stacked in the corner, Ray and Evan threw their arms around each other and burst into tears. Even Calvin stared at Evelyn in disbelief.

"There are five of us," she said lightly. "One barrel each. Calvin, can you carry two?"

"Yes. Yes, of course." Calvin's voice trembled with emotion. He stepped forward and touched the containers as if afraid they would vanish. Dust had settled on the outside. He carefully wiped it away before lifting one to test its weight.

"Evie, you're the one who found this. You take two. The three of us will split the other three."

Evelyn waved him off. "Last time you shared what you found without hesitation. This is just luck. Let's not argue over it. See if there's anything else useful. We should head back soon. Wendy is home alone."

"Alright." Calvin nodded heavily.

When he turned around, the other three pretended not to notice the redness in his eyes. They all looked away in silent understanding.

They pried open small lockers and emptied everything inside.

Protective suits from the lab. Masks. Gloves. Instant noodles. Hard candies. Alcohol. Iodine. Gauze. Glucose packets.

They found more than expected.

Calvin suggested they divide everything once they were home. The water barrels were tied securely and hoisted onto their backs. The remaining supplies were wrapped in curtain fabric, slung over shoulders, and carried by hand.

They returned fully loaded once again.

It was heavy and exhausting but everyone was smiling.

Evelyn felt lighter too.

A favor returned meant peace of mind. No one liked someone who only took and never gave.

She was no different.

Back home, she looked at the water she had just carried for miles and could not help but laugh at herself.

She had taken it out of her space, only to haul it back on her own shoulders.

Her acting skills were impressive. If she ever entered show business, she might even win an award.

The supplies from the lab were divided evenly. One share per person. The extra bottle of alcohol went to Evelyn.

Starting the next day, everyone threw themselves into training under Calvin.

At first, it was nothing glamorous. Stretching. Flexibility drills. Horse stance. Push-ups. Strength training. Speed work.

Only when they began to build a foundation did Calvin start teaching actual combat techniques.

The heat was suffocating. Even indoors, they could not train more than three hours a day.

After returning home, Evelyn pushed herself further. Overtime practice.

Ray and Evan were just as driven.

Maybe even more.

They were afraid of running into bad people again. Afraid of being abandoned because they were too weak.

When Evelyn dragged her aching body back into her apartment each day, even squatting or lifting her arms sent sharp soreness through her muscles.

She had thought she was in good shape.

But under Calvin's hands, she lost within two exchanges.

Without her crossbow, facing raw male strength head-on, she still fell short.

And she knew she could not rely on that weapon forever.

October arrived in the blink of an eye.

Evelyn's training was finally starting to show results. During this time, Calvin occasionally went out to assess the situation. With temperatures climbing to 53 degrees, there were no signs of human activity left outside.

Evelyn sketched a rough map of Elaris and marked several higher-elevation residential areas, giving the others places to try their luck.

Elaris was enormous. A super first-tier metropolis that once held thirty-five million people. Skyscrapers stood beside ancient towers and thousand-year-old city walls. History and modernity intertwined.

Even now, in the apocalypse, even reduced to a desolate shell, it remained a beacon in countless hearts.

As long as the city still stood, hope remained.

Every survivor carried a seed inside them. A seed that wanted to return here one day.

With a trekking pole in hand, Evelyn set out again on a scavenging run.

Strength filled her chest. Behind her, endless high-rises loomed in the night. Gradually, her silhouette disappeared into the swirling yellow sand.

Along the way, she spotted two vulture eggs.

They were larger than her palm, one end slightly pointed. The white shells were thick and faintly rough to the touch.

She scanned her surroundings. No vultures in sight.

Swiftly, she slipped the eggs into her spatial storage.

To curb their spread, you had to cut the problem at its root. Once inside her space, those eggs would never hatch.

She silently praised her own cleverness and continued forward.

The cracked, desertified ground was treacherously soft. With every step, sand poured into her shoes. Every thirty minutes, she had to stop, sit down, and dump out the accumulated grit.

Her destination today was the largest entertainment club in Elaris.

Back in high school, a rich classmate once bragged that his father was a shareholder there. On the top floor was a massive luxury car garage, a private domain belonging to the club's owner.

In her space, she already had her father's commuter car and a Unimog.

Still, she wanted to try her luck one more time.

After the apocalypse began, the wealthy had likely airlifted much of their assets away. But Evelyn believed there were always leftovers.

Even a single barrel of fuel would be worth the trip.

The road was full of potholes. One misstep and she could fall. Broken glass and nails were scattered everywhere. She had considered bringing out a bicycle or tricycle from her space, but now it was obvious they would be useless.

What once took an hour now required more than two.

Exhausted, Evelyn pulled a bottle of ice water from her space, drank deeply, and rested for three minutes before continuing.

She and Calvin's group had split up this time. They were heading toward Silvercrest Estates, a wealthy hillside residential area on the outskirts. There might be valuable finds there as well.

There were advantages to moving alone.

Evelyn took out her MP3 player. Once the earbuds were in, her steps felt lighter.

Occasionally, fierce gusts of wind would whip up walls of sand. She would crouch immediately, waiting for it to pass. When she stood again, dozens of pounds of sand slid off her body.

She could not help imagining she was trekking through a desert.

Maybe a camel would be waiting just ahead.

In the end, no camel appeared.

Instead, she reached the entertainment club, which looked no different from a ghost city.

This place had once been Elaris's ultimate playground for the rich. A paradise where someone could burn through millions in a single night.

Now, the white walls were stained with high-water marks. Massive columns at the entrance were cracked. Of the eight pillars, three had collapsed entirely.

The building had been modeled after a medieval castle.

Under the night sky, with the wind howling through broken stone, it truly looked haunted.

Chapter 45 Heatwave, Snake Infestation: Part 9

"I'll talk, I'll tell you everything. There are only eight of us. The people we captured include three women and two children. They're all locked up in Block E, Unit 1102. I've told you everything. Can you please let me go now?"

A short blade swept across his throat.

The man dropped instantly.

Calvin wiped the blood from the knife, dragged the body outside, and tossed it into a patch of mud.

"What are you thinking, Calvin?" Evelyn asked, watching him.

"Cut the weeds by the root. Eradicate evil completely."

Evelyn looked toward Block E and nodded. "Exactly what I was thinking."

They headed for Block E. None of the snake corpses here had been cleared. It was hard work climbing up to the eleventh floor. Evelyn stopped in front of the door marked 1102 and raised her hand to knock.

Cursing spilled out from inside, mixed with the muffled sobs and pleading cries of women.

They exchanged a look. Evelyn made a slicing gesture across her neck. Calvin nodded.

The moment the door opened, Evelyn fired.

The crossbow bolt pierced straight through the throat of a short, heavyset middle-aged man. He collapsed rigidly to the floor. Blood gushed from his neck, his mouth letting out broken, gurgling sounds as his eyes widened and life drained away.

Evelyn pulled out the bolt. Calvin took the lead as they entered the apartment.

A shirtless man lounging on the couch started to sit up at the noise. Before he could react, Calvin slit his throat.

Evelyn scanned the room.

Two women were bound hand and foot and dumped in the living room. Their clothes barely covered them. Their eyes were empty, their bodies bruised and battered. Beside them sat a dog cage. Inside, two children were tied up. It was impossible to tell their ages or even their genders. When they saw the crossbow in Evelyn's hands, they shrank back in terror.

From the bedroom came obscene laughter and a woman's faint, broken sobbing.

Calvin strode over and kicked the door open. He sent the man inside flying with a kick. Before the man could even process what was happening, the knife was already buried in his chest.

Trash littered the floor. Empty drink bottles and plastic wrappers were everywhere. On the balcony stood a plastic bucket. Even from a distance, the sharp stench of urine was unmistakable.

Evelyn took out her military knife and walked toward the dog cage. The two children inside were completely frozen with fear.

"I'm not going to kill you."

In one clean motion, the chain snapped with a sharp crack. Evelyn pulled the children out, cut away the ropes binding them, and tore off the black tape sealing their mouths. Then she walked over to the two women.

Only now did they seem to react at all, staring blankly at Evelyn.

The ropes around their wrists and ankles had cut deep into the flesh. The wounds were swollen, infected, horrific to look at.

Evelyn said nothing.

She cut the ropes off them, then yanked down the tattered curtains and covered their bodies.

After confirming all three men were dead, Calvin came over and gave Evelyn a small nod. He dragged the bodies to the window and threw them out. The bloody, putrid smell inside the apartment faded a little.

With the group dealt with, they prepared to leave. Wendy was alone at home, and Calvin was deeply uneasy.

"Can we go with you?"

One of the rescued women looked at Calvin hesitantly, her voice small. It sounded less like a question and more like a plea.

"They're all dead now," Calvin said. "You're safe."

"You're a police officer."

The woman stared at Calvin's right hand. Her tone was firm, certain.

"In times like these, looking for the police is useless. Everyone's just trying to survive."

"You have calluses from holding a gun. My husband was a police officer too. I don't know if you've ever met him." When she mentioned her husband, the dead emptiness in her eyes flickered with light, then quickly dimmed again.

"What's his name?" Calvin asked.

"Justin Moore."

Calvin looked at her, his expression complicated. "Justin Moore was my colleague. After the torrential rains started, I never saw him again."

The woman nodded stiffly, then lowered her head and didn't look at him anymore.

Calvin quietly said, "I'm sorry," then turned and left. He knew exactly what his responsibility was now. It was no longer saving others. It was honoring the promise he made to his wife, protecting his daughter with his life.

After Evelyn and Calvin left, the apartment fell into a long, heavy silence. Everyone crouched in their own corners. Even though the ropes had been cut away, the restraints in their minds were still tightly bound.

Back home, the cool air inside the apartment made Evelyn finally relax.

She wiped the blood from the arrowhead, but the image that kept replaying in her mind was the dog cage used to hold people.

That night, Evelyn had another nightmare.

She dreamed she was locked inside a narrow cage. Her limbs were tied, a rope looped around her neck. She screamed for help. Several blood-soaked men appeared in front of her, walking up and down, appraising her.

"This two-legged sheep we caught is pretty good. Let's stew her later."

"No, roasting tastes best."

"Has to be steamed."

"If it's two-legged sheep, it should be skewers. Hahaha…"

Evelyn jolted awake. She touched her forehead. The burning heat told her she had a fever.

After taking some fever medicine, she leaned back against the bed. Because of the heat, she had already put away the mattress and blankets, leaving only a pillow and a bamboo mat. The air conditioner was still running. Her body alternated between hot and cold, and her vision blurred.

Evelyn wondered if, if she died like this, there would be another life waiting for her.

The thought made her laugh softly. She raised her hand and lightly smacked her forehead.

"No more nonsense."

Only by living could there be a future. Darkness or light, you had to stay alive to see it.

By morning, her fever had eased a little. She heard movement outside her door. When she opened it, she saw Calvin collecting the remains in the building.

"They deserve to be laid to rest," he said.

Evelyn watched as he carried bones out into the hallway. Wendy followed him closely, like a little puppy that wouldn't leave his side. Evelyn went back inside, put on a mask and gloves, grabbed alcohol blocks and a lighter, and stepped out again.

Downstairs, all the remains were piled together and set ablaze. The weather was scorching. Wendy and Evelyn stood in the shade of the stairwell, while Calvin watched the fire in silence.

After several hours, the flames died out. So many lives, reduced in the end to scattered bones and ash.

Calvin dug a pit downstairs, gathered the ashes into a suitcase, and gently lowered it into the ground.

On her way back upstairs, Evelyn stopped at the ninth floor. She looked at the door to 902, pushed it open, and went inside.

Standing in that room made her feel heavy. There were still some clean books left in Claire's apartment. Evelyn wiped them down and put them away.

A wrinkled piece of paper slipped out from between the shelves. Evelyn bent down to pick it up. When she saw the messy handwriting on it, her gaze darkened.

Natalie and her mother had planned to take over 902 and make a move against Claire. Claire discovered their intentions and hid in the bedroom, locking the door. She thought she could ride it out. Instead, snakes flooded the apartment. Knowing she couldn't escape, Claire left this short note and chose to end her own life.

Evelyn, if you find this letter, I'm glad you're safe.

I should have listened to you and stopped being a fool who tries to save everyone. Unfortunately, I don't get the chance to fix my mistake.

Live well, my dearest Evie.

Chapter 115 A Score Never Left Unsettled

After the three men had gone, Evelyn looked at Adrian and could not help laughing.

“Let’s eat. I’m hungry.”

Even now, food was still on his mind.

“Shall I make another pot of red-cooked pork? We ought to celebrate. Your good days are just around the corner.”

Adrian frowned slightly. He had the distinct feeling there was something dangerous hidden in that sentence, and for once he was cautious about answering.

“What’s that supposed to mean?”

“That man took your photo just now. If Miss Hawthorne takes a liking to you, I expect she’ll soon send someone to fetch you off to her luxurious villa. Since we’re comrades who’ve been through life and death together, do try not to forget me once you’re living in comfort.”

“That would save time,” Adrian said flatly, sitting down and continuing his meal. “I could go there and kill her.”

“And after you kill her, do you think you’ll get away? Do you think I’ll get away? Do you think Calvin and the others will?”

Adrian lifted his eyes to hers. His expression remained as calm as ever. “Are you afraid?”

Evelyn nodded. “Everyone is afraid of power, especially when what they hold is more than power alone. Still, don’t worry. I’ll get you out of this.”

She took out several medicinal ingredients. “You’ll have to put up with being hideous for a few days.”

After supper, Evelyn began preparing a poison. When she had boiled down a pot of thick black medicine, she cut Adrian’s finger, took a few drops of his blood, and mixed them into it. Then she told him to spread it over his face and wash it off after half an hour. When the time was up, the handsome, finely cut features he had started with were gone, replaced by a face covered in lumps and angry swellings.

“Put some on your neck and hands as well. When they come again, show them this face.”

Adrian looked at her in silence. “You’re using my blood to restrain my blood?”

His blood could heal, but everything in the world had its opposite. Mixed into poison, his blood only made it stronger.

“I expect it’ll only last two or three days.”

Adrian looked at himself in the mirror, then the corner of his mouth curved slightly. “You’ve got more nerve than most.”

“Go back and wait. I think they’ll come soon enough.”

Adrian did not move. There was not the slightest sign that he meant to return to 602.

Instead, he stared at her face, then handed her the rest of the medicine. “You should put some on as well.”

“I don’t need to.”

“I think you do.”

He was insistent, and his expression made it perfectly clear that if she refused, he would do it himself. Evelyn remembered all too vividly the time he had forced blood down her throat. In the end, she took the mixture and spread a thin layer over her own face.

The reaction came quickly. In about ten minutes, her skin began to itch, and lumps the size of broad beans came up across her cheeks.

“Good thing I left Pip and Lark with Eddie. I really do plan ahead.”

After washing off the poison, Evelyn examined her newly ruined face, took out her mobile, and decided the moment deserved recording.

“Come on. Let’s take a photo together. A souvenir.”

And so their first photograph together came into being like that, both of them looking utterly dreadful.

Evelyn then put away more than half the things in the room so that nothing would seem out of place. Sure enough, only a few minutes later, there was another knock at the door.

She went to answer it with her face covered. The young woman standing at the front, wrapped in a cloak trimmed with fur, could only be the infamous Miss Hawthorne.

“Where is he?”

The man from earlier stepped forward, clearly intending to go in and drag Adrian out. Evelyn quickly stretched out an arm to block the way.

“So you’re the owner of that squirrel? I haven’t settled accounts with you yet. You dare stand in my way? Tie her up.”

“So you’re Miss Hawthorne?” Evelyn said coldly. “First you tried to steal my squirrel and my bird, and now you’ve come to my home to take a person. Do you always want what belongs to someone else?”

One of the men beside her raised a hand to strike Evelyn, but she slipped neatly aside and let the cloth drop from her face.

The sight of the lumps covering it sent them all stumbling back several paces at once. Miss Hawthorne recoiled as though she had been shown something filthy, her face twisting with disgust and revulsion.

“Bring that man out. Tie up this ugly woman and throw her outside the base.”

At that moment Adrian stepped forward and pulled Evelyn behind him. When his own even more dreadful face appeared, Evelyn suddenly looked rather presentable by comparison.

“What is this? Why is there an ugly man as well? Gavin, where’s the man from the photograph?”

Gavin stared at the two of them, sweat gathering on his brow. How could both of them have changed faces in the space of two hours?

“Miss, it is him. I swear it is. I don’t know why he looks like this now. The photo wasn’t edited. They must have eaten something that caused an allergic reaction.”

Miss Hawthorne’s face darkened with fury. She lifted one foot and kicked Gavin hard in the chest.

“Take them to the villa. Call the doctor.”

The bodyguards were clearly afraid the condition might be contagious and did not dare lay hands on Evelyn or Adrian. Gavin dragged himself upright, pulled out a gun and aimed it at Adrian, his voice vicious.

“Move.”

When they reached the Manor District, Evelyn took in the line of splendid houses with a dark, unreadable expression.

The moment Miss Hawthorne entered the villa, two men moved in at once to attend to her. It was the first time Evelyn had seen a scene like that, and she found it oddly amusing.

“Where’s the doctor?”

“Miss, Dr. Ashcroft is here.”

Dr. Ashcroft was the Hawthorne family’s private physician. Things had apparently been unsettled within the family lately, so he had been staying in Mr. Hawthorne’s villa to help restore his health.

“Miss, you sent for me?” Dr. Ashcroft was a middle-aged man in his forties, slightly stout, and breathing rather heavily after the short walk in.

“Take a look at these two. Are they reacting to food?”

The moment he saw the lumps on Evelyn and Adrian’s faces, he almost retched.

After examining them, he shook his head in defeat.

“Miss, it doesn’t look like food allergy or poisoning. If anything, it looks congenital.”

“How is that possible? Two hours ago their faces were perfectly fine. I took a photograph.”

Gavin, now badly flustered, hurriedly produced the picture and showed it to him. Dr. Ashcroft glanced at it, then stroked his chin.

“This has been edited, surely. No one really looks like that. They don’t even look human. Miss, Gavin must be making a fool of you on purpose.”

Gavin was furious and aggrieved in equal measure, trembling with frustration. Miss Hawthorne gave him a chilling look, then turned her gaze back to Evelyn and Adrian.

“The two of you can go.”

“Miss, there’s something wrong with them.”

“Get out. Kneel in the snow for three hours.”

Gavin shot Adrian a venomous look, but there was nothing for it. He stumbled out of the villa and knelt outside in the snow.

As Evelyn and Adrian passed him, he looked up at them with a savage, hateful glare.

After they had walked some distance away, Evelyn played idly with the embroidery needle in her hand and gave Adrian a long, meaningful look.

When Miss Hawthorne’s people had taken them away, Evelyn had been just about to make her move, only for Adrian to beat her to it. He had taken all five of her needles. As for when he acted, or how, she had not seen a thing.

But when Gavin glared at them in challenge a moment ago, Evelyn had seen it clearly. The needle flicked from Adrian’s fingertips and went straight into the back of the man’s skull without even the need for physical contact.

“There was something on the needle,” she said coolly. “The two of them are going to suffer for it.”

Her voice had turned cold. She had always been the sort to repay every slight in full. When a person deserved to die, not even the King of Hell could stop it.

“So this is your good plan?” Adrian clearly found it too mild, but Evelyn had insisted, and he did not dare argue.

“The important part isn’t revenge. It’s getting revenge and walking away from it unscathed. Do you understand now?”

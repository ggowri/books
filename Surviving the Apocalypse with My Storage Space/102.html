Chapter 102 I Will Come Back

With a bottle of poison in one hand and insecticide in the other, Evelyn sprayed with every ounce of strength she had left. By the time all thirty bottles of poison were empty, the screeching had thinned considerably. Only a few stragglers still circled overhead.

She collapsed back into the inflatable kayak. Adrian struck down the last bat with his military knife, then took out a gas cylinder.

The bats floated across the water. Some were dead, others only stunned. Adrian lit the cylinder and threw it into the mass of bodies.

A series of deafening explosions thundered behind them. Evelyn clapped her hands over her ears, her face tightening with pain.

By the time the kayak drifted into another branch of the cavern, she lowered her hands, only to be met with a loud buzzing inside her ears. The ringing left her dizzy and nauseous.

Several dead bats still lay in the kayak. Evelyn picked them up and flung them into the water. Even through her gloves, she could feel the sticky smear of bat blood on her clothes and across the rubber sides of the boat.

She rubbed at her ears and waited it out. After a while, the buzzing finally faded.

“What is it?”

Evelyn gave a confused little sound and turned her blindfolded face towards Adrian. “Were you speaking to me?”

He studied her for a moment, then snapped his fingers beside her ear. She heard it clearly.

“My ears were ringing just now, but it’s stopped. What did you say?”

“I said there’s a stone ledge ahead.”

Evelyn understood at once. “You go up and have a look. I’ll wait in the kayak.”

Adrian took the knife with him and leapt lightly onto the ledge. Evelyn reached for the cloth bag. The two little creatures inside were utterly still, probably frightened out of their wits.

“There’s an entrance here.”

Hearing his voice, Evelyn forced herself upright in the kayak. Her hands were so sore they felt almost useless. She had pushed them too hard spraying at the bats, and the muscles and nerves had taken a fair amount of strain.

Adrian hauled her up onto the ledge, then rinsed the filthy, blood-slick kayak in the water.

The moment they stepped into the hidden passage, Evelyn caught the stale smell of mould. Damp, icy air rushed over her, and she shivered.

Adrian led her down a long flight of steps until they reached a hidden chamber. It was reasonably clean and would do as a place to rest for the time being.

Once the firewood was lit, Evelyn removed her gas mask and stripped off the foul-smelling protective suit.

“Rest here for a bit. I’m going to see whether there are still fireballs outside.”

She frowned and called after him at once.

“You…”

But the words stalled in her throat. What exactly was she meant to ask? Whether he would come back?

How ridiculous, she thought. Lose your sight and your hearing for a while, and suddenly you turned timid and uneasy. Evelyn gave herself a silent mocking rebuke and waved a hand instead.

“Be careful.”

“I’ll come back.”

Adrian’s tone was as calm as ever. He took one of her guns and one of the knives with him.

Evelyn gave a faint, crooked smile but said nothing. Once he had gone, she curled herself into a corner, removed her clothes, and pressed medicinal patches against her lower back and tailbone.

She hissed through her teeth.

Only twenty years old, and already this body was covered in injuries.

She rinsed her eyes several times with clean water, then used eye drops and applied anti-inflammatory ointment around them. They had stopped streaming by now, though the stinging remained.

Her ears still rang from time to time, but it was far better than before. The fire was burning fiercely, and warmth was slowly returning to her body. Evelyn let Pip and Lark out for a bit of air. Both of them still looked listless and drained.

After tending to the rest of her injuries, she leaned back against the stone wall and closed her eyes to rest. Time trickled by, but Adrian did not return.

Her mind, however, stayed perfectly calm. Right now, she only wanted to recover her strength and let her eyes heal as quickly as possible. As for things beyond her control, she had never wasted much time brooding over them.

Pip came over and licked her fingers. Knowing the little thing was hungry, Evelyn placed a handful of sunflower seeds in front of it, then fed Lark a piece of raw meat.

With the fire warming the chamber, she ended up drifting off to sleep.

She did not know how long she had been asleep when a faint sound stirred her awake. Evelyn sat bolt upright and closed her hand around the gun.

It was not until she heard the familiar tread of footsteps coming down the stairs that she finally relaxed.

Adrian was back.

“The fireballs are still falling. The sky’s still dark.”

Evelyn straightened a little. She tried to appear at ease, but the fist clenched tightly in her lap and the stiffness in her body gave her away.

“What do you think is causing it?” she asked, meaning the darkness.

“A total solar eclipse. Planetary collision. Meteorite impact causing the fireballs.”

“And you think it’s just a solar eclipse?”

A total solar eclipse had five stages. In the final two, the light returned and the sun reappeared.

“It’s only a guess,” Adrian said. “The truth is, every day brings a different disaster. We may be dealing with a solar eclipse here, while somewhere else people are facing a tsunami or a volcanic eruption.”

“How far is it from here to the exit?”

He sat down opposite her, carrying the chill of the outside air in with him. Evelyn also caught a faint trace of blood on him.

“Not far. About an hour by kayak there and back.”

Evelyn took out the alarm clock and handed it to him so he could check the time. Adrian leaned closer.

“Half past seven.”

She began working out in her head how many hours had passed since the darkness began. Adrian added two more sticks to the fire, then held out a hand towards her. He did not need to say a word. She already knew what he wanted.

She took out a dressed wild chicken and handed it over. He skewered it on the military knife and began roasting it over the fire.

Before long, the whole hidden chamber was filled with the rich smell of meat. Evelyn had little appetite. She simply rested against the wall and closed her eyes again.

Every three hours, Adrian went back outside to check the situation. Only when he was gone did Evelyn deal with the awkward realities of being human.

When he returned again, it was already six in the morning.

“The fireballs have stopped. But everything outside is still burning.”

Then, with unmistakable regret, he added, “If I’d known, I’d have cleared out all the animals on Ashen Ridge first.”

Evelyn could not help the slight curve of her lips. When someone obsessed with food lost that much meat in one go, the pain must have been almost physical.

“It’s still bitterly cold. I checked with the thermometer. Even with all the fire outside, the temperature’s dropped to minus thirty-six. Give it about three days, and we should be able to leave.”

That settled her mind somewhat. Three days. Her eyes ought to recover by then.

Evelyn took out her phone, switched it on, and handed it to him so he could look at the map photo she had taken before.

“Have you seen this place? I photographed it last time I came in.”

Adrian nodded. “I know it. The gold I gave you last time came from there.”

At once Evelyn grew alert. “What’s inside?”

For a moment, Adrian said nothing.

Silence stretched between them for what felt like a very long time before he finally spoke, his voice low and quiet.

“It’s an abandoned human testing facility. There are thousands of failed test subjects in there. Do you still want to go?”

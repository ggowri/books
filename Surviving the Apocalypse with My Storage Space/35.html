Chapter 35 Flooding, Heatwave

On June 1st, the thunderstorms stopped. The cold wave retreated, and the temperature began to rise.

The air warmed from minus twenty degrees to five degrees above zero. Behind the gray sky, pale sunlight filtered through gaps in the clouds and reflected off the glass. When Evelyn stretched out her hand, she could feel a faint trace of warmth.

Still, something felt wrong.

Elaris was not at a high altitude. Now that the cold wave had passed, why did it feel so hard to breathe, as if the air lacked oxygen?

Her chest felt tight. Her head was light and heavy at the same time. It was the kind of reaction people only had when entering high altitude regions. Evelyn shook her head and took out a thermometer to check her temperature.

It was 37.5°C.

A mild fever. Another classic sign of oxygen deprivation.

This life was truly different from her last one. During the torrential rains in her previous life, the temperature had never dropped to minus twenty. Everyone knew that at standard atmospheric pressure, pure water froze at zero degrees. Water with impurities formed ice water mixtures below four degrees. Yet before, even at minus twenty, there had only been cold waves and frigid air, no large-scale freezing.

Unless the atmospheric pressure itself had been disrupted. Unless latitude and longitude had shifted.

And there was only one cause for that. Crustal movement. Tectonic plate movement.

Elaris was coastal. If the plates really had shifted, would the sea level rise and flood inland? Or would Elaris sink and become part of a new ocean?

Suppressing the unease in her chest, Evelyn opened the window and cleaned away the filth splashed up by the moving floodwater. The murky water was still flowing slowly. The air carried an indescribable stench. Evelyn quickly held her breath and shut the window, then let out a heavy exhale.

Animal and human corpses floating on the surface had soaked for too long, bloating grotesquely. If they exploded, it would probably become impossible to even leave the apartment.

Evelyn did a full deep clean of her home. She used an entire bottle of disinfectant, then lit mugwort incense in every room, the bathroom, the kitchen, and the balcony. Thinking of something, she took several flower pots out of her storage space, along with a bag of raw soil. She mixed in rooting powder, filled the pots, and planted a few seeds.

After spraying them with nutrient solution, she moved the pots onto the balcony.

With the rain stopped, drinking water became the biggest problem. Survivors began going out in groups to search for supplies. Some even waded directly into the floodwater, salvaging bottled drinks that had been submerged.

On June 2nd, the temperature rose to ten degrees. Even more people went out. Claire and Natalie came to ask Evelyn to join them in looking for supplies.

All the survivors in the complex emerged. Those without inflatable boats used bathtubs or makeshift rafts. Some clung to car tires, paddling their way out of the neighborhood with difficulty.

In just two months, half the people were gone.

Everyone looked miserable and exhausted. Hunger had dulled their eyes. Their faces were sallow, their hair greasy. You did not need to get close. From two or three meters away, you could already smell the stench clinging to their bodies.

During the two months of nonstop rain, official supplies had only been distributed once. A few rescue teams had appeared during the first week of the disaster. After that, there was no word from the authorities at all. Evelyn's radio had only picked up a single signal.

The survivors who once waited for rescue had finally given up hope.

They were forced to accept the truth.

The two months of torrential rain had been the beginning of the apocalypse.

And now, the apocalypse had truly arrived.

Evelyn did not go out with Claire and the others. Right now, everyone was pouring out all at once. Forget finding supplies. Even if someone did find something, it would only invite trouble. Hungry people lost their sanity. There was nothing they would not do.

Young women and children were the easiest prey.

Evelyn stayed home, working nonstop to steam buns. Foods with strong smells were off limits for now. She had no intention of turning herself into a target.

To blend in, whenever she had to open the door or step outside, she deliberately put on her dirtiest clothes. Other than brushing her teeth, she had not washed her face, hair, or showered in a long time. Her short hair looked greasy, but that made her safer.

The best camouflage was to disappear into the crowd.

She had more than enough food stored away. Enough to last her two lifetimes. But Evelyn would never take it out to play the good Samaritan. Human nature was not something you ever tested.

She once read a story. During a drought, a kind hearted wealthy merchant shared his grain with the villagers. Over time, he fed their greed. One day, when he could no longer produce even a single grain of rice, the villagers refused to believe him. They were convinced he was simply unwilling to help anymore. In their rage, they stormed his home, killed his family, and forced him to hand over the food.

The white flour buns were soft and fragrant. Evelyn packed them into bags and stored them directly in her space. She still had plenty of boxed meals left, at least enough for another year. She decided to steam more buns anyway. Steaming buns did not require turning on the range hood.

People who had gone out searching for supplies began returning one by one. The things they brought back were a chaotic mix. When Evelyn saw gas cylinders, she frowned. She had stockpiled plenty herself, but those things still made her uneasy. If one exploded, it would be over. Even if you did not die, you would be crippled, and everyone nearby would be affected.

Some rafts carried piles of bottled water. Anything soaked in floodwater should not be consumed. Floodwaters were full of bacteria. But no one cared anymore. When people were starving, they would eat anything. Even dirt.

By a little after seven in the evening, the people from Block D returned. Evelyn stood on the balcony and watched through binoculars for a while. Claire and Natalie were at the back. Calvin and William were at the front. Everyone was covered in blood and injuries. And it looked like two people were missing.

A few minutes later, loud shouting erupted outside. Soon after, Calvin's voice carried up.

"We ran into several groups of robbers. Everyone was slashed at least once. Mr. Miller was stabbed in the chest. Bob had his throat cut."

The two who had died were brought back by Calvin. Their families cried in the hallway for a while, then carried the bodies to the rooftop and burned them.

The stench of burning, rotting flesh drifted down from above. Evelyn felt it grow even harder to breathe.

The next morning, Claire came to find Evelyn. Natalie's mother had fallen ill. The cause was unknown.

Evelyn walked into Claire's apartment and immediately noticed two cans sitting on the table. She glanced back at Claire, who gave her an awkward smile.

"Margaret's sick and can't eat anything," Claire said quietly. "So I gave her a can. Natalie has lost so much weight, and she's still growing. Her period is already two weeks late this month. I was afraid she wasn't getting enough nutrition, so I gave her one too."

Evelyn said nothing, but Claire could clearly read the word foolish in her expression.

Natalie was squatting beside her mother's bed, staring at the weak, sick woman with panic and fear.

Evelyn walked over to examine her. She lifted Margaret's clothes and pressed a hand against her abdomen.

"You're four months pregnant," Evelyn said calmly. "The fetus has already died inside you. That's why the abdominal pain is so severe you can't get out of bed."

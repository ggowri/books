Chapter 55 Heatwave, The Club

The club had seventeen floors. Mud and sand had swallowed everything up to the second level.

After scrambling inside, Evelyn was hit with a wave of suffocating heat and stench. She adjusted her mask, dumped the sand from her shoes again, and took a few minutes to steady herself before pulling out her flashlight.

On the third-floor corridor, a gold metal directory sign was still hanging. She wiped away the sand and read the layout.

Bathhouse. Sauna. Restaurant. Gym. Guest rooms. Beauty salon. Karaoke bar. Foot massage. Game rooms. Tea lounge. Café.

The top floor was marked as private property. No entry.

Evelyn was in no rush. She would search one floor at a time. She had come all this way. She refused to leave empty-handed. One of her best qualities was that she never wasted a trip.

The moment she stepped onto the third floor, she froze.

Human skeletons were scattered everywhere.

From the way they lay tangled across the hallway, she guessed there had been a stampede. Too many people fleeing at once. The chaos must have been horrific.

The bathhouse was a total wreck. Nothing clean or valuable remained.

She continued upward.

The gym was in better condition. Aside from a few skeletons on the floor, most of the equipment was intact.

Evelyn picked up a dumbbell. Still solid. She immediately stored it in her space.

She collected two treadmills as well. Glancing at the brand logo, she clicked her tongue softly. Each one had cost over a hundred thousand before the apocalypse. Pure luxury.

There were three floors of guest rooms. Most had been ravaged by snakes, filthy and unusable.

But in a particularly large storage room, she struck gold.

Unopened bedding sets. Comforters. Electric kettles. Tea bags. Towels. Bath towels. Toothpaste. Toothbrushes. Body wash. Shampoo.

Aside from the cellar at Everpeace Monastery, this was the second time she had stumbled upon such a treasure trove.

It felt like opening a blind box and finding a diamond inside.

The storage room was enormous, likely the supply warehouse for the entire guest services department. Evelyn's eyes gleamed.

She slipped the flashlight strap over her head, pried open cabinet after cabinet, and swept everything into her space.

At this point, she did not even bother checking each item individually. If it was clean and undamaged, it went in.

It took her two full hours to clear the three guest floors.

This was what she called feast after famine. When it paid off, it paid off big.

Next came the beauty salon.

Though heavily damaged, the extravagant décor still stunned her. Crystal chandeliers. Gilded mirrors. Marble counters.

The gap between people's wealth, power, and status had always been vast. If not for the apocalypse, she might never have stepped into a place like this in her lifetime.

She had once heard that entering required a five-million membership card, and the balance had to be spent within a year.

Inside, she was immediately drawn to the array of aesthetic machines.

Before the world ended, cosmetic medicine had been booming. Beyond basic skincare, more and more people opted for subtle adjustments and surgical enhancements.

Evelyn studied medicine, but this field was not her specialty.

Glass vials of hyaluronic acid were scattered across the floor. She picked one up and examined the label.

It was an imported brand. One injection probably cost tens of thousands.

After another sweep of the beauty salon, she found nothing particularly useful. In the end, she only took a few treatment beds.

Next stop, the karaoke bar.

She headed straight for the liquor storage.

Back in her senior year of high school, her class had celebrated graduation at a karaoke bar. Most of them were laid out similarly. After some searching, she found a small convenience shop inside the complex.

Unfortunately, everything edible had been ruined. But several shelving units were still in good condition. She had been meaning to collect proper storage racks for a while now.

Sometimes, you searched high and low for something and found nothing. Other times, it practically fell into your lap.

After storing the shelves, Evelyn wiped down a chair and sat for a short break.

The heat and smell made this brutal work.

She removed her hat and mask, cleaned her face with a wet wipe, then took out a bottle of water from her space. Even after finishing it, the heat clung to her.

Her back, the soles of her feet, her waist, underarms, arms. Everything was drenched in sweat.

She closed her eyes for ten minutes. When her strength returned, she geared up again and continued the search.

The karaoke bar corridors twisted like a maze. After wandering in circles, she somehow ended up back at the small shop.

She let out a quiet sigh, checked the room numbers with her flashlight, and kept going.

Half an hour later, she finally found the liquor storage door hidden behind a potted plant in an office.

The moment she entered, the heavy scent of alcohol hit her head-on. Her vision blurred slightly. She paused, steadying herself, then carefully descended the steps.

Outside, it was suffocating. Inside, it was cool.

Rows of wine racks filled the room. Red wine. Cocktails. Bottles upon bottles.

She picked one up and gave it a cautious sniff.

When temperatures had once dropped -10 degrees, she had drunk a little strong liquor to warm herself. But red wine carried a completely different aroma.

She was no connoisseur, but she knew the basics. Wine did not have a strict expiration date. Only a peak drinking window. If stored properly, it could last for years.

Carefully, she swept the racks and every bottle into her space.

The empty cellar looked as though a swarm of locusts had passed through.

Leaving the liquor room, she continued upward.

Foot massage.

For some reason, just seeing those words made her laugh.

Every time authorities cracked down on vice, places like this were never far behind.

For most people, the first association with such establishments was not exactly wholesome.

After searching, she ended up taking more than a dozen foot tubs.

So much for her assumptions.

The game room connected directly to the tea lounge. Boardgame tiles were scattered across the floor. She stepped around them and entered the tea storage instead.

Tea, like wine, could last indefinitely if kept properly.

Sure enough, she found several sealed crates in the storeroom.

Today must have been her lucky day. Not a single floor had been a waste.

Close to two in the morning, Evelyn reached the top floor.

It was luxury upon luxury. Even the tiles beneath her feet were different from the rest of the building. She had no time to admire the craftsmanship.

She headed straight for the parking area.

Beyond the garage was even a private helipad.

The lives of the ultra-wealthy were beyond her imagination.

Still, she was more than happy to benefit from their leftovers.

Several cars remained parked in the corner.

Her heart nearly burst with excitement.

Without hesitation, she stored every vehicle into her space.

Though she had already stripped the building floor by floor, she was not ready to leave. The top level likely held more hidden treasures.

She would search thoroughly.

She checked the time.

It was two thirty in the morning.

She took out a piece of chocolate, replenished some energy, and began checking rooms one by one.

Perhaps the owners had already evacuated anything truly valuable.

After a full sweep, she found only two safes.

Still, imagining they might contain gold or jewels made her grin.

Perfect. If she ended up dead, at least she'd be buried in style.

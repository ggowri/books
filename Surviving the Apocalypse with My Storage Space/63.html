Chapter 63 Scorching Heat, The Refugees

After Evelyn returned home, she stripped the kitchen bare. The refrigerator, the cabinets, pots and pans, knives, every last bottle of seasoning, all of it went straight into her storage space.

She left behind only the bare essentials. Of the three security doors in the apartment, she removed two. They had been custom-made from the best materials money could buy. If they had to leave, she was taking them with her.

At ten o'clock that night, Evelyn, Calvin, and Eddie left Willow Heights right on schedule and headed for the auto repair shop behind the complex.

Evelyn knew the place well. Before the apocalypse, her father's sedan had been scraped once, and she had brought it there for repairs. The shop sat on higher ground. Even with the flooding, the water would probably only reach the second floor.

The three of them moved quickly. In half an hour, they reached the repair shop.

What stood before them was nothing but ruins. The flood had torn the place apart.

They fell silent.

"There's a small building next to it. Let's check that one," Calvin said.

He gripped his shovel and strode over. Branches, shattered glass, and twisted steel bars, all carried there by the flood, blocked the entrance. Without hesitation, he swung the shovel and got to work.

Evelyn and Eddie rushed over to help. Together, they cleared the debris from the doorway. Evelyn pulled out her lockpicking tools and opened the door.

Inside, chaos greeted them. The floor was a mess of wreckage.

On the third floor, in one of the rooms, they found several skeletons. Scattered across the floor were shed snake skins.

On the fourth floor, in what used to be a storage room, they found several spare tires, along with tools and gasoline.

After confirming the tire sizes, they carried everything downstairs. Before leaving, Evelyn picked up a piece of cloth and gently covered the skeletons in the room.

It took two trips to haul everything back. Once home, Evelyn grabbed a bottle of water and drank it down in one go. When she peeled off her clothes, nearly two pounds of dirt fell to the floor.

With spare tires secured, their confidence about leaving rose a little higher.

But a sudden change in the dead of night a few days later shattered the fragile peace of Willow Heights.

A massive wave of refugees poured into Elaris. They spoke in unfamiliar dialects and moved into different residential complexes, searching for shelter and supplies.

That day, Eddie and Calvin went out to dig for wild greens. They blended in with the newcomers and gathered some information.

"They're refugees from Hawthorne County, Newford County, and Stonehaven City," Eddie said. "Word is Drakepool Base is taking in survivors. They're all heading there."

Everyone knew those places. Hawthorne County and Newford County were more than a hundred kilometers away. Stonehaven City was even farther, a city nearly three hundred kilometers from Elaris.

A lot of people had come this time. Several buildings in Willow Heights were already packed with newcomers.

"It feels like thousands, maybe even tens of thousands," Eddie added. "I asked one of the guys today. He said there are still plenty more coming."

They gathered at Calvin's apartment, their expressions grim as they discussed what to do next.

"When some of them came in today, they saw us," Eddie said, unusually serious. "I'm worried they might band together and start looting."

"I still have a bit of leftover medicine," Calvin said. "It's a toxin I made from poisonous moths and toad glands. I'll bring it over in a bit. We can smear some on everyone's defensive blades. The security door downstairs is solid. I'm more worried about them climbing up from the outside."

"These people show up, and now there aren't any wild greens or giant rats left outside," Ray muttered, his mood sinking.

"Northside has basically been burned to the ground. Our district is the only place that's still relatively safe. It makes sense they'd flood in here," someone replied. "No one goes out for the next couple of days. Let's watch and see what happens."

Everyone was uneasy. Evelyn was no exception.

The refugees showed no sign of leaving for Drakepool Base anytime soon. During the day, things were barely manageable. But at night, they poured out in groups, scavenging for wild greens and giant rats. Fights broke out in the complex over a single plant. Shouting, screaming, crying echoed between the buildings.

Some of them gathered downstairs, begging the residents of Block D for food. When no one answered, they started hurling stones at the windows.

The bombs Calvin made were crude and small. They had very limited destructive power, but they were loud and produced thick smoke. Because materials were scarce, he managed to make only five. They could not afford to use them unless it was absolutely necessary.

Evelyn began clearing out the bedroom. She stored the bed, wardrobes, air conditioner, even the outdoor unit into her space. In the living room, she left only a folding cot, a fan, and a battery.

To make herself look pitiful enough, she had not washed her hair or face in days. She dug out the dirtiest, most tattered clothes she could find and wore them whenever she went out. A mask covered her face, and over that she wrapped a grimy scarf.

Among the refugees, there were few elderly people or children. Most were young men and women.

By the third day, a group gathered downstairs holding torches, shouting threats at the residents of Block D.

Evelyn stood in the fourth-floor hallway, a crossbow in her hands. From this position, her aim would be better.

"Listen up in there!" a man yelled from below. "You've got three minutes. Bring down every scrap of food you have. If you don't, I'll set this whole building on fire and burn you alive inside!"

Evelyn exchanged a look with the others. Anger and fear flickered across their faces.

She flicked a lighter and touched the flame to the tip of a bolt. The arrowhead caught fire instantly.

"I coated it with poison and gasoline," she said quietly. "At a time like this, killing one to warn the rest is enough."

The bolt shot out like a gust of wind. It pierced straight through the chest of the man holding the torch and buried itself in the sand behind him.

He collapsed stiffly to the ground. The people beside him scattered in terror.

"If you're not afraid to die, come on up!" Calvin bellowed. "I haven't had meat in a long time. If you're delivering yourselves to my door, I won't say no!"

The crowd below immediately began pleading for mercy.

Flames suddenly erupted from the dead man's chest. Within moments, his body was swallowed by fire.

Evelyn had stunned the would-be attackers. One came, she killed one. Two came, she killed two. At that moment, she looked like the Grim Reaper. Even Calvin and Eddie, standing behind her, felt a chill crawl up their spines.

Down below, the fleeing refugees caught the scent of burning flesh. One by one, their eyes gleamed in the firelight. As if possessed, they rushed toward the flames.

"Are they… going to eat him?" Evan's eyes widened. He stumbled back two steps, horrified.

Evelyn glanced down. In the darkness, their vicious faces were painfully clear to her as they fought over the "food." It was as if they had forgotten that the man had been their companion only moments ago.

Hunger could strip away humanity and leave nothing but the beast.

She had intended to retrieve the crossbow bolt from downstairs. Now, she wanted nothing more than to get away from the sight.

She turned and headed upstairs. The others cast one last look outside before quickly following her.

"Do you think Evelyn's going to break down?" Eddie asked quietly, worry creeping into his voice.

"She killed the one who provoked us with her own hands. Then she watched his own people tear him apart," Calvin said, patting Eddie on the shoulder. "Of course it won't sit easy with her. But she won't cry. She's not that fragile."

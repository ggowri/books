Chapter 67 Departure: Part 2

The roads were nearly impassable. After an hour, they had covered little more than ten kilometers. By afternoon, the convoy still had not made it out of Elaris.

From the crowd trailing behind on foot, arguments and scuffles broke out again and again. People fought over a single hard biscuit, fists flying without hesitation.

Wendy sat on Evelyn's lap, her small hands pressed against the window as she stared outside.

At six in the evening, the convoy suddenly stopped.

People climbed out of their vehicles and rushed into nearby abandoned buildings to relieve themselves. Some less restrained simply squatted by the roadside, only to be lashed by the men collecting protection fees. Cursing under their breath, they scrambled away.

Knock. Knock. Knock.

A filthy hand slapped against their car window.

Evelyn turned her head. A woman in her forties or fifties stood outside, her face gaunt and desperate.

"Please, give me something to eat. I'm starving. I'm old. Have some pity. Just a bite."

Around them, refugees on foot swarmed other vehicles, begging for food.

Only Evelyn and Wendy were in the car. The others had gone to find a restroom.

Evelyn ignored the woman completely. She took out the pocket watch hanging around her neck and checked the time.

Six thirty.

Eddie and the others had been gone for half an hour just to use the bathroom.

The knocking continued.

Evelyn tore off a piece of tissue and gently stuffed it into Wendy's ears. Then she closed her own eyes and leaned back, pretending to rest.

The air inside the vehicle was a suffocating mix of smells. Though the temperature had dropped, thirty degrees was still sweltering. Sweat. Unwashed bodies. Sour, stale odors that clung to everything.

A few minutes later, Eddie and the others returned.

The moment they opened the door, a wave of stench hit them. Their faces twisted in unison.

"Open the windows. Let the smell out."

"Can't," someone replied. "If we open them, people will swarm us and try to grab things. Just endure it. We'll open them once we're moving."

Calvin handed Evelyn a knife and pointed toward a building on the right.

"You go. That one looks cleaner. I'll stay back and cover you."

Evelyn lowered her head, carrying Wendy in her arms as she quickly headed toward the building.

Along the way, several people sized them up. Evelyn flashed the knife in her hand. The stares immediately withdrew.

She found a relatively clean room. After entering, she braced herself against the door and let Wendy go first.

Taking out a bottle of water from her space, Evelyn gulped down a large mouthful. Only then did her throat feel slightly less parched.

Wendy was obedient. She had not cried or complained the entire journey. She did whatever she was told.

When the little girl finished, Evelyn handed her two pieces of candy and told her to eat quickly. She wedged a board against the door before going to the back herself.

"Wendy, do you hear anything outside?" Evelyn whispered after finishing, pointing toward the door.

Wendy nodded immediately and held up two fingers.

"Two people talking?"

Another nod.

Evelyn slung Wendy onto her back and gripped the knife tightly in her palm.

The moment she opened the door, two men standing outside closed in around her.

"Leave the kid," one of them said coldly, machete in hand. "We'll spare your life."

They stepped closer.

"Go to hell."

Evelyn could not stand the way they looked at Wendy. She kicked the man in front squarely between the legs. As he doubled over in pain, her blade flashed across his throat.

The second man roared and raised his machete, but before he could strike, someone stabbed him from behind.

His eyes widened. He collapsed stiffly to the ground.

Evelyn met Calvin's gaze.

Without a word, they picked up the two machetes, dragged the bodies into the room, and left quickly.

Back at the car, Eddie immediately sensed something was wrong.

"Run into trouble?"

Evelyn nodded.

She placed Wendy into Eddie's arms, then pulled out a cloth and carefully wiped the blood from her knife.

"What's going on with the convoy? It's not even seven. They're stopping for the night already?"

"Eat something first," Calvin said. "If we're staying here tonight, everyone stays sharp. Watch the stuff on the roof. And the tires. And the fuel."

They each took out a little food from their backpacks, just enough to take the edge off their hunger. Calvin, Eddie, and Evelyn rotated driving duties.

At eight o'clock, the convoy suddenly lurched forward again. Everyone had no choice but to stay alert and continue.

"Earlier, I saw a few men drag a woman away," Ray said quietly.

"And that old lady who was begging for food? Someone knocked her out with a stick and hauled her to the back."

The atmosphere inside the vehicle turned heavy.

It was only their first day out, and already it felt like nothing was going right.

"Do not eat outside," Calvin reminded them again. "No matter how pitiful someone looks, don't give away food. Always move in groups. No one acts alone. That includes me and Eddie."

Eddie was driving now. Evelyn handed him a small bottle of medicated oil to smell. The sharp scent snapped him back to full alertness.

Drakepool Base was only thirty to forty kilometers outside Elaris.

But the direct road had been destroyed by flooding. They had to take a detour, adding several hundred kilometers to the journey.

The detour would pass through one city, two counties, and several small towns.

Calvin had learned that information by trading a pack of cigarettes. The route had been mapped out by previous scavenging teams. The people in the lead vehicles likely had connections inside Drakepool Base. That explained their arrogance.

"When we stop to rest, we can dig wild greens along the roadside," Evelyn said. "I checked. They're growing well."

"Good idea," Calvin agreed. "We split into two teams. Three people per team. One team digs while the other stays to guard the supplies. I'm most worried about the fuel. If someone runs out, they'll definitely try to rob others."

At two in the morning, the convoy stopped again.

One of the men collecting protection fees banged loudly on a metal basin. The noise cut through the darkness, and everyone fell silent.

"Rest in place for three hours. We move again after that."

As soon as he finished speaking, people began climbing out of their vehicles.

Evelyn, Eddie, and Ray formed one team. After taking care of urgent needs, they grabbed shovels and started digging for wild greens. Seeing them, others joined in.

At two in the morning, the sky was already faintly brightening.

Evelyn kept her head down, quickly prying up a cluster of greens and stuffing them into the cloth bag hanging at her chest.

After bending over for too long, she felt dizzy when she stood up. Probably low blood sugar. She slipped a piece of candy into her mouth. The sweetness steadied her.

Once her bag was full, she returned to the vehicle and switched shifts with Calvin, Wendy, and Evan.

Alone in the car, Evelyn quickly sorted through the greens. She kept only a small handful outside and stored the rest in her space.

She did not waste water washing them. Everyone simply wiped them off and ate. She did the same.

Eddie and Ray brought back even more than she had. Perhaps because of the drop in temperature, the wild greens outside Elaris were especially lush.

"Some old lady actually tried to grab my greens just now," Eddie complained as he climbed back in. "I kicked her. I'm half blind in the dark digging these things. And she wants to rob me?"

"What about your night vision goggles?" someone asked.

Eddie shook his head. "Not taking them out. I don't want them stolen."

Chapter 121 Trapped in the Medical Division

Evelyn was trapped inside the Medical Division. Apart from the occasional crackle of the broadcast system, no one had the slightest idea what was happening outside.

The Division had run every possible test on Julian. Blood work, scans, CTs, the lot. There was nothing wrong with him at all. If anything, his results suggested he had been eating rather too well. He was almost overnourished.

But Julian would not be swayed. He insisted again and again that there were worms inside his body. The laboratory researchers came to examine him several times, and even gave him the latest drugs they had on hand, yet his attitude remained as rigid as ever.

“Evelyn, they’ve just brought in a young woman. Go and have a look.”

“All right. I’m coming.”

Evelyn adjusted her protective suit and mask, then headed for the emergency floor, where she found Iris sprawled on the ground, crying out in distress.

There had been four girls in Evelyn’s university dormitory: Evelyn, Sophie, Iris, and Francesca.

Francesca had been taken by vultures. A few days earlier, Evelyn had seen Iris and Sophie at the Growing Grounds. Both of them had changed a great deal. In Evelyn’s memory, Iris had been slightly plump, while Sophie had a soft baby face. But the Iris before her now was gaunt enough to be barely recognisable.

Evelyn went over and began examining her.

“When did you start feeling unwell?”

“Yesterday afternoon.”

“Did you drink untreated water?”

“No. I drank boiled water.”

Evelyn started an IV. It contained the short-acting medicine the Medical Division had managed to produce.

It would not kill the worms. It only dulled the suffering for a while.

The Division had also brewed a huge vat of herbal medicine and made every patient drink it. It was meant to force the worms out. After taking it, people suffered terribly. Some vomited. Others had violent diarrhoea. That, apparently, was the whole point.

Evelyn had yet to see any real effect from it.

Iris’s symptoms were relatively mild. She had probably ingested only a small amount. After taking the medicine, she vomited twice.

“Doctor, I’m not going to die, am I?”

Evelyn said nothing. She was not foolish enough to make a promise like that.

“Your voice sounds familiar,” Iris said uncertainly. “Do we know each other?”

“Women’s voices often sound alike. Mine probably just resembles someone you know.”

Iris nodded, and at that moment Dr. Hargreaves beckoned to Evelyn from a short distance away. Evelyn rose at once.

“Don’t go, Doctor. You can’t just leave me here.”

Evelyn glanced at the hand gripping her arm, then calmly peeled Iris’s fingers away.

“There are other doctors here. Stay on the drip for now. If there’s a problem, call for me.”

When Evelyn reached Dr. Hargreaves, his expression was grim.

“Evelyn, one in every five deaths these past two days has been a child. There are hardly any children in the base to begin with. What are we meant to do?”

“Does the base have any solution at all?”

“I haven’t been told anything.”

“Evelyn, another child has just been brought in. Go quickly.”

Clara looked ready to collapse. She was swaying as she walked. Evelyn told her to rest for a while in the utility room, but Clara waved her off. Ever since Nurse Yates met with an accident, no new staff had been sent over from the Surgical Unit. On this entire floor, Clara was the only nurse left. If she stopped, everyone else would be under even more strain.

“Here.” Evelyn slipped a sweet into Clara’s palm and immediately hurried away.

The moment she saw Wendy in Calvin Shaw’s arms, Evelyn’s face changed.

“Calvin, what’s wrong with Wendy?”

“She said she wasn’t feeling well, so they brought us here. But when Eddie sent us the medicine you mentioned, we took it. Wendy even had an extra tablet. It’s probably only a cold, isn’t it?”

Evelyn took Wendy from him and headed straight for the utility room.

“Lock the door behind you.”

Once inside, she laid Wendy flat on the floor and began checking her over.

“Has she had cramping or vomiting?”

Calvin shook his head. “No. She just says she feels bad, not right. I keep asking what hurts, but she can’t explain.”

“Wendy, tell Evelyn, does it hurt here?” Evelyn gently pressed her chest. Wendy shook her head.

“And here?” She pressed her lower abdomen and stomach.

Wendy shook her head again. From what Evelyn could tell, this did not look like a nematode infection.

“Has she eaten anything unusual in the past couple of days?”

Calvin scratched furiously at his head while he thought. After a long moment, he smacked his forehead.

“A few days ago I bought her five tins of food. She ate all of them.”

Tinned food?

Evelyn immediately thought of the fishy smell from the tins and felt her own stomach turn.

“There was nothing on the label saying what meat it was made from, and there wasn’t any production date either. I think she may have eaten expired food,” Calvin said.

But tinned food usually kept for ages.

“Stay here for a bit,” Evelyn said. “I think it may be a reaction to the food. If it is only that, she should be able to leave before long.”

She stood up and took a glucose solution from her pocket, handing it to Calvin.

“Give this to Wendy. I’ll come back and check on her later.”

After stepping outside, Evelyn locked the utility room door.

Thankfully, Wendy’s symptoms did not resemble a nematode infection. If they had, Evelyn did not know how she would have saved her.

“Doctor, I don’t feel well.”

The instant Iris spotted Evelyn, she began shouting again.

Evelyn walked over and crouched in front of her. At that same moment, a woman lying nearby with her eyes closed suddenly started convulsing. Evelyn reacted on instinct, dragging Iris aside.

The woman convulsed for a few seconds, then began vomiting blood.

Even when death takes only a matter of minutes, it can still feel agonisingly slow, both for the person dying and for everyone forced to watch.

Some people were so frightened they burst into tears. Others, unable to bear it, stepped forward as though to help.

But the moment they saw the worms wriggling in the blood, they recoiled in horror.

The woman died slowly in agony, and in her final sliver of consciousness, she watched herself being dragged away.

“I don’t want to die. Doctor, save me. Please save me.”

“The base has abandoned us. The doctors won’t save us. Let’s force our way in and find medicine ourselves.”

Someone started shouting it, and after one brief stunned pause, others began to join in.

They surged into every room they could reach, tearing through cupboards and supplies in search of medicine. Evelyn and Dr. Hargreaves were shoved into a corner. Neither of them tried to stop it. They knew better than anyone that, when death was pressing in, getting hold of some supposed life-saving drug became the last fragile hope these people had left.

More truthfully, they had already lost their reason.

The security guards outside realised trouble had broken out and came in at once.

“Stop right there!”

They shouted several times, but it made no difference.

The patients stared at the weapons in their hands, and a feverish light came into their eyes. As one, they lunged.

The first person was shot and dropped instantly, but the ones behind did not stop.

A gun was wrested from one of the guards. Panic flashed across the guards’ faces. They tried to make a run for the door, but the tide of bodies surged after them and dragged them back.

Evelyn forced her way through the crowd, hauled Dr. Hargreaves up from the floor, then spotted Clara trembling in a corner. She seized Clara with one hand and Dr. Hargreaves with the other, and ran for the utility room as fast as she could.

Her hands were shaking so badly when she unlocked the door that she nearly snapped the key in the lock.

The moment she pushed it open and saw Calvin and Wendy inside, she let out a breath.

She locked the door again at once.

Dr. Hargreaves and Clara collapsed onto the floor, both of them shaking uncontrollably, their eyes filled with fear.

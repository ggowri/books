Chapter 50 Heatwave, Vultures: Part 2

At 3:40 a.m., Evelyn kept her head down and moved fast through the streets and alleys. The broken road was littered with shattered glass and lengths of rebar jutting up from the ground. Buses and private cars lay overturned in the mud. In the dim light, the outlines of malls and cinemas still hinted at the old days, when the city had been loud and bright and full of life.

A blast of hot wind swept through. Evelyn stopped instantly and held her breath. Only when it passed did she keep going.

Every few steps, she ran into another pile of dead snakes. Now and then she'd spot other people, moving as quickly as she was, slipping out of empty buildings. No one spoke. They ignored each other on purpose, heads lowered as they brushed past, each hurrying off in the opposite direction.

Sweat had already soaked her back when a sharp, unbearable pain shot up through her foot.

Evelyn lifted her right boot. Three nails had pierced the sole of her hiking shoe. They had gone straight through the thick rubber and into the bottom of her foot.

She pulled a wrench from her space and yanked the nails out one by one. Their rusted tips were streaked with blood. She didn't have time to clean the wound out here. She shoved her boot back on and forced herself onward.

At exactly 4:00 a.m., Evelyn made it back to Willow Heights. The moment she stepped into Block D, a blast of light rose from the horizon and swallowed the world.

She hurried upstairs. When the sun broke the edge of the sky, the heat wave nearly scorched her eyes. Evelyn suspected the temperature had climbed again.

She unlocked her door, stumbled inside, and stripped off her clothes and boots. Then she turned on the air conditioner.

Hands on her hips, she stood in front of it and let the cold air slam into her body.

After a few minutes, the burning in her skin finally eased. Evelyn took out the cooling herbal tea she'd brewed last time, cracked open a vial of heat-sickness tonic, and chugged it in one go.

The taste hit her like a punch. Her face contorted before she could stop it.

The temperature really had gone up another degree. Even the cactus on her balcony looked a little wilted. Evelyn carried her clothes into the bathroom and took a cold shower.

The injury on the bottom of her foot was bad. The nails had been rusty. Evelyn gave herself a tetanus shot, disinfected the wound, washed it out, and wrapped it in gauze.

This trip hadn't been only about scavenging. She'd also dumped all the trash that had piled up in her space. To avoid unnecessary trouble, she'd gone farther away to get rid of it.

She unloaded everything she'd collected last night and stacked it in the living room. After recounting and organizing it, she kept the rice, flour, grain, cooking oil, and essentials, and stored the rest away. She had a bit of a neat-freak streak. Even inside her space, everything had to be lined up properly.

Her space was basically a giant warehouse. Evelyn had no idea how big it really was, only that it never seemed to fill up, no matter how much she shoved inside. She looked at the pile on the floor and rubbed her chin. Next time she went out, she should find some shelves to keep things organized.

After a quick breakfast, she decided to sleep and make up for lost rest. She turned on the humidifier and lay down.

She slept straight through until three in the afternoon.

The moment she woke, she heard a violent pounding from the balcony. Rubbing her eyes, Evelyn stepped out, and the second she saw what was circling outside, she snapped fully awake.

A vulture beat its wings past her window. In its thick, powerful talons, it was holding a person.

The victim was thrashing wildly. The struggle only enraged the vulture more, and it slammed its wings against the glass of Evelyn's balcony, the impacts thudding hard enough to shake the pane.

Evelyn's appearance made the person in the vulture's talons react instantly.

"Help! Please help me, I see you. Save me!"

It was a woman. Her voice sounded young. But the moment she finished shouting, the vulture suddenly loosened its grip. The woman screamed as she dropped.

Right before she hit the ground, the vulture dove, snatched her back up, climbed dozens of meters into the air, then dropped her again.

Grab. Drop. Grab. Drop. Over and over.

Evelyn narrowed her eyes as she watched. It felt like the vulture was playing with her, the way a cat toys with a mouse. It doesn't kill right away. It frightens it, teases it, drags it out until it gets bored, then finishes the job.

But vultures were not patient animals like cats. They were vicious, brutal. When they caught prey, they tore in immediately. They didn't usually waste time on games like this.

Unless the woman had done something first, something that made it retaliate in this twisted way.

"Help… help… please…"

Her voice grew weaker and weaker. Eventually, she passed out.

Just then, someone knocked on Evelyn's door.

Evelyn glanced at the vulture, still doing whatever it pleased outside. Then she went back inside, changed her clothes, and only then opened the door.

"Evie, did you see that eagle outside?"

"It's a vulture," Evelyn corrected him, dead serious.

Calvin blinked. "A vulture? How would Elaris even have something like that?"

Evelyn didn't answer. She looked at the four people in the hallway.

Wendy was draped over Calvin's back, her hair a messy bird's nest. Her big eyes were fixed on Evelyn. Ray and Ian stood behind them, heads lowered, silent.

"Do you think it'll show up in swarms like the venom moths, the toads, and the snake tide?" Calvin asked worriedly.

"It's possible. If we don't go out during the day, we'll be fine. Vultures only hunt in daylight. They won't come out at night."

Only then did Calvin's expression ease. "So it's another crisis."

No one suggested going outside to save the woman. Everyone understood the same truth. Trying to rescue someone from a vulture's talons was pure fantasy.

"Ms. Evie, what happened to your foot?" Ray stepped closer, staring at her with worry.

"I went out last night. Stepped on nails on the way back," Evelyn said. "You two should be careful too. There's broken glass, nails, and rebar everywhere."

"I've got an ointment at home. I'll go get it for you," Calvin said, already turning to head upstairs.

Evelyn stopped him immediately.

"No need. I have ointment too, and it's not that bad. Also, the temperature went up again today."

Ian and Ray exchanged a look. In both their eyes, despair and fear sat heavy, refusing to fade.

"You can go check Maple Haven," Evelyn said. Maple Haven was the high-end complex she'd gone to last night.

Calvin's eyes lit up. He immediately looked at Ray and Ian. "Tonight, I'll take you two over there."

Everyone went back to their own apartments. Evelyn stepped onto the balcony and looked down. The vulture was already gone. On the ground below, a wide splash of blood had dried dark under the sun.

*****

Francesca regretted it more than anything, that impulsive moment when she'd picked up a stone and thrown it at the vulture.

She'd never expected it to hold a grudge. Or worse, to be able to pick her out from a crowd.

When the vulture seized her, Sophie and Iris didn't try to save her.

Looking at her ruined legs, Francesca felt nothing but regret, and an endless, burning hatred.

But she thought she'd seen Evelyn. Even through the glass, she wouldn't mistake her. That short-haired girl was Evelyn.

In the final moments of her life, Francesca hated everyone. Sophie. Iris. Evelyn. Not a single one of them had saved her.

*****

That night, Calvin took Ray and Ian and left Willow Heights. He locked Wendy inside his apartment.

The wound on the bottom of Evelyn's foot showed signs of infection. When she walked, she could only keep her right foot lifted.

Evelyn pulled out a map of Elaris and circled the areas with higher ground, residential complexes, industrial zones, and commercial districts.

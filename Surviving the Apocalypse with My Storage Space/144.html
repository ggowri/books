Chapter 144 Toxic Haze 6

The apartment block came down with a thunderous crash.

Evelyn was still clutching Toby, while Adrian had her in his arms, and the three of them looked absurd in that position.

The collapse sent up a vast cloud of dust. Evelyn coughed nonstop, grit stinging her eyes until they felt dry and raw.

Adrian moved fast. He carried them more than a hundred metres away before setting Evelyn down, and she immediately lowered Toby to the ground beside her.

The corroded building had finally given way.

There had still been dozens of people living inside.

Evelyn could hear faint cries for help. Residents from the neighbouring buildings came rushing out as well, and everyone began trying to rescue those trapped inside.

She looked down at Toby. The child was badly frightened. He stood there staring at the collapsed building, his bony little hands trembling without pause.

Evelyn took his hand and gave it a quiet squeeze.

More people from the safe zone arrived when they heard the noise and joined the rescue. The security team began organising everyone, and then a weak infant’s cry reached every ear there. Even so, after half an hour of digging through the rubble, no one could work out exactly where the sound was coming from.

Adrian glanced at Evelyn, then silently stepped forward and joined the rescue.

An hour later, they pulled the baby out.

The others were rescued one after another as well. More than thirty people had been living in that building. Fifteen of them died, including the woman who had bled to death after childbirth, and the man who had shielded the baby in his arms.

“That child’s got luck on his side,” someone said. “Poor little thing, though. Born today and already without parents. His father was a hero in the end. Held the baby so tightly his own spine was crushed by the concrete.”

“What on earth happens now? How is a newborn meant to survive?”

Evelyn looked at the wailing baby in the arms of one of the guards, then at the mangled bodies laid out on the ground, her expression unreadable.

Toby stared blankly at the child, then suddenly pulled free of Evelyn’s hand. He walked over to the guard holding the baby and reached out as though to touch him.

“My brother.”

“Is this your brother?” the guard asked, lowering the baby towards him.

Toby said nothing.

The people nearby all looked at him with pity.

Evelyn brushed the dust from Adrian’s hair. If he had not gone over to help and dug the baby out himself, the rescue would likely have gone on much longer.

Once it was over, the security team took away the bodies for burial. The survivors needed to be housed elsewhere.

Evelyn and the others joined in treating the injured. Several who had been pulled out alive died soon afterwards from blood loss.

Clara came to help as well. She mostly looked after Toby and Archie.

Archie was the baby. Clara had given him the name, and she wanted to adopt him.

“Have you really thought this through?” Evelyn asked. “Raising a child in a place like this won’t be easy.”

Clara gave a bitter little smile. “I know. But Archie’s too pitiful. No one else is willing to take him in, so he’d just be left to die. Elliot and I have talked it over. We’ve no idea how long life will stay like this, and ever since the acid-rain aftereffects started, I’ve had a feeling people may struggle to conceive at all in future. Evelyn, we’ve decided not to have children of our own. We’ll raise him as ours instead.”

“You ought to ask Toby what he thinks,” Evelyn said. “He is his brother, after all.”

She did not try to talk Clara out of it.

Clara nodded. “I know. I already asked him. He doesn’t mind. And Secretary Bennett is thinking of taking Toby in.”

That surprised Evelyn. Quiet, reserved Secretary Bennett wanted to adopt Toby?

“All right,” she said. “I’ve got a bit of flour. I’ll bring some over later. Make him a thin porridge with it.”

Clara thanked her and took Archie away.

Archie went back with Clara to be cared for, and Toby was taken in by Secretary Bennett. Whatever else happened, both boys had ended up somewhere better.

But buildings were collapsing every day now.

Nearly half the tall blocks in the safe zone had already come down.

All the remaining survivors began crowding into the villa district, the Administration Hall, and the Underground Quarter.

And once people were forced together, tensions rose again. First it was arguments over soil. Every area formed its own little faction, and outsiders were no longer allowed in to dig. Before long, it went further. People were not even allowed to enter another area to barter for food or supplies.

Wherever there were people, there was trouble.

Whitmore drew up another set of rules for the safe zone, appointing one manager for each area.

Even with only a little over ten thousand survivors left, there was still no shortage of things needing to be dealt with.

Evelyn kept out of all that. She focused on growing her vegetables and medicinal herbs. Every now and then someone came to ask her for treatment. As long as it was nothing too complicated, she never refused. People usually brought a cabbage or some maize as payment.

Whitmore had tried more than once, openly and otherwise, to win Adrian over, but every attempt failed. He even came to Evelyn twice and asked her to persuade Adrian to help them manage the safe zone.

What he really wanted was for Adrian to act as a sort of head guard, using force to suppress anyone who caused trouble.

Adrian had no interest in such things, and Evelyn had no wish to get involved either, so she simply fobbed him off with excuses.

“The nearby wood has all been picked clean,” Calvin said when he came by. “Secretary Bennett wants to organise a group to go farther out and collect firewood. Are you two coming? It’s turning cold again, and we ought to stock up.”

Farther out meant about two kilometres beyond the base.

With the toxic haze blocking their sight, and the road almost as difficult as walking in the dead of night, two kilometres was a long way.

Calvin had come to ask whether Adrian wanted to join them, but Evelyn turned him down.

She had plenty of firewood in her hidden space already, and she had taken a good deal of timber and shelving from the Medical Division basement before. There was no need to go to all that effort.

Apart from the occasional trip out to treat someone, Evelyn spent nearly all her time in the villa growing vegetables and herbs.

The temperature dropped by another two degrees. The haze carried a dampness to it that felt almost salty. If it got into your eyes, it stung badly.

Clara called Evelyn’s name from outside. When Evelyn stepped out, Clara handed her a white radish.

“You gave me flour last time. My radishes have come up now, so I brought the first one to you.”

Evelyn accepted it without ceremony. Then she glanced at Archie, strapped to Clara’s back, and checked his colouring. He looked very well cared for.

“He’s such a good baby,” Clara said. “Hardly ever cries or fusses. And Toby’s doing well too. I saw Secretary Bennett with him earlier. He’d found him a clean, thick set of clothes.”

“He was meant to come to you,” Evelyn said.

Clara grinned. “Yes. I liked him the moment I saw him. Everyone thinks I’m mad, but I’m happy. More than happy, really. I want to live properly now, more than I ever did before. I feel as though I’ve suddenly got courage I didn’t have.”

Evelyn pinched Archie’s little cheek. He blew a bubble at her.

They chatted for a while longer before Clara went home.

Evelyn went back inside and returned to tending her herbs. This batch was ready to harvest now, so she took out a hoe, dug them all up roots and all, and stored them away in her hidden space.

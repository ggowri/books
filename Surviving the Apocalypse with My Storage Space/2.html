Chapter 2 Stockpile

On top of the ten tons of rice, Evelyn placed an order for five tons of flour. She paid half the deposit, left the warehouse address with the supplier, and added the vendor's contact number. Then she made her way to a wholesale shop for cooking oil.

Her go-to excuse was simple: she was opening a supermarket back in her hometown. Most shopkeepers accepted it without question, and one even offered helpful advice—not to buy just one type of product, but to stock a variety of grains and oils.

Evelyn took the advice to heart. When it came to cooking oil, she ordered two hundred barrels of every type.

Salt, sauces, vinegar, seasonings, spice mixes, flavor packets—she swept the shelves clean like a one-woman army. For salt alone, she bought several tons.

When she passed by a seed shop, she didn't hesitate. She bought out their entire stock—grains and vegetables, everything. But that wasn't enough. She still needed fruit seeds, medicinal herbs, cotton, grasses, even tree seeds. No one knew how long the disaster would last. Ten years? A hundred? After the heat waves, plants had withered overnight. But as long as she had seeds, there was still hope.

At the sight of a liquor store, Evelyn stepped inside. When she asked for two massive vats of moonshine, the owner froze, staring at her for a long moment. Still, a customer was a customer. He took her money and promised to deliver everything personally the next day.

Evelyn spent the entire day at the wholesale market. As the sky began to darken, she made one last stop—a slaughterhouse.

There were several in Elaris, but this was her first time stepping into one.

The stench hit her the moment she approached the entrance. It was raw and thick with blood and rot. She'd already looked up the latest meat prices online, so she had a rough idea of what to expect.

As soon as she mentioned buying in bulk, the slaughterhouse owner gave her a strange look, like she'd lost her mind.

"Which market are you from? I haven't seen you before."

Evelyn forced a smile. "A friend referred me. I'm not from the market. My family's opening a canned food factory. I was told this was the best place to source meat."

The owner's suspicion eased. He nodded.

"How much are you looking to buy?"

Evelyn gave him a number. His eyes widened again, but after she handed over a hefty deposit, he didn't ask any more questions. He promised to deliver everything to her warehouse on time.

By the time she got home, the sky had gone dark. Evelyn calculated her expenses for the day, then pulled out her tablet and phone. She opened several apps and began placing more online orders.

After the apocalypse, water would become one of the rarest and most fought-over resources. Evelyn found a local plastic barrel manufacturer in Elaris. She placed an order for 2,000 water tanks—each capable of holding 3,000 liters—and 3,000 oversized water drums. With three months ahead of her, she had time to fill them all.

She clenched her fists, remembering how people had killed for a single bottle of water in her last life.

Water purifying tablets. Thermal clothing. Tactical jackets. Cotton boots. Rain boots. Goggles. Gloves. Hazmat suits. Disinfectant. Insulated face covers. Heat-resistant suits. Masks. Down coats. Socks. Underwear. Sanitary products.

Evelyn added everything to her cart and paid without hesitation.

As for medicine, she ordered them online, but the options online were limited. She'd have to buy more in person. Painkillers. Fever reducers. Antacids. Iodine. Alcohol. Hydrogen peroxide. Gauze. Allergy meds. Calcium tablets. Vitamins. Cephalosporin. Cold medicine. Ointment for frostbite.

She didn't stop there.

Shampoo. Body wash. Toothpaste and toothbrushes. Laundry detergent. Sunscreen. Just thinking about the way her skin had blistered in the scorching sun made her shiver.

She also bought twenty inflatable rescue boats and kayaks, plus life vests, lifebuoys, fishing nets, rods, waterproof mats—everything she could think of.

Toilet paper was something Evelyn decided to buy directly at the wholesale market. She also stocked up on matches, lighters, fire starters, and ignition sticks—ten full boxes, one hundred in each. That was a thousand fire tools. Enough to last her a lifetime.

She scratched her head, thinking. She still needed to buy generators, diesel, gasoline, kerosene, coal, gas canisters, camping stoves, bottled propane, and liquefied gas tanks. Those would be the trickier items to deal with.

As for solar panels, portable battery stations, firewood stoves, alcohol burners, and fuel blocks, she could get those at the hardware and trade market.

She also needed candles and flashlights. After the global blackout caused by the flood, humanity had been thrown back into the Stone Age.

Oh, and fresh produce. In the post-apocalyptic world, vegetables and fruits were more valuable than diamonds.

But this time, she had the space. A space where time stood still. Anything stored inside would never rot or spoil. The thought made her pulse race with excitement. She was going to stockpile vegetables and fruit like her life depended on it, because it did.

Deep into the night, Evelyn was still on her phone, shopping like a machine. She ordered decades' worth of clothing and shoes. Only when her stomach began to ache from hunger did she finally put the phone down. She stepped into the kitchen and quickly made herself a plate of pasta.

Once she was full, she walked into her parents' room. Carefully, she packed up their treasured books, bookshelves, wardrobes, and bed, placing them gently into her storage space.

By the time she lay down, it was already 1 a.m. After just a short rest, Evelyn got up, washed her face, and headed straight to the fruit wholesale market.

Elaris had a mild climate. Winters didn't get colder than 12°C, and summers never went past 27°C. Thanks to the weather, the city enjoyed a year-round bounty of fruits and vegetables.

Even in January, the fruit selection was still impressive, and prices were low due to the abundance. Evelyn decided to place an initial bulk order now and come back again in March when even more varieties would be in season.

She bought every type of fruit available by the ton. After all, once the apocalypse hit, fruit would become nothing but a memory.

By the time she left the fruit market, it was nearly ten in the morning. She headed straight to the vegetable wholesale market next.

The place was crowded, packed with buyers and sellers. Her large purchases didn't stand out at all.

Evelyn opened her phone and checked her memo list, making sure not to miss a single item.

Pumpkins, sweet potatoes, corn, potatoes, bitter melon, eggplants, cucumbers, garlic, chili peppers, radishes, ginger, leafy greens, napa cabbage, cabbage, tomatoes, lettuce, chives, all kinds of legumes and vegetable seeds.

She left no gap in her list.

She also bought 3,000 cartons of eggs.

Lunch was a quick bite at the farmers' market. After eating, she drove to the warehouse.

On the way, it suddenly occurred to her that she should buy fertilized chicken, duck, and goose eggs too. But that could wait—she'd order those online later.

Barely a few minutes after she arrived at the warehouse, a truck pulled in with her cooking oil delivery. Once the driver finished unloading, the rice delivery truck arrived right behind.

One of the drivers looked curious and tried to pry into her business. Evelyn handed him a red envelope. He smiled, nodded, and didn't say another word.

Just before nightfall, a truck from the water barrel factory showed up with her order—thousands of massive water containers.

Seeing them all stacked in her warehouse, Evelyn finally felt a small sense of relief settle in her chest.

She had also bought three industrial water purifiers, just in case.

Still, she didn't stop there. She placed an order with a purified water company for barrels of clean drinking water. She remembered all too well how brutal life had been without it. She never wanted to live through that again.

Back at home, Evelyn ate a cup of instant noodles. Then, she picked up her phone and went right back to ordering more supplies.

Heat packs, brown sugar, rock sugar, tents, mosquito repellent, battery-powered lanterns, pepper spray, stun batons, sleeping bags, thermal patches, winter coats, heat-resistant clothing, binoculars... and more.

Chapter 130 Settled, Leaving the Base

Two days later, the base announced the outcome to the residents.

The parasite incident had been triggered by the Hawthorne family’s vicious dog biting someone. The Hawthorne family was deemed seventy percent responsible for allowing their dog to harm people, while the base would bear thirty percent of the responsibility.

The base had mismanaged the situation. There had been no clean water and no strict quality control, allowing the livestock in the stockyard to contract rabies. When the livestock killed people, it was ignored; when the animals became sick, it was neglected. This vicious cycle ultimately led to irreparable consequences.

The deceased had not been properly handled, which was also the base’s responsibility. It was promised that a cemetery would be established on the western side of the base, with a monument commemorating the tens of thousands of residents who had perished in the disaster.

To compensate the residents, the Hawthorne family pledged to donate two-thirds of their supplies. All residents would receive two months of rent and utility waivers. The entire base was disinfected and purified, and after two days, clean water was made available directly from the taps.

The patriarch of the Hawthorne family, consumed by guilt over his failings, took his own life.

…

“What do you think of the settlement?”

“Nothing special,” Evelyn said, surprised that the root of everything had been a dog bite.

Thinking of the middle Hawthorne son who had died on Ashen Ridge, all her questions were answered.

Everything had traces leading back to that moment. He had kept a black dog, and after the earthquake, Evelyn had seen it crushed beneath an SUV. Around the dog lay scattered bones and meat scraps.

Animals breeding animals had ended up harming so many innocent people.

Evelyn approached Director Walsh, providing some supplies to help Officer Shaw and his team move into better housing, and she paid the rent for Units 601 and 602 for another three months. Living together these past few days had brought a sense of safety—someone was always looking out for each other.

She also resigned from the Medical Division, citing health reasons and the need to rest. Director Walsh looked at the supplies she had sent over and handled everything with a smile.

After Officer Shaw and his team moved to the seventh floor, Adrian had no choice but to return to his Unit 602. He was reluctant, but Evelyn was firm and decisive in sending him away.

Evelyn visited Dr. Hargreaves to inform him of her departure. He didn’t try to stop her, instead suggesting she might work at the Herb Gardens. It would be easier work, interacting with plants, and could help her cultivate patience and calm.

She refused. The base was now merely a refuge, not a workplace.

The base’s water purification efforts had paid off. Clean, clear water flowed from the taps, free from any chemical smell.

When the Hawthorne family distributed supplies, everyone—including Wendy—attended. Rations were handed out individually, small but appreciated.

Adrian also quit the hunting crews. Evelyn stayed in the room, avoiding the outside, and he mirrored her behaviour. Suddenly, the two unemployed had a lot of free time.

“I want to leave the base for a few days. Do you want to come?”

Adrian quickly packed, eyes full of anticipation.

The base was now open. Everyone could come and go freely. Evelyn wanted to see the outside world. The temperature had returned to normal, hovering around thirty degrees, but the lack of rain after the snow melted left an uneasy feeling.

They decided to leave for a while and took Pip and Lark with them. After informing Officer Shaw and the others, they departed the base.

Outside, many new roads had been built. Residents were beginning fresh lives, working in the wheat fields. Wooden carts rolled into the base one after another. Officer Shaw explained that the base was preparing for potential future cold snaps by stockpiling wood.

The livestock in the Underground Quarter had been cleared, and new chicks were already being raised and released into the stockyards. Life was gradually returning to normal—except for those who had died.

On the western side of the base, a cemetery had been established. A large monument stood at the forefront, engraved with the names of all deceased residents.

It served as both a warning and a grim memorial.

“Where are we going?”

Adrian followed Evelyn, with Pip beside him and Lark perched on his shoulder, eyes curious.

“First, we need to get rid of those three bodies. Leaving them in the storage space is unsettling.”

Adrian touched his nose, hesitant to argue—it had been his idea, after all.

“There’s a pit over there. We can just dump them in.”

Adrian had spent much time outside with the hunting crews. He knew the location of every pit and slope.

“Fine. Once we reach the safe area, I’ll get the car. We can drive.”

“I don’t know how,” he admitted.

“I do,” she replied.

“Evelyn, I know where the prey is. I can take you hunting.”

“That works. For a maximum of a month—we must return. June won’t be a peaceful month.”

At the giant pit, Evelyn looked down and released the three bodies from the storage space. Adrian kicked them in without hesitation.

“Let’s go.”

New green shoots sprouted across the forest floor, alongside wild grasses and edible plants. Nearby, a small path led to a spring-fed pond.

They rested for half an hour, ate lunch, and continued.

After a full day, they reached the safe zone. Adrian hunted while Evelyn checked water sources, temperatures, and any unusual changes in vegetation. By observing the plants, they could predict possible natural disasters and prepare in advance.

Her greatest fear remained insect swarms. Tiny, mosquito-sized flies filled the air, buzzing loudly and carrying viruses. A single bite could be fatal.

In the previous cycle, the insect disaster had lasted three years and never ended before her death. These flies attacked not just humans but all flora and fauna.

After scouting several mountains, they found no sign of the swarms. Adrian had caught plenty of prey along the way.

Heading southwest, they reached Faewood Ridge. Evelyn decided to stay here for a while. She found a flat area near a water source and parked the middle Hawthorne son’s RV.

“We’ll camp here for a week.”

Adrian cleaned his crossbow bolts and nodded in agreement.

“During these days, I can prepare more cooked food to store. What do you want?”

Adrian came forward eagerly, listing dishes seriously: “Salted duck, spiced beef, garlic pork, steamed carp, sweet and sour fish, bean paste fish, fish slices…”

“Stop, do you want to wear me out?”

He shook his head. “I can help.”

“No need. You’ll just get in the way.”

“Then we’ll make less. I can eat less too.”

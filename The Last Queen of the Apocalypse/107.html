Chapter 107 Join My Base

Rows of uniform buildings stretched neatly, spotlessly clean. No wandering survivors loitered on the streets, and patrol guards marched past at regular intervals.

Warm lights glowed from the houses on both sides, creating a peaceful atmosphere, almost like the old world before the apocalypse.

The car wound through a few turns and stopped in front of a two-story villa with a small front garden.

"Iris, this is Captain Flint's residence," Derek said.

Iris nodded and got out.

A man in casual clothes stepped out of the house.

Without his combat gear, Damon looked much gentler.

"Ms. Hale."

Iris smiled brightly. "Captain Flint! Good to see you."

"Welcome to Base K1."

Away from her own territory, Iris laid on the polite praise thick. "Wow! Your base is incredible—so many people, so lively. I'm really impressed. You clearly run things very well."

Damon wasn't used to her compliments and looked a little awkward, then spoke earnestly, "If you like it here, you should consider joining Base K1. I'd give you a high position. We need talented people like you."

Iris waved him off at once. "I'm just being polite. Don't take it to heart."

Damon's mouth twitched.

Actually, anyone who'd seen Iris's self-sufficient, fortified base would know she'd be insane to join someone else's.

Why would she voluntarily make life harder for herself?

Damon switched subjects. "Have you eaten yet?"

Iris patted her stomach. "Nope. Did you save dinner? We came on empty stomachs; you'd better treat us well."

Classic Iris—no beating around the bush.

Damon led the way. "I'll take you to the cafeteria."

On the way, Damon briefly explained Base K1's situation.

The base was nestled in a core area surrounded by mountains, with the tunnel as its only main entrance—great for defense, but no enough farmland. As more survivors arrived, food had become desperately scarce.

They ran a unified ration system: everyone got two pieces of bread and a bowl of vegetable soup per day at the public cafeteria.

For anything extra, residents were encouraged to scavenge outside. They only had to turn in half, keeping the rest.

The base also offered labor jobs. People who helped with construction earned bonus points, which could be exchanged for extra food.

A full day's work for an adult man earned about one point, enough for one more bread.

Under such tight conditions, Base K1 scraped by day to day.

Iris followed Damon to the second cafeteria. The first was for ordinary survivors; this one was exclusive to military personnel and administrators.

Damon swiped his ID three times at the machine.

The first meal was free; the next two deducted three points each from his account.

Soon, three sets of bread and hot soup were ready.

The bread was mixed-grain, and the soup had cabbage leaves and a few meatballs.

Clearly, even this simple fare was better than what the first cafeteria offered. This was the premium version.

Damon smiled. "Ms. Hale, our conditions here are pretty basic. I hope you don't mind."

Iris stared at the meal in disbelief. "You just got promoted, and you still eat the same thing as everyone else?"

"Yes."

Damon pushed his portion—which had a few extra meatballs—toward Iris as a gesture of hospitality.

Iris took a breath. "Honestly? You and your squad should join my base instead. Even my dogs would turn their noses up at this."

Damon froze.

Iris nibbled half a piece of bread out of courtesy, but she couldn't bring herself to touch the soup.

The smell alone was enough, and the low-quality meatballs were just as off-putting.

Her standards had risen too much. She simply couldn't stomach it.

She pushed the rest toward Logan instead.

After all, when there were 'good' things available, her own people came first. Even when mooching off Damon's meal, she stuck to that principle.

Luckily, Logan wasn't picky at all.

After they finished, Damon noticed Iris had barely touched her food.

"Let me show you our market later," he offered.

"Sure."

Iris was curious about the legendary Base K1 marketplace.

As they walked, she asked, "How many teams are going tomorrow?"

Damon thought for a second. "About seven teams total."

Iris frowned slightly. "The mission really isn't dangerous?"

"The warehouse and surrounding area are pretty empty," Damon explained. "But the main roads are thick with zombies. We'll move together as a group. You stick with us, and it should be manageable."

Iris nodded in understanding.

By then, they'd reached Base K1's market—an entire street of makeshift stalls.

People sat on the ground with their goods spread out in front of them—mostly clothes, jewelry, gold, silver, and jade.

There was even cash.

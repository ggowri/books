Chapter 125 Might Makes Right

For the first time in Zane's charmed, brilliant life, he encountered a crushing defeat he couldn't overcome.

He'd lost to Iris. Completely and utterly.

He stared into her eyes, each word cold and heavy. "I understand."

Iris chuckled softly.

He learned to bow his head fast.

Smart people always weigh gains and losses quickly.

She lifted her foot. "You can go."

Zane climbed to his feet. His men were also released, though all their weapons had been seized.

Everything went to Iris as promised—rewards for those who'd disarmed them.

Zane's group didn't dare ask for them back. He gave Iris one long, deep look, then led his team to their vehicles and drove away.

Once they were gone, the tension in the air melted away.

Iris had Logan continue distributing grain to Base K1.

Dust clouds swirled, so the other teams moved their trucks to a nearby clearing.

With night fallen, everyone decided to camp there.

Damon walked over to Iris. "Aren't you worried about making an enemy of Hope Base?"

Iris glanced up and grinned. "Shouldn't you be asking why Hope Base made an enemy of me? They messed with me, so they get what's coming."

Damon shook his head helplessly.

In this apocalypse, civilization had reverted to the law of the jungle: might makes right.

She had the power, so she could crush Hope Base.

The stronger she acted, the more others would fear her. Show weakness, and Hope Base would only push harder.

This world didn't reward backing down. One step back, and they'd take ten.

Her brutal display today—especially against the profit-obsessed Hope Base—would mark her as extremely dangerous, not to be provoked.

Why would they seek her out for revenge?

Distance also worked in her favor.

Hope Base was a hundred miles away, over zombie-infested terrain. They'd have to be suicidal to march through that danger just to fight her, losing hundreds of their own for a single strike.

Besides, Iris had already decided: from now on, she'd be Hope Base's worst nightmare. Wherever they appeared, she would be there to ruin them.

She was petty, and she held grudges for a hundred years. Get used to it.

Damon saw the satisfied glint in her bright eyes after her victory, and he turned away with a faint smile.

He could never be like that.

But he had to admit—he loved seeing her this way.

"Let's eat," he said. "Derek's heating up food."

Iris waved her hand. "Forget that. I'm in a good mood, so, dinner's on me."

She owed Derek and his team, at least, for drawing their guns to back her up without hesitation.

Iris paid back both grudges and favors.

She had Logan set up to cook noodles, pulling out meal packets, sausages, and instant noodles from her supplies.

Soon a huge pot bubbled, filling the air with rich, savory steam.

Iris's group and everyone from Base K1 got a big bowl loaded with noodles, meat, and sausages.

Before the apocalypse, this would have been cheap processed food.

Now? It tasted like heaven. Especially the instant noodles.

It was eleven at night. Everyone had fought all day, survived a life-or-death battle, and now sitting together eating hot noodles felt like pure bliss.

Most of them had been eating plain boiled vegetables with barely any oil back at their bases. That greasy, salty, savory broth made everyone want to lick the pot clean.

Slurping the golden, oily noodles, Base K1's soldiers were practically floating with joy.

Derek and the others crowded around Iris, calling her name with sincere admiration.

The four other bases watched, swallowing hard.

They ate their own plain rations—some had instant noodles, but nothing like Iris's feast, and none of the warmth.

They huddled with their own people, stealing glances at the lively group.

"Jace," Chloe said after watching for a while, turning to her brother. "Do you remember what I told you yesterday?"

"I do." Jace gave her the last piece of bacon from his bowl. "You were terrified. Lucky you didn't get bitten."

"No, Jace." Chloe set down her bowl and grabbed his arm seriously. "That zombie was inches from me... and it just stopped. I think Captain Hale saved me."

She pointed at Iris, her gaze firm.

After seeing Iris dominate everyone today, she was more certain than ever.

Iris had that kind of power. She couldn't think of any other reason the zombie would have frozen mid-attack.

Chapter 119 Damon's Woman

Strategic grain reserves were always built far from populated areas.

The surrounding land was desolate, and the lone highway leading here was completely empty—not a single car in sight.

The convoy rolled smoothly along the flat road.

As the sun sank and dusk began to fall, everyone finally spotted massive cylindrical silos rising from the ground like giant stone monuments.

They'd made it. They were finally going to have food.

One by one, the convoys followed Base K1 through the smashed main gates and stopped in front of the ten enormous structures.

The reserve ran on full automation. The entire complex was staffed by just ten workers—one per silo.

That was all. No one else.

A murmur swept through the group.

"We're finally here!"

"This is the national grain reserve! How much food must be inside?"

"Jesus, it says five thousand tons per silo!"

"Holy crap. There are ten of them—that's fifty thousand tons! Enough to feed all our bases for a hundred years!"

"Guys, we hit the jackpot!"

The remains of human civilization had left behind staggering wealth. Finding even one trove like this could carry survivors through countless crises.

Sure, 99.99% of civilization's treasures were now out of reach—sunken ships on the ocean floor, buried forever as relics of a fallen world. But even the tiny fraction that remained was more than enough.

Zane and Damon ordered everyone out of the vehicles.

Their first step was to sweep the area around the silos and confirm no other survivors were present. Then they checked the administration building, which was eerily quiet.

All ten workers were gone. There was no way to tell if they'd fled or turned.

After a quick discussion, Damon and Zane led the group inside the silos to inspect the grain directly.

Everyone held flashlights.

When they stepped into the first silo and saw mountains of grain piled high, joy erupted.

"Food! We actually have food!"

"It's full!"

But the celebration didn't last.

The second silo told a different story. It was moldy—completely ruined.

Months into the apocalypse, with no maintenance, the massive stores had become breeding grounds for rot and spontaneous combustion.

And once one section went bad, it poisoned the entire silo. Everything was totally inedible.

The third silo suffered the same fate: mold-ridden and useless.

By the time they checked all ten, they found over half rotten, one destroyed by fire, another waterlogged, and one overrun by rats and grain beetles—swarms of giant rodents scurrying everywhere.

Only two silos remained fully intact: the first one they'd entered, and the very last.

Just ten thousand tons of grain left.

Even so, it was far from a total loss.

Everyone immediately prepared to load up.

Zane announced, "Let me go over the split again. Base K1 and Hope Base each take forty percent. We collect our shares first, then the rest of you divide what remains based on the ratios I worked out. Any issues?"

The four smaller base leaders all nodded.

Even though each would only end up with an average of roughly 0.5%, it was still an enormous windfall.

Each team had brought ten heavy-duty trucks, and even then, they couldn't haul everything in one trip. It would take two runs.

The grain would keep their bases well-fed for years to come.

"No problem at all!" the four leaders replied in unison.

"I'm setting aside two hundred tons for you separately," Damon said to Iris.

At that, all eyes snapped to her.

Two hundred tons? Just handed over like that?

Glances toward Iris turned even more contemptuous, filled with open disdain.

It was obvious to everyone. This Iris was clearly Damon's woman.

He'd covered for her the entire trip. She'd done nothing, and now she was getting two hundred tons of grain.

Such luck...

Howard Marshman, a tall, thin member of Spark Base, couldn't help but sneer. "Chloe, you see that? Pretty girls really do get special treatment. You're both women, but look at the difference! Why's no one giving you free grain?"

Chloe's face darkened. "Howard! Shut your mouth!"

She wasn't mad for herself—she was furious he was talking about Iris that way. She firmly believed Iris wasn't that kind of person.

Howard smirked. "Captain Reed, maybe you should give your sister some tips. Tell her to doll up a little. If she's even half-decent looking, she might catch some big shot's eye. Then Spark Base could just ride coattails, too!"

Jace snapped. "Shut up! Don't you dare speak about my sister like that!"

Howard shrugged. "Hey, I'm just saying—before the apocalypse or after, it pays to be a woman."

"Howard! You're asking for it!"

Chloe lifted her hand, and a fireball flared to life.

As the flame lit the dark, she suddenly spotted eight or nine bat-like humanoid creatures hanging upside down from the silo ceiling above them.

Whoosh! A fireball blasted straight toward Howard's head.

Howard panicked and dove out of the way, shocked. "What the hell is..."

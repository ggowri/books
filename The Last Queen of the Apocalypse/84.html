Chapter 84 Zombie Kids

Beep—

Click.

The deal was done. Iris stepped back and swiped her card.

The door before her creaked slowly open.

Iris stood firmly behind Damon, her gaze fixed intently on the room unfolding ahead.

No lights flickered within—only utter darkness.

The corridor's faint glow spilled weakly into the entryway, revealing a small steel-plated antechamber.

At its center loomed a huge glass window, yet in the dim light, they could make out nothing but shadowy shapes beyond, hinting at a far vaster interior space.

Silence. Dead, suffocating silence.

But inside that pitch-black, sealed chamber, an inexplicable wave of pressure crashed over Iris—as if some unknown horror lurked within the shadows, watching.

She was a psychic metahuman.

Psychic power was exceedingly rare. Beyond sharpening her awareness of nearby zombies and metahumans when she concentrated, it also granted her powerful danger premonitions.

Descending to the second basement with this group, even walking through hallways swarming with zombies moments earlier, had not triggered her survival instincts. Yet the instant the door swung open, her heart began to pound violently.

A crushing sense of dread descended.

She attempted to extend her mental power inward, only to find it could barely penetrate the radiation-shielded containment room—not with her current strength, at least.

The only message her ability screamed was clear: Heavy oppression! And danger!

Iris halted at the threshold, refusing to step any farther.

Noticing her hesitation, Louisa spoke up. "Aren't you coming in?"

Iris replied coolly, "A wise man avoids perilous ground. I've changed my mind."

Louisa's face twisted into a sneer at once. "Hah! You're just a coward! Suit yourself. Captain Flint, let's move!"

She strode boldly inside.

Damon, however, paused and turned to Iris. "You sense danger in there?"

Iris glanced at him with a faint, amused smirk and held out her hand. "One hundred crystal cores, and I'll give you a critical warning."

Damon held her gaze, thinking for a full ten seconds before pulling out a pouch of crystal cores. "I only have seventy-eight."

Actually, he thought he was insane.

Iris snatched it from him. "Fine. I'll take what you've got. Consider it a discount—better than you dying in there and wasting them all."

Damon paused.

He pressed on. "What exactly is in there?"

Iris shrugged. "No clue. All I know is it's extremely dangerous."

Damon's jaw tightened.

Had he just paid seventy-eight crystal cores for absolutely nothing?

Just then, Louisa's voice echoed from inside. "Captain Flint! What are you waiting for? There's no danger whatsoever! Get in here and back me up!"

Damon said no more, raised his weapon, and stepped inside.

As he brushed past her, Iris—still clutching his pouch of crystal cores—offered one last solemn warning. "Remember. It's deadly in there."

Damon looked at her deeply, then entered alone, letting no one else follow.

This was the mission's core zone. No additional personnel were permitted entry.

The second he vanished inside, Iris pulled out her card.

"Logan, get the dogs and supplies to the truck first. Park it right at the entrance."

"Understood!"

Logan shot a worried glance toward the interior before heading upstairs with the dogs.

The moment he left, Derek and the others crowded around Iris.

"Iris, is there really danger inside?"

"What's in there?"

Suddenly, the lights inside the chamber blazed to life, revealing everything beyond the observation window.

Every single person froze, stunned speechless.

Behind the glass pane lay bodies strewn everywhere—a thick, dense carpet of corpses.

Not a single corpse was intact. Some were reduced to nothing but leg bones; others had their abdomens torn open and hollowed out; still others had half their skulls gnawed away.

The containment room was splattered with blackened blood, everything in complete disarray.

It looked exactly like a horror movie crime scene.

But what horrified them most was the small child amid the carnage.

"Over there!" Derek pointed shakily at the corner, where a small child huddled against a female corpse.

The kid looked no older than five or six, burrowing into the woman's embrace as if seeking comfort from her mother.

Then the child turned to face them.

Everyone's breath caught in their throats.

This was no sweet, innocent child's face—it was the visage of a demon child straight out of a nightmare.

Her entire skin was black and mottled. Her eyes, which should have been bright and pure, were hollow, terrifying gray voids. Her tiny white dress was soaked entirely in blood, and she clutched a ragged doll in her hands.

Sensing the people beyond the glass, she tilted her head and stared at them.

When she opened her mouth, black blood gushed out at once, and a piece of rotted flesh fell from the corner of her lip.

She... had been feeding on the corpse.

Before Derek and the others could recover from the shock, something even more bone-chilling occurred.

One... two... three... twelve... thirteen.

From the piles of scattered bodies, more than a dozen small zombies crawled out, all fixing their gaze directly on them.

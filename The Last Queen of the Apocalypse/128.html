Chapter 128 A Weird Man

She'd do good deeds, but not foolish ones.

She couldn't afford to take in all the elders from their base.

A string of numbers fluttered down, and Jace caught it at once.

The car beside them rumbled back to life. Iris's vehicle sped down the highway and vanished into the distance.

Jace stared at the empty road, clutching the note tight.

Chloe pouted beside him, staring out the window.

After tucking the paper safely away, he noticed her mood and nudged her arm.

"C'mon. They're gone. You can't go with them anyway."

Chloe turned her head. "Hmph!"

Jace softened his tone. "Fine, I won't talk to you like that again, okay?"

Chloe snapped around to glare. "Just say you were wrong. Is that so hard?"

Jace shot back. "I'm your brother! You gonna talk back now?"

Chloe's eyes stung with anger.

He'd always been like this, ever since they were little—never apologized.

She was so mad she wanted to jump out and go her own way, when his voice cut through her rage. "Okay, okay, okay. I was wrong. Sorry."

She turned to see Jace smiling at her.

She huffed. "You're still laughing!"

Jace ruffled her hair roughly, his voice quieting.

"Before Dad died, he told me to protect you. I don't know how to do it right... But if I had Captain Hale's strength, I'd never have to send you away to someone else."

Chloe's voice shook. "Jace..."

"I think Captain Hale was right," he went on. "I'm your brother, not your enemy. I won't hurt you with my words anymore. I'll fix whatever I do wrong. Mom and Dad are gone—only we're left in this world. We have to stick together."

Chloe looked down, hiding a tear.

Jace chuckled. "My ugly little sister's crying now? Thought you said you'd never cry again?"

Chloe shot up. "Jace Reed!"

He backed off at once. "Okay, okay, my bad. You're my pretty sister, the most beautiful girl in the whole world. Don't cry, you'll mess up your face."

"Hmph!" Chloe quickly wiped her eyes.

She looked at him. "Am I really that ugly?"

Jace answered fast. "Hell no! You just don't dress up. Later, I'll go to the town's shopping street and buy... grab you some nice clothes. Once you fix yourself up, you'll look like a fairy! Just like Captain Hale!"

Chloe's tears turned to laughter. "Jace, didn't you say you wanted to thank Captain Hale properly? Why did you only say one word?"

Jace frowned. "What're you getting at?"

Chloe giggled. "C'mon, tell me! Were you shy around her? I never knew you were that type!"

Jace's voice jumped. "Chloe, I'm warning you—don't push it!"

She patted his arm. "C'mon, admit it!"

Jace didn't reply, just started the engine. "Making fun of your own brother now? You'll pay for this!"

"Hahaha!"

The truck rumbled onward, filled with laughter.

After all, today had been a huge win.

For all the suffering, everyone was going home with full loads.

*****

Iris arrived back at Midsummer Base first.

After unloading everything, she checked the base—everything was secure, and she relaxed.

As for where to find new residents...

She decided to drive Base K1's car back to them personally.

The easiest way to recruit people was to poach from other bases, wasn't it? Base K1 had tons of people waiting to get in.

She told Logan, "Watch the base. I'm going to return the car."

Logan was confused. "How will you get back?"

Iris waved a hand. "They'll give me a ride!"

Logan was speechless.

Wasn't that overcomplicating it? Wouldn't it be simpler to radio Derek and have him come pick it up?

Iris drove Damon's car toward Base K1.

Around three in the afternoon, she arrived smoothly outside their gates.

The guards saw their own vehicle and began processing entry immediately.

Iris didn't rush in. She told the gate guards, "I'm here to return the car. Go tell Captain Flint."

The guard rushed off to report.

While waiting, Iris stood at the entrance and looked around.

On both sides, long lines of survivors waited to enter. They'd heard about the large base through K1's 24-hour radio broadcasts and came seeking shelter.

That was when she noticed one particular group.

A small cluster of people stood near the entrance, trying to hold their ground as the crowd pressed in from all sides.

"Don't push! Don't push—we'll all get in! We've finished quarantine. They'll definitely let us in. There's a child and a pregnant woman here, please stop shoving!"

A man in glasses and a faded white shirt tried to keep order amid the surging crowd.

But no one listened. In fact, they only pushed him further back.

Someone snapped, "Then give us your spot and we won't push!"

"Right! Easy for you to stand up front and talk!"

"A child and a pregnant woman? I've got an elderly man here! Why don't you let him go first?"

What shocked Iris most was that, beneath the jeers, the man actually gave up his place.

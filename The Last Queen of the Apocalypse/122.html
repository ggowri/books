Chapter 122 Reality Check

Until this moment, they had all looked down on Iris, even calling her a useless freeloader.

Now, witnessing her true strength, they felt as if they'd been slapped repeatedly—their faces burned with shame.

She could crush any one of them with just a flick of her finger.

In the crowd, Chloe stared at Iris cutting through zombies like wet paper, completely dumbfounded. Then her eyes blazed with admiration.

Iris... was actually this powerful!

Chloe had never believed Iris was the type to leech off men. She'd always sensed Iris possessed genuine power.

But this surpassed every last one of her expectations.

Iris was so strong that even she and her brother together—no, their entire base combined—couldn't hold a candle to her.

Suddenly, she recalled the zombie that had frozen mid-attack the night before.

Had Iris really saved her?

Chloe was utterly stunned.

Derek and the other Base K1 members watched Iris, practically turning into fanboys on the spot.

How awesome! She was absolutely the strongest!

Even Logan fighting beside her looked incredible.

Was Iris a literal goddess?

They were completely blown away.

Damon, shouldering his rocket launcher, watched with wide, amazed eyes.

Iris seemed... even stronger than before.

Base K1 had just made breakthroughs in superpower research, and he hadn't even gotten to tell her. Yet here she was, already twice as powerful as before.

How did she do it?

Everyone from Hope Base stared in shock. Even Zane, who rarely showed any emotion, had his usual composure shattered.

He gaped at her display, unable to believe his eyes.

This was the highest-tier power he had ever seen. The strongest, without question.

Though water-type power was considered fairly ordinary in their research—far from top-tier—Iris had turned hers into a weapon of mass destruction.

Zane stared at her, completely reassessing everything he'd thought about her.

Bang! Bang! Bang!

With Iris and Logan fighting at full strength, they quickly wiped out most of the zombies.

Seeing only two or three left, Damon and the others snapped back to their senses and rushed to help.

Gunfire roared, powers flared. Within minutes, all ten mutated zombies were dead.

They immediately climbed into the grain piles to rescue anyone who'd fallen in.

By nine that night, the situation was fully under control.

Of the dozen who'd sunk into the grain, only four survived. The rest had been bitten and turned.

The worst fate was Howard, the first to be dragged down. When they pulled him out, only bones remained—the starving zombies had devoured everything else.

Jace stared grimly at what was left of his teammate.

Howard had been his hometown friend and classmate.

He'd dropped out before finishing middle school—never the good type, always fighting, a troublemaker in teachers' eyes. He'd quit in ninth grade, vowing to make it big and prove everyone wrong.

In reality, he'd worked odd jobs in the city for years, achieved nothing, then returned home to idle around. He couldn't even get a girlfriend, let alone marry.

Last year, when Jace went home, Howard had joked that if he didn't find a wife by next year, Jace should let him marry his "unmarriageable tomboy sister."

Jace had beaten him up for it.

Everyone saw Howard as a loser, but Jace still remembered the sixteen-year-old kid, full of fire, saying he'd make something of himself.

Chloe walked over to Jace. "It's my fault. If I'd reacted faster..."

She'd tried to fight back, but it had been too late.

"Don't blame yourself," Jace patted her shoulder.

Together, they put Howard's remains to rest and buried him on the spot.

This was the apocalypse. Raw, cruel, unforgiving.

Death lurked around every corner. No one knew when their time would come.

And no one could protect everyone.

Not even Iris. She could only do her best to shield her own.

In a world where most lives meant nothing, she was no savior.

At most, when danger came for her and hers, she would strike back—and save whoever she could along the way.

If she saved them, fine. Beyond that, she couldn't save the world, and she wouldn't try.

She had to put herself first. Herself, her people, and her base.

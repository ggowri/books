Chapter 93 Iris Had the Power

"It's an armored vehicle!"

"They're here!"

The leader, Stuart Baker, stared at Iris's vehicle and gestured for his group to hold back. "You guys wait here. I'll go talk to them."

He walked toward the car. To show he meant no harm, he stopped about thirty feet away, waved, and removed the magazine from his gun.

Iris had Logan flash the headlights twice.

Stuart approached cautiously.

The apocalypse had its own unspoken rules. In this world, everyone was a potential threat. You trusted no one, and every stranger carried danger. No one could be sure what the other might do.

Stuart was making the first move to show goodwill.

Knock knock.

He tapped on Iris's window.

Iris rolled it down a fraction. The vehicle was high enough that even this small gap was well above his head, putting her in a dominant defensive position.

If he tried anything, he'd be at a fatal disadvantage while she could take him out effortlessly.

But she could still hear him clearly.

"Hey, you here to scavenge supplies too?" Stuart asked.

Iris nodded. "Yeah."

Stuart clapped his hands. "Perfect! How about we team up and clear this place together? We've been watching it for three days; we know the layout inside out! Once we take it, we split everything fifty-fifty!"

He pulled out a crumpled cigarette pack and carefully took out one stick.

Cigarettes were priceless now. People could barely find food, let alone smokes. They were real luxuries—smoke one, and it was gone forever.

Iris took the cigarette, then replied flatly, "You go first. I'll come in after you're done."

Stuart's smile froze on his face.

Could she at least not take his cigarette if she were gonna refuse?

He pressed on, "Look, it's dangerous out here. More people mean more safety, right?"

Iris didn't budge. "We'll be rooting for you. Good luck."

Stuart was dumbfounded.

Since the world ended, he'd met all kinds of people.

Normally, if someone refused to cooperate, they wouldn't take your gift. If they took it, they usually agreed to work together.

But Iris? She took his cigarette like it was nothing and turned him down flat.

Today was a new experience.

Stuart stared intently at the armored vehicle in front of him.

Faced with that kind of firepower, he gritted his teeth. "If you help us get in, we'll only take thirty percent. You keep seventy. How about that?"

A soft, amused chuckle came from inside the car.

That laugh broke his resolve entirely. "Fine. We take twenty, you take eighty!"

Silence.

"Just ten percent! We'll back you up! You can't take everything here anyway!"

A wisp of smoke drifted from the vehicle's roof.

Iris looked down at him. "Like I said. You go first. I'll enter after you've looted it."

Iris had no intention of sharing anything. Not even ten percent.

At her domineering reply, Stuart was completely out of ideas.

He had no bargaining chips at all. The gap in power was just too wide.

He stared at the vehicle for a long while, then reluctantly turned back to his group.

As soon as he returned, his team swarmed around him.

"Stuart, how did it go?"

"Who are they?"

"Did they agree to team up?"

Stuart glanced back at the massive armored car and waved a hand. "They don't want to work with us."

"So what now?"

Stuart looked at the farm ahead and sighed. "One last try, I guess."

Five minutes later, Iris watched the group give up entirely and trudge away in defeat.

She waved a hand. "Let's go."

The armored vehicle roared forward like a bullet, charging straight into the farm.

When you had absolute power, you just rolled over everything.

The group, now 600 feet away, watched the car smash through the gate they'd been stuck at for days. Every one of them stared in awe.

The strong always commanded admiration.

Stuart felt even worse.

Iris really didn't need his team at all.

She had the power. His group simply wasn't worth her time.

Just then, scattered surviving zombies inside the farm poured out through the broken gate. They caught the scent of humans outside and lunged straight for Stuart's group.

Panic flashed across their faces.

"Run!"

"Retreat!"

They didn't have Iris's strength. Facing even three zombies meant their only choice was to run. They had to get away.

After Iris's vehicle charged inside, she immediately released the dogs to clear the remaining infected workers.

When she stepped out, she stepped right on a book titled Postpartum Care for Sows.

Iris crouched and picked it up.

Good book. Worth keeping.

This was a medium-sized black pig farm—more accurately, a franchise branch of a large-scale pig farming operation.

A few years before the apocalypse, the city had overhauled pig farming.

Individual households were no longer allowed to raise pigs privately. All swine had to be raised in standardized, licensed facilities, then sent to designated slaughterhouses once they reached slaughter weight.

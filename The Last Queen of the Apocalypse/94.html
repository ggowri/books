Chapter 94 Kael Cross

This farm didn't even handle breeding—it only raised pigs to slaughter weight.

The facility operated under a corporate contract: the parent company supplied piglets, technical guidance, and recorded initial weights. They set target slaughter weights and fixed purchase prices.

They earned income solely from raising the hogs, paid by final weight. Their only job was fattening the piglets.

Private sales were strictly forbidden. Every piglet was registered on arrival, with scheduled slaughter dates requiring official verification.

Even dead piglets were disposed of by the parent company—no unauthorized meat sales allowed.

The system ensured food safety through full supply-chain control and traceability.

Iris flipped through the book she'd found, wondering if this farm had planned to raise extra pigs off the books.

She tucked it away and walked into the barns with Logan.

The farm had few zombie staff, most clustered near the entrance. The earlier car charge had crushed several; the rest either scattered or were finished off by the dogs.

Now, the sprawling farm was almost completely free of the undead.

When Iris stepped into the pig barn, a wave of stench hit her full force.

Fortunately, she'd arrived just in time.

The pigs were thin, but nearly seventy percent were still alive.

She said, "Logan, stand guard outside."

"Yes, ma'am!"

Logan left without hesitation.

Iris scanned the huge pens, moving stall by stall to collect the surviving pigs into her storage space.

The smell was absolutely repulsive. Livestock farming really wasn't for everyone.

When she reached the third pen, she sensed someone watching her.

Her mental power locked onto the target instantly, and she fired a high-pressure water orb.

Thud!

In the far corner, a man in a baseball cap rolled away nimbly, scrambling out of cover.

Once in the open, he grinned at Iris. "So you're a spatial-type metahuman!"

Iris studied him, her frown deepening.

She recognized him—the man who'd hung at the back of Stuart's group. What had stood out was that he felt neither fully human nor a typical metahuman.

"Who are you really?" she demanded.

The man raised both hands. "I mean no harm! I just want to join you!"

Iris arched an eyebrow. "Join me?"

"Exactly! You guys are clearly powerful. I want to be on your team, you're the only ones worth following!"

Iris stared at him. "What could you possibly offer to make me keep you?"

At her question, the man removed his baseball cap, revealing an eye patch.

Iris's eyes flickered with recognition.

Then he pulled off the eye patch, exposing a gray, zombie-like eye.

She blurted, "Kael Cross?"

Kael blinked in surprise. "How do you know my name?"

Iris's mind raced.

As someone who'd survived ten years in the apocalypse, she knew all the legendary figures.

Over that decade, the entire eastern region had produced ten major powerholders—each dominating their own base.

One of them was the half-zombie, Kael Cross.

Kael was the only known person who'd been infected but never fully turned.

Using his unique trait—zombies ignored him—he roamed the wasteland freely.

But what made him truly infamous wasn't his ability. It was his personality. He was cunning, greedy, completely untrustworthy, and loved betraying allies.

Using his zombie immunity, he jumped from base to base.

He never stayed long. Once he learned a base's layout and weaknesses, he moved on.

If the base was weak, he sold its secrets to stronger factions and led them in raids.

If the next base was vulnerable, he went back to the first and tipped them off.

Besides zombies, human raiders were the biggest threat in the apocalypse.

This one troublemaker had single-handedly destroyed several small settlements and one medium-sized base.

After each takedown, he took half the loot, partied until it was gone, then found his next target.

Why didn't he just loot zombie cities? Because he was addicted to the comforts of base life.

While others avoided vice, he drowned himself in gambling and women. After scamming resources, he wasted them all immediately.

Greedy, lustful, and unable to stand being alone—he could never survive on his own. All he did was con people, over and over.

Iris's old base had even caught his eye once. Luckily, it was remote enough that he couldn't pull off his usual schemes.

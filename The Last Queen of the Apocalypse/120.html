Chapter 120 Trapped

Howard's words died in his throat as his body was violently wrenched forward.

A skeletal zombie in a tattered manager's uniform dropped from above, its dried skin stretched tight over protruding bones. It grabbed Howard and dragged him straight into the pile of grain.

"Ahhhh—!"

Grain piles acted like quicksand. Once you fall in, it's nearly impossible to get out. And the more you struggled, the deeper you sank.

Howard vanished beneath the surface in seconds.

Zane and the others at the front froze. They snapped their flashlights backward. "What's happening?!"

"Zombies! Up there!" Chloe shrieked.

Everyone looked up.

The zombies scuttled away like cockroaches, swarming quickly over the piles of grain. With their twisted, backward-bending joints, they moved across the grain storage like cockroaches or centipedes—only far larger than either.

Each was hideously deformed, jaws gaping in guttural snarls.

Once they landed, they burrowed straight toward Howard's position.

A bloodcurdling scream erupted from deep within the grain.

Damon roared, "Open fire!"

The group jolted into action. Gunfire cracked through the silo.

Damon's men engaged immediately, shooting at the zombies skittering across the grain.

But more people were being dragged down.

In the dark, flashlights waved wildly as survivors were pulled into the grain one after another.

Hiss—

A flare shot upward, bathing the entire space in harsh light. Damon's squad reacted fast.

With the space lit up, they could finally see zombies crawling all over the walkway beneath their feet.

The creatures slammed their jaws into the metal grating, tearing gaping holes in the mesh with a single bite.

Gunfire erupted again along the walkway.

But no one expected the bullets to do so little.

The zombies' dried, hardened skin was like steel. Bullets ricocheted off in sparks, leaving only tiny craters.

Mutated zombies with ironhide skin and bones... This was unlike anything they had ever encountered.

They'd battled mutants before, but none with skin so dry and impenetrable. And they were swift.

"Fall back to the entrance!" Damon shouted.

The group bolted toward him, fending off attacks from all sides as they retreated.

With every step, the zombies below tore more of the walkway apart.

Holes riddled the metal path.

Zombies burst from the sides, trying to drag people into the grain.

Those who stumbled mid‑flight sank at once, swallowed by the golden quicksand.

In the blink of an eye, their sixty‑strong group had been cut in half.

They fired guns to clear a path, but soon realized superpowers worked far better than bullets—the zombies still possessed a natural vulnerability to it.

Damon's men discarded their firearms and unleashed their powers.

Fireballs, water orbs, shards of metal, and arcs of lightning blasted in every direction, hammering the swarming undead.

The barrage bought them precious time.

They threw everything they had, holding back the horde from all angles.

Just as they neared the exit, a massive shadow dropped at the end of the passage with a deafening crash.

The figure turned.

It was an enormously fat zombie, its body bloated and rotting, flesh hanging in tattered strips.

The manager's uniform it had once worn was shredded, fused into its decaying mass. Half its face hung loose as it locked its gaze on the group.

It charged.

The entire walkway shuddered under its weight.

With the giant zombie blocking the front and zombies swarming from behind, they were trapped with no way out.

One of the zombies latched onto the last person in line.

"Ahhhh—!"

He was grabbed by the ankle and yanked downward.

His teammate lunged to grab his hand, but a sickening crack echoed. A zombie crawled up the dangling man's back and drove straight for his heart.

The horde of centipede-like zombies closed in, boxing them in like fish in a barrel, tearing at them.

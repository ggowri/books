Chapter 90 Irie

But satellite signals wouldn't last much longer. Without maintenance, the satellites would either fall out of orbit or malfunction within a few years.

Iris had never used a satellite phone in her past life, but she'd relied on something far simpler for communication: a radio.

Devices like radios and old fax machines, which sent and received low-frequency signals, functioned as basic shortwave stations. By the later stages of base civilization, every settlement had its own radio station.

The moment Iris heard Damon mention it, understanding clicked.

A radio? Of course she had one.

Her house was packed with electronics—any of them could be tuned into a transmitter.

She just hadn't had anyone to talk to.

A faint smile curved her lips. "Give me your frequency."

*****

6:30 PM.

Night had just fallen when Iris returned safely to her base.

As soon as she stepped inside, she headed straight for the bunker.

She dumped cooking and pet-feeding duty on Logan again.

She had bigger fish to fry.

Rubbing her hands in excitement, Iris prepared to claim her huge reward from the day's mission.

[Base detected. Unlock Super Electronic Butler?]

Iris said without hesitation, "Yes!"

The second she confirmed, a holographic supercomputer materialized in the open space on the second floor.

She stepped over and pressed the power button. The screen lit up, and a tiny, electronic version of herself appeared on the holographic display.

The image sharpened steadily. After three seconds, it opened its eyes and looked at Iris.

"Hello, my dear master. Please name me."

Iris studied the digital butler with satisfaction. "Your name is Irie."

"Yes, my dear master. Irie will serve you at once."

Irie's crisp, mechanical voice echoed through the room. Instantly, every device on the second floor beeped in unison, their indicator lights flashing as they linked into a single network.

"Master, I have connected to all electronic facilities in the base. I will monitor and manage all operations 24/7."

Iris lit up.

Don't be fooled by Irie's lack of a physical body—it would become the most powerful, foundational administrator of her entire base.

All the security cameras would be its eyes. The full-coverage radar would be its sensory network. And the patrolling combat drones would be its enforcement arms. Every piece of equipment in the base would be an extension of its will.

From now on, Iris wouldn't have to micromanage everything.

If the radar detected intruders, Irie would immediately send drones to intercept without waiting for repeated orders. It would drive them off first, and attack if necessary.

If the drones faced overwhelming danger, they would retreat strategically instead of being destroyed while waiting for commands.

In short, her entire base defense system now had a brain.

That meant Iris no longer had to fear leaving the base unguarded. Irie would watch over everything.

On top of that, it could handle all the daily affairs for the residents.

All the tedious work that had been driving her crazy—point calculations, supply exchanges, production management—could now be handled by Irie through a single computer in the residential area.

Sophie wouldn't have to run back and forth delivering messages and goods anymore.

And unlike humans, machines never tire. While Iris slept, Irie would keep managing the base nonstop.

In other words, she now had the most complete, perfect base manager imaginable. One that would never betray her.

Iris smiled. "Perfect, Irie. From now on, you oversee everything in the base. Keep Midsummer Base under constant surveillance."

"Yes, my dear master."

Irie spun in a small circle on the screen before vanishing back into the system.

Iris was already planning how to expand Irie's authority.

*****

The next day.

Iris brought Logan and a truck loaded with supplies to the residential area.

The residents working nearby all greeted her warmly when they saw her.

"Ms. Hale!"

"Ms. Hale, you're here!"

Iris nodded to her nineteen residents and parked the truck in front of an empty villa.

Just then, Sophie came bouncing over. "Ms. Hale! You're here! I was just about to find you!"

Iris raised an eyebrow. "What for?"

"To exchange supplies! Ms. Hale, there's a lot again!"

Sophie cheerfully held out a piece of paper covered in dense scribbles of items.

She grinned up at her. "By the way, Ms. Hale, when are you having another market? The last one was so much fun! I can't wait to go again and buy yummy food!"

Iris smiled and tugged gently at her pigtails. "From now on, you can buy things anytime you want."

She waved her hand, and Logan climbed down from the truck. It was the same delivery truck Iris had seized on the highway.

Once on the ground, Logan began unloading the supplies.

Chapter 110 Justice Served

At these words, Damon waved a hand in dismissal. "Get him out of here. Now."

Benjamin never even got the chance to lay a finger on Iris before guards dragged him away.

Yet in that split second, he was utterly convinced—that was Iris.

She'd grown so powerful. And she hadn't even spared him a glance.

Still, he deserved every bit of this.

Overwhelmed by regret, Benjamin wanted nothing more than to slap himself.

If only he'd treated Iris better back then. If only he'd chosen her instead... He never would have ended up like this.

He'd truly thrown away a treasure.

The guards hauled him away without mercy.

"Captain Flint, how does your base normally handle incidents like this?" Iris asked.

Damon's response was sharp and unyielding. "Internal discipline isn't my domain, but one rule stands—whoever takes a life pays with their own."

Iris smiled at his words.

How fitting. What goes around comes around.

In this resource-starved apocalypse, medical supplies were more precious than gold. Benjamin and Amanda were already plagued with health problems; after such a brutal beating, they wouldn't last long.

She hadn't expected his end to be like this—beaten to death, abandoned by the son he'd doted on all his life.

Truly, karma was a beautiful thing.

Being the "kind-hearted" woman she was, Iris could not bear to see injustice go unaddressed.

She spoke with feigned righteousness. "Captain Flint, shouldn't you arrest the attackers at once? Those two from earlier don't have long to live. You should lock the culprits up with them right away. If anything happens to the victims, at least justice can be served swiftly.

"You can't wait until they're dead to make arrests—what if the killers vanish by then? You can't let the victims die with wrongs unavenged."

Damon found her logic impeccable.

He ordered at once. "Arrest the attackers and confine them with the victims. The second the victims draw their last breath, execute the culprits immediately!"

"Yes, sir!"

Having "stood up for the victims," Iris congratulated herself on being a truly good person.

Surely no one was more compassionate than she.

Anyone else would have turned a blind eye, or even kicked them while they were down. But she? She was delivering justice.

So very kind.

Iris was almost touched by her own benevolence.

If anyone ever dared call her cruel again, she'd be the first to object.

*****

After touring the market, Iris had formed a complete picture of Base K1.

One word described it perfectly: miserable.

Without military protection, the base would have collapsed long ago from sheer mismanagement.

It had taken in refugees without restraint, leaving resources critically scarce.

Thousands of displaced people wandered idly, with almost no employment to be found.

If not for the soldiers holding the base together and constantly scavenging for supplies, it would not have survived another day.

In short, they were starved for resources.

No wonder Damon was willing to travel so far for the grain reserve.

Damon showed her to a spare room in his villa, with Logan staying in the adjacent chamber.

"Ms. Hale, we leave at first light tomorrow. Get some proper rest."

Iris nodded. "Very well."

As Damon turned to close the door, he paused and called after her. "Oh, I noticed you barely touched your dinner tonight. There are snacks and crackers in the drawer if you get hungry."

"All right."

Iris shut the door.

Just as Damon had said, she found a box of premium crackers inside the drawer.

Not bad hospitality—better than she would have offered.

She'd have to return the favor next time.

*****

The next day.

Having spent the night at Damon's residence, Iris rose early. When she descended the stairs, a convoy was already lined up outside.

Alongside several pickup trucks stood dozens of heavy-duty cargo vehicles.

Damon approached her. "Ms. Hale, you and Logan may take the vehicle you arrived in. We'll load your share of supplies onto it."

Iris smiled. "Sounds great."

Iris and Logan climbed into their car and fell in behind Damon's convoy, heading toward Briarfield City.

It would be a long journey—three days round trip if all went well.

They planned to meet up and camp overnight on the highway.

They departed at 7:30 a.m. and did not reach the meeting point until nearly 3 p.m.

Along the way, they'd had to clear blocked roads and pick the most navigable routes.

Highways like this were especially jammed at on-ramps and off-ramps, though the middle stretches were clearer. The convoy had lurched to a stop and started again countless times over nearly seven hours.

When they arrived, a long stretch of highway ahead had been cleared, with dozens of vehicles gathered at the front.

These trucks were far less polished than Base K1's—mostly scavenged freight and box trucks picked up from roadside ruins.

As Base K1's imposing convoy drew near, a crowd rushed to surround them.

"Base K1 is here!"

"It's Base K1!"

"No wonder Base K1 is so powerful—just look at their fleet!"

"Base K1's integrated with the military, of course they're formidable!"

Damon stepped out of the lead vehicle, and representatives from the other group approached to greet him.

"Greetings! I'm Ethan Walker from Future Base. We're settled at the chemical plant in western Briarfield City, with over three hundred members. Pleased to meet you!"

"Greetings! I'm Dylan Harris from Stellar Base. Our base is in the southern development zone of Briarfield City, home to more than four hundred people."

Chapter 126 The Chosen One's Shock

Jace looked toward Iris, his gaze darkening.

Then he patted Chloe's head. "Eat first. Tomorrow I'll go thank her. Either way, she saved us today."

Chloe nodded hard. "Okay!"

On Iris's side, the group finished their meal.

Base K1's men began packing up—some cleaning, others loading grain.

They'd brought every spatial-type metahuman they had, plus dozens of extra-large cargo trucks. They managed to haul away most of their share.

This food would sustain their base for years, solving their worst supply shortage.

Iris waited until they finished, then walked into the first granary alone.

Five thousand tons. Piled to the ceiling.

She placed her hand on the grain, and it flowed rapidly into her space. Within minutes, the massive silo was completely empty.

She moved to the last intact granary, then took every scrap of remaining grain—all vanished into her storage.

After claiming everything, her space was nearly full. She'd struggle to store large supplies for a while.

Just then, a voice chimed in her mind.

[Ding. Super Lord System activated.]

[Please increase base population. When population doubles, spatial capacity will double as a reward!]

Iris's eyes lit up.

A new mission, finally!

This system was perfect, always giving her exactly what she needed.

Tomorrow, she'd head straight back and start recruiting people!

Satisfied, she emptied both silos and returned.

*****

The next day.

Everyone prepared to head back.

Before leaving, Damon found her. "Should I leave some men to haul more grain for you?"

Iris waved him off. "Don't bother. I'll handle it myself."

She was eager to get back and grow her population. Not wanting to waste time chatting, she took Logan, got in their loaded vehicle, and left first.

Watching her drive away, Damon thought back to that day at the Pharmaceutical R&D Factory. He already had his answer.

His gaze lingered on the retreating vehicle.

"Add another lock here before we leave," he told Derek.

"Yes, Captain Flint!"

Their convoy departed last, after everyone else was gone.

Not long after, another group returned.

The leader stared at the empty yard, his gaze cold and sharp. "Move quickly!"

"Yes, sir!"

Hope Base had waited nearby all night after being chased off by Iris. As soon as everyone left, they rushed back to loot whatever was left.

With that much grain, there was no way they could take it all in one trip.

Zane led his men straight to the first granary, only to find an extra lock.

He sneered. "Just one lock?"

He stepped closer. As he neared, the lock shuddered violently, then snapped clean in two.

His psionic power had done it.

Zane's cold eyes watched the lock shatter.

Did they really underestimate him that much? Even if he feared Iris, he wasn't afraid of some cheap lock.

He strode inside—and froze.

The enormous granary was now completely empty.

The last one was the same. Not a single grain remained.

The silos were so hollow that footsteps echoed.

Zane stood there, stunned.

They'd taken everything? But they'd only had a few trucks! Even overloaded to the limit, it was impossible.

And the main grain share was supposed to be Iris's. How had she taken it all?

Zane's breath caught as an even bigger realization hit him.

Iris wasn't just a water-type metahuman—she was also spatial-type. And her storage space was unimaginably huge.

Understanding crashed over him, turning his world upside down.

He stood frozen for a long time, his icy composure shattering like glass.

All his pride, all his confidence, was completely destroyed by Iris.

Since birth, everyone had called him the chosen one.

Advanced math at three, all high school material by five, university at seven, PhD at fifteen, countless research papers published...

His background even made his achievements look small. His father was the largest shareholder of the Aurora Group, and his mother was a political elite.

Even in the apocalypse, he'd awakened the rare, king-tier psionic power.

Calling him born with a silver spoon was an understatement.

God had practically chased him with a ladle, shoving success down his throat.

This glittering, envied life had made Zane unbearably arrogant. Ordinary people were just stupid, useless bugs to him—not one worth his attention.

Now, the heaven-blessed prodigy felt his chest twist violently.

His eyes locked onto the empty granary as numbers and possibilities surged through his mind, breaking past every boundary he'd ever known.

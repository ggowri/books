Chapter 111 The Gathering

"Greetings! I'm Mason Nelson from Victory Base. We're situated on the peninsula at the Cerebo City border, with a population of over five hundred."

"Hello! I'm Jace Reed from Spark Base. We're based in Hawk's Hollow, on the outskirts of Briarfield City, with two hundred members."

"I'm Damon Flint of Base K1." Damon greeted them cordially.

"We've heard nothing but great things about Base K1! If it weren't so far, we would've begged to join long ago!"

The leaders of the smaller bases all wore warm, flattering smiles as they addressed Damon.

Just then, Iris stepped over.

Damon turned to introduce her. "This is Iris Hale, from Midsummer Base."

"Midsummer Base?"

Everyone exchanged confused glances. None of them had ever heard of such a settlement.

"They prefer to keep a low profile," Damon added.

His words carried an immediate, unflattering subtext to the group.

Low profile? It must be tiny.

One of them spoke up. "Captain Hale, how large is Midsummer Base?"

Iris smiled faintly. "Around ten or so people."

The dismissive air around them thickened.

Only ten? What a pitifully small operation.

Still, since she was with Damon, they held their tongues. They brushed her aside and swarmed Damon instead, bombarding him with questions about resources.

"Captain Flint, do you have ample food supplies?"

"Captain Flint, got any extra meat to spare?"

"Captain Flint, how are your vegetables growing?"

"Captain Flint, we'd like to trade for some weapons. Do you have any extras?"

The true powerhouse in their midst was completely overlooked. Not a single person took Iris seriously.

Iris thought little of these petty base leaders anyway.

Just then, a girl with a buzz cut and a distinctly tomboyish look approached her. "Did you really build your own base?"

Iris glanced up. "Who are you?"

The girl continued, "I'm Chloe Reed. I came with my brother, Jace Reed, from Spark Base. We're in Hawk's Hollow, north of Briarfield City."

Iris murmured, "North of Briarfield City... Hawk's Hollow."

"That's fairly close to our territory," Logan noted.

The western border of Iris's domain directly abutted Hawk's Hollow in Briarfield City.

Chloe stared in disbelief. "You actually run your own base?"

Iris raised an eyebrow. "Why is that so hard to believe? Small as our numbers are, it's still a base."

Chloe frowned. "It's just... I've never met a woman who started her own base before."

She studied Iris earnestly and held out a hand. "Nice to meet you. Chloe, fire-type metahuman."

Iris looked at the outstretched hand. "Iris Hale. Water-type."

Their palms met. Chloe's hand was warm, almost hot to the touch.

At that moment, the deep rumble of engines echoed from behind them. Another convoy was approaching.

Every vehicle in Hope Base's fleet was spotless white. It looked like a corporate fleet—and that was essentially what it was.

As one of the three major large-scale bases, Hope Base had originally absorbed a huge number of researchers.

Its founding members all came from Aurora Group, a massive monopolistic tech corporation.

Aurora Group was a giant enterprise, overseeing more than twenty research institutes across fields including pharmaceuticals, medical equipment, bioengineering, and even military defense research.

Before the apocalypse, Aurora Group had been the largest corporation in Briarfield City. Its headquarters straddled the border between Briarfield City and Cerebo City, covering a vast area with its own fully self-contained ecosystem.

Before the zombie virus outbreak, it employed hundreds of thousands of people, and even entry-level staff held master's degrees—a true gathering of elites.

When the apocalypse struck, Aurora Group weathered the initial zombie hordes, riots, and chaos. These high-caliber survivors used the compound's internal resources to clear the undead, rebuild their enclave, and establish a major stronghold.

Aurora Group was reborn as Hope Base.

Its survivor population numbered an impressive one thousand.

Naturally, their admission criteria were brutally strict. They wanted no ordinary people, only top-tier talent.

In their view, taking in average survivors was a waste of precious resources.

With supplies so scarce in the apocalypse, why support masses of low-contribution burdens when an elite and a regular person consumed the same rations?

From Iris's memories, Hope Base was infamous in the years to come for its cold rigidity and utter ruthlessness.

Its leader, Zane Foster, ranked among the top ten most cruel men alive. They called him "the robot with a heartbeat."

The man had a famous maxim: "Every life has a price."

To him, all humans were commodities. He calculated a person's lifetime value based on age, physical condition, abilities, consumption, and output—this was the price tag of their life.

In the eyes of this cold, unfeeling man, anyone whose value fell below his standard was nothing but defective trash.

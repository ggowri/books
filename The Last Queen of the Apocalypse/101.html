Chapter 101 You Had it Worse

He stood beside Iris at the window, watching the only two who had escaped.

A bloodthirsty smile tugged at his lips. "My mom knew them."

Iris glanced at Kael. His face was streaked with fresh blood, his grin unhinged.

Harsh daylight poured through the window, but it only touched the narrow space where they stood. Everything else lay swallowed in shadow.

Click. A small flame flickered to life.

Kael cupped the lighter, shielding it as he leaned in to light Iris's cigarette.

She wasn't addicted. But in this suffocating apocalypse, sometimes a cigarette was the only thing that steadied her nerves.

Kael went on, voice almost casual. "My mom was a mistress. Not the quiet kind, the kind who'd do anything to claw her way into a rich life."

"She was a gold digger, always chasing wealthy men. Then she met my dad. Once she figured out he was rich, she stuck to him like glue. She was good at it—got pregnant with me after just a few nights.

"But having me made her bolder. She thought she'd become Mrs. Cross. But that was never happening. My dad wasn't stupid. She was just a toy to him.

"And the Cross family? Those sharks would never let her in. If it wasn't for me, they would've thrown her away long ago.

"When her rich-wife dream fell apart, she only cared about money. She was a gambling addict—blew every cent. And she kept bringing men back to the apartment my dad rented for her.

"I had to make my own instant noodles when I was hungry. I was only three back then. Sometimes, I even had to make a bowl for whatever random guy she brought home. Haha... ever had to do that?"

Kael laughed loudly.

Iris said nothing, just looked at him calmly, and gave a thumbs-up. "You had it worse."

"Haha," Kael laughed until he coughed. "That's not even the worst. At least I had food.

"I'll tell you a secret. I used to cry a lot as a kid. And it worked. Every time I cried, my dad gave me money. Too bad I didn't get to cry for long before he died.

"Car crash. Dead on impact. After that, no one gave my mom money anymore. So she dragged me to the Cross mansion every day to cry and beg. When they refused, she just dumped me there.

"Around that time, the real Mrs. Cross had twins via IVF. Two boys.

"How could my stupid mom think I was still useful?

"When she realized I was worthless, she abandoned me. I was... ten years old."

Kael took a long drag, exhaled a cloud of smoke, and stared at the fleeing couple outside.

"I stood at the gate alone. Starving for days. When I cried from hunger, those two gave me a bowl of dog food. The Cross family has a dog. They fed me its leftovers."

Iris stayed quiet.

She watched the pair disappear around the corner, and thought she heard distant screams and sobs. But it was too far to be sure.

She looked at Kael, who'd finished his story and was staring at the ground.

She took a deep breath. "That's all in the past. This is a new era now."

Kael laughed softly, slowly lifting his head. "Yeah, you know what? When the apocalypse started, I was so fucking happy. Wouldn't it be great if everyone just died?"

Iris finally understood why he'd been such a disaster in the past life. He literally wanted the whole world to burn.

She thought for a long time, searching for the right words. In the end, all she could manage was some corny encouragement.

"Listen, there's always a rainbow after the storm. Life's still full of hope. Now that you're with me—I'm your family. Your warm, caring family."

Kael fell silent.

Iris thought she'd said something pretty touching.

Surely his loyalty would go up now, right?

She stared seriously into his eyes.

Kael looked down and chuckled. "Can I ask you something?"

Iris said, "Go on."

Kael smiled. "If you really think of me as family... why are you always on guard around me?"

Iris shot back, "Who says I'm on guard?"

Suddenly, Kael leaned in, closing in on her face in an instant.

WHAM!

He went flying with a hard kick.

Kael crashed to the ground, clutching his stomach. "And you say you're not on guard?"

Iris pressed her lips.

How could she not be? She had to be.

She tried to cover. "That doesn't count. Just talk normally. I'd do that to anyone."

Kael stood up, still holding his stomach. His eyes darkened for a second, then he quickly put on a grin, acting completely unbothered.

"No need to explain, Captain Beauty. I really was moved by what you said today. Let's head back."

Iris nodded. "Okay."

Then Kael spoke up. "Actually... could you wait here a bit? I wanna go clean up the scene downstairs. They were, after all..."

Iris waved a hand. "Sure."

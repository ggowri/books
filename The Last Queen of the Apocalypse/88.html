Chapter 88 The Shocking Revelation

Everyone stared in utter shock at Iris, whose palm had just unleashed an immensely powerful water-type power.

Iris... was a metahuman too. And an absurdly strong one at that.

Even Logan, stuck outside and frantic with worry, froze completely.

Iris was a metahuman? Just how many secrets did she have hidden?

Bang!Crash!

In an instant, the wall of water burst outward. Within seconds, all the surrounding zombies were sent flying by the high-pressure torrents.

The countdown blared. "Seven!"

"Run! Now!" Iris shouted.

Derek and the others snapped back to their senses. There was no time to gawk.

They propped each other up and sprinted desperately through the final seconds.

"Five!"

"Four!"

"Three!"

Iris was naturally faster. She kept hurling condensed energy orbs to fend off the zombies as she rushed for the main door.

She swiped her card.

This door needed two cards to enter, but only one to exit.

"Two!"

"One!"

The door slid open at the last second, and the whole group stumbled through, utterly exhausted.

"Beep— Countdown complete. Self-destruct sequence activated."

The door slammed shut behind them. This time, not even a keycard would open it again.

The zombies that had caught up threw themselves against the glass. Many were still dripping wet from the water attack, clawing and snarling, looking even more hideous and terrifying.

Then thick white mist began spraying from the overhead vents.

"We have to move now," Damon panted, having narrowly escaped with his life. "The entire first and second basements are about to implode!"

At that, everyone forced their shaking legs onward, mustering every last ounce of strength.

That was when they spotted a woman lying in the corner.

Her neck was twisted a full 180 degrees. She was completely still.

Beside her, a huge pile of supplies had spilled across the floor.

Damon's gaze darkened at the sight of Louisa's corpse.

"She's really dead?"

"Captain Flint, I killed her," Logan stated.

Iris's eyes turned cold. "You're not about to pick a fight with us over this, are you?"

Damon held her gaze, then said flatly, "Thank you."

But Iris noticed a subtle shift in his expression. He was different now.

Damon turned to the supplies scattered around Louisa's body.

Besides two pieces of equipment, there was ample food and a stack of gold bars—easily one to two hundred of them, each weighing a little over a pound.

Most noticeable of all were the bags of noodles. Dozens upon dozens of hundred-pound sacks, along with a mountain of other high-value goods. Altogether, it filled roughly 70 to 100 cubic feet.

Louisa had claimed her storage space only held about 35 cubic feet. She'd been lying the entire time—hoarding her family's most valuable possessions and food while deliberately understating its capacity to mislead everyone.

The more Damon saw, the colder his killing intent grew.

The Barton family truly deserved to be torn to pieces.

"Grab everything and let's go," he ordered coldly.

"Yes, sir!"

Damon ignored the noodles and had his men carry the equipment first.

Everyone moved to lift the gear. Once the machines were loaded, the group filed into the elevator one by one.

Iris acted unusually polite, letting everyone else board first.

Glancing at the crowded car, she said, "Never mind, I'll wait for the next one. Don't want to overload it. Logan, go help them carry everything upstairs."

"Yes, ma'am!"

Damon watched her closely as Iris smiled sweetly, swiped the card, and waved from outside as the elevator began to rise.

She looked as harmless and helpful as could be.

The second the doors shut and the display ticked upward, Iris's smile widened even more.

Today was going to be a very profitable day.

Others might lack storage space, but she certainly didn't.

Iris looked at all the gold bars and food spilled from Louisa's dimension pouch, waved her hand, and stored everything away.

Some people were useless alive, but at least they contributed nicely in death.

She glanced at Louisa's corpse, pulled out her AK, and fired two more rounds into her. Just to be absolutely, one hundred percent sure she was dead.

There. That felt satisfying.

Cross Iris, and you'd regret it—even in the afterlife.

Iris had never been the forgiving type. Revenge was her creed, best served a hundredfold.

Once she was done, she strolled casually to the elevator and pressed the button. The doors opened at once.

To her surprise, Damon was standing inside. He'd come back down.

She blinked. "Why are you still here?"

Damon looked at her. "I was worried about you. The basement levels are going to collapse soon. We need to leave immediately."

"Oh." Iris swiped her card and closed the doors. "What do you mean, collapse? Will it wipe out all the zombies?"

Damon nodded. "Once the self-destruct activates, both underground levels will be completely destroyed and sealed off."

Chapter 112 I Saved Them All

The elites at the top calculated everything through cold, precise numbers, every choice geared toward maximum profit—this joint operation included.

A convoy of white vehicles rolled to a stop ahead. There were five cars in total.

Seven people in crisp white uniforms stepped out. Five men, two women.

At their head walked a young man in his mid-twenties, the other six trailing half a step behind.

"Hope Base is here!"

"That's Zane Foster himself!"

"It's really him!"

The four small-base leaders who'd been fawning over Damon instantly rushed over to greet Zane and his team.

"My brother is so pathetic."

Iris heard Chloe mutter beside her.

She turned to look.

Chloe lifted her chin in disdain. "Instead of out clearing zombies and scavenging supplies, he'd rather grovel to people who don't care about him."

Iris smiled faintly and patted the spot beside her. "Come sit with me."

Chloe glanced around, then hopped onto the highway guardrail next to Iris.

Iris pulled a bag of chips from her magic pocket and asked, "How many people are in your base?"

Chloe eyed the offering, hesitated for two seconds, then shook her head. "No thanks. We have just over two hundred. Mostly villagers from our hometown and nearby areas."

Since she refused, Iris tore the bag open herself. "What's the age mix like?"

Chloe's brow furrowed. "Lots of elderly. You know how villages are—mostly old folks left behind. Young people like me and my brother? Less than fifty."

Iris chuckled. "Then your brother better keep sucking up. Show up late, and he won't even get crumbs."

Chloe froze.

After a moment of frustration, she shook her head firmly. "No. In my book, we can't rely on anyone. In this apocalypse, we only have ourselves."

Iris laughed as she ate, holding the bag out again. "Have some. I like listening to you."

Chloe pressed her lips together. "I'm not good at talking. Growing up, no one in my family ever cared what I had to say."

"I do," Iris said. "Keep going. Consider these chips payment."

Chloe stared at the bag. This time, she hesitated only a second before taking some.

"Fine. I haven't really talked to anyone since the world ended. I like you too."

She asked again, "But, you really built your own base?"

Iris nodded. "Mm-hmm."

Chloe leaned in, curious. "Why did they let a woman run it? Do they actually listen to you?"

"I saved every last one of them," Iris said flatly. "So yes, they listen."

Chloe's eyes widened. "Seriously?"

"Doubt it? Ask him. I rescued him, too." Iris nodded toward Logan.

Chloe looked up at the man who'd stood like a statue the whole time, silently guarding Iris.

Logan glanced at her and gave a cool nod. "Everyone in our base was saved by Iris."

Chloe sucked in a sharp breath, suddenly full of admiration.

She saved them all by herself... and she was a woman!

Since the apocalypse began, Chloe had felt how far women's status had fallen.

Even as a fire-type metahuman who could fight just as well as her brother, she had no real say in base affairs.

Everyone overlooked her by default. Some even claimed women belonged at home, having children and tending to the weak—that even with powers, they could never match men. Men were naturally superior, and women should obey without question.

Those words made Chloe want to tear someone apart.

And she had it easier at Spark Base; her brother was the leader, and she had abilities. Other women with nothing fared far worse.

Hearing Iris had rescued everyone and built her own base filled her with sincere respect.

No matter how small the base, Iris was the undisputed leader.

She was deeply envious.

Though she had no idea just how massive Iris's "small base" really was—yet.

A cold voice cut through the chatter. "All right. Now that everyone's here, we can begin."

Zane stood in the center in his white uniform, ignoring everyone but Damon.

"Captain Flint, you said seven teams would be attending. Where's the last one?"

Damon responded promptly. "Midsummer Base. They arrived with me."

He gestured toward Iris. "That's Iris Hale, leader of Midsummer Base."

All heads turned and saw two people sitting casually on the roadside guardrail, munching chips and chatting like they were at a picnic.

The crowd fell silent.

The scene was jarringly out of place.

While everyone else stood tense and formal for the inter-base meeting, the pair snacked without a care in the world.

Damon wasn't surprised. He knew Iris well enough.

He turned to the others. "Let's go say hello."

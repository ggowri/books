Chapter 109 Regret

Once inside the base, they could only live in the outer slums.

Benjamin and Amanda were too weak to work, while Ryan lazed around all day doing nothing—yet ate the most, stealing what little the two old ones had.

Adeline was the only one in the family who could earn food.

Now that Ryan had mostly recovered, he made up his mind to take Adeline and leave, cutting off all ties with the two "useless burdens".

Amanda cried out loud. "Ryan, you can't do this! Don't you remember how we raised you?

"Your father left his village to fight for a better life for you the second you were born. He married into a rich family and took all the humiliation... Have you forgotten? We're family! You can't abandon us!"

"Family my ass!" Ryan kicked them hard. "If you two weren't so worthless, would I be living like this?

"Before the apocalypse, you couldn't even buy me a decent house! My fiancée got an abortion and left me! Now I can't even have a full meal! Trash like you call yourselves family? Just die!"

Ryan kicked them violently in the head.

Amanda curled into a ball after just a few blows.

Years of malnutrition and illness had broken her body; she gasped and spat blood.

Benjamin screamed. "Mom! Mom! Ryan, you'll kill your own grandmother!"

Ryan showed no mercy. If anything, he grew more excited.

"Good riddance! A leech like her is better off dead!"

Benjamin's heart turned to ice. He snarled desperately. "You beast! I'll kill you!"

"You're the old beast! Can't you just shut up?" Ryan stomped his head repeatedly, until Benjamin's face was covered in blood and his teeth shattered.

Just as Benjamin was about to be beaten to death, someone shouted.

"Captain Flint is coming!"

Ryan gave Benjamin one last angry kick, sent him rolling, then grabbed his "useful" mother and left.

Only Benjamin and Amanda lay on the ground, bloodied, screaming in rage and despair.

"You bastard... I'm your father! I'm your father!"

In that moment, Benjamin was swallowed by regret.

How had he raised such a monster?

He'd given Ryan everything, carried him all the way through the city during the outbreak. But the second he recovered, he abandoned them like trash.

Then he thought of Iris.

For all her defiance, she had always valued loyalty.

Benjamin had come from a remote village. To settle in the city, he married into Iris's mother's family, even though he already had a wife and a son back home.

He was rarely around when Iris was young. Her mother never complained, but Iris always did.

Later, when he brought Amanda from the village, she began to bully Iris's mother. Five-year-old Iris had pounced like a little wolf cub, biting Amanda until she bled and refusing to let go.

Back then, Iris had only wanted to protect her home. Protect her mother.

Benjamin knew her well. Once she claimed someone as her own, she would never let anyone hurt them.

If Iris were here, they would never end up like this. She would protect him, no matter what.

The regret was suffocating.

Barely conscious, he caught sight of a familiar figure in the crowd.

A young woman stood surrounded by base guards, dressed in a sharp combat uniform, her hair pulled into a high ponytail.

Damon, the base's new vice administrator, walked at her side.

She stood tall in her boots, looking down from a world completely out of his reach.

Benjamin tried to lift his head, but someone grabbed him.

"Take them back to their quarters," Damon said coldly.

He and Amanda were quickly carried off in the opposite direction.

He kept staring at the woman beside Damon, but all he saw was her steady, confident figure walking away, acknowledged and respected by everyone.

She was like a distant moon. The distance between them was so vast that he began to doubt himself.

Was that really his daughter, Iris?

The same Iris they had treated like a blood bag, ignored, dismissed as useless?

No one answered him.

She didn't even spare him a glance. To her, he was nothing more than air, a stray ant beneath notice.

She watched without emotion, then turned and walked on.

Benjamin broke down completely. He sobbed loudly, shouting in her direction.

Damon noticed and glanced at Iris. "Do you know them?"

Iris kept walking, not even glancing back. "No."

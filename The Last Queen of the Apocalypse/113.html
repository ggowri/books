Chapter 113 Stay Out of Trouble

"No need." Zane flicked a glance toward Iris, his cold eyes utterly devoid of warmth, before he looked away.

He turned to address the group.

"All of you must register. Fill out your full group information. Rations will be distributed based on each team's contribution during the mission.

"Captain Flint is exempt, of course. We'll honor our original agreement."

Damon nodded.

Once Zane finished speaking, his subordinates stepped forward with a stack of forms and handed them out to each group.

Damon received one as well—not for himself, but for Iris and Logan.

Only Hope Base knew the location of the Eastern Grain Reserve. But as academics with little combat strength, they couldn't secure the supplies alone. So they'd reached out to Damon for muscle.

At the same time, they didn't fully trust him either.

What if the armed force turned on them once they arrived and seized the entire warehouse?

That was why Hope Base had organized this large, multi-base operation.

Even if it meant sharing more supplies, they gained protection. More bases also meant keeping Base K1 in check.

The smaller bases all took their forms respectfully and stepped aside to fill them out. They were small fry—they knew better than to act out.

"Chloe!"

"Chloe Reed!"

Jace waved a form and called sharply to his sister, who was still munching chips.

Chloe scrambled to her feet. "I have to go."

Iris smiled. "Off you go."

She stayed seated.

A moment later, a hand appeared in front of her.

She looked up to see Damon. She held out the chips in offering.

Damon hesitated briefly.

"You need to fill out a registration form." He held out the paper with his other hand.

Iris took it and skimmed the contents.

It demanded highly detailed personal information: name, age, hometown, pre-apocalypse job, superpower type, base population, and internal structure—all sensitive data.

She lifted her head. "Who's this for?"

Damon nodded toward Zane. "Hope Base."

Iris just dropped a few chips onto the form. "Done."

Damon stared, speechless.

She brushed off her pants and stood. "So Hope Base provides the map, and your people provide the firepower?"

Damon nodded. "Yes."

Iris gestured for him to lean closer. He bent down, his ear near her.

With their backs to the others, she murmured, "Want to pull off something big?"

After a short pause, Damon said helplessly, "Iris, don't start trouble."

Iris scoffed. "Then tell me, how are the supplies split?"

"Hope Base takes forty percent, I take forty percent, and the last twenty goes to the smaller bases based on contribution."

To keep her from causing chaos, he added quickly, "Iris, I don't want you fighting. Just come with me to collect our share. Stay out of trouble."

Iris tilted her face up toward the sky and sighed inwardly at how simple-minded Damon was.

Teaming up with such an honest man...

But without him, she wouldn't be getting such a good deal. Better not push it.

She sighed, "Alright. But you said I don't have to fight."

Damon nodded firmly. "Mm."

Iris slapped the untouched form into his hand, then turned and walked back to her car with Logan.

One by one, the other teams submitted their completed forms.

Once everything was collected, night deepened. The various bases set up camp around their vehicles.

Naturally, Hope Base pitched their tents right in the center, surrounded and protected by everyone else. While the other teams took turns patrolling, Hope Base's members simply rested.

Iris settled into the car, reclining the back seat to sleep alone. Logan set up a tent outside and stood guard.

Just as Iris closed her eyes, she heard a faint rustling sound.

Shush... shush...

Something was dragging through the grass.

As a psychic metahuman, Iris's hearing was far sharper than most.

Sensing something off outside, she sat up immediately and peered through the window. But the world outside was pitch black.

Heavy clouds blotted out the sky, covering the moon completely. Not a glimmer of light could be seen.

To avoid drawing zombies, no one was using lights.

The rustling grew louder in her ears. She unleashed her full mental power outward.

Her current range was just over seventy feet—barely anything on this desolate roadside.

Then the thick clouds slowly drifted apart. An enormous blood-red moon broke into view.

The deep crimson moon hung in the black sky like an open wound, eerie and oppressive, casting a faint, ghastly glow over the ground.

At last, Iris could see clearly into the wilderness beyond the road.

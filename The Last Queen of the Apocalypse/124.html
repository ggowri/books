Chapter 124 No Qualifications to Negotiate

Zane's face turned pitch black. "We led you here."

"Just answer the question!" Iris's gaze pierced him. "Did any of you from Hope Base kill even one zombie? Just one!"

Zane's lips pressed into a hard line. He couldn't say a word.

Kill zombies? Of course not.

That was dangerous, dirty work—not their role.

Iris let out a cold laugh. "Yesterday, you said work done by others doesn't count. No contribution, no reward. But I'm generous. I'll give you a chance.

"Ask anyone here if any team fought on your behalf. If even one person speaks up, I'll count it as your contribution."

Iris threw his own words back at him, publicly exposing his hypocrisy.

She was really being 'nice' now.

Zane and his team scanned the crowd hopefully. But not a single person moved.

He had thoroughly alienated everyone. Everyone had seen how Hope Base acted the entire trip. And his latest proposal had cut their shares in half.

Why the hell would anyone stand up for him? And helping him would also mean angering Iris.

They weren't K1 Base with close ties and real clout. They had no reason to risk it.

"See?" Iris smiled sweetly at Zane. "Even when I'm willing to bend the rules, no one wants to help you."

Zane's fists clenched. "Captain Hale, do not forget—we brought you to this location!"

"Tsk. Let me correct you. You brought them." Iris wagged a finger. "Captain Foster, are you having memory problems? You wrote me off a long time ago. You cut me out of the distribution, didn't even count me as part of the group.

"You can't deny me my share and then claim the map counts for me too, right? That doesn't make any sense."

Zane's knuckles whitened. He stepped toward Iris, and his overwhelming psionic domain began to spread.

Just as it was about to reach her, a katana pressed against his chest.

The second Iris drew her sword, Zane's men raised their guns and aimed at her.

Derek and the others didn't wait for Damon's order. They instantly unlocked their weapons and targeted Hope Base.

Iris didn't flinch. She shouted, "Disarm Hope Base—hundred tons of grain to whoever does it!"

In an instant, fighters from every base swarmed forward and stripped Hope Base's members of their guns.

Most of them were spatial-type metahumans or researchers with weak combat skills. They stood no chance.

Zane's expression shattered.

"Everyone halt!" he snapped.

Iris giggled. "Oh, Zane, you really are too young to read the room. You can't order anyone to stop—only yourself."

"Captain Hale, you are unbearably arrogant," Zane gritted out.

He stared at Iris for a second, then retracted his domain.

Immediately afterward, a hard kick to the chest sent him flying.

Iris stood over him, looking down. "Prejudiced and ignorant, weak and reckless—that's real arrogance.

"Let me make this clear: I saved all your lives. The grain is mine to give, not share. You never had the right to negotiate. If we're tallying debts, Hope Base owes me your lives.

"This is what I mean. If I were you, I'd keep my head down."

She stepped lightly on his chest with a smile.

Meeting her eyes, Zane's usually calm heart raced.

He saw the aura of a ferocious tiger.

Truly powerful people radiate dominance from the soul—gender and age mean nothing.

Iris had the strength, the skill, the right to rule.

He truly had no standing to negotiate with her.

From the start, he'd clung to Hope Base's reputation, thinking he could still control the situation. But he'd offended all the other bases.

And Base K1 stood with Iris.

From beginning to end, he never belonged at the negotiating table.

If Iris refused to give him a single grain, he had no counter—morally, logically, or by force.

Morally, Iris had saved them.

Logically, he had excluded her from his own plan.

By force? Don't even bother.

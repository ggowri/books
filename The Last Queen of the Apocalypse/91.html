Chapter 91 Midsummer Base General Store

The nearby residents rushed over to help.

"Ms. Hale, what's all this?"

"Ms. Hale, what's it for?"

Iris announced proudly, "We're opening a store."

Everyone froze in surprise.

Half an hour later, a fully stocked supermarket—ordinary before the apocalypse, but extraordinary now—stood before them.

Staring at the shelves lined with food of every kind, everyone felt like they were dreaming.

They could actually shop just like the old days.

In the center of the store hung a large electronic screen listing all item prices, with Irie's small digital avatar floating beside them.

Iris went on, "This is the Midsummer Base General Store. This is Irie, our base administrator. From now on, whenever you want to buy something, just come in.

"No card needed—Irie will use facial recognition to access your account and deduct points automatically for whatever you take.

"If you have items to trade, just set them on the shelf to the right. There's an electronic scale; once weighed, Irie will update your balance right away.

"If you want to double-check, there's a card reader at the door to review your balance on the way out."

Sophie's eyes lit up. "Wow! I wanna try!"

"Go ahead."

Iris nodded, and Sophie immediately darted to the snack aisle, grabbed a lollipop, tore open the wrapper right there in the store, and began munching happily.

Nathan pulled out Sophie's card and scanned it.

Beep. The screen showed: 99.5 points.

Sophie's card had held exactly 100 points before—a bonus for her family's work and Iris hiring her as a helper. The lollipop had deducted 0.5 points automatically, leaving 99.5.

Nathan felt a mix of joy and reluctance at the 0.5-point cost for one piece of candy.

He was happy his daughter could enjoy snacks.

This was the apocalypse, after all. Outside, zombies roamed, people starved, and millions fled for their lives... yet here in their base, they could still eat lollipops. That took unimaginable supplies and security.

But he also winced a little.

Points weren't easy to earn. One point could buy a full pound of grain. Sophie just spent half a point on a single lollipop—way too pricey.

He'd have to keep an eye on her spending from now on, so she didn't burn through all her points.

"Ms. Hale, I wanna trade some supplies!" Kevin called out. "I've been growing cucumbers. Can I trade those?"

"Of course," Iris nodded.

Kevin immediately ran back and returned with five pounds of fresh cucumbers.

After weighing, the store system spoke, "Beep. Five pounds of cucumbers. Value: 2.5 points. Confirm exchange?"

Vegetables were worth half as much as grain.

Kevin grinned. "Confirm!"

Once the trade went through, Kevin checked his account and saw the extra 2.5 points immediately.

Everyone else burst into excitement at the sight.

They crowded into the store—some buying goods, some trading produce for points, others just wandering and looking.

Inside were all the daily essentials: flour, grain, cooking oil, salt, sugar, toiletries, tissues, sanitary products, and a small but nostalgic selection of pre-apocalypse snacks: lollipops, cola, soft bread rolls.

In an instant, everyone felt a deep sense of comfort, as if they'd stepped back into the world before the apocalypse.

They all shared the same thought: Midsummer Base was getting better every single day.

Iris had promised them a good life, and she was delivering. Everyone's sense of belonging to the base surged.

Just then, a notification sounded in Iris's mind.

[Beep. Base prosperity increased by 100.]

[Current base prosperity: 180.]

Iris was surprised one small store had boosted prosperity so much. But when she thought about it, it made perfect sense.

Trade was a basic human need. Even in her past life, in those small, resource-scarce bases, there had always been busy trading zones where people bartered constantly.

An internal base store was absolutely essential.

Watching the busy scene, Iris imagined the future: once farm production stabilized, she could set up full-scale farming, livestock breeding, fish ponds, and then processing plants...

Her base would grow steadily, built on real, solid strength.

After finishing the store setup, Iris returned to her bunker.

Restocking and deliveries were entirely handled by Irie—she didn't need to lift a finger.

Irie would send drones to transport supplies from the warehouse based on real-time inventory. When stock ran low or consumption spiked, Irie would alert Iris so she could adjust the inventory list if needed.

Irie was the perfect assistant, solving her biggest headache: not having enough people.

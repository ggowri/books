Chapter 298

Collins’ face had already turned pale, yet he kept shaking his head stubbornly.

“No… no… this isn’t real,” he muttered. “I didn’t do it.”

Even with the clear recording echoing through the hall, he continued denying it. His head moved repeatedly from side to side as if refusing to accept the reality in front of him.

“I didn’t do it,” Collins insisted louder. “This is fake! Charles fabricated everything!”

The police officer’s expression darkened.

He had already heard enough.

Slowly, the officer turned toward the two girls standing beside him. His face was stern now, and the patience in his eyes had completely disappeared.

“Look,” the officer said coldly, his voice sharp and commanding. “You two are minors. The law is already willing to give you some leniency because of that.”

The girls froze.

“But if you continue lying to the police,” he continued, “you will spend even more time in jail. So think carefully before you speak again.”

The two girls gulped nervously.

Their earlier confidence was gone.Fear had replaced it.

At that moment Charles stepped forward calmly.

“If you tell the truth,” he said evenly, “I will triple whatever amount he promised you.”

The room instantly went silent.

Everyone stared at the girls.

The two girls looked at each other for a few seconds.

Then their defenses completely collapsed.

“Okay! Okay! We’ll tell the truth!” one of them suddenly cried out.

The other nodded quickly.

“Yes! We will say everything!”

The crowd erupted into whispers.

“So Collins really did it?”

….

The first girl pointed toward Collins with trembling fingers.

“He paid us,” she said.

The hall fell silent again.

Everyone stared at Collins.

The girl continued speaking nervously.

“He approached us first,” she explained. “He told us to get close to Charles and make it look like he slept with us.”

The second girl hurriedly added, “He said if we could make it convincing enough, it would ruin Charles completely.”

Gasps spread through the crowd.

“And we have evidence,” the first girl said quickly.

She pulled out her phone with shaky hands.

“Look.”

She opened several messages and payment records. “There are transfers,” she said. “He paid us part of the money already.”

The police officer stepped forward and examined the phone.

There were clearly recorded payments.

Screenshots of voice calls.And messages discussing the plan.

The whispers inside the hall became louder.

“What the heck…”

“It’s over for him.”

“He really framed Charles.”

“I can’t believe someone would do this.”

People looked at Collins with shock and disgust. Some even moved away from him as if they no longer wanted to stand near him.

Across the room Charles calmly turned his gaze toward Collins.

Collins stood frozen.

His face had gone completely stiff as he stared at the evidence.

The entire situation had turned against him.

Charles looked at him coldly.

“These girls are minors,” Charles said slowly. “And you involved them in something this disgusting.”

He shook his head slightly.

“You are more horrible than I thought.”

Collins immediately shouted back.

“No! That’s not true!”

His voice cracked.

“They are lying! All of them are lying!”

But the police officer had already heard enough. He stepped forward.

“Mr. Collins,” the officer said firmly, “you are being placed under arrest for fabricating evidence and for involvement in criminal activity with minors.”

The officer signaled to the other policemen.

They stepped toward Collins.

Across the hall Tracy suddenly stood up in shock. “I don’t believe any of this,” she said firmly.

Her eyes moved between Collins and Charles.

“If you are going to arrest him… then you will have to arrest Charles too.”

Charles looked at Tracy calmly.

“Why should I be arrested?” he asked. “What exactly did I do?”

Tracy’s expression was tense. Her emotions were clearly in turmoil.

“Who knows?” she said sharply. “You are always a master planner. Maybe this is your grand scheme from the beginning.”

Several people in the hall murmured again.

Charles simply looked at her without reacting.

The police officer who had been handling the situation shook his head.

“All the evidence currently points to Mr. Collins,” he said firmly. “If we need to question Mr. Charles later, we will call him. But right now, he is not under arrest.”

Tracy refused to accept it.

“No,” she said stubbornly. “I will never accept this. No!”

At that exact moment, the doors of the hall opened again.

Another group of police officers walked in.

Their boots echoed across the quiet floor as they approached.

The officer leading the first team frowned in confusion. The new team leader stopped in front of him and both men exchanged a quick salute.

They looked at each other with clear confusion.

“What are you doing here?” the first officer asked.

Then the second officer turned his attention toward Charles.

“Are you Charles?” he asked.

Charles nodded calmly.

Before anyone else could speak, Tracy let out a cold laugh.

“You see?” she said bitterly. “He’s already in another problem. I told you we can’t trust someone like him. im sure they are here to arrest him”

But the officer shook his head.

“We are not here to arrest him.”

The officer looked directly at Charles.

“We verified what you reported earlier,” he said. “Everything you told us is true. Thank you for informing us.”

Then he slowly turned toward Collins.

His expression became serious.

“Mr. Collins,” he said firmly, “you are under arrest for fraud, impersonation, attempted financial deception, and falsely claiming business connections to obtain money.”

The room exploded with shock.

“What the heck?”

“What does that mean?”

“Fraud? Did they just say con?”

People stared in confusion.

Collins’ entire body trembled.

He already knew exactly what this was about.

Tracy stepped forward quickly.

“What the hell is this?” she demanded. “What are these charges about?”

The officer answered calmly.

“Mr. Collins lied about knowing Mr. Akeem in Dubai. He claimed he had secured a business deal with him.”

Several guests gasped.

“We contacted Mr. Akeem to verify the claim,” the officer continued. “He confirmed that he has no idea who Mr. Collins is.”

The hall fell into stunned silence.

“He was planning to deceive you,” the officer said, looking at Tracy. “After receiving the investment money, he intended to escape. In fact, he had already arranged for a boat to leave tonight.”

At that moment Tracy’s vision suddenly blurred.
